# Which Subsidies Can Chinese Business Owners in Japan Get for a Small Shop or Retail Store? A Complete Guide

For Chinese business owners running retail stores in Japan—especially small shops, convenience stores, and food stores—the question of whether they can apply for Japanese subsidies is both confusing and full of promise. The truth is, **the Japanese government and local municipalities offer a wide variety of subsidies for small and mid-sized retailers**, and as long as you understand the policies and prepare thoroughly, Chinese business owners in Japan can absolutely secure meaningful financial support. This article walks you through exactly what retail subsidies are available to Chinese business owners in Japan.

## The Main Types of Subsidies Available to Chinese-Owned Retail Stores in Japan

### Licensing and Residency Requirements

First, let's clear up a key question: **do you need Japanese nationality or permanent residency to apply for a subsidy?** The answer is no. Chinese business owners in Japan are eligible to apply for Japanese subsidies as long as they hold one of the following statuses:

- Permanent residency
- Long-term residency (3 years or more)
- A corporate representative who holds Japanese nationality or permanent residency
- A completed corporate registration such as a kabushiki kaisha (K.K.) or limited company

This means that even if you hold a work visa or a Business Manager visa, you can still apply for subsidies by setting up a corporation jointly with a Japanese spouse or a Japanese business partner.

### Baseline Business Conditions

A retail store applying for a subsidy needs to meet the following:
- A registered business license (営業許可)
- A physical storefront and inventory
- Monthly revenue within a certain range (generally JPY 500,000 or more)
- At least 1–2 employees or clearly designated operating staff
- A bank account and complete bookkeeping records

## Local Japanese Subsidies: The Main Categories of Retail Store Subsidies

### Regional Revitalization Business Subsidy (地方創生推進交付金)

This is **the most common and most accessible retail subsidy**. It is funded by the national government and administered on the ground by local municipalities (prefectures and cities/wards/towns).

**Subsidy amount:** JPY 500,000–5,000,000 (depending on the region and the scale of the business)

**Eligibility requirements:**
- Opening a new store or expanding operations in a designated area
- Submitting a business plan that includes a 3-year operating plan
- Demonstrating that the business can create jobs or stimulate local consumption

**Application process:**
1. Confirm the current fiscal year's subsidy announcement with your local municipality
2. Prepare a business plan, financial projections, and proof of identity
3. Submit your application (usually between April and June)
4. Undergo review (2–3 months)
5. After approval, sign an agreement and receive payments in line with your progress

Mr. Wu, a Chinese business owner from Hangzhou, opened a food retail store in Itoshima City, Fukuoka Prefecture, and received JPY 3,000,000 through the regional revitalization subsidy, which he used for renovations, shelving, and initial inventory—successfully lowering his startup costs.

### Small-Business Leadership Reinforcement Support Program (小商業等リーダーシップ強化支援事業)

Led by the Small and Medium Enterprise Agency, this subsidy program is **aimed specifically at small retail stores with annual revenue under JPY 50,000,000**.

**Subsidy amount:** JPY 500,000–2,000,000

**Key supported activities:**
- Digital transformation (POS systems, online sales)
- Store design and renovation
- Employee training and professionalization
- Product development and marketing

**Notable features:**
- Relatively friendly to Chinese business owners, with a comparatively lenient review
- Does not require creating new jobs—support for existing operations is fine
- Can be combined with other subsidies at the same time (you cannot apply twice for the same program)

Ms. Wu, who runs a Chinese-ingredient store in Shinjuku, Tokyo, applied for JPY 500,000 through this subsidy to build an e-commerce platform and a WeChat storefront, successfully raising her share of online sales from 10% to 35%.

### Productivity Revolution Promotion Program (生産性革命推進事業)

**Eligible recipients:** All small and mid-sized retail businesses, including sole proprietors

**Subsidy amount:** Up to JPY 8,000,000 for equipment purchases, up to JPY 3,500,000 for software adoption

**Scope of support:**
- POS and inventory management systems
- Hardware such as refrigerated cases and heating equipment
- Building online sales platforms
- Security and surveillance systems

**Special advantage:** This subsidy typically covers **two-thirds of the cost**, so the business only bears one-third. One Chinese owner running a convenience store in Osaka invested JPY 300,000 of his own money, received a JPY 600,000 subsidy, and successfully upgraded all of his POS equipment.

## Subsidy Schemes Targeting Specific Business Types

### Food Retail Store Subsidy (食品流通構造改善事業)

**Applicable store types:**
- Fresh food stores
- Chinese-ingredient stores
- Imported food stores
- Convenience stores (with food sales)

**Subsidy amount:** JPY 300,000–3,000,000

**Supported projects:**
- Food safety certification (HACCP adoption)
- Cold-chain equipment maintenance and upgrades
- Food waste processing systems
- Building direct-from-source supply systems

Many Chinese operators have used this subsidy to upgrade from traditional sales to standardized cold-chain operations—not only securing funding but also strengthening their food-safety competitiveness.

### Tourist-Area Retail Store Subsidy (観光地活性化事業)

**Applicable scope:**
- Retail stores located in popular tourist areas
- Stores selling local specialties or cultural/creative goods
- Stores that can attract tourist spending

Chinese owners running stores in tourist destinations such as Kyoto and Osaka can additionally apply for a tourist-area subsidy, usually in the range of JPY 500,000–5,000,000. The condition is that the store must demonstrate it can increase tourist spending or promote local culture.

### Shopping-District Revitalization Subsidy (商店街活性化事業)

**Eligibility requirements:**
- Membership in the local shopping-district association
- Location within a designated shopping district
- A commitment to participate in joint shopping-district events

**Subsidy amount:** JPY 200,000–2,000,000

**Supported activities:**
- Store signage and exterior remodeling
- Joint shopping-district marketing
- Costs of joint promotional events

This is **the most easily overlooked yet also the most accessible subsidy**. Many shopping districts have a budget every year, but applicants are in short supply. Joining a shopping district requires only simple procedures and opens up additional subsidy opportunities.

## Region-Specific Subsidies and Tax Incentives

### The Unique Subsidies of Municipalities Nationwide

Each of Japan's 1,741 cities, wards, towns, and villages has its own dedicated subsidy policies. Chinese business owners can look these up on the website of their local municipality.

**Common types:**
- Tokyo's 23 wards: new-business startup subsidy (up to JPY 3,000,000)
- Kanagawa Prefecture: SME management reinforcement subsidy
- Osaka Prefecture: startup challenge subsidy (up to JPY 5,000,000)
- Kyoto City: traditional-commerce revitalization subsidy
- Fukuoka Prefecture: entrepreneur support subsidy (up to JPY 4,000,000)

### The Small Enterprise Mutual Aid System (小規模企業共済)

Strictly speaking, this is not a subsidy, but it is **a system that every small retail store owner should join**.

**Features:**
- Monthly contributions of JPY 400–84,000 (your choice)
- All contributions are deductible before tax
- You can apply for low-interest loans when financing is difficult
- You can receive a lump sum or installments upon retirement or store closure

One Chinese store owner who had been operating for 5 years contributed JPY 80,000 per month for a cumulative total of JPY 4,800,000, and received JPY 1,850,000 in tax benefits—equivalent to subsidizing 38% of the cost.

## Concrete Steps and Points to Watch When Applying for a Subsidy

### The Detailed Application Process

**Step 1: Gathering information (1–2 weeks)**
- Visit the Small and Medium Enterprise Agency website (chusho.meti.go.jp)
- Check your local municipality's website for subsidy information
- Consult the local Chamber of Commerce and Industry
- Note the application deadlines (usually April and September)

**Step 2: Assessment and preparation (2–4 weeks)**
- Confirm that you meet the eligibility conditions
- Prepare your business license, tax-filing certificate, and proof of bank account
- Write your business plan (the key document)

**Step 3: Preparing application documents (3–5 weeks)**
- Business plan: describing your 3-year operating goals, financial projections, and competitive advantages
- Fund-usage plan: itemizing each expenditure in detail
- Current financial statement: including a balance sheet and income statement
- Proof of identity: passport copy, residence card, proof of residence
- Business documentation: business license, corporate registration certificate, tax-filing history

**Step 4: Submitting the application (1 day)**
- Can be submitted by mail, online system, or in person
- Keep all proof of submission
- Record the submission date and confirmation number

**Step 5: Waiting for review (1–3 months)**
- The subsidy provider may request supplementary explanations
- Reply promptly to emails and phone calls
- Keep your contact channels open

**Step 6: Execution after approval**
- Sign the subsidy agreement
- Carry out the business according to plan
- Keep all expenditure receipts and invoices
- Submit progress reports periodically
- Submit a summary report once the business is complete

### Common Reasons Applications Fail

Chinese applicants should pay particular attention to the following issues, which are the main reasons applications are rejected:

1. **Inappropriate Japanese expression**: Vague wording and unclear logic in the plan. We recommend inviting a Japanese person or a professional translator to help.

2. **Non-compliant financial records**: Disorganized bookkeeping, delayed tax filings, incomplete invoices. You must hire a professional accountant.

3. **Lack of clear business goals**: The subsidy provider cannot tell how the applicant will use the money. You need detailed, specific, measurable goals.

4. **Incomplete identity documents**: Failing to submit a residence-card copy, missing a certificate of no criminal record, and so on. Check off each item on the list before submitting.

5. **Duplicate applications with other subsidies**: The same program or the same expenditure cannot be paid for with multiple subsidies. Understand how subsidies work together.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: I'm a Chinese national with a work visa—can I apply for retail subsidies for Chinese business owners in Japan?

**A:** Applying directly as an individual will be difficult, because most subsidies require the applicant to have the intent of long-term residency. However, you can solve this by registering a kabushiki kaisha or limited company and becoming a representative director jointly with a Japanese spouse or a Japanese business partner. Once the corporation is established, the corporation itself can apply for subsidies even if you hold a work visa. We recommend consulting a specialist (a Gyoseishoshi) to complete the incorporation, at a cost of around JPY 100,000–200,000. Another approach is to wait until you obtain permanent residency and then apply on your own, which greatly simplifies the procedure.

### Q2: I've been running my store for 3 years already—can I still apply for a subsidy?

**A:** Yes. Many subsidies are aimed not only at new entrepreneurs but also support the expansion and improvement of existing businesses. The regional revitalization subsidy, the small-business leadership reinforcement support program, and the productivity revolution promotion program all accept applications from existing businesses. The key is to demonstrate that the subsidy will be used to **expand operations, upgrade equipment, develop new products, or enter new markets**. For example, if you want to expand from offline to online sales, upgrade your store equipment, or hire new staff, these are all reasonable uses for a subsidy. But if your reason for applying is simply "business difficulties," it will usually be rejected. We recommend drawing up a clear 3-year growth plan to support your application.

### Q3: Does a subsidy have to be repaid? Are there obligations after applying?

**A:** A subsidy **does not need to be repaid**—it is a policy tool by which the government supports businesses. But a subsidy is not "free money"; you need to fulfill specific obligations. First, you must use the funds according to the approved plan and cannot divert them to other uses. Second, you need to keep all expenditure receipts (invoices, receipts, contracts, etc.) for auditing. Third, you must submit periodic progress reports and a final completion report. Fourth, some subsidies require you to keep the business operating for 3–5 years after receiving the subsidy. Fifth, the subsidy provider may conduct an on-site inspection. These obligations may seem complicated, but as long as you operate properly and keep careful records, they are not difficult to fulfill. We recommend hiring a professional accountant to help handle them, at a cost typically of 2–5% of the subsidy amount.

### Q4: How long does it take to apply for a subsidy? When can I receive the money?

**A:** From application to finally receiving the subsidy usually takes **6–12 months**. The specific flow is: submit the application (April or September deadline) → review period (6–8 weeks) → approval notice (about 2 months later) → sign the agreement (1–2 weeks) → begin business execution (actual expenditures take 3–12 months) → submit the completion report (2–4 weeks) → audit and acceptance (1–2 months) → subsidy payment (usually paid in 2–3 installments). This means that if you apply in April, you may receive the first payment around December at the earliest. So, when applying for a subsidy, don't treat it as a means to solve an immediate cash crunch, but rather as financial support for 3–6 months down the road. We recommend preparing cash flow 3–6 months in advance to bridge this timing gap.

### Q5: If the subsidized project fails or doesn't achieve its planned goals, will the subsidy be clawed back?

**A:** That depends on the nature of the failure. If the goals were not met **due to objective reasons** (such as a pandemic, disaster, or market changes), it usually will not be clawed back. For example, if you planned to hire 3 employees but only hired 2 because a pandemic reduced foot traffic, this kind of situation is generally understood. But if it was **due to improper use of the subsidy** (such as using a store-renovation subsidy to buy a personal home, or cutting corners so that actual expenditure is far below the declared amount), then you will be required to repay it and may face fines and credit damage. In the most serious cases, the subsidy provider will claw back the full amount and demand a penalty. That is why honest, proper use of the subsidy is essential. If during execution you find you cannot complete the plan, you must proactively report to the subsidy provider and apply to revise the plan—this is seen as a responsible attitude and can usually earn understanding.
