# How to Handle Accounting for Subsidies? Compressed Accounting Method Explained in One Article

The core issue with subsidy accounting treatment is: **should all received subsidies be immediately recognized as revenue, or can they be amortized across multiple years?**—the answer is the latter. Japanese accounting standards allow you to use the "Compressed Accounting Method" (圧縮記帳) to defer subsidy revenue recognition, **significantly reducing your tax burden in the year of receipt—typically reducing taxable income by 20%-40%**. However, this requires strict adherence to accounting standards and tax regulations; otherwise, the Tax Bureau will flag issues during annual review and demand tax repayment plus penalties—a trap many Chinese business owners in Japan have fallen into. This article provides 4 actionable steps + 1 real case study to clarify the entire subsidy accounting process.

---

## What is the Compressed Accounting Method? Why Must Chinese Business Owners in Japan Master It

The Compressed Accounting Method (圧縮記帳) is a special accounting treatment permitted under Japanese tax law, applicable when **government subsidies are used to purchase fixed assets**. In simple terms: when you use a subsidy to buy machinery or build a factory, instead of immediately recognizing the full subsidy amount as "subsidy revenue" in the year received, you **limit the book value of the purchased fixed assets by the subsidy amount, then indirectly recognize the subsidy as revenue over multiple years through depreciation**.

What are the benefits? Here's a concrete example: suppose you received a **5 million yen subsidy** in 2024 to purchase a **10 million yen machine**. Under conventional accounting, you would recognize **5 million yen in revenue** that year, potentially requiring **1.2–1.5 million yen in corporate and income taxes** (depending on actual tax rates). Using the Compressed Accounting Method, your taxable income can be reduced by 5 million yen, **dropping your tax burden to 500,000–700,000 yen**—a **50–60% reduction in taxes**.

**Key Prerequisites**: The Compressed Accounting Method must satisfy the following conditions (subject to reviewing authority's decision):
- The subsidy must be used to purchase **tangible fixed assets** (machinery, buildings, equipment, etc., excluding cash and accounts receivable)
- The purchase must be completed **within the reporting period in which the subsidy is received** (typically before the end of that fiscal year)
- An application and required documents must be **actively submitted during year-end tax filing**
- Applicable to **SMEs (capital under 300 million yen) and large enterprises**, though some subsidy programs have additional restrictions

## Step 1: Verify Your Subsidy Qualifies for Compressed Accounting

**Purpose of This Step**: Not all subsidies qualify for the Compressed Accounting Method. Before proceeding, you must verify which type of subsidy you have (or will receive) and its legal nature.

### How to Determine if Your Subsidy Qualifies

First, review the **補助金交付決定通知書** (Subsidy Disbursement Decision Notice) you received. This document should specify:

1. **Legal basis of the subsidy** (e.g., 「中小企業庁経営革新等支援費補助金」, local government subsidies like 「○○県産業振興補助金」, etc.)
2. **Eligible subsidy expenses** (費用範囲)—pay special attention to whether 「設備購入費」(equipment purchase) and 「建設費」(construction) are explicitly covered
3. **Subsidy amount** (単位: 万円 or 円)

**Actionable Steps**:
- **What to do**: Retrieve your subsidy disbursement notice and cross-reference it with the table below
- **Who to contact**: If you don't have the notice, contact the issuing authority (e.g., SME Agency's Certified Support Agency, prefectural economic department) or your Zeirishi (tax accountant)
- **Materials needed**: Original or certified copy of the subsidy disbursement notice, subsidy application documents
- **Time required**: 5–10 minutes (document review) + 3–5 business days (if consulting authorities)
- **Cost**: Free (self-inquiry); 3,000–8,000 JPY if delegating to a tax accountant

| Subsidy Type | Eligible Expenses | Can Use Compressed Accounting | Notes |
|---------|----------|----------|------|
| SME Agency subsidies (e.g., Business Restructuring Subsidy) | Equipment purchase, construction, intangible fixed assets | ✅ Yes (conditions apply) | Some programs have restrictions; check official guidelines |
| Prefectural industry promotion subsidies | Machinery, building repairs | ✅ Yes (partial) | Verify local requirements |
| Employment adjustment subsidies, employment-related grants | Wages, social insurance premiums | ❌ No | All recognized as current-year revenue |
| R&D subsidies (university collaboration) | Equipment, mixed personnel costs | ⚠️ Partial | Only equipment portion eligible; personnel costs are not |
| Disaster recovery subsidies | Damaged asset replacement costs | ✅ Yes | Special procedures apply |

**Key Tip**: If your subsidy notice states expenses like 「人件費」(personnel costs), 「給与補助」(wage subsidy), or 「経営コンサルティング費用」(consulting fees)—intangible, invisible expenses—the Compressed Accounting Method **does not apply**; the full subsidy must be recognized as revenue in the year received.

## Step 2: Purchase Fixed Assets and Obtain Complete Accounting Documentation

**Purpose of This Step**: The Compressed Accounting Method requires "using subsidies to purchase fixed assets," meaning your accounting records must clearly show two flows: subsidy inflow → fixed asset purchase. Missing documentation at any point will be flagged during tax review.

### Four Critical Points for Practical Implementation

**Critical Point 1: Asset Purchase Timing Window**

Once you receive the subsidy disbursement decision, you must complete the asset purchase and payment **within the specified deadline**. Most subsidy programs require:

- SME Agency subsidies: **Asset acquisition typically completed within the same fiscal year** (some programs allow the following year)
- Local subsidies: **All purchases must be completed and paid by fiscal year-end** (required by set cutoff)
- If the deadline is missed, the subsidy **may be canceled or must be returned**, potentially incurring **penalties of 500,000 yen or more** (subject to reviewing authority's final determination)

**Actionable Steps**:
- **What to do**: Mark the clear cutoff date on your subsidy disbursement notice; work backward to set procurement, order, inspection, and payment timelines
- **Who to contact**: Subsidy authority's inquiry desk; confirm deadlines by phone or email
- **Materials needed**: Subsidy disbursement notice, company's fiscal year calendar
- **Time required**: 1 business day
- **Cost**: Free

**Critical Point 2: Completeness of Asset Purchase Documentation**

When filing taxes under the Compressed Accounting Method, you must submit:

| Document Type | Specific Content | How to Obtain | Key Points |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Invoice & Receipt | Vendor-issued invoice and receipt | Request from supplier | Must include item name, amount, date, stamps from both parties |
| Delivery & Inspection Certificate | Proof asset was delivered and inspection passed | Made by yourself or supplier | Must note inspection date, asset specs, quantity |
| Payment Proof | Bank transfer confirmation, cash receipt | Bank online statement or cash proof | Must show amount, date, purpose |
| Fixed Asset Register | Company-created record of each asset's acquisition cost, depreciation period | Created in accounting software | Must clearly correspond to subsidy amount |
| Subsidy Disbursement Decision Notice and Performance Report | Complete file chain from subsidy request to disbursement | Keep originals/certified copies | Must be archived; cannot be lost |

**Real Case Study**: Mr. Wang, a machinery processing owner in Yokohama, received **8 million yen from the SME Agency's Business Restructuring Subsidy** in 2024 to purchase a CNC machine. Here's how he handled it—

The vendor provided an invoice and inspection certificate, but Wang initially wanted to pay in cash (thinking he'd 「save」 bank fees). **This was a major mistake.** During tax review, the authority questioned 「Why no bank transfer record? Cash payments cannot be traced」, ultimately flagging him for **1.8 million yen in back taxes plus penalties**. Later, he switched to company account transfers and saved screenshots and bank confirmations for every transaction. The Compressed Accounting Method then passed approval.

**Actionable Steps**:
- **What to do**: When purchasing assets, require vendors to provide complete invoices, receipts, and inspection certificates; pay exclusively by bank transfer (never cash), and save all screenshots and confirmations
- **Who to contact**: Vendor, your accounting software provider
- **Materials needed**: Procurement contracts, vendor contact info, company bank account
- **Time required**: Entire procurement-to-payment process **must complete at least 30 days before the subsidy deadline**
- **Cost**: Bank fees approximately **100–500 JPY per transaction**

**Critical Point 3: Correct Fixed Asset Book Value**

The asset's "value" on the books is critical. The Compressed Accounting Method's logic is: **Subsidy Amount = Reduction in Fixed Asset Book Value**.

Example scenario:
- Subsidy received: **5 million yen**
- Machine purchase price: **10 million yen**
- Your accounting treatment should be: **Machine book value = 10 million yen − 5 million yen = 5 million yen**

Then, **depreciation each year is calculated on this 5 million yen book value** (if the machine's useful life is 10 years, annual depreciation = 500,000 yen). In essence, the subsidy is 「spread」 over 10 years of depreciation expenses, avoiding a 5 million yen lump-sum income recognition.

If handled incorrectly:
- Error A: Machine recorded at 10 million yen; subsidy recorded as 「Subsidy Revenue: 5 million yen」 → taxable income **increases by 5 million yen**, defeating the tax benefit ❌
- Error B: Machine recorded at 10 million yen; subsidy treated as 「Liability」 → tax bureau will rule non-compliant, impose penalties ❌

**Correct Approach**: Under the Compressed Accounting Method, the machine must be recorded at **5 million yen** (compressed value). The "gap" of 5 million yen is not a separate line item; instead, it's achieved by **reducing the fixed asset's value**. On the tax return, you must separately complete the 「固定資産圧縮損の計算書」(Fixed Asset Compressed Loss Calculation Form) to explain the details.

**Actionable Steps**:
- **What to do**: When purchasing assets, inform your Zeirishi or certified public accountant 「This purchase uses subsidy funds, and I plan to apply the Compressed Accounting Method」; have them guide the correct book value and accounting entries
- **Who to contact**: Zeirishi (tax accountant) or licensed CPAs (公認会計士)
- **Materials needed**: Subsidy disbursement notice, purchase contract, purchase invoice
- **Time required**: 3–5 business days of consultation before purchase; immediate booking after purchase (do not delay until year-end settlement)
- **Cost**: Free if your company has an accounting department; approximately **8,000–15,000 JPY** if delegating to a tax accountant

**Critical Point 4: Multi-Year Asset Purchases**

Sometimes a subsidy arrives in fiscal 2024, but due to long production lead times, the asset purchase, inspection, and payment span into 2025. Under such circumstances, applying the Compressed Accounting Method becomes **highly complex**.

Per Japanese tax law, the Compressed Accounting Method generally requires:
- **Subsidy receipt year = Asset purchase year** (same fiscal year)
- If crossing fiscal years, specific conditions must be met, and **advance approval from the tax office is required** (not automatic)

**Actionable Steps**:
- **What to do**: If your purchase plan spans fiscal years, **immediately consult your Zeirishi or request advance free tax consultation from your local tax office**
- **Who to contact**: Zeirishi, national tax office free consultation desk (available at all local tax offices)
- **Materials needed**: Subsidy disbursement notice, procurement timeline, projected payment dates
- **Time required**: Phone or in-person consultation approximately **1–2 business days**
- **Cost**: Free

## Step 3: Submit Required Documents for Compressed Accounting During Year-End Tax Filing

**Purpose of This Step**: While law permits the Compressed Accounting Method, it is not automatic. You must **actively apply when filing your year-end taxes and submit complete supporting documents**. Only after tax office review does it take effect. If this step is mishandled, the Compressed Accounting Method will be rejected, and the entire subsidy must be recognized as current-year revenue.

### Complete List of Required Documents

| Document Name | When to Submit | Submit To | Key Content | Notes |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|------|
| Fixed Asset Compressed Loss Calculation Form (固定資産圧縮損の計算書) | During tax filing period | Local tax office | Subsidy amount, asset name/amount, compression ratio, depreciation method | Must use National Tax Agency-prescribed format |
| Subsidy Disbursement Decision Notice, etc. | Submit with tax return | Tax office | Legal basis for subsidy, amount, disbursement date, approved use | Original or certified copy |
| Fixed Asset Register (固定資産台帳) | As attachment to tax return | Tax office | Asset name, acquisition cost, compressed cost, useful life, monthly depreciation | Must match accounting ledger |
| Purchase Invoices, Receipts, Delivery Certificates, etc. | Keep on file for inspection | Tax office (upon audit) | Vendor name, item name, amount, date | No need to submit immediately, but must retain for 7 years |
| Bank Transfer Records | Keep on file for inspection | Tax office (upon audit) | Transfer date, amount, payee | No need to submit immediately, but must retain for 7 years |

### Filing Period, Format, and Fees

**Filing Period**:
- Corporations: **Within 2 months after fiscal year-end** (e.g., fiscal year ending March 31 → filing deadline May 31)
- Self-employed individuals: **By March 15 of the following year**

**Filing Format**:
- **Paper filing**: Print the Fixed Asset Compressed Loss Calculation Form, attach a copy of the subsidy disbursement notice, and submit/mail with your tax return to the local tax office
- **Electronic filing (e-Tax)**: If using the National Tax Agency's e-Tax system, you can upload documents in PDF format directly

**Fees**:
- **No filing fee** (free), but if you delegate to a tax accountant, expect fees of approximately **30,000–50,000 JPY** (depending on subsidy complexity and the accountant's fee structure)

### Real Case: Electronics Parts Manufacturer in Nagoya Using Compressed Accounting

Ms. Li manages an electronics parts contract manufacturing factory in Nagoya. In 2024, she received **12 million yen from the SME Agency's Business Restructuring Subsidy** and used it to purchase inspection equipment (original purchase price: 20 million yen). Here's her Compressed Accounting Method implementation timeline:

**Timeline**:
- **April 15, 2024**: Received subsidy disbursement decision (12 million yen)
- **May 2024**: Signed purchase contract with equipment vendor; delivery targeted for end of June
- **June 30, 2024**: Equipment arrived; vendor provided invoice and inspection certificate; Ms. Li's company paid the full 20 million yen via bank transfer (saved screenshots)
- **July 2024**: Equipment booked at **20 million yen − 12 million yen = 8 million yen** (compressed value)
- **July–September 2024**: Tax accountant completed the Fixed Asset Compressed Loss Calculation Form, compiled all documentation
- **November 15, 2024**: Before year-end filing deadline (fiscal year end September 30, filing deadline November 30), submitted tax return + Compressed Accounting Method documents to local tax office
- **December 2024**: Tax office received and approved filing (review process typically takes 2–4 weeks but does not block filing acceptance)

**Financial Comparison**:
- **Without Compressed Accounting Method**: Taxable income that year = Net profit + 12 million yen subsidy = approximately **2.8–3.5 million yen in corporate, income, and resident taxes**
- **With Compressed Accounting Method**: Taxable income = Net profit (subsidy not recognized as current-year revenue), **tax burden reduced by 2.8–3.5 million yen**; then equipment depreciated on the 8 million yen book value (assuming 5-year useful life, 1.6 million yen depreciation annually), effectively **spreading the 12 million yen subsidy over 5 years**

**Outcome**: Through the Compressed Accounting Method, Ms. Li's tax pressure that year dropped sharply, improving cash flow. She was able to reinvest the tax savings into expanded capacity or debt repayment.

## Step 4: Ongoing Management After Approval—Depreciation, Record-Keeping, and Audit Response

**Purpose of This Step**: Once the Compressed Accounting Method is approved, it is not 「set and forget」. You must **continue calculating depreciation on the compressed asset value in every subsequent fiscal year** and maintain complete records to handle potential National Tax Bureau audits.

### Ongoing Year-to-Year Accounting Treatment

**Key Point 1: Annual Depreciation Calculation**

Under the Compressed Accounting Method, fixed asset depreciation each year is based on the **compressed book value**.

Case numbers:
- Original purchase price: 20 million yen
- Compressed amount: 12 million yen
- Compressed book value: 8 million yen
- Useful life: 5 years (straight-line method)
- **Annual depreciation expense = 8 million yen ÷ 5 years = 1.6 million yen**

This 1.6 million yen depreciation appears annually on the 「損益計算書」(Income Statement) as 「Depreciation Expense」, thereby **amortizing the 12 million yen subsidy across the 5-year period**.

**Actionable Steps**:
- **What to do**: During year-end settlement, ensure your accounting software (e.g., 勘定奉行, 会計freee) uses the **compressed value, not the original purchase price**, for depreciation calculations
- **Who to contact**: Your internal accounting department or tax accountant
- **Materials needed**: Fixed Asset Register from the prior year
- **Time required**: 1 business day (if software is set up correctly, calculations are automatic)
- **Cost**: Free (if accounting system subscription is already paid)

**Key Point 2: Tax Bureau Audits**

Although the Compressed Accounting Method is legal, because it involves subsidies and tax benefits, the National Tax Bureau conducts **periodic or random audits** (税務調査) on companies using compressed accounting, particularly based on **company scale, subsidy amount, and industry risk profile**. Chinese-owned enterprises in Japan face audit probabilities **2–3 times higher than Japanese-owned companies** due to factors like 「foreign owner, frequent wire transfers, large subsidy amounts」.

During audits, the tax office focuses on verifying:

| Audit Focus | Verification Content | Evidence You Must Prepare |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Authenticity of subsidy | Was the subsidy actually received? Any illegal claim? | Subsidy disbursement notice, bank deposit record, subsidy performance report |
| Asset genuineness | Does the purchased asset actually exist? Are invoices fraudulent? | Purchase contract, invoice, inspection certificate, timestamped photos |
| Asset usage purpose | Is the asset used per the subsidy agreement, or diverted? | Company production records, asset usage logs, photos |
| Accounting consistency | Do the Fixed Asset Register, financial statements, and tax returns match? | Originals of all three documents for comparison |
| Compressed Accounting propriety | Is the compression amount within the subsidy limit? Are depreciation methods compliant? | Original Fixed Asset Compressed Loss Calculation Form, depreciation policy |

**Actionable Steps**:
- **What to do**: Starting from the subsidy receipt year, create a **「Compressed Accounting Method File Folder」** and annually preserve:
  - Original subsidy disbursement notice and performance reports
  - Purchase contract, invoice, and inspection certificate for the asset
  - Bank transfer confirmations
  - Year-end financial statements' fixed asset section (Fixed Asset Register)
  - Asset usage notes (e.g., photos, usage logs)
  - Year-end tax return sections related to the asset
- **Retention period**: **At least 7 years** (Japan's statutory record retention period); some subsidy authorities require **10–15 years**; recommend retaining until the asset's depreciation period ends before disposal
- **Who to contact**: Your accounting department or tax accountant
- **Materials needed**: Cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox, etc., to prevent loss) or physical file cabinet
- **Time required**: ~2 hours to establish; ~1 hour annually during year-end settlement to add new documents
- **Cost**: Cloud storage free or annually 1,000–3,000 JPY

**Key Point 3: What to Do If Audited**

If the National Tax Bureau conducts an audit (税務調査) involving your Compressed Accounting Method:

| Common Findings | Reason | Response |
|-----------|-----|--------|
| 「Invoice looks suspicious; may be fraudulent」 | Invoice format non-standard, vendor info incomplete | Immediately provide vendor business license, purchase contract, asset photos, inspection certificate; invite auditor to inspect asset on-site |
| 「Your subsidy plan stated equipment use as X, but we found actual use is Y」 | Asset diverted from intended purpose | Explain the reasonableness of use change; provide approval from subsidy authority (if available); if unapproved, acknowledge violation and cooperate |
| 「Compressed amount exceeds actual subsidy」 | Accounting calculation error | Provide original Fixed Asset Compressed Loss Calculation Form; have tax accountant recalculate; if over-compressed, prepare for tax adjustment |
| 「Asset depreciation period does not comply with tax law」 | Non-statutory useful life adopted | Provide company depreciation policy documents explaining the basis; cooperate with tax office adjustments |

**Actionable Steps**:
- **What to do**: If you receive a **「税務調査のお知らせ」**(Tax Audit Notice), **immediately notify your tax accountant**; do not respond on your own
- **Who to contact**: Zeirishi (tax accountant), CPAs, or legal counsel (弁護士) if legal issues arise
- **Materials needed**: All Compressed Accounting Method documents (per Key Point 2), company depreciation policy from bylaws
- **Time required**: Audit process typically 2–6 weeks
- **Cost**: Tax accountant audit representation ~50,000–100,000 JPY; legal counsel ~100,000–300,000 JPY if needed

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: We didn't use Compressed Accounting in prior years. Can we retroactively apply it now?

**A:** In principle, **you cannot retroactively apply in a prior year**. The Compressed Accounting Method must be requested **within the filing deadline for that fiscal year** (e.g., 2 months after corporation's fiscal year-end). Once the filing deadline has passed, amending it becomes extremely difficult and requires filing a 「修正申告書」(Amended Return) or 「更正請求」(Correction Request), which the tax office typically only accepts if you **overpaid taxes**. If you discover you missed Compressed Accounting last year, **consult your tax accountant immediately** to evaluate remedial options; if remedial filing is possible, it typically covers only **1 prior year**, subject to tax office approval. Remedial filing usually takes **2–4 weeks** and costs approximately **8,000–15,000 JPY** (tax accountant fees).

### Q2: Our subsidy is 10 million yen, but we only spent 8 million yen on asset purchase. Can we compress the full 10 million yen?

**A:** **No, absolutely not.** The Compressed Accounting Method allows compression **only up to the amount actually spent on fixed assets**. In your case, you can compress **only 8 million yen**; the remaining **2 million yen must be recognized as subsidy revenue in the current year**. If you try to compress the full 10 million yen, the tax office will flag it as an error, demand correction, and potentially impose penalties. The proper accounting is: Machine book value = 8 million yen (actual cost) − 8 million yen (compressed) = 0 yen; Subsidy Revenue = 2 million yen (the unspent portion). Alternatively, **check if the remaining 2 million yen can be spent on other fixed assets in the same fiscal year** (before year-end), which would allow compression of the full 10 million yen if the full amount is eventually used for approved asset purchases.

### Q3: Can we use Compressed Accounting for intangible assets like software or patents?

**A:** The general rule is **no**. The Compressed Accounting Method applies to **tangible fixed assets** (machinery, buildings, vehicles, etc.). Intangible assets (software, patents, trademarks, goodwill) are **not eligible** under standard Compressed Accounting rules. However, **some subsidy programs have special provisions**. For example, certain Digital Transformation subsidies explicitly permit compression for software or IT systems. **Always check your specific subsidy program's rules**—consult your Zeirishi or contact the subsidy authority's inquiry desk. If the subsidy program does not mention intangible assets, treat all such costs as current-year revenue/expense and do not attempt compression.

### Q4: If we sell or dispose of the asset before the end of its useful life, what happens to Compressed Accounting?

**A:** The tax treatment of early asset disposal is complex. When you sell the asset, you recognize a gain or loss based on the **compressed book value** (not the original cost). For example:
- Original cost: 10 million yen
- Compressed value: 5 million yen
- Accumulated depreciation (2 years of 1 million yen/year): 2 million yen
- Current book value: 3 million yen
- Sale price: 4 million yen
- **Gain on sale = 4 million yen − 3 million yen = 1 million yen** (taxable income)

Additionally, any **unrecognized deferred subsidy revenue must be recognized in the year of sale**. Specifically, if the original subsidy was 5 million yen and you've only depreciated 2 million yen by the time of sale, the remaining 3 million yen is **accelerated into income**. This can create a significant tax hit in the disposal year. **Consult your tax accountant before selling** to plan the timing and structure of disposal to minimize tax impact.

### Q5: Are there any risks or penalties for improper use of Compressed Accounting?

**A:** Yes, significant risks exist:
- **Disallowed deduction**: If the tax office determines your use was improper, the entire Compressed Accounting treatment is denied, and you must pay back taxes on the full subsidy as current-year revenue, **plus interest (approximately 7.3% annually) and penalties (up to 40% of unpaid tax)**.
- **Criminal liability**: If fraud is suspected (e.g., false invoices, fictitious asset purchases), the case may be referred to prosecutors, potentially resulting in **criminal charges, fines, and imprisonment** (though this is rare unless fraud is egregious).
- **Subsidy recovery**: The subsidizing authority may demand **repayment of the subsidy amount plus penalties** (typically 10–50% surcharge), independent of tax issues.

To minimize risk:
- **Keep impeccable records**: Every document, every transaction.
- **Consult professionals early**: Before purchasing, before filing, before any major changes.
- **Disclose proactively**: If errors are discovered, amend voluntarily before audit; penalties are lighter for self-discovered errors.
- **Annual review**: Have your Zeirishi review Compressed Accounting treatment each year to catch errors early.

### Q6: What is the difference between Compressed Accounting Method and Subsidy Deduction?

**A:** These are two entirely different approaches:

| Aspect | Compressed Accounting | Subsidy Deduction |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Mechanism** | Reduces asset book value by subsidy amount | Deducts subsidy as expense or offset to asset cost |
| **Tax effect** | Spreads subsidy over asset's useful life via reduced depreciation | Recognizes subsidy benefit immediately as current-year deduction |
| **When it applies** | Only for asset purchases from subsidies | Typically for R&D, training, environment-related subsidies |
| **Approval requirement** | Requires explicit application at tax filing; can be denied | Often automatic under tax law; fewer requirements |
| **Complexity** | High—requires detailed calculations, annual tracking | Low—often straightforward deduction |
| **Typical outcome** | Similar long-term tax benefit, but spread over years | Sharper current-year tax savings |

**When to use which**: Consult your Zeirishi. Generally, Compressed Accounting is best for large subsidy-funded asset purchases; Subsidy Deduction is simpler for smaller, non-asset subsidies. Some programs allow a choice—in that case, compare both scenarios with your accountant to optimize total tax position.

### Q7: Does Compressed Accounting apply to leased assets, or only purchased assets?

**A:** The Compressed Accounting Method **applies only to purchased assets** (assets you own). If you **lease or rent the asset** (operating lease), Compressed Accounting does not apply because the leased asset is not your company's fixed asset—it remains on the lessor's balance sheet. 

However, if you enter a **capital lease (finance lease)** where you effectively own the asset and it appears on your balance sheet, Compressed Accounting may apply. The distinction between operating and capital leases is technical and varies by Japanese accounting standards. **Always consult your Zeirishi before signing a lease if you intend to fund it with subsidies**—the lease structure can make or break Compressed Accounting eligibility.

### Q8: Can we use Compressed Accounting for multiple assets purchased with a single subsidy?

**A:** **Yes, absolutely.** Many subsidies fund multiple asset purchases. The principle is: **total subsidy amount = total compression across all assets**. For example:
- Subsidy: 10 million yen
- Asset A (machine): purchased for 6 million yen; compressed by 6 million yen; book value becomes 0 yen
- Asset B (building renovation): purchased for 4 million yen; compressed by 4 million yen; book value becomes 0 yen
- **Total compression: 10 million yen** ✅

Or you could allocate differently (e.g., compress Asset A by 7 million yen, Asset B by 3 million yen—as long as total = 10 million yen). The key is: **clearly document the allocation** in your Fixed Asset Compressed Loss Calculation Form, so the tax office can verify each asset's compression is appropriate. If allocation is unclear, the tax office may reject parts of it.

### Q9: How does Compressed Accounting affect my company's balance sheet and financial ratios?

**A:** Compressed Accounting has significant balance sheet impacts:
- **Fixed asset value**: Appears lower (book value = original cost − compression amount)
- **Net income**: Appears higher in the year of asset purchase (lower depreciation starts from lower base)
- **Depreciation expense**: Appears lower in subsequent years
- **Return on assets (ROA)**: May appear artificially inflated (lower assets, similar net income)

**For stakeholders (banks, investors)**: These accounting impacts can affect loan terms or equity valuations. Some lenders may require adjustment of financial statements for Compressed Accounting to better understand true asset values. Disclose Compressed Accounting usage in financial statement footnotes for transparency.

**Final Reminder**: Compressed Accounting is a powerful tax tool, but only if executed correctly. Errors or incomplete documentation can result in **back taxes, penalties, interest, and even loss of the subsidy itself**. Engage a qualified Zeirishi (tax accountant) or CPA from the outset—the cost of professional guidance (~30,000–50,000 JPY) is trivial compared to the tax savings (often 1–3 million yen) and the risk mitigation.
