# Development Grant for Employment of Specified Job Seekers: A JPY 2.4 Million Hiring Grant Guide Every Chinese Business Owner in Japan Should Know

Chinese business owners running companies in Japan, have you ever faced this dilemma: you want to grow your team, but hiring costs are high; you want to hire specific groups to diversify your company, but government subsidy information is hard to obtain. In fact, to encourage companies to hire specified job seekers, the Japanese government has rolled out a subsidy program called the Development Grant for Employment of Specified Job Seekers, with grants of up to JPY 2.4 million available. This article gives you a detailed breakdown of the program's core content, application requirements, grant amounts, and the practical procedure.

## Basic Concept of the Development Grant for Employment of Specified Job Seekers

The Development Grant for Employment of Specified Job Seekers (hereinafter "this grant") is an employment subsidy program established by Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare to promote equal employment opportunities and help specific groups find work smoothly. The core purpose of the program is to encourage companies to hire job seekers who are in a disadvantaged position in the labor market or who face difficulty finding work, lowering companies' hiring costs through government subsidies and achieving a win-win outcome for both companies and job seekers.

For Chinese business owners in Japan, this grant program carries significant meaning. First, it can substantially reduce your human resources costs—with a maximum grant of JPY 2.4 million, this amount is enough to offset most of a new employee's initial training and wage costs. Second, if you are planning to hire middle-aged and older job seekers, persons with disabilities, or the long-term unemployed, this grant can help you achieve that goal at a lower cost. Third, by hiring employees through this program, your company also gains the added value of enhanced social recognition.

This grant is administered uniformly by the labor bureau of each prefecture, but the specific application requirements, grant amounts, and procedures vary depending on the "specified" category to which the job seeker belongs.

## Detailed Breakdown of the Five Categories of Specified Job Seekers and Grant Amounts

### The Five Categories of Specified Job Seekers

This grant applies to the following five categories of specified job seekers. Understanding these five categories is crucial, because the grant amounts differ significantly by category.

**Category 1: Middle-aged and older job seekers (45 and above)**

Japan is facing an aging population, and job seekers aged 45 and above often face age discrimination in the labor market. The government encourages companies to break down this barrier. If you hire a job seeker aged 45 or above as a regular employee and continue their employment for at least one year, you can receive a grant of up to JPY 500,000 (for SMEs) or JPY 400,000 (for large companies).

**Category 2: Persons with disabilities**

This is the category with the highest grant amount under this grant. If you hire a person holding a disability certificate as a regular employee and continue their employment for at least two years, you can receive a grant of up to JPY 2.4 million (for SMEs) or JPY 1.8 million (for large companies). This is the reason we emphasize "up to JPY 2.4 million" in the title.

**Category 3: The long-term unemployed (unemployed for at least one year)**

To help the long-term unemployed reintegrate into the workplace, the government encourages companies to provide opportunities. Hiring a long-term unemployed person (unemployed for at least one year) as a regular employee can earn a grant of up to JPY 700,000 (for SMEs) or JPY 600,000 (for large companies).

**Category 4: Single mothers or single fathers**

This category reflects the government's support for single-parent families. Hiring a single mother or single father as a regular employee can earn a grant of up to JPY 600,000 (for SMEs) or JPY 500,000 (for large companies).

**Category 5: Recipients of public assistance (livelihood protection)**

To help the most disadvantaged job seekers escape poverty, the government established this category. Hiring a recipient of public assistance as a regular employee can earn a grant of up to JPY 400,000 (for SMEs) or JPY 300,000 (for large companies).

## The Core Requirements for Applying for This Grant

### Conditions Your Company Must Meet

To successfully obtain the Development Grant for Employment of Specified Job Seekers, your company must meet the following conditions:

First, the company must be a company in Japan covered by employment insurance. If your company has not yet enrolled in the employment insurance system, you need to complete registration first. Generally, as long as your company has more than one employee, you are required to enroll in employment insurance.

Second, the company must not have any major employment-environment problems in the year prior to applying for the grant. Specifically, none of the following may apply: large-scale layoffs, improper hiring, serious violations of the Labor Standards Act, and so on. If your company has these problems, it will be rejected even if it meets the other conditions.

Third, during the grant application period and the grant period, the company must maintain good compliance with labor standards. This means you need to ensure that all employees' working hours, wages, benefits, and so on comply with Japan's Labor Standards Act.

Fourth, if the company belongs to a specific industry (such as adult entertainment), it may not be able to apply for this grant. You need to confirm with the labor bureau whether your industry is eligible.

### Conditions the Employee Must Meet

Not all job seekers can be brought within the scope of this grant. The employee must meet the following key conditions:

**Status and situation requirements**: The employee must be a job seeker in Japan and, before being hired, must be unemployed or in insufficiently stable employment. This means that if the job seeker already had a full-time job before being hired, they usually do not qualify. But there are exceptions—for example, if the job seeker was previously a part-time employee and is now converting to full-time, this situation may qualify.

**Verification of specific status**: In the case of a person with disabilities, they must hold a disability certificate (issued by the prefectural welfare office). In the case of a long-term unemployed person, proof of unemployment is required. These supporting documents must all be submitted to the labor bureau at the time of application.

**Age and work capacity**: Although middle-aged and older job seekers must meet the requirement of being 45 or above, this does not mean you can hire anyone at will. The job seeker still needs to have basic work capacity and be able to perform the position you offer.

**Employment form requirements**: The employee must become a regular employee of the company (usually meaning a fixed-term labor contract or an indefinite-term labor contract), rather than a temporary or dispatched worker. This point is very important for Chinese business owners—you cannot hire a part-time employee in order to obtain this grant.

## The Actual Application Process and Required Documents

### Preparation Stage Before Applying

Before formally submitting your application, you need to do thorough preparation work. Although this stage does not directly produce a grant, it lays an important foundation for the entire application process.

**Step 1: Confirm your company's eligibility**. You need to confirm that your company is already enrolled in employment insurance and has had no serious violations of the Labor Standards Act in the past year. You can call the local Public Employment Security Office (Hello Work) to inquire, and they will tell you in detail whether your company is eligible. Many Chinese business owners discover at this step that their company may have some non-compliant issues, such as not correctly calculating overtime pay or not providing sufficient days off. If you have these problems, we recommend addressing them first.

**Step 2: Clarify your hiring plan**. Determine which category of specified job seeker you want to hire. This choice is crucial, because it directly affects the grant amount you can obtain. For example, if you plan to expand your workforce, you might consider hiring job seekers aged 45 or above or the long-term unemployed. If your company has a sense of social responsibility, you might consider hiring persons with disabilities, which not only earns the highest grant but also enhances your company's social image.

**Step 3: Register your hiring information at the Public Employment Security Office**. You cannot hire directly from headhunters or private channels; instead, you must post hiring information through the Public Employment Security Office. This way, unemployed job seekers can learn about your job opportunity through official channels. You need to submit detailed job information to the local Hello Work, including job duties, salary, benefits, work location, and so on. Usually, the hiring information must be posted on Hello Work for at least two weeks before you can proceed with subsequent hiring activities.

### Specific Steps in the Application Stage

Once your company's hiring conditions have been confirmed and the hiring information has been posted, you can enter the formal application stage.

**Step 1: Hiring and onboarding**. You select a suitable candidate from the job seekers at Hello Work, sign a labor contract with them, and bring them into the company's employment insurance as a regular employee. This labor contract must clearly specify the employment period (such as 1 year, 2 years, etc.) and comply with all requirements of Japan's Labor Standards Act. When signing the contract, you must keep all originals, because these are all necessary documents when applying for the grant.

**Step 2: Submit the grant application**. Usually, you need to submit the grant application within a certain period after the new employee is onboarded (usually 1–2 months). The application is submitted to the local labor bureau (prefectural labor bureau). The application form can be downloaded from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's official website or obtained directly from the labor bureau. The application form requires you to fill in detailed information such as company information, employee information, and labor contract information.

**Step 3: Submit the required documents**. The grant application requires a series of accompanying documents, including:
- Labor contract (original or copy)
- The employee's proof of identity (disability certificate, certificate of eligibility for unemployment benefits, etc.)
- The company's registration certificate
- Proof of enrollment in employment insurance
- The employee's résumé

These documents must all be complete; any missing item may cause the application to be rejected.

**Step 4: The labor bureau's review**. After the labor bureau receives your application, it conducts a detailed review, usually taking 1–3 months. It will verify all the information you submitted, confirm that the employee truly meets the definition of a "specified job seeker," confirm that your company meets the application conditions, and so on. During this process, the labor bureau may ask you to provide supplementary materials or conduct an on-site inspection.

**Step 5: Receive the grant**. If your application is approved, the labor bureau will issue a payment decision notice. Then, depending on the employee category you selected, the grant is paid in installments. For example, if you hire a person with disabilities, the grant is usually spread over 2 years, paid once every 6 months.

### Common Document Errors and a Guide to Avoiding Pitfalls

In my many years of consulting experience, I have seen many Chinese business owners have their applications rejected due to document errors. The most common errors include:

**Error 1: A non-standard labor contract**. Many companies' labor contracts are too simple and do not clearly specify the employment period, job duties, wage calculation method, and so on. We recommend using the standard labor contract template provided by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, or consulting a professional labor and social security attorney.

**Error 2: Incomplete proof-of-identity documents**. For example, the employee's disability certificate has expired, or the certificate of eligibility for unemployment benefits has expired. You need to ensure that all supporting documents are valid at the time you submit the application.

**Error 3: The company itself has a violation of the Labor Standards Act**. Even if your newly hired employee meets all conditions, if your company has violations in other areas (such as other employees' working hours exceeding the limit), the labor bureau may reject your application. This reminds Chinese business owners that the prerequisite for obtaining a grant is the compliant operation of the company.

## Special Note: The Unique Advantages of Hiring Persons with Disabilities

Among the five categories of specified job seekers covered by this grant, persons with disabilities are undoubtedly the category most worth paying attention to, for three main reasons:

### The Highest Grant Amount

The grant amount for persons with disabilities is the highest, with SMEs able to obtain JPY 2.4 million. This amount is enough to pay a regular employee's wages for a year and a half. More importantly, this grant is paid to you as a subsequent reimbursement after you have already paid the employee's wages and benefits. In other words, this is not an investment you need to front, but a direct reward from the state to your company.

### A Longer Grant Period

The grant period for persons with disabilities is 2 years, whereas other categories usually have only 1 year. This means you can receive a longer period of financial support, which helps with the company's medium-term financial planning.

### Additional Tax Benefits

In addition to the employment grant, Japan also provides tax benefits to companies that hire persons with disabilities. Under the "tax system for promoting the employment of persons with disabilities," companies can enjoy the following benefits:
- Income tax benefits: depending on the number of persons with disabilities employed and the total wages, a certain percentage of income tax can be deducted.
- Corporate tax benefits: for corporate enterprises, a corresponding reduction in corporate tax is also available.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: Does my company need to report to the labor bureau before hiring?

**A:** No. But you need to register your hiring information at the local Public Employment Security Office (Hello Work). The usual process is as follows: first, you submit hiring information to Hello Work (this step is mandatory and a prerequisite for the grant); second, you select a person from the job seekers at Hello Work; third, you sign a labor contract with the employee and complete the employment insurance procedures; finally, you apply for the grant with the labor bureau within the prescribed time. In other words, you don't need to obtain the labor bureau's approval in advance, but you must hire through the official Hello Work channel. If you violate this procedure and hire directly from other channels, the labor bureau will reject your application even if you meet all other conditions.

### Q2: If the employee I hired resigns during the grant period, will the grant be clawed back?

**A:** This depends on the specific circumstances. If an employee resigns during the grant period, the labor bureau will usually stop paying the remaining grant. For example, if you hire a person with disabilities and should receive a JPY 2.4 million grant (paid over 2 years), but the employee leaves in the 6th month, then you may only be able to obtain the first payment of the grant, and the remaining grant will be canceled. But there are also exceptions: if the employee's resignation is due to the company's fault (such as unpaid wages, harassment, or serious violations of the Labor Standards Act), the labor bureau may require you to return the grant already obtained, and may even impose a fine. Therefore, to protect your interests, be sure your company complies with all labor laws and maintains a good working relationship with employees. We recommend keeping all relevant documents when an employee resigns, so as to prove that the resignation was caused by the employee's personal reasons.

### Q3: Can I hire multiple specified job seekers at the same time to obtain multiple grants?

**A:** Yes. You can hire multiple specified job seekers at the same time, and each can apply for a grant separately. For example, you can hire two persons with disabilities and one long-term unemployed person at the same time, and in theory obtain three independent grants. But note that these hires must all be genuine, proper, and consistent with the company's actual development needs. The labor bureau will conduct a review, and if it suspects that you have hired fictitiously in order to defraud the grant, it will reject your application and may even penalize your company. Therefore, you cannot hire employees you don't need in order to obtain a grant. In addition, the labor bureau may conduct spot checks on your company to verify whether these employees are genuinely on the job and working normally.

### Q4: If my company is newly established, can I apply for this grant?

**A:** Usually you can, but there are certain restrictions. A newly established company must have been operating for at least one year to apply for most categories of grants. This is because the labor bureau needs to assess whether your company is stable and able to employ people over the long term. If your company has been established for less than one year but is already enrolled in employment insurance, you can first consult the local labor bureau to learn whether there are special provisions for startups. Some local governments may provide additional support. In addition, if your company was established a short time ago but you personally have long-term management experience (for example, having previously worked at another company), this may increase the labor bureau's confidence in you.

### Q5: The grant period for persons with disabilities is 2 years—when does this 2-year period start counting from?

**A:** The 2 years are counted from the day the employee becomes a regular employee of your company. Specifically, it is the employment start date stated on the labor contract. For example, if you and the employee sign a labor contract on April 1, 2024, then the 2-year grant period runs from April 1, 2024 to March 31, 2026. During these 2 years, the grant is usually paid in 4 installments (once every 6 months), or in 2 installments (once a year); the specific payment frequency is set by the labor bureau in your area. Note that even though the grant is paid in installments, you must ensure that the employee remains employed throughout the entire 2-year period. If the employee resigns during the 2-year period, you will lose the subsequent grant. This is also why companies need to do a good job of employee training and management, to ensure that employees can work over the long term in a stable way.

## Practical Advice and Risk Prevention During the Application

### Effective Communication with Hello Work

Many Chinese business owners have problems communicating with Hello Work due to language barriers or cultural differences. We recommend that you:

First, visit your local Hello Work multiple times before applying and build a good relationship with the staff. Hello Work staff are usually happy to help companies, and they can explain the entire process to you in detail and point out things you might have missed.

Second, if your Japanese is not fluent enough, consider hiring an interpreter to accompany you to Hello Work. Although this adds some cost, it can prevent problems caused by language misunderstandings.

Third, keep all records of your communications with Hello Work, including phone records, emails, and meeting memos. These records may be useful in your subsequent application with the labor bureau.

### Document Keeping and Records

When reviewing an application, the labor bureau carefully examines all the documents you provide. Therefore, you need to ensure that:

All documents are clear and legible, without any signs of alteration. If the original is soiled or damaged, you should make a clear copy.

The labor contract must be signed and stamped by both the company and the employee. Some Chinese business owners may not fully appreciate the importance of "stamping," but in Japan, a company's official seal and personal seal are both legally important marks.

You need to keep all the employee's wage records and attendance records to prove that the employee is genuinely on the job. The labor bureau may ask to see these records.

### Follow-Up Supervision and Reporting

After obtaining the grant, your responsibility does not end. The labor bureau will conduct periodic supervision during the grant period, which may include:

Regular written reports: The labor bureau may require you to submit a report every six months or every year, describing the employment status and job performance of the employee.

On-site inspections: Labor bureau staff may visit your company to conduct an on-site inspection, interview the employee, and learn about their actual work situation.

Non-compliant behavior may lead to the grant being clawed back: If the labor bureau finds that you have engaged in any non-compliant behavior during the grant period (such as harassment of the employee, unpaid wages, improper dismissal, etc.), it will require you to return the grant already obtained.

Therefore, obtaining the grant is only the beginning; maintaining compliant operation is the long-term requirement.
