# A Subsidy Application Guide for Chinese-Run Massage and Seitai Clinics in Japan

Chinese business owners running massage or seitai (body-alignment) clinics in Japan face the same operating pressures as Japanese businesses, but due to differences in how they access information, language barriers, and other factors, many miss out on valuable government subsidy opportunities. In fact, Japan's central government and local authorities roll out numerous massage-clinic subsidy and seitai-clinic grant programs every year—whether for a new opening, equipment purchases, staff training, or digital transformation, you may be able to obtain subsidies ranging from 150,000 to 5 million JPY. This guide breaks down, from a hands-on perspective, how Chinese-run therapy businesses in Japan can apply for subsidies systematically.

## The Main Sources and Types of Massage Clinic Subsidies

The subsidies that Japanese massage and seitai clinics can apply for come mainly from three levels of government: national, prefectural, and municipal (city/ward/town/village). Understanding the categories of subsidies and their respective characteristics is the first step to a successful application.

### National-Level Subsidies

National-level subsidies are usually large in amount and highly competitive, but broad in coverage. The main ones include:

The **Sustainability Subsidy for Small Businesses (小規模事業者持続化補助金)** is one of the most popular options, with an ample annual budget, aimed at small companies with no more than 20 employees. As small businesses, massage and seitai clinics can apply for subsidies of 500,000 to 2 million JPY. These funds can be used for hardware investment such as renovating and upgrading your premises and purchasing massage tables and heat-therapy equipment, as well as for softer investment such as developing an online booking system or building a website. The subsidy rate is usually 2/3 of the eligible expenses.

The **Monozukuri, Commerce, and Service Business Capability Enhancement Support Program (ものづくり・商業・サービス経営力向上支援事業)** is aimed primarily at companies with an innovative mindset. If your massage clinic has introduced new therapy equipment, developed a unique seitai-clinic service process, or built a digital management system, you meet the eligibility requirements. This type of subsidy can reach 5 million JPY or more, but the application is relatively difficult and requires a detailed business plan and an explanation of the innovation.

The **IT Adoption Subsidy (IT導入補助金)** is designed specifically for companies introducing information technology. If a Chinese-run therapy business in Japan plans to introduce a customer management system, online payment, a booking app, and the like, it can apply for this subsidy. The amount is usually between 300,000 and 4.5 million JPY, with a subsidy rate ranging from 1/2 to 3/4.

### Local Government-Level Subsidies

Every prefecture and municipality has its own SME support policies. Take Tokyo as an example: the Tokyo Metropolitan Small and Medium Enterprise Support Center rolls out 5–10 different types of subsidy programs each year, many of which apply to massage and seitai clinics. The subsidy policies in areas with large Chinese communities, such as Osaka Prefecture and Fukuoka Prefecture, are also quite substantial.

The advantages of local-level subsidies are: the application is usually easier than at the national level, the review cycle is shorter (3–6 months), and many programs have relatively relaxed requirements for foreign business owners—you only need to hold a valid business license. The subsidy amount is generally between 300,000 and 2 million JPY.

### Industry-Specific Subsidies

In recent years, Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare and Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry have launched dedicated subsidies for the health and beauty industry. Since 2023, the government has tilted more resources toward therapy services related to "extending healthy life expectancy" in particular. If your seitai clinic emphasizes functions such as preventive medicine and rehabilitative therapy, it is easier to obtain this type of subsidy.

## Common Applicants for Massage and Seitai Clinic Subsidies

### The New-Opening Stage

If you are preparing to open a new massage or seitai clinic, almost every step—from site selection, renovation, and equipment purchases to staff recruitment—may fall within the subsidy's scope. Many Chinese business owners should start preparing their subsidy application 3–6 months before opening, because some subsidies require you to invest your own funds first and apply for the subsidy afterward, with the subsidy payment reimbursed only after the project is closed out.

Real-world case: A Chinese-run massage clinic in Shinjuku, Tokyo, applied for the Sustainability Subsidy for Small Businesses in 2023, using it for renovation upgrades and introducing new therapy equipment, and was approved for 1.5 million JPY. Because they planned ahead, their application emphasized service design tailored to the Japanese customer base, earning praise from the reviewers.

### Expansion and Upgrading of Existing Premises

Even if you have run a massage clinic for many years, you can apply for a subsidy to upgrade and renovate. Whether adding service offerings, updating equipment, expanding floor space, or improving hygiene conditions and the customer experience, these are all legitimate uses funded by subsidies.

A common need among Chinese-run therapy businesses in Japan is to raise service standards to a more "Japanese" level—for example, introducing a reservation system rather than a purely walk-in system, building a hygiene management system, and training staff in Japanese-language communication. These improvements fully meet the eligibility requirements of most subsidies.

### Staff Training and Qualification Upgrades

If you plan to provide on-the-job training for staff, subsidies for obtaining qualifications, or to hire new employees for skills development, this investment can also be offset through subsidies. Especially for Chinese business owners who want to recruit and develop Japanese employees, the Human Resource Development Grant (人材育成関連補助金) can substantially lower training costs.

## The Specific Process and Steps for Applying for a Massage Clinic Subsidy

### Step 1: Gathering and Screening Information

The success of a subsidy application depends on choosing the right program. We recommend screening in the following order:

1. **Confirm your company's eligibility.** Check whether your company size (employee count), annual revenue, tax status, and so on meet the basic requirements. Most massage clinic subsidies require the company to be registered (either a Japanese corporation or a sole proprietor), to hold a valid business license, and to have no major compliance violations in the past 2 years.

2. **List your business plan for the next three months.** Clarify the important investments or reforms you intend to make in the near term. This will help you quickly pinpoint the right subsidy program.

3. **Search through official channels.** METI's Mira Sapo plus (ミラサポplus) is a comprehensive subsidy search platform where you can filter by region, industry, and subsidy amount. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's subsidy information is published on its official website and at local Hello Work offices (Public Employment Security Offices).

4. **Consult professional organizations.** Every area has free SME support centers and Societies of Commerce and Industry that can give you targeted advice based on your specific situation. Large cities like Tokyo and Osaka also have Chinese-language support services.

### Step 2: Preparing the Application Documents

A complete subsidy application usually requires the following documents:

- **Business Plan (事業計画書)**: This is the most crucial document. It needs to clearly explain your current business situation, the purpose of the investment, the expected outcomes, and your fund-usage plan. For a massage clinic subsidy application, we recommend emphasizing tangible results in the plan—improved service quality, better customer satisfaction, upgraded staff skills—rather than just technology investment. Keep it to 5–10 pages, written in Japanese (if your Japanese isn't fluent enough, you can ask a Japanese-language professional to help).

- **Settlement Reports & Tax Returns (決算書・税務申告書)**: You need to submit financial statements and tax filing documents for the past 2–3 years. These are used to prove that your business operates steadily.

- **Quotations (見積書)**: If the subsidy involves purchasing equipment or renovation, you need at least 2–3 supplier quotations to demonstrate that the purchase price is reasonable.

- **Business License (営業許可証)**: Supporting documents such as massage therapist qualification certificates and the clinic's business license.

- **Certificate of Registered Matters (法人登記簿謄本)**: Used to prove the company's legal status.

### Step 3: Key Points When Writing the Application Materials

When writing the business plan, several details will significantly affect the review outcome:

**Clear investment logic**: Explain why you need this investment. For example, if you're applying for a seitai-clinic grant to purchase new therapy equipment, don't just write "purchase advanced equipment"—instead, be specific: "X% of our current customers report lower-back pain, and we plan to introduce heat-therapy equipment to meet this need, which we expect to raise customer satisfaction from 80% to 92% and increase monthly revenue by 15%."

**Concrete numerical metrics**: What government reviewers value most are quantifiable expected outcomes. It's worth listing before-and-after comparison data in the plan: employee count, monthly revenue, customer count, repeat-visit rate, and so on.

**Social or regional contribution**: Emphasize the value of your massage or seitai clinic to the local community. For example, whether you've created local jobs, whether you provide convenient health services to the elderly population, whether you take part in community health activities, and so on. One advantage of Chinese-run therapy businesses in Japan is cultural diversity—you can highlight introducing a variety of therapy cultures and enriching the diversity of Japan's health services.

### Step 4: Submitting and Following Up

Most subsidies use an online application system (such as a GBizID account). Before submitting, be sure to check that all documents are complete and consistent. Some applications require attaching a pledge letter guaranteeing that the investment is genuine and will proceed as planned.

After submission, the application usually enters a 2–4 week initial review stage, followed by a 2–8 week comprehensive evaluation period. During this time, the reviewing body may request supplementary materials or conduct an interview. For Chinese business owners in Japan, if expressing yourself in Japanese is difficult, you can have a Japanese employee or a professional advisor accompany you to the interview.

## Common Pitfalls in Massage Clinic Subsidy Applications and How to Avoid Them

Several traps that many Chinese business owners have fallen into during subsidy applications are worth noting:

**Pitfall 1: Focusing only on the subsidy amount.** Programs with large subsidy amounts tend to be highly competitive and difficult to apply for. Sometimes applying for a smaller subsidy is actually easier to get approved. We recommend choosing based on your actual needs rather than blindly chasing large amounts.

**Pitfall 2: Falsifying or exaggerating the business plan.** Reviewers are highly experienced, and an exaggerated plan is easily seen through. Once you are deemed dishonest, not only will this application be rejected, but it may also affect your eligibility for future applications. We recommend keeping expectations conservative but realistic.

**Pitfall 3: Ignoring the subsidy's follow-up requirements.** After a subsidy is approved, you must invest according to your application plan, keep all invoices and payment receipts, complete the project within the prescribed period, and submit a closeout report. If you ultimately can't proceed as planned, or the investment deviates significantly from what was applied for, you may be required to return the subsidy and could even face a fine.

**Pitfall 4: Not paying attention to application deadlines.** Subsidies all have strict application deadlines, and usually there are only 1–2 application windows per year. Miss it and you'll wait a year. We recommend starting to track the full year's subsidy schedule at the beginning of the year and preparing your application materials in advance.

## Special Subsidy Opportunities for Chinese-Run Therapy Businesses in Japan

As a Chinese-owned business in Japan, you may have some additional subsidy opportunities:

### Multicultural Business Subsidies

Some local governments offer "multicultural coexistence" subsidies that encourage businesses serving foreign residents. If your massage or seitai clinic intends to provide professional services to Chinese residents in Japan, international students, or foreign tourists, this type of subsidy may apply.

### Tourism-Promotion-Related Subsidies

If your massage clinic is located in a tourist area, or you plan to develop massage-experience products aimed at inbound tourists, you can apply for tourism-related subsidies. In recent years the Japanese government has been strongly promoting "health tourism," including hot springs, massage, and wellness experiences, and the level of subsidy support in this field is increasing.

### Support for SME Business Innovation Plans

After filing a "Business Innovation Plan" (経営革新計画) and obtaining the approval of the prefectural governor, you become eligible to apply for a range of follow-on subsidies and financing benefits. Although this process takes 3–6 months, once successful it opens many doors to financing.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: I'm a sole proprietor. Can I apply for a massage clinic subsidy?

**A:** Absolutely. Most of Japan's SME subsidies do not treat corporations and sole proprietors differently. The application requirements for sole proprietors are usually simpler, because you don't need to provide a corporate Certificate of Registered Matters—only your personal notification of business commencement and tax returns. We recommend checking whether your My Number (マイナンバー) is already linked to the tax system, which will speed up the application review. If you run several massage or seitai clinics at the same time, you can apply for multiple subsidies depending on the situation, but you need to ensure that each subsidy's purpose is entirely different. Many Chinese sole proprietors have used subsidies to expand from a single store to a multi-store operation.

### Q2: My massage clinic is in leased premises. Can I apply for a subsidy for renovation and remodeling?

**A:** Yes, but you need to be mindful of the lease-contract terms. Most subsidies allow renovation-investment subsidies for leased premises, but on the following conditions: (1) the lease term is long enough—at least 3–5 years—to ensure the value of the subsidized investment is realized; (2) the landlord consents to the remodeling; (3) the remodeling does not involve structural changes to the building (renovating non-load-bearing walls is usually fine, but large-scale remodeling involving water, electrical, and gas lines may require additional approval). We recommend communicating with your landlord before applying, and it's best to spell out remodeling rights and responsibilities in the renovation contract. In one real case, a Chinese business owner had their application rejected because they hadn't confirmed the landlord's consent in advance. In addition, the renovation quotation must come from a licensed renovation company; individuals or unqualified contractors may not be recognized.

### Q3: How long is the review period for a subsidy application? How soon after approval can I get the money?

**A:** This timing varies by subsidy type. National-level subsidies (such as the Sustainability Subsidy for Small Businesses) usually require a 2–4 month review period, and from submitting the application to the approval announcement takes about 3–4 months. After approval, you don't receive the subsidy immediately—instead, you must first invest according to your application plan, keep all invoices and payment receipts (usually for 3–7 years), then complete all the investment within the prescribed period (usually 6–12 months after approval) and submit a closeout report. Only after the reviewing body examines your closeout report and confirms all expenditures are compliant will the subsidy be disbursed to your bank account, and that process takes another 1–2 months. So from application to finally receiving the subsidy, the whole cycle is usually 6–8 months. This is why I advise business owners to plan ahead and not count on a subsidy for emergency cash flow.

### Q4: My massage clinic's revenue declined over the past year. Will this affect my subsidy application?

**A:** It won't directly cause your application to be rejected, but it needs a reasonable explanation. Subsidy reviewing bodies understand that businesses experience fluctuations, especially under the impact of the pandemic, when many service industries went through difficult times. The key is to clearly explain in your application the reason for the decline and how you plan to improve your business situation through this subsidized investment. For example, if you're applying for a subsidy to improve services, upgrade equipment, or expand your customer base, these measures should be analyzed in detail in the business plan, including market research, target-customer analysis, and the expected path to a revenue rebound. Conversely, if you don't have a clear improvement plan, the reviewer may doubt the subsidy's effectiveness. Some subsidies even have a dedicated "support for businesses in difficulty" category aimed at companies hit by external shocks, which can actually make approval easier. We recommend consulting a professional subsidy advisor to select and design an application plan based on your specific situation.

### Q5: If my subsidy application is rejected, can I apply for the same subsidy program again?

**A:** Yes, but we recommend carefully analyzing the reasons for the rejection before the next application cycle. Subsidy application bodies usually don't explicitly disclose the reasons for rejection (out of fairness considerations), but you can proactively consult the professionals at a support organization; they can help you analyze where the problem might lie based on the general application standards—whether the plan's logic was unclear, the financial data unreasonable, or the investment content insufficiently innovative. After improving, reapplying in the next application window usually raises your success rate. If you continue to be rejected, you can try applying for a different type of subsidy. Many Chinese business owners give up easily after a first failed application, but in reality persistence often pays off. I've seen cases where business owners were approved on their second or third application. At the same time, be careful to avoid resubmitting exactly the same program and content; you should optimize and adjust based on the feedback.
