# Can I Apply for a Subsidy Without Japanese Employees? Complete Guide to Employment Requirements

The Japanese government has established hundreds of subsidy programs to support business growth, and **the majority do not require Japanese employees**——as long as you meet core conditions such as operational stability and appropriate use of investment proceeds, micro-enterprises without employees, individual business owners, and startups are equally eligible to apply. This article systematically analyzes common misconceptions about employment requirements, employee provisions across different subsidy schemes, and practical application strategies for Chinese business owners in Japan, helping you quickly determine whether your enterprise qualifies and how to avoid rejection risks.

---

## The Truth About 「Employment Requirements」 in Subsidy Programs

Many Chinese business owners in Japan mistakenly believe that applying for a subsidy requires a fixed employee roster. This is the **most common misconception**. Japan's subsidy system is divided into two major categories:

**Category One: No Employee Restrictions (approximately 60% of all subsidies)**  
This includes core programs such as the Business Restructuring Subsidy (事業再構築補助金), IT Adoption Subsidy (IT導入補助金), and Sustainability Subsidy for Micro-Enterprises (小規模事業者持続化補助金), which have **no mandatory requirements** for either corporations or individuals, with or without employees. These subsidies evaluate: the content of the enterprise's investment project, management philosophy, and growth potential, rather than workforce size.

**Category Two: Employee Count Ceiling (approximately 30%)**  
For example, some SME Agency subsidies define 「small and medium-sized enterprises」 with employee caps——300 people or fewer for manufacturing, 100 people or fewer for services. However, these are **upper limits, not lower limits**; one-person or zero-employee enterprises still qualify.

**Category Three: Industry/Target-Specific Subsidies (approximately 10%)**  
Such as startup subsidies for specific regions or women entrepreneur incentive programs, which may include bonus points for 「hiring X new employees」 but are not mandatory conditions.

According to 2023 SME Agency data, among enterprises that successfully obtained the Business Restructuring Subsidy, **approximately 35% were zero-employee individual business owners or micro-enterprises with 1–2 employees**, with acceptance rates essentially equal to those of enterprises with 5+ employees (application adoption rate approximately 40–45%). The key lies in the innovation and feasibility of the project content, not employee count.

## Most Common Subsidy Types and Award Amounts for Zero/Few-Employee Enterprises

### Business Restructuring Subsidy (事業再構築補助金) — The Most Flexible Option

This is Japan's largest-scale subsidy program. The 2024 public-offering guidelines show:

**Subsidy Amounts**
- Standard Track (通常枠): Maximum 30 million JPY
- Global V-Shaped Recovery Track (グローバルV字回復枠): Maximum 50 million JPY

**Subsidy Rate: 2/3 or 3/4** (depending on application category and enterprise size)

**Employment Requirement: None**. Corporations, individual business owners, and non-profit organizations can all apply; **there is absolutely no restriction on current employee count**.

**Key Conditions** (weighted more heavily than employee count):
- Average monthly sales for the 3 months immediately following public announcement are 10% or more (and 20% or more for certain tracks) lower than average monthly sales for the same period in the past (demonstrating operational difficulty)
- Obtain confirmation from a Certified Support Agency (認定支援機関) in your submitted Business Revitalization Plan

**Micro-Enterprise Case Study**: Ms. Li from Shenzhen operates a housekeeping service sole proprietorship in Tokyo with no employees. Due to pandemic-related sales decline of 18%, she applied for the Business Restructuring Subsidy and received 12 million JPY approval for developing a housekeeping app and expanding branch locations. The review agency primarily assessed the app's market viability and branch expansion plan, not her employee count.

### IT Adoption Subsidy (IT導入補助金) — Ideal for Small-Business Digitalization

**Subsidy Amount: Maximum 4.5 million JPY** (2024 fiscal year)

**Subsidy Rate: 1/2**

**Eligible Enterprises**
- Corporations with capital of 300 million JPY or less, or with 1,000 full-time employees or fewer; individual business owners
- Enterprises eligible for the Sustainability Subsidy for Micro-Enterprises (小規模事業者持続化補助金)

**Employment Requirement: Explicitly unrestricted**. When sole proprietors (1-person operations) apply, they only need to demonstrate annual sales above a certain threshold.

**Subsidy Coverage Areas**:
- Software adoption (accounting software, CRM, inventory management, etc.)
- Hardware adoption (POS systems, servers, etc.)
- Education and support costs associated with implementation

**Zero-Employee Enterprise Data**: According to the Japan IT Adoption Subsidy Association, among 2023 approved cases, **sole proprietors comprised 26%**, with over 95% having no full-time employees. Average subsidy amount was approximately 1.8 million JPY.

### Sustainability Subsidy for Micro-Enterprises (小規模事業者持続化補助金)

**Subsidy Amount: Maximum 2 million JPY** (general track); Maximum 2.5 million JPY (special track)

**Subsidy Rate: 2/3**

**Most Relaxed Employment Requirement**:
- Number of full-time employees: 20 people or fewer (5 people or fewer for commerce/industry)

This is the **most friendly subsidy program for zero-employee enterprises**. Sole proprietors and small shops operated by couples both qualify.

**Subsidy Coverage** (broader than IT Adoption):
- Equipment investment
- Store renovation
- Advertising and marketing
- New product development
- Special preferences available for female entrepreneurs (potential for increased subsidy rate)

**High Application Success Case for Chinese Enterprises**: Mr. Chen, who runs a ramen restaurant in Kyoto (individual business owner, no employees), successfully obtained the subsidy 3 times within 2 years, accumulating 4.5 million JPY, used for store expansion, delivery system implementation, and SNS advertising. Each application-to-approval cycle averaged **5–6 months**.

- Declare in the subsidy approval phase 「we plan to hire new employees」

## Subsidy Application Flow and Employment Requirement Confirmation Timeline

### Stage-by-Stage Response Guide

**Stage 1: Pre-Application Preparation (3–4 months before application)**

□ Organize past 3 years' tax filings and financial statements (corporations), or past 3 years' final tax returns (individuals)  
□ Review bank account transaction records (to verify sales and expenses)  
□ Locate and schedule consultation with a Certified Support Agency (referrals available from tax accountant associations, chambers of commerce)  
□ **Confirm with a specialist at this stage whether zero-employee application is possible**——conditions vary by subsidy type

**Stage 2: Business Plan Development (6–8 weeks before application)**

□ Develop business plan through meetings with support agency (typically 2–4 consultations)  
□ **In the 「Employment」 section, clearly state:**
  - Current employee count: 0
  - Hiring plans during subsidy implementation period: specify yes/no
  - If new hiring is planned, clearly document timing, number of hires, position titles, and salary

□ Confirm presence/absence of bonus-point qualifications (green strategy alignment, etc.)

**Stage 3: Application Submission (during public-offering period)**

□ Submit application 2–3 weeks after public-offering begins (most subsidies have 4–6 week public-offering periods)  
□ Create account and submit through electronic application system (jGrants, etc.)  
□ No revision opportunity after submission, so conduct final verification with support agency before submitting

**Stage 4: Review Period (2–4 months)**

□ Receive adoption/rejection notice (document screening + optional interview)  
□ **Upon rejection, you may request feedback from subsidy office** (reasons typically provided in writing)  
□ If a zero-employee enterprise is rejected, it is usually NOT due to the employment requirement——the issue is likely the persuasiveness of the business plan or appropriateness of fund usage

**Stage 5: Business Implementation Period (12–24 months)**

□ Execute equipment purchases and service implementations funded by the subsidy  
□ **If new hiring was planned, completion by deadline is mandatory** (proven by employment contracts and payroll records)  
□ Submit performance report (receipts, photos, sales records, etc.)

**Stage 6: Subsidy Finalization and Transfer (3–6 months after project completion)**

□ Project completion report review  
□ Final subsidy amount confirmation  
□ Bank transfer execution

## Five Common Rejection Reasons for Zero-Employee Enterprises

Rejection due to 「employment requirements」 is **extremely rare**, but the following reasons cause zero-employee enterprises to face higher rejection rates.

### Reason 1: Operational Foundation Deemed Unstable (Rejection Rate 35–40%)

**Common Scenarios**
- Past 3 years' sales trend is inconsistent or shows decline
- Bank account balance is below 50% of application amount

**Improvement Strategy**
- Submit detailed cash-flow projections (monthly for 3 years)
- Clearly state 「because we operate without employees, fixed costs are low and operations are stable」
- Mention loan history if available (evidence of bank-recognized creditworthiness)

### Reason 2: Vague Business Plan or Low Feasibility (Rejection Rate 45–55%)

**Common Scenarios**
- 「New product development」 written without specifics
- Unclear what will be purchased with subsidy funds
- Sales forecasts lack supporting rationale

**Improvement Strategy**
- Provide detailed competitive analysis (compare with 3–5 competitors in same industry)
- Attach cost estimates for equipment/services to be purchased with subsidy
- Base sales forecasts on historical data (e.g., if claiming 「20% year-on-year growth」, substantiate the basis)
- Structure subsidy-eligible expenses to comprise **80%+** of subsidy limit (surplus funds risk being deemed ineligible)

### Reason 3: Insufficient Confirmation from Certified Support Agency (Rejection Rate 20–25%)

**Common Scenarios (Business Restructuring Subsidy)**
- Submit business plan without having support agency review it
- Support agency provided approval stamp but provided no substantive support

**Improvement Strategy**
- Conduct 3+ meetings with support agency (maintain records)
- Ask support agency 「what would subsidy reviewers prioritize」 and incorporate feedback

### Reason 4: No Tax Filing History or Insufficient History (Rejection Rate 50–70%)

**Common Scenarios**
- Individual business owner but no tax returns filed for past 2 years
- Discrepancy between tax filings and actual sales

**Improvement Strategy**
- Before applying, submit past 3 years' tax returns (1 year acceptable for first-year startups)
- Consult with a Zeirishi to organize 「supporting documents」 for tax returns (receipts, bank records)

### Reason 5: Zero-Employee Enterprise Overpromises on Future Large-Scale Hiring (Rejection Rate 25–35%)

**Common Scenarios**
- Write 「sales will triple from subsidy, so we'll hire 10 people next year」
- Unrealistic plans lose reviewer trust

**Improvement Strategy**
- If hiring plans exist, present **gradual, realistic plans** (e.g., 「Year 1: hire 1 sales administrator; Year 2: hire 2 salespeople」)
- No need to artificially inflate hiring plans——continued zero-employee operation is acceptable

**Rules**
- Employee salaries are typically NOT subsidy-eligible expenses
- Social insurance burden increases from new hiring → absorbed by company
- However, if you demonstrate at final report that 「subsidy enabled growth that resulted in hiring」, you may qualify for **bonus subsidy** (subject to program terms and reviewing authority's decision)

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: Can I receive a subsidy as a zero-employee enterprise and then shut down after completion without repaying it?

**A:** Subsidy repayment obligations typically do not apply. However, repayment is required in these cases: ①you did not execute the business plan contents, ②you used funds for non-eligible expenses, ③fraudulent filing is discovered within 3 years. Shutting down a zero-employee enterprise is legally unremarkable, but 「promising new hiring to receive subsidy, then shutting down without implementation」 creates high repayment risk. You must fulfill your final performance report obligation. Typically, you can shut down without issue 6 months after subsidy completion reporting.

### Q2: Can foreign business owners (with work-authorization visa) apply for zero-employee subsidies?

**A:** Yes, possible. Foreign business owners who have properly filed opening notifications and completed tax filings in Japan are eligible for subsidy applications. Confirm: ①if corporation, is representative director properly registered, ②if individual business owner, do you have 3 years' blue or white tax return filings, ③is bank account in enterprise or personal name. Chinese business owners with 「Business Manager (経営・管理)」 residency status (formerly 「Investor/Business Manager」) can apply to all subsidies. If your visa is 「Technical/Humanistic Knowledge/International Services」, you are treated as 「side business」 and may face restrictions on programs like IT Adoption Subsidy——consult Immigration Services Bureau in advance.

### Q3: If I hire 1 employee during subsidy application, does it increase adoption likelihood?

**A:** Not directly. However, it **demonstrates planning to the positive**. For example: 「applying as zero-employee → hiring 1 administrative staff before adoption → noting 「already secured 1 employee」 in business plan」 may earn reviewers' evaluation of 「high plan implementation likelihood」. But falsification is strictly prohibited. You must prove genuine employment exists (employment contract, payroll records). If hiring occurs after transfer decision, it may earn bonus points in final report. Starting with 「zero-employee individual operation」 as your strategy is also legitimate——the persuasiveness of your business plan is paramount, not employee count.

### Q4: Can zero-employee enterprises operate equipment purchased with subsidy funds?

**A:** Yes. Many zero-employee enterprises use subsidies for automated/self-service business models. Examples: vending machine installation, online payment systems, cloud accounting software, etc.——operable without employees. This actually aligns with the subsidy's purpose of 「reducing labor through equipment to improve operational efficiency」. However, you must clearly explain during application 「how this equipment will be operated」. For example: 「AI inventory management system will eliminate 2 hours of manual work daily → freed time will be redirected to SNS advertising and customer service」 significantly strengthens persuasiveness.

### Q5: If my spouse and I operate an individual business, can I list 「zero employees」 on the subsidy application?

**A:** If either spouse is 「employee」 receiving salary, the enterprise has 1+ employees and misrepresenting it as zero is falsification. The following count as 「zero employees」: ①both spouses operate as business principals (no salary, profit-share only), ②partner assists but receives no salary (effectively co-principal), ③incorporating as corporation with one principal and partner as staff (salaried = 「1 employee」 classification). Check the 「salary」 line on tax returns to determine classification. The Sustainability Subsidy targets 「5 employees or fewer (commerce/industry)」, so couple enterprises receiving salary are still eligible.

### Q6: After subsidy approval, if I cease meeting eligibility criteria (e.g., employee count exceeds SME threshold), is there cancellation risk?

**A:** Risk exists. Particularly if requirements are no longer met **during implementation**, risk is high. For example, Business Restructuring Subsidy excludes 「corporations with 1,000+ full-time employees」; if you had 800 at application but reached 1,500 mid-implementation, discovery could result in transfer cancellation. However, improved business conditions (increased hiring) are generally viewed positively. At final report, explaining 「subsidy enabled business expansion, leading to increased hiring」 typically poses no problem. In fact, this is considered 「successful subsidy policy outcome」. If concerned, consult the subsidy office during final reporting stage.

**1. Reframe 「Zero-Employee」 as 「Operational Efficiency」**

Rather than defensive positioning 「we cannot hire」, high-success enterprises reframe: 「we leverage automation and digital tools for efficiency」. This transforms zero-employee status from a weakness into a modern business model strength. Review agencies increasingly value this positioning, especially in a post-pandemic context where remote work and automation are normalized.

Example in Business Plan: Instead of 「we have no employees」, write: 「our model minimizes fixed labor costs through AI-driven operations, allowing capital reallocation to product innovation. We will prioritize hiring only after achieving profitability targets (Year 2–3 projections).」

**2. Invest Planning Time (Investment = Persuasiveness)**

Successful applicants invest 40–60 working hours in business plan development (roughly 6–8 weeks with support agency guidance). This is not excessive; it typically yields 2–3 draft cycles before final submission. Each cycle incorporates reviewer feedback, strengthening persuasiveness.

Low-success applicants often rush: 「complete in 2 weeks, submit quickly」. This strategy backfires. Reviewers can discern preparation depth, and insufficient planning reduces adoption likelihood by 30–40%.

**3. Secure 「Certified Support Agency」 Endorsement Early**

Zero-employee enterprises must obtain Certified Support Agency confirmation for programs like Business Restructuring Subsidy (mandatory) or gain their advocacy for others. This third-party validation significantly increases credibility. Agencies bring 20+ years of subsidy review experience and understand reviewer priorities.

## Risk Mitigation: Five Pitfalls Unique to Zero-Employee Applications

Even with perfect operational foundation and business plan, zero-employee enterprises face specific risks:

### Pitfall 1: Misaligned Expectations Between Business Plan and Subsidy Category

**Risk**: You write 「want to hire 2 people」 in Business Restructuring Subsidy (hiring not its purpose), or 「want to purchase office furniture」 in IT Adoption Subsidy (outside scope). Misalignment→ automatic rejection.

**Prevention**: Match project type to subsidy type before planning. Each subsidy has specific eligible expense categories (available on each program's official website). Consulting your support agency is essential here.

### Pitfall 2: Insufficient Documentation of 「Business Performance」

**Risk**: Sole proprietor business plan relies on verbal explanations without supporting evidence (sales records, customer contracts, testimonials). Reviewers cannot verify feasibility.

**Prevention**: For zero-employee enterprises, provide:
- ✓ Past 2–3 years' tax return statements (not just summary, full documents)
- ✓ Bank statements showing deposit/withdrawal pattern (sales inflows distinct from expenses)
- ✓ Customer reference letters (if B2B business) or sales records (if B2C)
- ✓ Testimonials or case studies (especially if new service/product)

### Pitfall 3: Vague 「Use of Funds」 Narrative

**Risk**: Write 「purchase equipment」 without specifics: model, cost, expected ROI unclear. Reviewers cannot assess whether subsidy amount is proportionate to planned improvements.

**Prevention**: For each subsidy-eligible expense, provide:
- Equipment model and cost (with manufacturer brochure or quote)
- Measurable expected improvement (「current 2-hour daily data entry → 30 min with new software」)
- Implementation timeline (month 1–3: purchase; month 4–6: staff training; month 7–12: performance monitoring)

### Pitfall 4: Overstated Hiring Commitment Without Feasibility

**Risk**: Write 「will hire 5 people」 in Year 1 to gain bonus points, but business plan projects only 10% revenue growth——impossible to support 5-person payroll. Reviewers recognize the inconsistency and reject the entire plan.

**Prevention**: Ensure hiring plans match financial projections. Example:
- Projected Year 1 revenue: 5 million JPY; Margin: 40%; Operating profit: 2 million JPY
- If one employee costs ~4 million JPY annually (salary + social insurance), Year 1 hiring is infeasible.
- Realistic: hire Year 2 when margin improves to 50%+ or revenue reaches 10 million JPY.

### Pitfall 5: Forgetting Post-Approval Reporting Obligations

**Risk**: You receive subsidy approval, execute the project, but submit performance report carelessly (missing receipts, unclear photos, inconsistent timelines). During verification, discrepancies emerge, and subsidy is clawed back or program eligibility is revoked for future cycles.

**Prevention**: Maintain contemporaneous records from Day 1:
- ✓ Keep ALL receipts (original + scans stored digitally)
- ✓ Take dated photos of equipment during purchase, delivery, installation
- ✓ Maintain project timeline log (each milestone with dates)
- ✓ Document any deviations from original plan with explanatory notes
- ✓ Consult support agency 2 weeks before final report submission for dry-run review
