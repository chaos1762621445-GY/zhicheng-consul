# Which Subsidies Can Chinese-Run Minshuku and Ryokan Businesses in Japan Apply For? A Complete Guide

Japan's minshuku and ryokan industry has weathered ups and downs in recent years, and to support the development of the lodging sector, the government has rolled out a range of subsidy policies. As a Chinese business owner in Japan, if you run a minshuku, ryokan, private lodging (minpaku), or other accommodation facility, understanding the eligibility and process for these subsidies is key to expanding your business and cutting costs. This article takes an in-depth look at the specifics of subsidies for Chinese-run minshuku and ryokan industry subsidies, to help you find the funding support that fits your enterprise.

## The Overall Landscape of Minshuku and Ryokan Subsidies

Support for the lodging industry from the Japanese government and local municipalities is growing stronger year by year. According to data from the Japan Tourism Agency, in 2023 international tourists to Japan reached 25.04 million—a record high. To respond to this wave of growth and upgrade infrastructure, both the national level and the prefectural level have set up various lodging-industry grants.

When applying for these subsidies, Chinese minshuku operators need to be mindful of several prerequisites:
- Holding a legal lodging business license or a simple-lodging registration
- Meeting the eligible-region requirements of each subsidy
- Submitting properly formatted application documents and a business plan

The main sources of subsidies currently fall into three major categories: **national-level subsidies**, **local municipality subsidies**, and **industry association subsidies**.

## National-Level Subsidies—Lodging Industry Grants

### Japan Tourism Agency Subsidy Programs

Japan's national Tourism Agency rolls out subsidy programs for the lodging industry every year. **The most significant one for FY2024 is the "Accommodation Facility Productivity Improvement Project" (Accommodation Facility Productivity Improvement Project)**, a subsidy program to help accommodation facilities improve operational efficiency, increase the number of guest rooms, or enhance facility quality.

**Eligibility:**
- Operators of accommodation facilities in Japan (including minpaku, ryokan, hotels, etc.)
- The proposed project must have a productivity-improvement effect
- The applicant must be a corporation or sole proprietor
- The project investment amount typically needs to be at least 1 million yen

**Subsidy rate and amount:**
- Subsidy rate: usually 1/2 or 1/3 of the investment amount
- Subsidy ceiling: about 5 million yen for small-scale projects, and 10 million yen or more for large-scale projects
- The specific amount depends on the application year and project type

**Scope of supportable projects:**
- Guest room renovation and upgrades; adding new guest rooms
- Improvement of shared facilities (large public baths, restaurants, lobbies, etc.)
- Introduction of reservation systems and management systems
- Installation of energy-saving equipment
- Barrier-free renovation

**Real case:** A Shanghai-born minshuku operator running a 15-room minshuku in Kanagawa Prefecture invested 3.5 million yen in upgrading the rooms' WiFi, remodeling the air-conditioning system, and setting up an online reservation system. By applying for this grant, they obtained a subsidy of 1.5 million yen, leaving an actual out-of-pocket cost of only 2 million yen.

### Employment Promotion Subsidy

If your minshuku needs to hire new staff due to expansion, you can apply for the **"Older Workers, etc. Diversified Employment Promotion Project."**

**Eligibility:**
- Newly hired employees who meet certain conditions (such as older workers, people with disabilities, etc.)
- An employment contract term of at least 1 year
- Meeting the requirements for enrollment in employment insurance

**Subsidy content:**
- For each qualifying employee hired, a subsidy of 500,000–800,000 yen is available
- The subsidy period is usually 6–12 months

## Local Municipality Subsidies—Region-Specific Support

### Prefectural-Level Lodging Industry Grants

Almost every prefecture has its own lodging-industry subsidy policy. In the regions where Chinese minshuku operators are most concentrated, the subsidy policies are as follows:

**Kyoto Prefecture:**
- "Accommodation Facility Function Improvement Project Subsidy," with a subsidy rate of up to 2/3
- Focused on supporting projects that convert traditional private homes into minshuku
- Subsidy amount of up to 8 million yen
- As a hub for traditional minshuku, Kyoto offers substantial support for Chinese-run machiya (townhouse) minshuku

**Osaka Prefecture:**
- "Inbound-Ready Accommodation Facility Development Support Project," aimed at improving facilities for international guests
- Subsidy rate of 1/2, up to 5 million yen
- Includes multilingual signage, multi-country payment systems, and more

**Tokyo:**
- "SME Facility Repair Grant System," not limited to any industry—the lodging industry can also apply
- Subsidy rate of up to 1/2, amount of 500,000–10 million yen
- Requires the facility to be within Tokyo and to have been operating for 3 years or more

**Fukuoka Prefecture:**
- "Accommodation Facility Development Cost Subsidy," aimed at new construction or renovation of accommodation facilities
- Subsidy rate of 1/3 or 1/2, depending on project scale
- Maximum subsidy amount of 15 million yen, suitable for larger-scale investment

### Municipal-Level Subsidies

Subsidies from municipal and lower-level authorities are usually more down-to-earth and easier to obtain. For example:

**Kyoto City:**
- "Subsidy to Promote Barrier-Free Conversion of Simple Lodging, etc.," a dedicated program supporting barrier-free renovation
- Subsidy rate of 1/2, up to 2 million yen

**Chuo Ward, Osaka City:**
- Newly established minshuku enterprises can receive an initial-cost subsidy of 500,000–1 million yen

**Fukuoka City:**
- "Accommodation Facility ICT Adoption Subsidy Project," supporting investment in information systems
- Subsidy amount of 500,000–2 million yen

## Dedicated Subsidies for Eco-Friendliness and Upgrade Renovations

### Energy-Saving Renovation Subsidies

As carbon-neutrality goals advance, energy-saving renovation subsidies have become a new growth area.

**Ministry of the Environment-related subsidies:**
- The accommodation facility portion of the "Decarbonization Promotion Project Cost Subsidy"
- Supports LED lighting conversion, high-efficiency air conditioning, and solar panel installation
- Subsidy rate: usually 1/3 or 1/2
- Subsidy ceiling: 5 million–10 million yen
- Prioritizes projects with a clear CO2-reduction effect

**Concrete example:** A 40-room ryokan that invests 6 million yen in installing a solar system and LED lighting can obtain a subsidy of 3 million yen.

### Disaster-Prevention Renovation Subsidies

In 2024, with abnormal weather becoming more frequent, the level of support for disaster-prevention facility upgrades has increased.

**Eligible projects:**
- Seismic reinforcement
- Waterproofing and flood-prevention facility renovation
- Installation of emergency power generation systems
- Construction of disaster-preparedness supply storage

**Subsidy rate:** up to 1/2, amount of 1 million–8 million yen

## Application Process and Practical Operation Guide

### Step 1: Information Gathering and Eligibility Confirmation

1. **Determine your facility type:**
   - Ryokan business license (general ryokan, simple lodging)
   - Minpaku registration (Private Lodging Business Act)
   - Other lodging businesses

2. **Confirm your geographic location:**
   - Note the prefecture, city/ward, and town/village
   - Look up the latest subsidy list on your local municipality's official website
   - Note the deadlines (most subsidies have annual application periods)

3. **Key website roundup:**
   - Japan Tourism Agency: www.mlit.go.jp/kankocho/
   - The official websites of each prefecture
   - J-Net21 (SME Business Support Portal)
   - The websites of local Chambers of Commerce and Industry

### Step 2: Project Planning and Budget Preparation

Most subsidies require the submission of detailed application documents, at the core of which is a solid business plan.

**Required document checklist:**
- Application form (designated format)
- Business plan (1–3 pages, covering the current state, goals, implementation content, and expected effects)
- Income and expenditure budget (an itemized investment table, precise to the man-yen [10,000 yen] unit)
- Facility overview (floor area, number of guest rooms, existing equipment)
- Track record report (if you have had similar projects)
- A copy of the license

**Key points for preparation:**
- Data must be precise: don't estimate—you need invoices or quotations
- Effects must be quantified: e.g., "expected to increase room occupancy by 15%," "annual expected revenue increase of 20 million yen"
- Goals must be reasonable: don't overstate—the evaluating officials will scrutinize
- The timeline must be clear: when you will purchase, when you will construct, when you will inspect and accept

**Characteristics of successful cases:** An approved business plan usually contains: a clear current-state analysis → clear improvement goals → a concrete implementation plan → quantified expected effects → a reasonable funding requirement.

### Step 3: Application Submission

1. **For online applications:** Most subsidies now use "jGrants" (the government subsidy management system) for online application
   - Register a jGrants account
   - Fill out the application form
   - Upload PDF files (be mindful of file size limits)
   - Confirm submission

2. **For mail applications:** Some local municipality subsidies still use mail
   - Prepare the documents in the required number of copies
   - Send by registered mail to the designated department
   - Keep the mailing receipt as proof

3. **Points to note when submitting:**
   - Submit strictly by the deadline (extensions are usually not accepted)
   - Ensure all required documents are complete
   - The application form formats of different subsidies vary greatly and cannot be used interchangeably
   - If you are unsure about Japanese documents, you can have a professional institution check them

### Step 4: Post-Application Communication and Review

- **Review period:** usually 1–3 months
- **Possible additional inquiries:** the municipality may request supplementary materials or conduct a phone call/interview
- **Inspection and acceptance:** before the subsidy is paid, you must submit completion certificates, invoices, and photos

### Step 5: Subsidy Payment and Reporting

- **Payment method:** usually paid in installments (e.g., 80% after handover, 20% after the full report)
- **Subsidy period:** from the application year, retroactive to the project implementation date
- **Annual report:** you need to report actual results and changes in operating data

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: I run my minshuku as an individual and have not registered a company—can I apply for a subsidy?

**A:** Yes, you can. Japan's subsidy system makes no absolute distinction between sole proprietors and corporations. The key is that you need a legal business license or minpaku registration, as well as a valid Japanese bank account. That said, the application document requirements will differ somewhat. A sole proprietor needs to submit a personal tax return (final tax return) rather than a company financial statement. We recommend that even individual operators keep proper bookkeeping records, as this is important both for the application and for subsequent review. If you are worried about the language barrier or the complexity of the process, you can commission an accountant or subsidy consultant to apply on your behalf; the fee is usually 5–10% of the subsidy amount.

### Q2: My minshuku just opened last year and doesn't yet have complete performance data—will this affect my subsidy application?

**A:** This won't directly cause your application to be rejected, but it does require special handling. Many subsidies have no hard "years in operation" requirement for applicants but will ask you to submit existing performance data. If you have only a few months of data, we recommend adding a good explanatory note in your business plan: describe in detail your customer mix since opening, your average room occupancy, customer feedback, and so on. At the same time, submit a persuasive future development plan explaining why this investment will bring returns. Some subsidies are designed specifically for newly established lodging operators, such as certain local "New Business Startup Support Subsidies"—well worth focusing on. Rather than waiting until you have 2–3 years of data before applying, you're better off actively inquiring now; you may well find a more suitable program.

### Q3: If one subsidy application is rejected, can I reapply the following year? Or apply for multiple subsidies at once?

**A:** A rejected subsidy can, in principle, be reapplied for the following year, but you need to analyze the reason for rejection and make improvements. Common reasons for rejection include: the project content doesn't match the subsidy's purpose, the budget is unreasonable, the plan isn't specific enough, or the competition that year was too intense. You can ask the application department for feedback, which is very helpful for improving your application. As for applying for multiple subsidies at once, this question is more complex. **In principle it is allowed, but there is one important restriction**: the same project (for example, the same renovation work on the same minshuku) cannot receive subsidies more than once. That is, you cannot apply for both a national-level subsidy and a local subsidy to support the same expense. However, you can apply for multiple subsidies to support different projects or different expenses—for example, using subsidy A for room renovation and subsidy B for a system update. When submitting your application, be sure to clearly label the purpose of each expense to avoid duplicate claims.

### Q4: For the income and expenditure budget required in a subsidy application, do I need to get a formal quotation from a construction company or supplier?

**A:** Yes, this is required. At the initial application, you need at least one formal quotation (estimate) from a specific construction company or supplier. This quotation must be a formal document bearing the company's seal, date, validity period, and itemized list—it cannot be a WeChat screenshot or a WeChat quote. Why so strict? Because the municipality needs to confirm that your project costs are reasonable and to prevent inflated reporting or waste. The recommended approach is: before applying, contact at least 2–3 suppliers to obtain quotations, and choose one with a reasonable price to attach to your application. After approval, you can adjust based on actual needs, but the final actual invoice amount cannot deviate from the quoted amount in the application by more than 10–15% (the exact percentage varies slightly by municipality). If the actual expense clearly exceeds the quotation, you may need to bear the excess yourself, or you may need to apply for a change in advance.

### Q5: I've heard that after a subsidy comes through, there is a chance of "repayment"—what does that mean? If the project doesn't achieve the expected effect, does the subsidy have to be returned?

**A:** This is a very good question. The risk of subsidy "repayment" does indeed exist, but it is not that you must return it just because the project "has no effect." There are two main repayment situations. The first is **false reporting or improper use of funds.** If a later audit finds that you inflated costs, diverted funds to other uses, or ceased operations, you must repay. The second is **the ongoing-operation requirement after project completion.** Many subsidies stipulate that within 3–5 years after receiving the subsidy, you must continue operating the facility and cannot transfer or close it. If you violate this condition, you must repay part of the subsidy. In addition, some subsidies do have requirements on "effect"—for example, a commitment to achieve 50% annual room occupancy; if an audit two years later finds it is actually only 30%, you may also need to repay. But such strict repayment ratios are rare. **The recommended approach is:** estimate the expected effects conservatively when applying and don't over-promise; keep all invoices and construction photos; and keep operating the facility rather than abandoning it midway. Do this, and there will basically be no repayment risk.

## Common Mistakes in Subsidy Applications and a Pitfall-Avoidance Guide

1. **Mistake: Ignoring the application deadline**
   - Many subsidies have only a fixed application period each year (such as April–June); miss it and you have to wait until the next year.
   - We recommend starting to track information every January—don't wait until the deadline is near to prepare.

2. **Mistake: A business plan that is too broad or vague**
   - Expressions like "improve the facility" or "enhance the customer experience" are too general.
   - You should be specific: "install new split air conditioners in 12 single rooms, expected to reduce power consumption by 20%."

3. **Mistake: Neglecting reporting and record-keeping**
   - After a subsidy is completed, you need to submit a completion report, including photos, invoices, and proof of use.
   - Without these records, the subsidy cannot be paid and you may even be required to repay it.

4. **Mistake: Japanese documents that fail to convey the meaning**
   - If the Japanese expression is off, the evaluating staff may not understand your project's intent.
   - We strongly recommend having a professional Japanese business writer or institution check them—the cost is worth it.

5. **Mistake: Over-relying on a single subsidy**
   - Pinning all your hopes on one subsidy leaves you passive if it is rejected or the amount is insufficient.
   - You should prepare 3–5 candidate subsidies at the same time and apply for multiple projects.

## Advantages and Points to Note for Chinese Minshuku Operators

### Potential Advantages

- **International operating experience**: Chinese operators usually understand the needs of Asian customers (guests from China, Taiwan, and Hong Kong) better, and can emphasize this in the business plan; goals of attracting more international guests together with multilingual renovation projects are easy to get approved.

- **Cost control ability**: Chinese networks are extensive and procurement costs are often lower, which can make your project budget more reasonable and increase the odds of approval.

- **Community support**: There are many experienced minshuku operators within the Chinese community who can share subsidy application experience with one another.

### Special Points to Note

- **Language and documents**: All application documents must be in Japanese. If your Japanese ability is limited, be sure to find a professional translator or consultant—don't force it.

- **Tax compliance**: While applying for a subsidy, your business tax filing (consumption tax) and income tax filing must be proper. The municipality will check your tax records.

- **Building credit**: In Japan, once you successfully obtain a subsidy, your credit improves and your next application becomes easier. We recommend taking your first project seriously and not diverting the funds to other uses.
