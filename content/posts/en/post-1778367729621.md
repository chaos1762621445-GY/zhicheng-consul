# The Complete Guide to the Human Resource Development Grant: Up to 75% of AI Training Costs Covered by the Government — A Must-Read for Chinese Businesses in Japan

Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare runs the Human Resource Development Grant (人材開発支援助成金), a government subsidy program created specifically for employee training. Any eligible Chinese business in Japan that conducts AI-related training or vocational-skills training can receive up to 75% of the training costs — in other words, for a 1,000,000 JPY AI training course, the company only pays 250,000 JPY. This article systematically explains the core conditions, application process, and common pitfalls of this program, helping you understand precisely how to bring high-quality training to your team while easing cost pressure.

---

## Overview and Core Value of the Human Resource Development Grant

The Human Resource Development Grant (hereafter "the training grant") is administered by Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, and is designed to encourage companies to invest in developing their employees — especially in national priority industries such as digitalization, AI, and green energy. As of fiscal 2024, the program benefits about 28,000 companies each year, with total grants exceeding 20 billion JPY.

**Why should Chinese businesses pay particular attention to this program?**

First, the subsidy rate is far higher than other SME support programs: general training courses have a subsidy rate of 60%, but for strategic courses involving AI, DX (digital transformation), or green skills, the rate rises to 75% — reducing three-quarters of the cost pressure that would otherwise be borne out of pocket. Second, the program is relatively lenient on company size, years since establishment, and industry — whether you are a startup founded just 2 years ago or a manufacturer with 100 employees, you may qualify. Third, the disbursement timeline is relatively stable: generally, compensation is received about 3–4 months after the training is completed (subject to the reviewing authority's decision).

According to fiscal 2023 data from Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, applications for AI-related courses grew 47% year over year, with an approval rate of 68% — reflecting the government's high priority on developing DX talent. For Chinese businesses in Japan, seizing this window of policy dividends is especially crucial.

## Subsidy Amounts and Rates in Detail: How Much Can You Get?

### Base Subsidy Amount Structure

The Human Resource Development Grant is divided into two main parts: the **Training Cost Grant** (訓練経費助成金) and the **Wage Grant** (賃金助成金).

**Training Cost Grant (訓練経費助成金):**
- **General Off-JT training** (classroom training): subsidy rate 60%, with a cap of 60% of training expenses
- **AI/DX and other strategic priority fields**: subsidy rate raised to 75%, meaning a 1,000,000 JPY course requires only 250,000 JPY out of pocket
- **OJT training** (on-the-job training): also a 60% subsidy rate, but calculated based on hours × hourly wage

Specific figures follow the fiscal 2024 public-offering guidelines, but the subsidy caps are typically set within the following ranges:
- Company size of 300 or fewer employees: an annual cap of about **3,000,000 JPY**
- Company size of 300 or more employees: the cap may be raised to **4,000,000 JPY** or more

**Wage Grant (賃金助成金):**

If employees cannot work normally while receiving training, the company can apply for a wage grant:
- The grant is **80%** of the wages the employee would have earned during training
- The cap is about **1,200 JPY** per hour (the exact figure follows the reviewing authority's notice for the given year)

### Subsidy Rate Differences by Course Type

| Course type | Subsidy rate | Typical use case | Notes |
|---------|--------|---------|------|
| General Off-JT training | 60% | General skills, management training | Most stable subsidy |
| AI/DX training | 75% | Data analysis, automation, ChatGPT applications | Priority direction in 2024 |
| Labor productivity improvement training | 75% | Courses directly related to production efficiency | Must show link to company strategy |
| Core-talent development training | 70% | Developing department heads and technical leaders | Requires a detailed development plan |
| OJT training | 60% | Learning while working | Calculated by actual instruction hours |

**A conversion example:**

Suppose your Chinese-owned manufacturing company (50 employees) wants to enroll 5 core employees in a 6-month hands-on AI application course, with a total course cost of 2,500,000 JPY.

- At a 75% subsidy rate: grant = 2,500,000 JPY × 75% = **1,875,000 JPY**
- Company's out-of-pocket cost: 2,500,000 JPY − 1,875,000 JPY = **625,000 JPY**
- The company's cost drops sharply from the original 2,500,000 JPY to 625,000 JPY — a 75% reduction in pressure.

This is precisely why AI training subsidies are especially attractive to cost-sensitive small and medium Chinese businesses.

## Eligible Applicants and Requirements: Does Your Company Qualify?

### Basic Eligibility Conditions

A company applying for the Human Resource Development Grant must simultaneously meet the following conditions (subject to the reviewing authority's official review):

**1. Employment structure requirements**
- The company must have legally concluded labor contracts with its employees (regular employees and fixed-term contract workers both qualify)
- The employees receiving training must have already worked at the company for **6 months or more** before training begins
- The employees must remain employed for **3 months or more** after training ends (this is a key condition to prevent abuse of the grant)

**2. Company size and business condition**
- **In principle, there is no company-size limit**: from a one-person sole proprietor to a large enterprise, all may apply
- However, there are clear requirements for labor-insurance enrollment: the company must enroll the employee in employment insurance and social insurance
- The applying company must have 2 or more years of operating history (startups generally do not qualify)

**3. Legal compliance and financial soundness**
- The company must **not have received any major penalty from the Labor Standards Inspection Office** within the past 3 years
- The company must not be in bankruptcy, settlement, or restructuring proceedings
- The company's taxable income should be positive, or, if negative, accompanied by a reasonable explanation

**4. Social insurance and tax status**
- Social insurance and employment insurance payments must not be more than 1 month in arrears
- Corporate tax and local tax from the prior year should be paid on time (minor delinquencies may be workable, but we recommend consulting in advance)

### Special Notes for Chinese Businesses

**Corporation vs. sole proprietor:**
Although sole proprietors can legally apply, their actual approval rate is relatively low (about 30%), because it is difficult for a sole proprietor to prove the condition that "the employee remains employed for 3 months after training." We recommend that Chinese businesses with support needs consider registering as a limited company or a stock company.

**Regarding foreign employees:**
Employees of Chinese, Vietnamese, and other nationalities can fully participate as training targets, as long as they hold a valid work qualification (technical intern, highly skilled professional, engineer/specialist in humanities/international services, etc.) and have formally signed a labor contract. But the applying company itself must be a domestic Japanese company.

**Bonus for membership in a Chinese chamber of commerce or association in Japan:**
Although not a hard condition, if the company is a member of a local Chinese chamber of commerce, or actively participates in social-contribution activities, mentioning this in the application materials helps build the reviewer's trust — it does not guarantee approval, but it can increase the chances of passing.

## Scope of Eligible Expenses: What Can and Cannot Be Reimbursed?

### Categories of Eligible Expenses

Understanding the "scope" of the subsidy is key to a successful application. Japan's grant system has strict definitions of eligible expenditure items, and only expenses meeting the definition can be included in the subsidy base.

**✅ Clearly eligible items:**

1. **Course fees from external training organizations** (most common)
   - Training fees from universities, vocational schools, and corporate training organizations: **100% eligible**
   - Online course fees (Udemy, LinkedIn Learning, etc.): must be declared in advance, usually **100% eligible**
   - Training license fees for AI-related SaaS tools: **partially eligible**, but you must clearly provide proof of the training content

2. **Materials and instructor fees**
   - Purchase of designated materials: **100% eligible**
   - In-house instructor compensation: subsidized in the OJT form (**60% eligible**, calculated by hourly wage)
   - Fees for external instructors: **100% eligible**, but invoices and proof of the instructor's qualifications are required

3. **Wages and travel expenses of the trained employees**
   - Regular wages during the employee's training period (if unable to work): **80% eligible**, capped at about 1,200 JPY/hour
   - Commuting/transport and accommodation costs during training: **100% eligible** (invoices required)

4. **Equipment and system purchase costs** (conditional)
   - Hardware purchased specifically for the training project (such as GPU servers for AI experiments): **50–70% eligible**, requires prior declaration and approval
   - Annual license fees for specialized software (directly related to the training): **partially eligible**

**❌ Clearly ineligible items:**

- Upgrade costs for the company's existing everyday office equipment (even if used for training)
- Cafeteria and dormitory operating costs not directly related to training
- Overseas study-tour costs for company management (unless you can prove the acquired knowledge is used for training design)
- Recruitment and selection costs
- Infrastructure investment for company operations
- The same expense that has already received another government subsidy (no duplicate subsidies)

In this case, the company would originally have paid 2,250,000 JPY out of pocket, but after the subsidy its actual out-of-pocket cost is only **440,000 JPY** — an effective subsidy rate of **80%**, exceeding the standard 75% cap. This is because, through a reasonable combination of expenses, some items reached a 100% subsidy rate.

## Application Process and Timeline: How Long From Enrollment to Receiving the Grant?

### Standard Application Process (5 Stages)

The Human Resource Development Grant application is divided into two major phases — the "advance declaration" and the "post-completion application" — and the entire cycle typically takes **8–12 months**.

**Stage 1: Advance preparation (1–2 months before applying)**
- Confirm the course content, cost, and schedule with the training provider
- Gather company materials: business permit (営業許可証), employment-insurance enrollment confirmation, financial statements (決算書類) for the past 2 years, etc.
- Employee consent forms: obtain written consent from the employees participating in training
- **Key deadline**: be sure to complete these preparations at least 60 days before training begins

**Stage 2: Advance declaration (60 days before training starts)**
- Submit the "plan notification" (計画届出) to your local Hello Work (public employment office) or a commissioned Human Resource Development Service Center
- Main documents include:
  - Training plan (訓練計画書): course name, schedule, instructor qualifications, cost breakdown
  - Company labor-management records (企業の労務管理実績表): the company's payroll and attendance records for the past six months, etc.
  - Contract with the cooperating training company (訓練協力企業との契約書): the contract with the training organization (a key review item)
- **Review period**: usually 15–30 days; the local agency will provide written notice of whether it agrees (not a guarantee)
- **Key tip**: this stage is most likely to trigger a request for supplements due to incomplete materials or unclear logic, so we recommend allowing 2 weeks for back-and-forth revisions

**Stage 3: Training implementation (3–12 months, varies)**
- Carry out the training according to the approved plan, retaining all documentation:
  - Instructor attendance records and sign-in sheets
  - Invoices (course fees, materials fees, instructor fees)
  - Employee payroll and attendance records
  - Transcripts or training-completion certificates
- **Key requirement**: be sure to complete the training within the specified time; being late or absent beyond the prescribed ratio will affect the grant amount (an attendance rate of ≥80% is usually required)
- **Financial-audit red flag**: all expenditures must go through the company account; cash payment or reimbursement after personal outlay is not allowed (although the latter is theoretically acceptable, it easily raises audit questions)

**Stage 4: Post-completion application (within 1 month after training ends)**
- Submit the "payment application" (支給申請書) and all expenditure documentation to your local Hello Work
- Core documents that must be submitted:
  - Payment application form (支給申請書, the official form)
  - Documents on training results (訓練の実績に関する書類): attendance records, grades, completion certificates
  - Documents proving expenditure (経費の支出を証する書類): invoices, bank transfer records, receipts, etc.
  - Confirmation of employment-insurance status (雇用保険被保険者の確認): confirming the employee's employment status before and after training
- **Key detail**: the company name and registration number on invoices must exactly match the applying company; bank transfer records must be clear, with no payment on behalf of others or use of another's name
- **Submission deadline**: usually within 60 days after training fully ends; being late may cause the grant to be rejected

**Stage 5: Review and disbursement (3–4 months after application)**
- Hello Work conducts a document review, usually taking 4–8 weeks
- If the reviewer has questions, they will contact the company by email or phone for supplementary explanation
- After confirmation, the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare issues a "payment decision" (支給決定) notice
- The grant is paid to the company's designated bank account (**subject to the reviewing authority's decision**; the exact arrival time may vary)

### Detailed Timeline Example

| Time point | Action | Duration |
|---------|------|------|
| Month -2 | Prepare materials, confirm training organization and course | 2 weeks |
| Month -1.5 | Submit advance declaration to Hello Work | 2 weeks |
| Month -1 to Month 0 | Review, supplement materials, approval | 3–4 weeks |
| Month 1–6 | Carry out training, retain documentation | Variable |
| Month 6.5 | Training ends, compile materials | 2 weeks |
| Month 7 | Submit post-completion application to Hello Work | 1 week |
| Month 7–10 | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare review | 4–8 weeks |
| Month 10 | Receive grant (expected) | End of same month |

**Total cycle**: from deciding to participate in training to receiving the grant, it usually takes 10–12 months, and even at the fastest it takes 8 months (subject to the reviewing authority's decision).

## Common Pitfalls and Bonus Points: Avoid Traps and Raise Your Approval Rate

### The 5 Most Common Pitfalls in Applications

**Pitfall 1: Assuming all training expenses are reimbursable**

❌ **Wrong case**: A Chinese import-export company declared "employee overseas study-tour costs" as training expenses and was rejected outright.

✅ **Correct approach**: Rather than declaring an overseas study tour, partner with a foreign training organization for online courses — those costs are fully reimbursable.

**Pitfall 2: Only remembering the post-completion materials after training ends**

❌ **Wrong case**: A manager from a company from Shenzhen said, "The training is all done, let's hurry and gather the materials now," but because the original sign-in sheets and invoices from the training period were missing (already lost), the review could not proceed.

✅ **Correct approach**: From the very first day of training, keep daily attendance records, instructor signatures, and every invoice. We recommend designating a finance or HR staff member as the "dedicated grant administrator" to collect and organize these documents.

**Pitfall 3: Dismissing or reassigning employees immediately after training ends**

❌ **Wrong case**: The head of a Chinese manufacturing company assumed that "training is a talent investment; it doesn't matter if the employee leaves after the money arrives," but at the post-completion review it was found that the employee had already left before the grant was approved, causing the entire grant to be canceled.

✅ **Correct approach**: The grant conditions clearly require the "employee to remain employed for 3 months or more after training ends," or the grant will be clawed back. If an employee truly needs to leave, be sure to handle it before training begins or in the 4th month after the grant arrives.

**Pitfall 4: Confusing "advance declaration approval" with "guaranteed receipt of the grant"**

❌ **Wrong case**: After a company's advance declaration was approved, it told employees "the government will cover all the training costs," but afterward, due to insufficient attendance or invoice defects, it received only a partial grant.

✅ **Correct approach**: Advance declaration only grants the "eligibility" to participate; the actual disbursement still requires a detailed later review. When communicating with employees, you should say, "Under government policy, we have the opportunity to receive a 70–75% subsidy," rather than making an absolute promise.

**Pitfall 5: Letting the training organization handle all the application procedures so the company can "sit back and enjoy the benefits"**

❌ **Wrong case**: A company handed the entire grant application over to an external training organization, only to discover that the organization's application materials contained false statements about course content, altered dates, and so on. The company took the blame and was placed on the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "list of improper applications."

✅ **Correct approach**: An external organization can help prepare materials, but the company must review every application document itself, especially the parts involving training content, schedule, and costs. The safest approach is to work with a professional Sharoshi or a subsidy advisor.

### 3 Bonus Points for Approval (No Guarantee of Success, but They Do Raise the Odds)

**Bonus point 1: A medium- to long-term talent-development plan**

What reviewers value most is whether "the company is genuinely investing in talent," not a "passing whim." Clearly state in the application:
- "In 2024–2026 we plan to develop 50 core employees with AI skills, in three cohorts"
- "This training is the first cohort, followed by a plan for internal dissemination and application rollout"

This upgrades the subsidy from "one-time cost relief" to a "strategic talent investment," and significantly raises the chance of approval.

**Bonus point 2: An overseas parent company or cross-border training collaboration within the group**

If your Chinese business is the Japanese subsidiary of a large group, or collaborates on talent development with a headquarters back home, this is a very strong positive signal. You can emphasize in the application:
- "After this AI training succeeds, we plan to standardize the course content and roll it out to the group's other Asian branches"
- "It fully aligns with the parent company's DX strategy, reflecting the broader trend of global corporate upgrading"

**Bonus point 3: A connection to regional revitalization or social contribution**

If the training involves local industrial upgrading, or your company contributes to employment in its area, you can reflect this in the application:
- "We have already created X jobs locally, and this AI training will further raise the skill level of these roles"
- "Manufacturing in this region is facing industrial upgrading, and our AI training plan can become a local model case"

This gives the local Hello Work reviewer a sense of recognition that "you are doing something beneficial."

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: My company is wholly Chinese-owned and was established in Japan just 1 year ago. Can I apply for the Human Resource Development Grant?

**A:** In principle, you do not qualify. The Human Resource Development Grant requires the applying company to have "2 or more years of operating history." This is to ensure the company has a stable operating foundation and can continue employing staff after training ends. If your company was established in 2023, the earliest you can apply is 2025. However, we recommend consulting your local Hello Work once immediately after establishment to confirm whether any exception applies (though such exceptions are very rare). If you are truly in a hurry, consider first applying for other subsidy programs aimed at startups, such as the "Startup Support Grant" (起業支援助成金).

### Q2: I want to enroll my entire 30-person team in AI training, with a budget of about 8,000,000 JPY. How much can be subsidized?

**A:** First, congratulations on your foresight, but note the annual subsidy cap. For companies with 300 or fewer employees, **the annual subsidy cap is usually 3,000,000–3,500,000 JPY** (the exact figure follows that year's public-offering guidelines). Assuming a 75% subsidy rate, 8,000,000 JPY × 75% = 6,000,000 JPY, but due to the annual cap, the grant you can receive would be limited to about 3,000,000 JPY. ### Q3: I have already conducted AI training for my employees and only heard about this grant afterward. Can I still apply?

**A:** The Human Resource Development Grant generally requires the company to submit a plan application to the competent authority (the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare) **before applying** or **before training begins**, and to implement training only after approval. If the training is already fully completed, in most cases you cannot apply, because the grant is an **advance-incentive type** rather than after-the-fact compensation. We recommend immediately consulting your local Labour Bureau to confirm the deadline; some regions may accept a remedial application within 3 months of the training start, but this is subject to the official public-offering guidelines. For future training projects, plan the application in advance.

### Q4: What materials are needed to apply for this grant? As a Chinese business owner, will language be a barrier?

**A:** The application materials usually include: basic company information, employee roster, training course outline, budget breakdown, proof of instructor qualifications, and so on. All must be submitted in **Japanese** to the Labour Bureau. As a Chinese business owner, you can overcome the language barrier in the following ways: (1) hire a Japanese social-insurance advisor or a Gyoseishoshi to handle the application on your behalf (cost about 100,000–300,000 JPY); (2) commission an HR-outsourcing company experienced in subsidy applications; (3) consult your local SME support center, most of which provide free Japanese-translation assistance. We recommend allowing **at least 2 months** before the application deadline to prepare the materials.

### Q5: If my company's application is rejected, is there any recourse? How long until I learn the result?

**A:** After a rejection, the company can file an **objection** (異議申立) with the competent Labour Bureau within the prescribed period, usually a 30-day application window. Common rejection reasons include: the number of employees does not match, the training content does not meet requirements, or financial records are incomplete. **The result is usually notified within 4–8 weeks of submission**, subject to the official public-offering guidelines. We recommend keeping copies of all application documents; if rejected, you can consult a professional advisor to analyze the cause and reapply for the following fiscal year. Most companies have a higher success rate on their second application.

### Q6: After receiving the grant, if an employee leaves before the training is complete, do I have to repay it? How is this income treated for tax purposes?

**A:** After the grant is paid, **an employee's departure generally does not constitute grounds for repayment**, because the grant targets the company's training-investment behavior. However, if the company cannot submit complete proof of training implementation (attendance records, completion certificates, etc.), part of the grant already paid may be required to be returned, subject to the reviewing authority's decision. For tax purposes, a government grant is **non-taxable income** and is not included in corporate income tax, but the labor costs, instructor fees, and other expenses the grant covers still need to be reasonably classified. We recommend consulting a Zeirishi immediately after approval to ensure the accounting treatment is compliant and to avoid the risk of a subsequent tax audit.
