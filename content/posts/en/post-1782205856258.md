# 2026 Japan Subsidy Calendar: An Overview of Application Windows by Program

For Chinese business owners in Japan, mastering the subsidy application calendar is essential to filing on time and securing operating funds. Japan opens dozens of subsidy programs for applications each year, but because of strict timing requirements and fierce competition, missing an application window means losing an entire year's chance to apply. This article walks you through the major 2026 subsidy schedules, application deadlines, and coping strategies in detail.

## Understanding How Japanese Subsidy Windows Work

Before diving into the 2026 subsidy calendar, you first need to be clear on the logic behind how Japanese subsidies operate. Unlike many countries, Japanese subsidies are typically not open for applications year-round; instead, they follow a "public-offering period" system—each subsidy accepts applications intensively within a specific window, and anything past the deadline will not be accepted.

According to statistics from the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) and the Small and Medium Enterprise Agency, Japan offers roughly 150–200 SME-related subsidies to private enterprises each year. Broken down by industry, manufacturing accounts for 35%, services for 28%, construction for 18%, and others for 19%. Chinese business owners are mainly concentrated in service industries such as retail, food and beverage, and import-export trade, so understanding the application schedules in these fields is especially critical.

## Overview of the 2026 Mainstream Subsidy Application Schedule

### Business Innovation Grant (Business Innovation Support Subsidy)

**Application window**: Mid-April to end of June 2026 (typical)

The Business Innovation Grant is one of Japan's most important SME subsidy programs, with an annual budget of roughly 20 billion yen. This subsidy targets SMEs implementing a "business innovation plan," with a subsidy rate of up to 2/3 and a ceiling ranging from 1 million yen to 40 million yen (depending on the scale of the business).

If a Chinese enterprise plans to undertake a major business realignment, new product development, or digital transformation in 2026, it should complete its preliminary preparation—including drafting the business plan and confirming consultations with a certified support institution—between **mid-February and the end of March**. Because application materials are complex and the review cycle is long (typically 3–4 months), we recommend allowing at least a 4-month preparation period.

### Small Business Sustainability Subsidy (Small Business Sustainability Subsidy)

**Application window**: Mid-January to end of March 2026 (Round 1); mid-May to end of July 2026 (Round 2)

This subsidy has the broadest coverage of any program, with more than 1 million businesses applying on average each year. The ceiling is 1 million yen with a subsidy rate of 3/4, and it requires no repayment. It is particularly well-suited to newly launched or small Chinese micro-enterprises with 20 or fewer employees.

2025 data shows an average review cycle of 2 months and an approval rate of about 70%. We recommend submitting your application **at least two weeks** before the deadline, because servers often become congested with concurrent requests on the closing date. Many applicants who wait until the last day to submit end up timing out and being unable to apply.

### Business Restructuring Subsidy (Business Restructuring Subsidy)

**Application window**: Early March to mid-May 2026 (Round 1); mid-July to end of September 2026 (Round 2)

The Business Restructuring Subsidy is a core policy tool through which the Japanese government supports corporate innovation and strategic realignment. The ceiling ranges from 10 million yen to 150 million yen, with a subsidy rate of up to 2/3. If your enterprise needs to make a major strategic pivot (such as shifting from traditional retail to e-commerce, or from offline services to an online model), this subsidy may be the best choice.

This subsidy requires applicants to provide a detailed business plan, financial projections, market analysis reports, and more, and its review standards are strict. The average review cycle is 3–4 months. If you plan to apply in the first half of 2026, we recommend beginning to assemble your materials as early as **mid-December 2025**.

### Digital Transformation Subsidy (Digital Infrastructure Adoption Subsidy)

**Application window**: Mid-February to end of April 2026 (typical)

As the Japanese government pushes forward the building of a digital society, the importance of digital subsidies keeps rising. The Digitalization / AI Adoption Subsidy (formerly the IT Adoption Subsidy) targets SMEs adopting digital tools such as accounting software, inventory and sales management systems, and CRM systems. The normal category typically has a subsidy ceiling of 4.5 million yen and a subsidy rate of up to 1/2 (up to 2/3 if the minimum-wage-proximity requirement is met); it requires a joint application with a registered IT adoption support provider.

Because Chinese enterprises in Japan vary widely in their level of digitalization, this subsidy is highly practical. Import-export trading companies in particular can use the subsidy to purchase cross-border e-commerce platforms, foreign-exchange management systems, and the like. The application threshold is relatively low, and the approval rate is usually between 65% and 75%.

### R&D Subsidy (Monozukuri Subsidy)

**Application window**: Early April to mid-June 2026 (typical)

This is a dedicated subsidy for manufacturing and high-tech service industries. The ceiling ranges from 7.5 million yen to 10 million yen, with a subsidy rate of 1/2 to 2/3. If an enterprise is involved in product innovation, process improvement, or the application of new technologies, this subsidy can cover the costs of purchasing equipment, technology R&D, talent development, and more.

The review cycle is fairly long (4–5 months) and requires detailed professional documents such as technical proposals and cost estimate tables. We recommend beginning to prepare your technical proposal and expert consultations **6 months** in advance (early January).

## The 2026 Subsidy Application Calendar, Organized by Month

To help Chinese business owners plan more intuitively, the following lays out the major application milestones by month:

**January**: Small Business Sustainability Subsidy Round 1 (expected to open in mid-January); a concentrated release of various local government subsidies

**February–March**: Digital Transformation Subsidy; the preliminary preparation period for the Business Innovation Grant; the SME Agency's spring subsidy roundup release

**March–April**: Business Innovation Grant; Business Restructuring Subsidy Round 1; the R&D Subsidy application launch

**April–May**: Small Business Sustainability Subsidy Round 2; Round 1 deadlines for various subsidies

**May–June**: Business Innovation Grant deadline; labor-related subsidies (talent development, wage increases, etc.) typically open during this period

**July–August**: Business Restructuring Subsidy Round 2; financing-linked subsidies (paired with Japan Finance Corporation loans)

**September–October**: Round 2 or autumn supplementary applications for various subsidies; the release of pilot programs for the following fiscal year's subsidies

**November–December**: Year-end special subsidies; the disclosure of subsidy outlines for the following fiscal year

## Practical Application Strategies for Chinese Business Owners

### The Golden Rule of Time Planning

No matter which subsidy you apply for, you should follow these time-planning principles:

**Preparation phase: Start 2–3 months before the application window opens.** Although the formal application system is not yet open at this point, you can contact support institutions for consultation, assemble your application documents, formulate your business plan, and so on. Take the Business Innovation Grant as an example: start consultations in early February, complete a first draft by the end of February, and secure certification by mid-March—so that by the formal application in mid-April, you are fully prepared.

**Application phase: Don't wait until the last week.** Statistics show that the non-conformity rate (missing materials, formatting errors, etc.) of applications submitted within the final 7 days runs as high as 30%, while for applications submitted two weeks early it is only 5%. Server congestion is also a real risk—on the Round 2 deadline of the small business subsidy in 2024 (July 31), the system experienced five large-scale timeouts within the final 6 hours.

**Multi-round application strategy**: Most subsidies have 2–3 application opportunities per year. If your Round 1 application is not approved, you can revise your submission for Round 2 based on the feedback. But be sure to obtain the feedback within **1–2 weeks after the Round 1 deadline** so that you have enough time to make revisions.

### Making Use of Support Institutions

Japan's Chambers of Commerce and Industry, SME support centers, and certified support institutions all offer free consultation services. Chinese enterprises should establish contact with support institutions at the very start of the preparation phase and have them help review application documents. These institutions are extremely familiar with the key points of each subsidy application and can typically help enterprises raise their approval rate by 10–15 percentage points.

The key is to find a consultant who is familiar with your region and industry. Chambers of Commerce and Industry in many large cities (Tokyo, Osaka, Nagoya) have Chinese-speaking staff or can arrange translation.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: If I miss the deadline, is there any way to recover?

**A:** In principle, the application deadlines for Japanese subsidies are hard cutoffs, and anything past the deadline will not be accepted. In practice, however, there are several remedies: First, most subsidy programs have 2–3 application opportunities per year, so you can reapply in the next round. Second, some subsidies conduct a "reserve-fund supplementary" application round at year-end—that is, when the government still has budget left over at the end of the year, it suddenly opens up an additional application opportunity, but such opportunities are uncertain and usually have only a 1–2 week application period. Third, you can keep an eye on subsidies at the local government level (prefectures and municipalities); these subsidies are often scheduled to avoid overlapping with the central government's, and competition is relatively lower. Fourth, some industry associations or Chambers of Commerce and Industry also roll out industry-specific subsidies or financing incentives that can serve as alternatives. We recommend developing the habit of regularly checking information sites such as the "Subsidy Portal Site" every month.

### Q2: Can the same enterprise apply for multiple subsidies at the same time?

**A:** The answer to this is nuanced and depends on the specific rules of each subsidy. Generally speaking, the same business activity cannot receive subsidies from multiple programs at once—that is, the same cost item cannot be subsidized by two subsidies simultaneously. However, the same enterprise can absolutely apply for different subsidies for different projects. For example, you can apply for the Small Business Sustainability Subsidy for a digitalization upgrade while applying for the Business Innovation Grant for new product development. The key is to ensure that the business activities and cost items funded by the two subsidies do not overlap. Before applying, always confirm this clearly with a support institution to avoid being judged after the fact to have received an improper subsidy and being required to repay the full amount. Some subsidies (such as the Business Restructuring Subsidy) explicitly prohibit being used in combination with other central government subsidies—a point that requires extra attention.

### Q3: Has the 2026 subsidy schedule already been fully finalized?

**A:** As of now (early 2025), the complete 2026 schedule has not yet been made public. The Japanese government typically announces the next fiscal year's subsidy programs and schedules item by item between November and December of the preceding year. Historically, the application timing of the major subsidies (Small Business Sustainability Subsidy, Business Innovation Grant, Business Restructuring Subsidy, R&D Subsidy, etc.) is relatively stable, with fluctuations usually within 2–4 weeks. The timing listed in this article is therefore inferred from the historical patterns of the past 3–5 years and has strong reference value, but before actually applying you must confirm the latest announcements online. We recommend keeping an eye right now on official channels such as the "SME Agency" website, the "Subsidy Portal Site," and the Bank of Japan's "Financial Support Information," and updating your application plan the moment the 2026 schedule is finalized. Also, sign up for these sites' email alert services to ensure you don't miss any important notices.

### Q4: Do I need Japanese tax registration and a financial account to apply for a subsidy?

**A:** Yes, this is a basic requirement. To apply for any Japanese government subsidy, an enterprise must have the following qualifications: formal registration as a corporation or sole proprietor with a Japanese tax office, a Japanese bank account (for the subsidy disbursement), and at least one year of submitted financial statements (some subsidies have special policies allowing an exemption for startups that have been established for less than a year). For Chinese business owners, even a Japanese subsidiary can apply as long as it has completed Japanese corporate registration and obtained a tax registration number (corporate number). Sole proprietors (freelancers) can also apply for some subsidies, though the amounts are usually lower. When applying, you will need to submit a "Certificate of All Registered Matters" (corporate registration information) from Japan's National Tax Agency and a copy of your most recent financial statements. If the enterprise's account is less than a year old, some subsidies will require an initial plan document in place of historical financial data. We recommend ensuring all registration and account procedures are complete 3–6 months before you plan to apply for a subsidy.

### Q5: Can non-Japanese application materials be accepted?

**A:** In principle, no. All formal subsidy application materials must be submitted in Japanese, including the business plan, financial statements, application forms, and so on. In practice, however, there is some flexibility: First, if you are applying for an industry-specific subsidy (such as special subsidies aimed at foreign investment), English may be permitted. Second, support institutions can help you translate Chinese materials into Japanese, and this service is usually free. Third, some local governments provide translation assistance in order to attract foreign investment. Fourth, for portions that need to cite Chinese literature or data, you can annotate them within the Japanese application, but you must attach a Japanese translation. One practical piece of advice: use a professional Japanese translation service (many Chambers of Commerce and Industry can make recommendations); the cost is usually between 20,000 and 50,000 yen, and for a subsidy with an amount of 1 million yen or more, this investment is well worth it. Do not attempt an unprofessional translation yourself—substandard Japanese will greatly reduce reviewers' trust in your application.

## Subsidy Selection Advice for Different Enterprise Sizes

### Micro-enterprises (0–5 employees)

Main choices: Small Business Sustainability Subsidy; local government small-amount subsidies

The characteristic of these enterprises is that application materials are relatively simple and do not require complex financial projections. We recommend making full use of the Small Business Sustainability Subsidy's two application opportunities, aiming to file two different small-amount projects in January and May respectively. In addition, the municipality where you are located usually has special subsidies aimed at startups or micro-enterprises with even lower thresholds.

### Small enterprises (6–20 employees)

Main choices: Small Business Sustainability Subsidy; Business Innovation Grant; Digital Transformation Subsidy

Enterprises of this size begin to have a certain organizational foundation and can try applying for more complex subsidies. We recommend preparing two application tracks at once: a baseline track (the Small Business Sustainability Subsidy as a fallback) and an advanced track (the Business Innovation Grant or a digital subsidy as your reach). If the enterprise is involved in any technological innovation, you may also consider the R&D Subsidy.

### Medium enterprises (21–100 employees)

Main choices: Business Restructuring Subsidy; R&D Subsidy; industrial transformation subsidies

The subsidy amounts for these enterprises can be higher, so you should aim for projects that can secure subsidies of 10 million yen or more. The Business Restructuring Subsidy is particularly well-suited to this size, because what it requires is a complete strategic plan rather than simple day-to-day improvements. The review is highly demanding, but the returns after approval are substantial.

## A Final Practical Reminder

Compiling a "2026 Subsidy Application Timetable" and posting it in the office is a wise move. It should include: the application opening date and deadline for each subsidy, a checklist of required materials, and the contact information for support institutions. Start this work each November to ensure all information is collected and verified by December.

Because of potential language and cultural differences, Chinese business owners are advised to start preparing earlier than local Japanese enterprises. If local Japanese enterprises prepare one month before the application window opens, you should start 2–3 months ahead. This both ensures the quality of your application and keeps you ready for any sudden schedule adjustments.

Obtaining a subsidy is not just financial support—more importantly, it is the government's recognition of your enterprise's development direction, which is a positive help in subsequent financing, partnerships, recruitment, and more. For that reason, view applying for a subsidy as a process of strategic corporate planning and development certification; it is worth investing sufficient effort.
