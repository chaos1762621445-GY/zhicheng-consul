# Independent Subsidies in Tokyo's 23 Wards: What Exclusive Subsidies Does Your Ward Offer?

If you run a business in Tokyo or are planning to start one, you may have heard of the various subsidies offered by the Japanese government and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government. But did you know that, beyond the metropolitan-level subsidies, **each of Tokyo's 23 special wards (the "23 Wards" for short) operates its own independent business and startup support programs**? This means your ward may have subsidy opportunities you have never even heard of.

I have seen quite a few Chinese business owners miss out on hundreds of thousands of yen in funding support simply because they were unaware of the exclusive subsidy policies in their own ward. This article takes a deep dive into the Tokyo 23-Ward subsidy system to help you find the funding sources that truly fit your business.

## Overview of the Tokyo 23-Ward Subsidy System

### Why should you understand ward-level subsidies?

Although Tokyo's 23 special wards are all within Tokyo, **their industrial characteristics, business environments, and support policies differ greatly from ward to ward**. Take Chiyoda Ward and Arakawa Ward as an example: both are in Tokyo, but their subsidy priorities are completely different.

- **Chiyoda Ward**: focuses on IT, finance, and innovative startups
- **Arakawa Ward**: focuses on manufacturing, traditional crafts, and labor-intensive industries
- **Taito Ward**: emphasizes tourism-related industries and traditional commerce

The core advantages of ward-level subsidies include:

1. **Less competition**: compared with metropolitan subsidies, fewer companies apply, so the approval rate is higher
2. **Flexible conditions**: designed for the ward's industrial characteristics, and potentially more friendly to micro and small businesses
3. **Local support**: the ward office can provide more targeted pre-application guidance and post-award oversight
4. **Stacking**: some businesses can apply for both metropolitan and ward subsidies at the same time

### Basic categories of subsidies

Business subsidies in Tokyo's 23 wards can broadly be divided into four types:

| Subsidy type | Features | Amount range | Eligible parties |
|---------|------|---------|---------|
| Startup subsidy | No repayment required; supports startups | 500,000–3,000,000 JPY | Companies less than 3 years old |
| Management improvement subsidy | Improve management, upgrade industry | 1,000,000–5,000,000 JPY | Existing companies |
| Capital investment subsidy | Purchase machinery/equipment, IT systems | 2,000,000–10,000,000 JPY | All companies |
| R&D / HR development subsidy | Technical R&D, staff training | 500,000–10,000,000 JPY | Innovative companies |

## A Detailed Look at the Subsidy Policies of Major Wards

### Subsidy characteristics of the central wards (Chiyoda, Chuo, Minato)

These three wards are Tokyo's core business districts, home to a large number of financial institutions, IT companies, and foreign-affiliated firms.

**Overview of Chiyoda Ward subsidies:**

- **Startup Promotion Subsidy**: cap of 2,000,000 JPY, supporting businesses that start up in Chiyoda Ward. Requirements: the company's registered address is in Chiyoda Ward, it has been in business no more than 3 years, and it employs at least 1 person
- **Industrial Competitiveness Enhancement Subsidy**: cap of 3,000,000 JPY, designed specifically for cutting-edge industries such as tech innovation, AI, and blockchain
- **Application cycle**: usually one round each in April and October (twice a year)

**Overview of Chuo Ward subsidies:**

- **Regional Industrial Competitiveness Enhancement Subsidy**: cap of 2,500,000 JPY, encouraging companies that align with SDGs goals
- **Store Rent Subsidy**: for retail and food & beverage businesses hit by hardships such as the pandemic, subsidizes 30–50% of rent, up to 500,000 JPY/year
- **Characteristics**: Chuo Ward places special emphasis on tourism industries and the sale of traditional crafts

**Overview of Minato Ward subsidies:**

- **Startup Company Support Subsidy**: cap of 5,000,000 JPY (the highest in Japan), aimed especially at strategic industries such as AI, IoT, and health/medical care
- **Female Entrepreneur Support**: female founders can receive an additional 15% subsidy bonus
- **Internationalization support**: dedicated English-language application materials and an international-company matching program

### Subsidy characteristics of the subcenter wards (Shinjuku, Shibuya, Ikebukuro)

These three wards are centers of commerce, culture, and entertainment, and their subsidy policies lean more toward creative and tourism industries.

**Overview of Shinjuku Ward subsidies:**

- **Shinjuku Ward Business Trial Subsidy**: cap of 1,500,000 JPY; its notable feature is that it **can be used for developing online sales channels** (very helpful for cross-border e-commerce Chinese businesses)
- **Intra-Ward Industrial Collaboration Promotion Subsidy**: encourages cooperation with companies within the ward, subsidy cap of 2,000,000 JPY
- **Store Renewal Subsidy**: store renovation subsidy, up to 1,000,000 JPY

**Overview of Shibuya Ward subsidies:**

- **Shibuya Ward Challenge Company Subsidy**: 2,500,000 JPY, focused on the market-validation stage of early-stage companies
- **Diversity & Inclusion Support**: bonus policies for diverse companies (e.g., LGBTQ-certified, high proportion of foreign nationals)
- **Characteristics**: Shibuya provides the strongest support for the creative, design, and fashion industries

**Overview of Ikebukuro Ward subsidies:**

- **Ikebukuro Station-Area Regional Industry Revitalization Subsidy**: 2,000,000 JPY, aimed especially at projects related to commerce around Ikebukuro Station
- **Startup Challenge Subsidy**: a very low entry point, starting from 500,000 JPY, suitable for micro businesses

### Subsidy characteristics of the eastern wards (Taito, Sumida, Koto)

These three wards are a mix of traditional and emerging industries, with subsidy priorities in manufacturing, handicrafts, and tourism.

**Overview of Taito Ward subsidies:**

- **Monozukuri, Commerce, and Service Innovation Subsidy** (Monozukuri Subsidy for short): you can apply for the metropolitan subsidy, but Taito Ward additionally provides a **bonus subsidy capped at 1,000,000 JPY**
- **Tourism Industry Revitalization Subsidy**: 2,000,000 JPY, targeted especially at guesthouses (minpaku) and experiential tourism projects
- **Traditional Crafts Industry Support**: exclusive to handicraft businesses, with a subsidy rate of up to 50–60%, capped at 1,500,000 JPY

**Overview of Sumida Ward subsidies:**

- **Monozukuri Company Capital Investment Subsidy**: up to 4,000,000 JPY, the highest equipment subsidy among Tokyo's 23 wards
- **SME Manufacturing Management Improvement Subsidy**: 1,500,000 JPY, encouraging digital transformation in manufacturing
- **Staff training subsidy**: when staff attend skills training, a subsidy rate of 30–50% on training costs

**Overview of Koto Ward subsidies:**

- **Business Creation & Industrial Promotion Subsidy**: 2,500,000 JPY, focused on innovative business models
- **Logistics & Distribution Industry Support**: aimed specifically at logistics companies, subsidy cap of 3,000,000 JPY
- **Waterfront-area industry promotion**: an additional bonus for companies operating in coastal areas

### Subsidy characteristics of the western residential wards (Setagaya, Suginami, Nakano)

These three wards are characterized by dense housing and consumption-driven economies, and their subsidies lean toward lifestyle services and the education industry.

**Overview of Setagaya Ward subsidies:**

- **Setagaya Ward Shopping-District Revitalization Subsidy**: 1,000,000–2,000,000 JPY, encouraging shopping districts to take part in local revitalization
- **Support System for Female and Other Entrepreneurs**: dedicated channels for women, seniors, and immigrant founders, with faster approval
- **Regional Brand Communication Subsidy**: helps local brands go international, capped at 2,000,000 JPY

**Overview of Suginami Ward subsidies:**

- **Suginami Ward Industrial Promotion Grant**: 1,500,000 JPY, broad in coverage with relatively lenient application conditions
- **Telework Adoption Subsidy**: companies adopting remote-work systems can receive a subsidy of 500,000–1,000,000 JPY
- **Characteristics**: Suginami Ward is especially friendly to micro and small businesses, with no high entry-amount requirement

**Overview of Nakano Ward subsidies:**

- **New Business Development Subsidy**: 2,000,000 JPY, encouraging existing companies to develop new lines of business
- **Sustainability Management Promotion Subsidy**: subsidy for environmentally friendly and sustainable management, up to 1,500,000 JPY

## Application Process and Preparing Materials

### General application steps (5 stages)

**Step 1: Confirm eligibility**

- The company or individual has a business office registered (営業所登録) in that ward
- No deficiencies in corporate tax and other tax filings
- Companies in debt or bankruptcy cannot apply
- Some subsidies require that the business be no more than 3 years old, that employee numbers fall within a certain range, and so on

**Step 2: Gather application materials**

Core materials include:
- Certified copy of the corporate registry (company registration certificate)
- Final tax returns for the previous 2 periods
- Business plan (usually 3–5 pages, covering background, goals, use of funds, etc.)
- Quotation (required for equipment subsidies)
- Supplementary materials required independently by the ward office (such as employment contracts, lease agreements, etc.)

**Step 3: Prior consultation (very important)**

**Many applicants skip this step, but it is actually the most critical factor in the approval rate.** Every ward office has an industrial promotion department or a business support department that provides free 30–60 minute prior consultations:

- Booking method: usually by phone or online reservation on the ward's official website
- Consultation content: verify eligibility, refine the business plan, understand the review criteria
- Timing: best done 2–4 weeks before the formal application

**Step 4: Formal application**

- Submit the application form and document package (usually there is a dedicated form)
- Most wards accept both paper and electronic applications
- Note: **strictly observe the application deadline** (usually a fixed cutoff date; late submissions are not accepted)

**Step 5: Review and results**

- Review period: usually 4–8 weeks
- Some subsidies may require an oral presentation/defense or a written review
- Unsuccessful applications may come with feedback; you can request an explanation of the reasons

### Tips for preparing common documents

**Key elements of the business plan** (40% of the application's success rate depends on this document):

1. **Background and opportunity**: Why start/invest now? What pain point exists in the market? (no more than 200 characters)
2. **Business model**: How do you make money? How do you differ from competitors? (specific, quantified)
3. **Financial planning**: What exactly will the subsidy be spent on? What are the expected revenue and profit? (needs concrete figures and assumptions)
4. **Implementation timeline**: phased, specific goals and completion times
5. **Risks to success**: face challenges honestly and explain your countermeasures

**Special advice for Chinese applicants**:
- If your Japanese is limited, you can hire a professional subsidy consulting firm to help with translation and document preparation (fees are roughly 50,000–150,000 JPY)
- Many ward offices now offer Chinese-language consultation services or have Chinese-speaking staff; you can request an interpreter during the prior consultation
- We do not recommend translating Japanese application materials entirely on your own, as quality issues may affect the review outcome

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: If my company has already received a metropolitan subsidy, can I still apply for a ward subsidy?

**A:** Yes, but with restrictions. Most ward subsidies can be stacked with metropolitan subsidies, **but the same specific item (for example, the same piece of equipment) cannot be double-funded**. For instance, you could use a metropolitan subsidy to cover 60% of a machine's purchase cost and then use a ward subsidy to cover 30% of that same machine, but you cannot claim the same 100% of the cost from both subsidies. The key is to **clearly state the composition of your funding sources** in the business plan—which portion comes from the metropolitan subsidy and which from the ward subsidy. In fact, many savvy business owners do this: use the metropolitan "Monozukuri Subsidy" to cover 80% of equipment costs, then apply for the ward's "store improvement subsidy" to cover 50% of renovation costs, with the two subsidies complementing each other. We recommend consulting the ward office's Industrial Promotion Division directly before applying; they will tell you exactly whether stacking is possible. I have seen companies obtain a metropolitan subsidy, a ward subsidy, and bank financing all at once, achieving a large project with zero of their own capital invested.

### Q2: I am a sole proprietor (freelancer) with no corporation. Can I apply for a ward subsidy?

**A:** **It depends on the specific subsidy, but most ward subsidies are open to sole proprietors.** Note that sole proprietors usually need to meet the following conditions to apply: 1) have a fixed business office in that ward (which can be a virtual office or a small shop); 2) have at least one year of tax-filing records (either blue or white returns are acceptable); 3) have no tax deficiencies. For example, Minato Ward's startup subsidy explicitly states that "sole proprietors are also eligible," with a cap around 70% of the corporate amount. But there are exceptions—some Shinjuku Ward subsidies aimed at "companies" explicitly exclude sole proprietors. We recommend contacting your ward office directly and bringing a copy of your tax return for a prior consultation. I suggest that when a sole proprietor sets up a studio or shop, they consider registering as a one-person corporation (1人会社), which broadens the scope and amount of subsidies they can apply for, while also enjoying certain corporate-tax benefits.

### Q3: How long does subsidy approval take? When do I actually receive the money?

**A:** **Subsidy disbursement is usually "pay first, get reimbursed later," which is the point that most disappoints applicants.** The typical flow is: submit application → review (4–8 weeks) → adoption notice (approval) → carry out the project (self-funded) → submit completion report and invoices (1–2 months) → ward office review and inspection (4–8 weeks) → finally, disbursement. **From application to receiving the money, the entire cycle is usually 5–8 months.** For example, if you apply in April, get approved in June, carry out the project from July to October, and submit your report in November, you won't receive the money until January of the following year. This means you need your own funds to support the implementation phase of the project. Some banks' "subsidy financing" can solve this problem—the bank will lend money in advance based on the subsidy adoption notice, effectively covering the cost with the bank's money and repaying the bank once the ward office disburses. Unlike a loan, a subsidy **never has to be repaid**, so banks are willing to provide financing at relatively low interest rates. I recommend planning your project's funding sources four months before applying for a subsidy, rather than scrambling to raise money only after approval.

### Q4: What restrictions are there on how a subsidy can be used? Can it be used for staff salaries or office rent?

**A:** **The restrictions on subsidy use are strict, and this is a key focus of government oversight.** Generally, allowable uses include: 1) purchasing machinery, equipment, computers, and other assets; 2) store renovation and office remodeling; 3) new-product R&D and testing costs; 4) marketing and website development; 5) staff skills-training fees. **Non-allowable uses include**: salaries of existing employees (except possibly initial training subsidies for newly hired staff), everyday office rent, bank loan interest, and past costs already incurred. It is especially important to note that **all expenses must have invoices and contracts and cannot be used for cash transactions or gray-area dealings**. Before disbursement, the ward office carefully reviews all invoices to ensure they comply with the subsidy's usage rules. If improper use is found, at best you will be required to refund and pay a penalty; at worst you will be placed on a list of "companies that improperly used subsidies" and be barred from applying for any government subsidy for 3–5 years. I have seen a Chinese business divert a subsidy to other uses and ultimately be required to refund the full amount and pay a fine—an expensive price. I recommend submitting a full list of expenses to the ward office for pre-approval before spending the subsidy, which completely avoids the risk.

### Q5: My Japanese isn't good enough. Can I submit application materials in Chinese or English?

**A:** **Official application materials must be in Japanese, but many wards now provide Chinese-language consultation and assistance.** The specifics vary by ward: Minato, Shinjuku, Shibuya, and other demographically diverse wards all have departments staffed with Chinese-speaking personnel who can provide prior consultations in Chinese. But formal application materials (business plan, contracts, etc.) must be submitted in Japanese. There are several viable options: 1) hire a professional subsidy-application agency, which has Japanese-language specialists, at a cost of about 50,000–150,000 JPY; 2) find a Japanese-speaking consultant or friend to help prepare the application materials; 3) use the Japanese templates provided by the ward office, combined with translation software and a check by a professional. **We do not recommend relying entirely on translation software**, because subsidy applications demand a high level of logical coherence and legal accuracy in Japanese expression, and machine translation is prone to ambiguity. If your ward has a Chinese-language consultant, be sure to make full use of that resource. Many Chinese business owners give up on applying for subsidies due to language issues, when in fact, with proper preparation in advance, language problems can be completely overcome. Some generously funded subsidies attract few applicants, so even after Japanese companies have filled their quotas, slots remain—and many Chinese business owners have no idea.

## Avoiding Common Application Pitfalls

### The time-management pitfall

**The most common mistake is missing the application deadline.** Many subsidies operate on a periodic application system (such as one round each in April and October), and once the deadline passes you have to wait half a year. Recommendations:
- Subscribe now to your ward's subsidy-information emails (you can usually register on the ward office's website)
- Mark all key deadlines on your calendar and start preparing 4 weeks in advance
- Don't wait until the day before the deadline to start preparing

### The cash-flow pitfall

**Subsidies are "pay first, get reimbursed later," and without sufficient own funds to cover costs upfront, the project will stall.** Recommendations:
- Before applying, assess whether you have adequate own funds or financing channels
- Proactively ask your bank whether it offers subsidy-financing products
- Draw up a detailed funding plan, including an expenditure timeline

### The oversight pitfall

**After receiving a subsidy, you must still undergo inspection and reporting by the ward office.** Recommendations:
- Keep complete invoices and contracts for all subsidy expenditures (retain for 5–7 years)
- Submit all required reports on time (completion report, accounting report, effect report)
- Maintain good communication with the inspector designated by the ward office, and proactively ask if you have questions
