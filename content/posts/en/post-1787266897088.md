# Top 5 Government Subsidies for Chinese Business Owners in Japan in 2026: Horizontal Comparison and Selection Guide

## Introduction: Five Subsidy Options—Why These Are Best Suited for Chinese Enterprises

In 2026, Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI), Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, and SME Agency have launched new rounds of subsidy recruitment. For Chinese business owners operating in Japan, selecting the right subsidy program is critical—application document preparation typically requires 30-60 days, and missing the recruitment window means waiting until the next fiscal year. The five flagship subsidy programs covered in this guide are: **Business Restructuring Subsidy** (focused on business model innovation), **Monozukuri Subsidy** (for manufacturing and technology-driven enterprises), **Sustainability Subsidy for Small Enterprises** (supporting stable operations of micro-businesses), **IT Adoption Subsidy** (enabling digital transformation), and **Employment Adjustment Grant** (providing personnel cost support). Together, they cover scenarios ranging from startups to mature enterprises, and from service industries to manufacturing. This guide will help you quickly identify the option best suited to your enterprise's current situation by examining hard metrics such as subsidy caps, subsidy rates, eligible applicants, and application timelines, as well as real-world scenario fit for each program.

## Business Restructuring Subsidy: Ideal for Chinese Enterprises Pursuing Radical Transformation

### Core Parameters Overview

**Subsidy Cap**: Structured by investment scale; maximum of **250 million JPY** (Large-Scale Growth Category); **Standard Category (Tsūjō枠)** capped at **15 million JPY**; **Global V-shaped Recovery Category** (targeting international market investments) capped at **30 million JPY**.

**Subsidy Rate**: SMEs typically receive **2/3** (66.7%); large enterprises receive **1/2** (50%).

**Eligible Enterprises**: SMEs with 5+ employees (manufacturing excluded—20+ for that sector); both corporations and sole proprietors may apply.

**Recruitment Cycle**: 2026 is expected to see 2-3 recruitment windows throughout the year; each window typically runs for approximately **30-40 days**; review period approximately 3-4 months.

**Expected Payment Period**: Approximately 6-12 months after notification of adoption for completion of expenses, followed by settlement procedures.

### Applicable Scenarios

Your Chinese enterprise should prioritize this program if facing these challenges:

- **Obsolete Business Models**: Traditional brick-and-mortar retail/food service seeking to shift to online O2O or delivery platforms; or import-export trading companies aiming to build proprietary e-commerce brands
- **Market Environment Changes**: Impacted by pandemic or consumption shifts in recent years, requiring fundamental adjustments to product lines or target customer segments
- **Cross-Sector Expansion**: Existing business stable, but seeking to pioneer new industries in the Japanese market (e.g., manufacturing companies entering brand retail)
- **International Investment**: Seeking to make reverse investments from Japan into new projects or supply chain optimization in China or Southeast Asia

**Typical Case Study**: A Chinese dumpling chain with 15 existing locations experienced declining foot traffic post-pandemic. Applied for this subsidy using **12 million JPY** to invest in a **central kitchen + cold-chain logistics + B2B group dining supply chain**, expanding the retail model into fresh-produce distribution. Received **8 million JPY** subsidy (2/3 ratio), self-funded **4 million JPY**, with 12-month project timeline.

### Advantages

1. **Highest Subsidy Amount**: Compared to other programs, easily exceeds 10-million-JPY thresholds for large-scale transformation projects
2. **High Recognition**: A flagship METI initiative; offers positive weight for subsequent financing and government procurement opportunities
3. **Broad Applicability**: Accepts innovation in virtually all industries and business models
4. **Parallel Application**: Can simultaneously apply for other subsidies (e.g., IT Adoption Subsidy for digitalization) without conflict

### Disadvantages and Application Barriers

1. **Intense Competition**: Each recruitment window typically sees thousands of applications; approval rates around **40-50%** (subject to reviewing authority's decision), above average subsidy levels but still highly selective, requiring careful business plan design
2. **Stringent Business Plan Requirements**: Must submit a **detailed business plan** (typically 20-30 pages) covering market analysis, financial projections (3+ years), investment rationale, etc.; demands high Japanese-language competency and financial reporting standards from Chinese enterprises
3. **Heavy Reporting Burden**: After adoption, must submit interim reports every six months and undergo post-project evaluation; long overall timeline with multiple tracking points
4. **Financing Timing Gap**: Subsidy payments typically lag 6-12 months; enterprises must pre-fund investments, creating significant cash-flow pressure
5. **Chinese Enterprise-Specific Risks**: Reviewers may scrutinize Chinese business models, China-Japan market data, exchange-rate risks more carefully; requires more robust justification

## Monozukuri Subsidy: Tailored for Manufacturing Enterprises and Process Innovation

### Core Parameters Overview

**Subsidy Cap**: **10 million JPY** (Standard Category); **Global Expansion Type** up to **30 million JPY**.

**Subsidy Rate**: **1/2** (50%)—more conservative than Business Restructuring Subsidy, but also indicating lower review difficulty.

**Eligible Enterprises**: Manufacturing SMEs with 20 or fewer employees are prioritized (enterprises with 20-50 employees may apply but face stricter review); sole proprietors typically ineligible.

**Recruitment Cycle**: Usually **3-4 recruitment windows** annually; each window approximately **30 days**; review period approximately 2-3 months.

**Eligible Expenses**: Machinery and equipment, tools and instruments, outsourced services (process design, prototype development, etc.), personnel costs (direct manufacturing workers), and other expenses (up to 50% of total budget).

### Applicable Scenarios

This program is purpose-built for manufacturing-sector Chinese enterprises:

- **Production Process Upgrades**: Traditional CNC machine shops transitioning to automated production lines; electronics assembly firms introducing SMT equipment
- **Product Innovation R&D**: Food manufacturers (e.g., tofu skin, bean product producers) developing new health-food product lines requiring testing and equipment investment
- **Quality Certification**: Pursuing ISO, medical device approvals, etc., requiring certification and testing equipment
- **Export Product Enhancement**: Existing Japanese customer base; seeking higher-tier orders through technical upgrades

**Typical Case Study**: A Chinese precision metal-parts processing firm with 18 employees, primarily serving Japanese automotive suppliers. Faced production bottlenecks with aging equipment. Decided to acquire **three CNC machining centers + one online inspection system**, totaling 20 million JPY investment. Applied for Monozukuri Subsidy and received **8 million JPY** (1/2 ratio); self-funded **12 million JPY**. Expected 40% capacity increase; revenue gains from next year cover costs within 12 months.

### Advantages

1. **Stable Subsidy Rate**: 50% rate, while not as generous as Business Restructuring, provides clear industry benchmarks and predictable enterprise expectations
2. **Clear Review Logic**: Eligible expenses (equipment purchases, technical development) and review standards are transparent; less subjective than transformation-focused subsidies
3. **Manufacturing Bias**: Japan's METI continues strong policy support for manufacturing upgrades; maintains substantial budgets and stable recruitment cycles
4. **International Recognition**: Aligns with Japan's 「ものづくり大国」(manufacturing superpower) strategy; approved enterprises gain credibility in international business and financing

### Disadvantages and Application Barriers

1. **Strict Industry Restrictions**: Limited to manufacturing, construction, transportation, and other production-related sectors; pure service, trading, and retail ineligible
2. **Employee Count Requirements**: 20 or fewer employees qualify at priority level; firms exceeding 50 face significant scoring reductions; limits applicability for larger Chinese enterprises
3. **Equipment Procurement Timeline**: Typically 3-6 months from application to equipment delivery, while recruitment windows allow only 30 days; requires advance product research and planning
4. **High Technical Documentation Requirements**: Applications must detail precisely how acquired equipment improves capacity, yield rates, costs, etc.; demands strong technical documentation skills in English/Japanese from Chinese enterprises
5. **Maintenance Restrictions**: Acquired equipment typically cannot be disposed of or repurposed for **3 years** post-purchase; represents cash-flow risk for capital-constrained enterprises

## Sustainability Subsidy for Small Enterprises: Most User-Friendly Micro-Business Grant

### Core Parameters Overview

**Subsidy Cap**: **500,000 JPY** (Standard Category); **Special Category** (enterprises affected by pandemic or inflation) up to **2 million JPY**.

**Subsidy Rate**: **2/3** (66.7%)—relatively high among all subsidies.

**Eligible Enterprises**: Sole proprietors and corporations with **5 or fewer employees**; all industries eligible (food service, retail, services, etc.).

**Recruitment Cycle**: Usually **2-3 recruitment windows** annually; each window approximately **30-45 days**; review period approximately 1.5-2 months—fastest among all subsidies.

**Eligible Expenses**: Signage and logo creation, website design, flyers and catalogs, SNS advertising, store renovation, fixtures and equipment, accounting software adoption, etc.—focused on small-scale 「経営改善」(business improvement) investments.

### Applicable Scenarios

This is the 「easiest entry point」subsidy for Chinese micro-enterprises:

- **Small-Shop Upgrades**: Chinese food shops, beauty salons, repair shops seeking brand image enhancement (new logos, websites, renovations)
- **Digital Transformation Beginnings**: Traditional small businesses (laundromats, traditional medicine clinics) taking first steps with online reservation systems and POS terminals
- **Post-Pandemic Recovery**: Micro-enterprises impacted by prolonged pandemic or recent inflation needing low-cost market activation
- **Early-Stage Startup Funding**: New enterprises (less than 1 year old) seeking first government funding tranche

**Typical Case Study**: A Chinese female entrepreneur operating a traditional medicine massage clinic in Nagoya with 4 employees including herself, annual revenue ~12 million JPY. Struggled with high customer acquisition costs. Applied for Sustainability Subsidy for Small Enterprises with **350,000 JPY budget: website development (150,000), Google Maps SEO optimization (100,000), Xiaohongshu/TikTok video production (100,000)**. Approved for **230,000 JPY subsidy**; self-funded **120,000 JPY**. Within three months, online customers increased from 15% to 42% of clientele.

### Advantages

1. **Lowest Application Barriers**: 5 or fewer employees—easily accessible; application documents concise (typically 5-10 pages); minimal Japanese documentation required
2. **Fastest Review Speed**: Approval typically 1.5-2 months—40% faster than other programs; fund disbursement also relatively quick
3. **Higher Subsidy Rate**: 66.7% rate means lower self-funding burden; minimal cash-flow pressure
4. **Multiple Reapplication Opportunities**: Same enterprise may apply **2-3 times annually** (across different recruitment windows)—more flexible than other subsidies' one-per-year limitation
5. **Low Implementation Difficulty**: Eligible expenses are standard business expenditures (websites, renovations, advertising); no complex technical validation or financial projections

### Disadvantages and Application Barriers

1. **Minimal Funding Cap**: Standard 500,000 JPY insufficient for any substantial investment; even 2-million-JPY special category limited for major transformation
2. **Strict Employee Ceiling**: Cannot exceed 5 employees; hiring 6th employee triggers automatic eligibility loss
3. **Strict Eligible Expense Definitions**: Must be directly related to 「経営改善」(business improvement); production costs, employee wages, rent, and other fixed expenses ineligible
4. **Intense Special-Category Competition**: 2-million-JPY special category approval rates only approximately **30-35%** (subject to reviewing authority's decision)—far below standard category's 60%+; requires clear hardship evidence
5. **Fund Disbursement Lag**: Despite fast review, actual subsidy deposit still requires 2-3 months; pressure remains on cash-flow-constrained small shops

## IT Adoption Subsidy: Digital Transformation Tool for the Modern Era

### Core Parameters Overview

**Subsidy Cap**: **4.5 million JPY** (Standard Category); **Cybersecurity Promotion Category** up to **6 million JPY**; **Digital Infrastructure Introduction Category** (for small enterprises) up to **500,000 JPY**.

**Subsidy Rate**: **1/2** (50%)—standard government subsidy ratio.

**Eligible Enterprises**: SMEs and small-enterprise operators with **5+ employees**; all industries eligible.

**Recruitment Cycle**: Usually **4-5 recruitment windows** annually (most flexible subsidy)—each window approximately **30-40 days**; review period approximately 2-3 months.

**Eligible Expenses**: Accounting software, CRM customer management systems, POS systems, attendance management, inventory management, e-commerce platform development, cybersecurity software, etc.—condition: **must be introduced through certified IT specialists (認定事業者); DIY adoption ineligible**.

### Applicable Scenarios

In an era where digitalization drives competitive advantage, this is the most direct subsidy track:

- **E-Commerce Upgrades**: Traditional wholesalers, manufacturers building proprietary online sales channels or e-commerce platforms
- **Internal Management Digitalization**: Addressing common Chinese-enterprise challenges—「workshop-style management」(Excel spreadsheets, manual reconciliation)—upgrading to standardized financial systems
- **Remote Work Systems**: Particularly for cross-border Chinese enterprises in Japan, establishing collaboration platforms connecting China, Japan, Southeast Asia
- **Integrated Customer Data**: Retail and service businesses establishing CRM systems to track customer behavior and boost repeat purchases
- **Cybersecurity Reinforcement**: As remote work and online transactions increase, Chinese enterprises recognizing data-security importance

**Typical Case Study**: A Chinese import-export trading company with 20 Japan-based and 30 China-based employees currently managing orders via email and WeChat, spending 3 days monthly on financial reconciliation. Decided to implement **enterprise ERP system (3 million JPY) + international CRM (1 million JPY)**, totaling 4-million-JPY investment. Through certified IT specialist A's solution design, received **2-million-JPY subsidy** (1/2 ratio); self-funded **2 million JPY**. Six-month implementation reduced order processing cycle by 40% and reconciliation time by 80%.

### Advantages

1. **Highest Recruitment Frequency**: 4-5 annual windows—far exceeding other subsidies (typically 2-3)—maximum flexibility
2. **Mid-to-High Subsidy Amount**: 4.5 million JPY sufficient for mid-scale digitalization projects (ERP, e-commerce platforms, etc.)
3. **Strategic Alignment**: Highly aligned with Japan's 「Digital Society 5.0」national strategy; sustained policy support
4. **Professional Third-Party Oversight**: Mandatory certified IT specialist involvement (consultants, system integrators) ensures rational system selection and implementation quality
5. **Financing Credibility**: Banks and VCs score companies using IT Adoption Subsidy higher; digitalization signals positive financing indicators

### Disadvantages and Application Barriers

1. **Mandatory Third-Party Intermediation**: Cannot self-procure software and claim subsidy; must delegate to certified specialists, meaning: a) consulting design fees (typically 5-10% of project cost) **not covered by subsidy**; b) enterprise bears full risk if specialist selection is poor
2. **Limited System Selection**: Must choose from specialist's approved system pool; cannot adopt non-mainstream systems enterprise prefers; actually reduces flexibility
3. **Long Implementation Timeline**: System assessment, customization, data migration, staff training typically require 6-12 months—bottleneck for enterprises needing rapid deployment
4. **Chinese Enterprise Challenges**: Limited certified specialists for international systems (multi-currency, multi-timezone, China-Japan integration); fewer options, higher pricing
5. **Inherited Maintenance Costs**: Post-implementation system maintenance and upgrade fees typically borne by enterprise (not subsidy-covered); long-term cost pressure on smaller firms

## Employment Adjustment Grant: Conservative Personnel Cost Support

### Core Parameters Overview

**Grant Limit**: **Up to 15,000 JPY per worker per day** (maximum ceiling); **eligible support period maximum 100 days** (note: may span multiple years).

**Grant Objectives**: Particularly as 「Emergency Employment Adjustment Grant」during economic downturns, natural disasters, pandemic response, etc.—provides **80% coverage of paid leave wages** for employees facing temporary closures/furloughs due to **non-enterprise factors**.

**Eligible Enterprises**: SMEs with **5+ employees**; specific industries (tourism, transportation, food service, etc.) prioritized.

**Application Conditions**: Enterprises apply based on actual paid leave compensation to employees; application lag typically **2-3 months** (Employment Adjustment Grant is a reimbursement model).

**Cumulative Limit**: Continuous benefit eligibility approximately **3 years maximum** (within-period total limited to 100 days); counter resets annually.

### Applicable Scenarios

Designed for 「crisis employment maintenance」—applicable to:

- **Temporary Order Reductions**: Chinese manufacturing enterprises experiencing sudden 30-50% client order cuts; short-term difficulty reassigning production capacity; need temporary shift suspensions or rotating furloughs
- **Seasonal Business Fluctuation**: Export-trading companies facing exchange-rate volatility, import-policy shifts causing material-cost surges; need temporary import-procurement suspension
- **Supply-Chain Disruption**: Pandemic, earthquakes, force majeure halting upstream suppliers; downstream production impossible; need production shutdown
- **Skill-Upgrade Periods**: Employees requiring training (new equipment operation, new process certification); training-period productivity limited; subsidy applicable

**Typical Case Study**: A Chinese electronics component-processing firm with 100 employees experienced a major client suddenly reducing orders by 60% due to inventory excess; estimated 3-4 months needed to find replacement clients. Original planned staffing 100, now needing only 60. Implemented **rotating furloughs (2 days/week per person), estimated 15 days/month per person**. Employment Adjustment Grant payment: 40 people × 15 days × 15,000 JPY/day × 80% = 7.2 million JPY (estimated 3 months); enterprise self-funded 20% = 1.2 million JPY. Successfully navigated slow season while retaining core technical staff employment relationships.

### Advantages

1. **Minimal Cash-Flow Impact**: 80% coverage of wages already paid by enterprise—essentially 「reimbursement-model funding」—no enterprise pre-funding required
2. **Employee Protection**: By maintaining employment contracts, aligns with Japanese 「no-easy-dismissal」labor culture; enhances enterprise reputation and stability
3. **Relatively Simple Application**: Compared to subsidies requiring detailed business plans, primarily requires wage records, furlough logs, furlough notices, etc.
4. **Industry Recognition**: Especially in manufacturing and transportation—「consensus」employment-maintenance tool; minimal review-agency skepticism

### Disadvantages and Application Barriers

1. **Passive Nature**: Not proactive marketing or innovation investment; rather, crisis response—psychologically 「forced」rather than 「progressive」
2. **Strict 「External Factor」Determination**: Must prove furlough/closure stems from **non-enterprise economic factors** (client order reduction, supply-chain interruption, etc.); enterprise-caused decline, intentional downsizing ineligible; reviewers conduct rigorous verification
3. **Reimbursement Lag + Timing Gap**: Fund disbursement typically 2-3 months delayed; enterprise must first self-fund 80% of wages; cash-flow pressure exists
4. **Short Eligible Period**: Maximum 100 days (approximately 3-4 months); once exceeded, no further disbursement; long-term struggling enterprises cannot rely on this
5. **Chinese Enterprise Documentation Risks**: Requires accurate wage records, attendance logs, furlough notices, union consent forms and other formal documentation; many Chinese micro-factories lack standardized record-keeping; easily flagged as 「incomplete documentation」—causes review delays

## How to Choose? Match Your Situation

### Scenario 1: Annual Revenue ≤30 Million JPY, Micro Manufacturing (5-20 Employees)

**Primary Recommendation: Monozukuri Subsidy**

**Rationale**: Your enterprise scale is the 「ideal customer」for this subsidy—20-or-fewer-employee manufacturing firms achieve **60-70% approval rates** (subject to reviewing authority's decision), far exceeding average 50% across other enterprise types. 10-million-JPY cap suffices for production-line or multi-unit equipment upgrades. 50% subsidy rate means ~5-million-JPY self-funding—manageable for 30-million-JPY annual-revenue enterprises.

**Secondary Option**: To simultaneously upgrade company financial/inventory-management systems, apply for **IT Adoption Subsidy as complementary measure**—both can proceed in parallel without conflict.

**Pitfall Avoidance**: Skip Business Restructuring Subsidy unless pursuing clear 「cross-sector transformation」(e.g., OEM→proprietary brand); otherwise, 15-million-JPY ceiling offers no appeal while application complexity and review difficulty multiply unnecessarily.

### Scenario 2: 20-50 Employees, Seeking Growth—Chinese Service-Sector Enterprise (Food, Beauty, Education, etc.)

**Primary Recommendation: Business Restructuring Subsidy**

**Rationale**: Service sectors optimally pursue 「business model innovation」pathway. Typical investment directions:

- **Food Service**: Traditional locations → central kitchens + delivery + third-party platforms + B2B group catering
- **Beauty**: Brick-and-mortar → online appointment booking + membership management + product retail + franchising
- **Education**: Offline courses → live streaming + recorded libraries + SaaS intellectual-property licensing

These represent 「legitimate re-architecture」most reviewers recognize. 15-million-JPY cap appropriately maps to investment scale (branch expansion, IT systems, inventory), with typical self-funding 1/3 ratio (~5 million JPY)—sustainable for 30-50-million-JPY annual-revenue enterprises.

**Critical Success Factor**: Business plan documents must be **professionally Japanese-language written**—ideally by certified SME Management Consultant (中小企業診断士) or Certified Support Agency (認定支援機関) to achieve reviewer credibility. Chinese enterprises lacking Japanese business-writing capability should seriously budget for professional ghostwriting assistance (typically 50,000-150,000 JPY).

### Scenario 3: Under 5 Employees, Small Shop or Solo Business

**Primary Recommendation: Sustainability Subsidy for Small Enterprises**

**Rationale**: Designed precisely for you—zero application friction, 1.5-month review cycle, 66.7% subsidy rate. Even 500,000-JPY cap suffices for website redesign, local SEO, point-of-sale upgrades, shop renovation.

**Reapplication Advantage**: Unlike other subsidies, apply **2-3 times yearly** across recruitment windows (e.g., Feb, June, Oct launches)—enabling annual 100-150 million JPY aggregate subsidy if repeatedly approved, though individual caps remain 500,000-2 million JPY per window.

**Supplementary Option**: If also modernizing operations, consider **IT Adoption Subsidy's 「Digital Infrastructure Category」(500,000 JPY cap)**—dovetails perfectly with your existing recruitment-window schedule; many small firms stack both.

### Scenario 4: Manufacturing + Digital Transformation Simultaneously

**Parallel Recommendation: Monozukuri Subsidy (for equipment) + IT Adoption Subsidy (for systems)**

**Rationale**: Non-conflicting—first covers capital equipment (CNC, robots, testing apparatus; 10-million-JPY ceiling), second covers software/systems (ERP, quality-control platforms, e-commerce; 4.5-million-JPY ceiling). Both recruitment windows differ, permitting staggered application timelines.

**Cash-Flow Note**: Both subsidize 50% (or 66.7% in select cases); combined 15-million-JPY potential subsidy requires ~15-million-JPY self-funding—realistic for mid-size manufacturers (50-100 million JPY annual revenue).

**Timing Strategy**: Apply Monozukuri first (longer approval window, 2-3 months), then IT Adoption during next recruitment cycle—ensures equipment procurement precedes system integration, logically sequenced.

### Scenario 5: Crisis Navigation—Sudden Order Collapse or Supply Shock

**Primary Recommendation: Employment Adjustment Grant (immediate) + Business Restructuring Subsidy (medium-term)**

**Rationale**: Employment Adjustment Grant addresses **immediate personnel-cost relief** (80% wage reimbursement for furloughed workers); buys 3-4 month breathing room via 100-day limit. Simultaneously, launch Business Restructuring Subsidy application during relief period to develop **strategic pivot** (new markets, products, business models) addressing root cause, not just symptom.

**Timeline**: Employment Adjustment Grant processes ~2-3 months reimbursement lag; use that window for Business Restructuring plan preparation. Upon Business Restructuring approval (3-4 months after submission), commence transformation investments financed by subsidy while employment-adjustment period concludes.

## Application Tips for Chinese Entrepreneurs: Navigating Non-Native Language & Bureaucratic Barriers

### Issue 1: Business Plan Japanese Proficiency

**Problem**: Most Chinese entrepreneurs, while fluent in operational Mandarin/Japanese, struggle with formal business-plan Japanese required for subsidy applications.

**Solution**:
- Budget **50,000-150,000 JPY** for professional ghostwriting from 行政書士 (Gyoseishoshi/administrative scrivener) or 認定支援機関 (Certified Support Agency specializing in subsidy applications)
- Verify ghostwriter's **track record** with Chinese-enterprise subsidy wins before engagement
- Provide detailed English or Mandarin background; professional translator handles Japanese formalization

### Issue 2: Financial Documentation Standardization

**Problem**: Chinese enterprises often maintain informal, Excel-based bookkeeping; Japanese subsidy reviewers demand standardized tax returns, balance sheets, profit-loss statements.

**Solution**:
- Engage 税理士 (Zeirishi/tax accountant) or 会計士 (CPA) specializing in Chinese-enterprise clients to audit and standardize 3 years' financial records
- Cost: typically 20,000-50,000 JPY per year of records; but eliminates subsidy-disqualification risk
- Generate official 決算書 (financial statements) with tax-filing stamps for reviewer confidence

### Issue 3: GビズID Registration

**Critical**: Many subsidies now require 【GビズID】(Government Business ID) for online application portals. Chinese entrepreneurs often lack this.

**Action**:
- Register at **www.gbiz-id.go.jp** 4-6 weeks before intended application (processing takes 2-3 weeks)
- Requires 個人番号カード (My Number Card) or corporate 法人番号 (corporate number)
- If delayed, use 行政書士 proxy registration service (~5,000 JPY)

### Issue 4: Mandatory Third-Party Validation (認定支援機関)

For most subsidies, submission **must**originate via certified agencies:
- **Certified Support Agencies** (金融機関、商工会議所、コンサル等)
- Chinese-community resources:
  - 在日中国商工会 (China-Japan Chamber of Commerce)
  - Regional 商工会議所 with Chinese-speaking staff
  - Shisei Consulting Co., Ltd. (株式会社 志成コンサル) specializing in Chinese-entrepreneur subsidies

**Tip**: Interview 3-5 agencies; compare track records, fee structures, application timelines before commitment.

### Issue 5: Cultural Risk—「Guanxi」vs. Japanese Bureaucratic Transparency

**Pitfall**: Some Chinese entrepreneurs assume 「connections」(guanxi) influence subsidy approval. **FALSE**.

**Reality**: Japanese subsidy review is exceptionally transparent, rule-governed, objective-scoring. Personal connections, gifts, dinner invitations **zero impact**—and could trigger corruption investigations.

**Correct Approach**: Excellence lies in application quality—rigorous business plan, accurate financials, compelling narrative. No shortcuts.
