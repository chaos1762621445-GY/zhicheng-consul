# Which Subsidies Can Chinese-Run Education & Training Institutions in Japan Apply For? The Complete Guide

If you run an education or training institution in Japan, have you ever considered applying for government subsidies to cut operating costs and expand your school? Many Chinese owners of education and training businesses have no idea which subsidies they qualify for, and as a result miss out on substantial government funding. According to data from Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) and various local governments, the education and training sector leaves billions of yen in subsidy budgets underused every year.

This article walks you through, in detail, the various subsidies that Chinese-run education institutions in Japan can apply for—including specific eligibility requirements, amounts, procedures, and answers to frequently asked questions.

## Three Main Sources of Subsidies for Education Institutions

In Japan, subsidies for education and training institutions come primarily from three tiers: national-level subsidies, local government subsidies, and industry-specific support subsidies. Understanding these three sources is essential to applying systematically.

### National-Level Subsidies (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare)

As the principal authority for human resources administration, Japan's MHLW allocates funding each year for vocational training, skill development, and other education projects. The most important of these is the **Human Resource Development Grant** (人材開発支援コース補助金).

This grant supports the vocational competency development of small and medium-sized enterprises and their employees. If your training school provides adult vocational re-education or skills training, you may be eligible. The subsidy typically covers 50–80% of actual training costs, up to a maximum of 6 million JPY per project.

There is also the **Career-Up (Regularization) Grant** (キャリアアップ助成金). The advantage of this grant is that it is relatively simple to apply for, covering areas such as employee training and qualification acquisition. If your institution organizes training courses for staff, you can apply for up to 800,000 JPY.

### Local Government Subsidies

Each prefecture and municipality has its own subsidy policies. For example:

- **Tokyo Metropolis**: "Tokyo SME Vocational Training School Grant Program" (東京都中小企業職業訓練校助成事業), providing subsidies for facilities, instructors, and more to accredited training institutions
- **Osaka Prefecture**: "Monozukuri Human Resource Development Program" (ものづくり人材育成事業) subsidy, focused on supporting skills-based training
- **Kanagawa Prefecture**: "Subsidy for Vocational Ability Development" (職業能力開発に関する補助金), supporting training projects in emerging industries

What these local subsidies have in common is that they are **closely tied to local industrial development**. If your training program aligns with the region's priority industries (such as IT, welfare/care, tourism, or manufacturing), your chances of approval rise significantly. Amounts range from 500,000 to 5 million JPY, depending on project scale and regional policy.

### Industry- and Population-Specific Subsidies

Subsidies targeting specific fields and specific populations are also well worth your attention. For example:

**Nursing-Care Workforce Securement Subsidy** (介護人材確保補助金): If your institution provides nursing-care or medical-related training, you can apply. Such subsidies tend to be larger because Japan faces a severe shortage of care workers.

**Women's Advancement Promotion Subsidy** (女性活躍推進補助金): If your institution specializes in vocational training or entrepreneurship training for women, you can apply.

**Foreign Talent Development Subsidy** (外国人材育成補助): For institutions providing Japanese-language training or vocational skills training to foreigners, this is an important funding source.

## The Five Subsidy Types Chinese-Run Education Institutions Can Most Easily Obtain

Based on our experience serving hundreds of Chinese-owned businesses in Japan, the following five subsidy types are the best fit for Chinese-run education institutions, and have the highest success rates.

### 1. Human Resource Development Grant (up to 6 million JPY/year)

**Who can apply**: Companies or corporations operating any form of education or training institution

**What it covers**:
- Vocational training course development costs
- Instructor training costs
- Training equipment procurement costs
- Online teaching platform development costs

**Subsidy rate**: Typically 50–80% of actual expenditure

**Case example**:
A Chinese school principal running a Japanese-language cram school in Tokyo wanted to develop a specialized business-Japanese course. By applying for the Human Resource Development Grant, they secured 3.8 million JPY in funding to hire a professional course developer, purchase online teaching equipment, and conduct market research. This not only lowered the initial outlay but also earned government recognition, strengthening the institution's brand.

**Key conditions**:
- The company needs a certain scale (having 5 or more employees makes approval easier)
- The training project must relate to actual work
- A detailed course development plan must be submitted
- It is best to consult a professional advisor before applying to assess feasibility

### 2. Career-Up (Regularization) Grant (up to 800,000 JPY/year)

This is one of the easiest subsidies to apply for, with a relatively streamlined procedure.

**Scope**:
- Employee skills training (up to 150,000 JPY per employee)
- Qualification acquisition subsidies
- Continuing education for teaching staff
- Management training

**Application procedure**:
1. Plan the employee training project
2. Submit the application to the Labour Bureau with jurisdiction
3. Submit invoices and proof of results within 6 months of completing training
4. Receive the subsidy after passing review

**Real-world case**:
A Chinese-run education institution organizes staff to attend pedagogy workshops, counseling training, and the like every year. By applying for the Career-Up Grant, it receives 600,000–800,000 JPY in subsidies annually. This principal was clever: they tied employee training to improvements in the institution's teaching quality, creating a virtuous cycle.

### 3. Small-Scale Business Sustainability Subsidy (up to 2 million JPY)

Since the pandemic, the Japanese government has stepped up support for small-scale businesses. This subsidy targets micro-enterprises with 20 or fewer employees.

**Permitted uses**:
- Classroom renovation and remodeling
- IT system upgrades (such as online teaching platforms)
- Purchase of teaching materials and equipment
- Advertising and marketing
- Business process improvement

**Subsidy rate**: 2/3 of actual expenditure (up to 2 million JPY)

**Eligibility conditions**:
- 20 or fewer employees
- A physical business address
- Continuous tax filing for the 3 years prior to application
- The subsidized project has clear objectives and effectiveness evaluation

Many small Chinese-run cram schools (individual or two-to-three-person operations) meet these conditions. For example, a principal running a small Japanese-language class in Kawasaki, with only 3 employees, obtained 1.5 million JPY through this subsidy to renovate the classroom and purchase electronic whiteboards and an online teaching system.

### 4. Regional Revitalization Promotion Grant (amount varies by region)

If your education institution is located in a depopulating area or a regional city, you can apply for regional revitalization-related subsidies.

**Applicable regions**:
- Regional small and mid-sized cities
- Rural areas
- Areas designated as "potentially vanishing municipalities" (消滅可能性都市)

**What it covers**:
- Local talent development
- Support for returnees starting businesses
- Region-specific education projects

**Amount range**: 500,000–10 million JPY (varies by region and project)

**Example**:
A Chinese principal opened an institution in Yamagata Prefecture specializing in training agricultural technology personnel. Through Yamagata's regional revitalization subsidy, they obtained 5 million JPY to build a hands-on training base and introduce advanced agricultural technology teaching equipment. They also received promotional support from the prefectural government.

### 5. Employment Adjustment Subsidy and New Business Development Support Grant (up to 10 million JPY in specific periods)

Although this subsidy's application window is time-limited, it is often rolled out during periods of economic transition and is worth watching continuously.

**Who it targets**:
- Companies adjusting their business due to market changes
- Institutions launching new training programs or new campuses
- Institutions introducing new teaching models

**Recent case**:
During and after the pandemic, many education institutions shifted from in-person to online or hybrid formats. When applying for this type of subsidy, many institutions received 2–4 million JPY in support for building online teaching systems, retraining and redeploying staff, and more.

## The Actual Process of Applying for Education Institution Subsidies

Understanding subsidy categories is only the first step. Actual application requires following a strict process and submitting detailed documentation.

### Step 1: Clarify Your Own Conditions and Institutional Qualifications

Before applying for any subsidy, you need to confirm:

**Legal qualifications**:
- Are you a formally registered corporation or sole proprietor?
- Is your business address compliant (some subsidies do not accept leased commercial buildings, etc.)?
- Are your tax filings in order? Have you had any overdue tax payments in the past 3 years?

**Nature of the institution**:
- Is it a for-profit institution? (Non-profits have different subsidy channels)
- Number of employees (this directly affects eligibility)?
- Annual revenue scale (some subsidies have requirements on company size)?

These seemingly simple questions are often the root cause of application failures. We have seen many Chinese-run institutions rejected because of unclear tax records or non-compliant registration documents.

### Step 2: Choose the Right Subsidy Program

**Sub-step 2-1: Information gathering**
- Visit the Japanese MHLW website (mhlw.go.jp)
- Consult the prefectural Labour Bureau with jurisdiction
- Consult the industrial promotion division of your municipality
- Refer to the subsidy database of the Japan Finance Corporation

**Sub-step 2-2: Assess the fit**
Create an assessment table and check, one by one, whether your institution meets each subsidy's conditions. Do not apply blindly, because a failed application can affect your eligibility to apply the following year.

### Step 3: Prepare Detailed Application Documents

This is the most critical stage. **Japanese subsidy applications have extremely strict documentation requirements.**

**Documents you must prepare (general)**:
- Proof of institutional registration (certified copy of the register / 登記簿謄本)
- Tax returns and financial statements for the past 3 years
- Bank account proof
- Proof of the representative's identity

**Program-specific documents**:
- A detailed training course plan
- Details of the instructor team (teacher credentials, degree certificates, etc.)
- Student recruitment plan and expected outcomes
- Itemized budget (precise down to each expenditure)
- Institutional self-assessment report

**A specially important document: the Business Plan (事業計画書)**

This document determines whether your application succeeds. It needs to include:
- Why the subsidy is needed (analysis of the current situation)
- What goals will be achieved after the subsidy (specific and measurable)
- A detailed implementation plan and timeline
- Expected social and economic benefits
- Risk management and countermeasures

**Common reasons for failure**:
- The plan is too broad or not specific
- The budget list is not detailed enough ("Training fees: 300,000 JPY" is not specific enough—you must list the course names, class hours, instructor names, and unit prices)
- No clear outcome evaluation metrics

### Step 4: Submit the Application and Follow Up

**How to submit**:
Most national-level subsidies are submitted through an electronic system (e-Gov), but local subsidies may require paper submission. Confirm the deadline (there is usually a fixed window each year between March and June).

**Review cycle**:
It typically takes 2–4 months from application to decision. During this period, you may be asked to submit supplementary documents or attend an interview/defense. **Maintain timely communication with the review department.**

**Receiving the subsidy**:
After approval, you do not receive the full amount immediately. It is usually split into:
- Advance payment: about 30–50%, received before the project begins
- Completion payment: about 50–70%, received after the project is completed and passes inspection

This means your institution needs sufficient funds to front the initial costs.

## Common Misconceptions and Cautions for Chinese-Run Education Institutions Applying for Subsidies

### Misconception 1: Any education institution can apply

In reality, most subsidies require the training content to relate directly to **vocational competency development**. Purely hobby-oriented training (such as calligraphy or painting classes) is very hard to get approved. But language training, vocational skills training, management talent training, and the like have higher approval rates.

### Misconception 2: Underestimating the workload of applying

Many owners think "just fill out a form and you get the subsidy." In reality, simply preparing the application documents takes weeks of work. If your institution lacks the relevant in-house personnel, we strongly recommend hiring a professional advisor to assist—this advisory fee is worth it.

### Misconception 3: Ignoring post-subsidy oversight requirements

After receiving a subsidy, the government conducts strict post-hoc inspections. You need to:
- Keep all expenditure receipts and invoices
- Report on project progress regularly
- Conduct effectiveness evaluations (student numbers, training evaluations, etc.)
- Undergo inspections by the review department

Violating these requirements may result in being ordered to return the subsidy and can also damage your credit. We have seen an institution ordered to return the entire subsidy and pay a penalty because its reported data was false.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: My Chinese-run education institution was just established. Can I apply for a subsidy?

**A:** Most subsidies have requirements on how long you have been established, usually requiring the company to have been in operation for at least 1–2 years and to have filed taxes for at least one year. This is because the government needs to assess the company's operational stability and credit record. However, a few subsidies are specifically aimed at new startups, such as the "Founding & Startup Support Subsidy" (創業・起業支援補助金). If your institution was just established, we recommend first focusing on improving your operations and tax filings, while finding out whether your local government has subsidies aimed at new startups. After a year, you can apply for most national-level subsidies. If you urgently need funds, consider a local government startup subsidy, or apply for a low-interest loan from the Japan Finance Corporation. A combination of subsidy and loan is often the optimal choice.

### Q2: My institution has fewer than 5 employees. Can I still apply for a subsidy?

**A:** Absolutely. Although some subsidies favor companies of a certain scale, to encourage small and micro-enterprises, the Japanese government has set up subsidy programs specifically for small-scale businesses (20 or fewer employees). The "Small-Scale Business Sustainability Subsidy" (小規模事業者持続化補助金) is a prime example, with amounts reaching up to 2 million JPY. In addition, if you operate a Japanese-language school or other training institution as a sole proprietor, you can apply for subsidies aimed at sole proprietors. Having few employees can actually be an advantage, because the review is relatively simple and your financial situation is easier to verify. But note that the content you report must be truthful and accurate, since smaller companies are, if anything, more likely to be subject to focused scrutiny.

### Q3: What certification must my training courses obtain to apply for a subsidy?

**A:** Special certification is not necessarily required, but you must meet the specific requirements of the subsidy program. For example, if you apply for the Human Resource Development Grant, the course must relate to the actual work of the company or its employees and be able to demonstrate clear learning objectives and evaluation methods. Some subsidies require you to hire instructors with specific qualifications (such as a Japanese-language teaching certificate or a corporate-training instructor qualification), but they do not require the course itself to obtain any official certification. The safest approach is to provide your course plan and instructor qualifications to the application window for a prior consultation before applying, and seek their opinion. Some subsidy programs allow applicants to hold a prior consultation (事前相談), which can significantly improve the success rate. Do not wait until the formal application to discover that your course does not meet the requirements.

### Q4: Do subsidies need to be repaid? What is the repayment period?

**A:** This is a common misunderstanding. **Most subsidies (助成金) are non-repayable grants**, which is fundamentally different from a loan. The various subsidies from Japan's MHLW are all one-time, non-repayable support. But there are three important "buts": First, if a post-hoc review finds that you provided false information or used the subsidy in violation of the rules, you must return it in full and may face penalties. Second, some subsidies have an "accrual period" (加算期間), meaning that for 3–5 years after receiving the subsidy, your company's operating status will be subject to ongoing oversight, and if operations deteriorate or taxes go unpaid, you may be ordered to return it. Third, a small number of subsidy products (such as certain loan interest-subsidy programs) mix subsidy and loan, and the loan portion must be repaid. Always clarify the nature of the subsidy before applying. Generally, national-level "grants" (助成金) are not repayable, most "subsidies" (補助金) are also not repayable, and only a few programs have repayment clauses.

### Q5: Can I apply for multiple subsidy programs at the same time? Would that be double-dipping?

**A:** In principle, **the same business project cannot receive multiple subsidies for the same expenses**. For example, the same course-development project cannot apply for both the Human Resource Development Grant and the Small-Scale Business Sustainability Subsidy. However, if you have multiple independent projects, you can apply for different subsidies for each. For example: applying for the Small-Scale Business Sustainability Subsidy for institutional office renovation, and the Career-Up Grant for employee training—these two projects are independent and can be applied for simultaneously. But if both subsidies involve "purchasing teaching equipment," you cannot apply for the same item twice. The specific criterion is: each expenditure can only be covered by one subsidy. When applying for multiple subsidies, you must clearly state in the application which expenditure corresponds to which subsidy, otherwise it is easy to be deemed a violation. We recommend that, after selecting your subsidy programs, you have an advisor or the review department help you plan, to avoid the risk of disqualification due to double application.

### Q6: As a foreign-capital company or a limited company founded by Chinese owners, are there restrictions on applying for subsidies?

**A:** Legally speaking, as long as you are formally registered in Japan as a corporation or sole proprietor and meet the other conditions, nationality and ownership structure should not be an obstacle. But in practice there are a few points to note: First, most subsidies require the company to have paid taxes in Japan for at least 1–2 years. Second, some local subsidies (especially revitalization subsidies) may prioritize Japanese-capital companies, though they will not explicitly reject foreign capital. Third, requirements for corporate governance transparency are relatively high, and if the company has a complex cross-border shareholding structure, you may encounter more questions during review. We recommend that Chinese business owners ensure: company registration documents are in order, tax filings are complete, bank accounts are clear (do not mix personal and company accounts), and financial statements comply with Japanese accounting standards. This way you will not be rejected because of the nature of your company. Some subsidies even have clauses supporting "multicultural businesses," which may actually be advantageous for Chinese-owned companies.

## Self-Check List Before Formally Applying for a Subsidy

Before formally applying, check each of the following items:

**Institutional compliance (10 items)**:
- [ ] Are you formally registered as a corporation or sole proprietor?
- [ ] Do you have a legal teaching venue (a clear lease agreement)?
- [ ] Have you filed taxes? Have there been any overdue filings in the past 3 years?
- [ ] Do you have a proper bank account and financial records?
- [ ] Have you established written company rules and management systems?
- [ ] Do you meet the relevant requirements of the education administration authorities?
- [ ] Do all employees (if any) have signed labor contracts?
- [ ] Have you paid social insurance and taxes as required?
- [ ] Do you have clear teaching-quality assurance measures?
- [ ] Have you kept complete student rosters and course records?

**Subsidy program fit (6 items)**:
- [ ] Does your institution type (corporation, individual, for-profit/non-profit) meet the subsidy's requirements?
- [ ] Is your employee count within the subsidy's supported range?
- [ ] Does your annual revenue meet the requirements?
- [ ] Does the training content relate to vocational competency development?
- [ ] Can you provide instructors who meet the qualification requirements?
- [ ] Do you have a clear student recruitment plan and evaluation metrics?

**Document readiness (8 items)**:
- [ ] Are your financial statements complete and compliant with Japanese accounting standards?
- [ ] Can you provide complete tax-filing records for the past 3 years?
- [ ] Have you prepared a detailed business plan?
- [ ] Is the budget list detailed and specific?
- [ ] Are the instructor team's qualification proofs complete?
- [ ] Have you taken on-site photos of classrooms, equipment, etc.?
- [ ] Have you prepared student evaluations or proof of teaching outcomes?
- [ ] Have you consulted the application window and obtained prior guidance?

If any of the above 30 items cannot be checked off, it needs to be resolved before applying.
