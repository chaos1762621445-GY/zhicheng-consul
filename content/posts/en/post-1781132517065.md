# Labor-Saving Subsidy vs. AI Adoption Subsidy: The Optimal Choice Guide for Chinese-Owned Businesses in Japan

As a Chinese business owner in Japan, you may have heard the two buzzwords "Labor-Saving Subsidy" and "AI Adoption Subsidy." But what exactly is the difference between them, and which one is better for you? Although both types of subsidy aim at "cutting costs and boosting efficiency," they differ markedly in their target applicants, funding scale, technology path, and review focus. This article compares these two popular subsidies across multiple dimensions—the subsidy cap, eligibility, industry fit, and more—to help you clarify your thinking and find the financing plan that best fits your company's stage of development.

---

## The Labor-Saving Subsidy (Business Improvement Grant, 業務改善助成金): Interpretation and Practice

### Core parameters and subsidy scale

The Labor-Saving Subsidy (usually referring to the Business Improvement Grant–type products rolled out by local governments across Japan) is a traditional solution-oriented subsidy aimed at **lowering labor costs and raising production efficiency**. According to public statistics from Japan's Small and Medium Enterprise Agency in 2024, the typical range for the Labor-Saving Subsidy is between **JPY 500,000 and JPY 5 million**, with a subsidy rate mostly of **2/3–3/4** (i.e., the company bears 1/3–1/4).

Take Tokyo's "SME Labor-Saving Subsidy" as an example: the cap can reach **JPY 3 million**, and eligible items include production equipment investment, business-process reengineering, and labor outsourcing. The application cycle is usually 6–8 months (from the public offering announcement to the funds arriving), with **1–2 recruitment windows per year**. The number of applications per year is roughly 2,000–4,000, and the average adoption rate is between 30% and 50%—subject to the reviewing authority's decision.

The key point is this: the "labor-saving" in the Labor-Saving Subsidy covers **any solution that can reduce the input of human labor—mechanical equipment investment, process optimization, personnel replacement, and so on**—and is not limited to IT or AI.

### Eligibility and applicable scenarios

**Who can apply?**
- SMEs with 5–300 employees (this varies somewhat by region)
- A good tax-filing record and no serious legal violations
- Limited companies or sole proprietors registered in Japan (Chinese-owned businesses are equally eligible)
- Financial statements for the past 3 years must be provided when applying

**Examples of applicable scenarios:**
1. **Manufacturing plants**: purchasing automated production lines, punch presses, welding robots, etc., with an expected reduction of 5–10 employees' worth of repetitive labor
2. **Logistics companies**: investing in automated sorting systems and WMS systems to cut manual operation time
3. **Restaurant chains**: introducing kitchen auto-cooking machines, self-order kiosks, and inventory management software
4. **Construction companies**: using surveying drones and 3D-printed building-part models
5. **Chinese-owned medical clinics**: investing in electronic medical record systems and self-check-in kiosks to reduce front-desk pressure

### The core advantages of the Labor-Saving Subsidy

1. **A relatively mature review logic**: reviewers have a straightforward understanding of "mechanical labor-saving"; there is no need to over-explain how AI works, and the technology is highly acceptable.

2. **Broad coverage**: it can subsidize hardware (mechanical equipment), software, and even consulting fees; typically hardware accounts for 60%–70% and software and consulting for 30%–40%.

3. **Relatively simple ROI verification**: effectiveness is measured by "reduction in employee count" or "decrease in overtime hours," and the data is easy to obtain. Take one Chinese-owned manufacturer as an example: it invested JPY 1.2 million to purchase an automatic packaging machine, and when applying demonstrated that it could reduce 2 employees at an annual salary of about JPY 4 million, for an ROI period of 3.6 months. Cases like this usually have an approval rate above 60%.

4. **A relatively fast time to actual payment**: in some regions the government budget is ample, and funds arrive within about 3–4 months after project completion.

5. **Good compatibility with other subsidies**: it can be stacked with the Employment Adjustment Subsidy, human-resource development subsidies, and the like.

### The barriers and drawbacks of the Labor-Saving Subsidy

1. **A high self-funded proportion**: although the subsidy rate is as high as 2/3, the company still has to bear about 1/3, which puts significant pressure on companies with tight cash flow. For example, a project requiring a JPY 3 million investment means the company must front JPY 1 million.

2. **Restricted equipment selection**: it must be **equipment or services already circulating on the market with a clear purchase invoice**; it does not support custom development or technologies still in the trial phase. One Chinese-owned company once wanted to apply using a self-developed system and was rejected.

3. **Pre-purchase required**: most subsidies adopt an "invest first, subsidize later" model; the company must first pay the full amount or the bulk of it, then apply on the strength of the invoices, which creates considerable funding pressure. A few localities support "pre-approval before purchase," but the process is more complex.

4. **A long verification period**: the subsidizing side usually requires a "performance report" to be submitted within 12–24 months after application, using data to prove that labor costs really were reduced; if this cannot be verified, a return of the subsidy may be required.

5. **Large differences among local governments**: for the same subsidy name, the cap, subsidy rate, and review standards differ noticeably from region to region. Applying in Tokyo may be quite easy to pass, but in Okayama Prefecture the difficulty may double.

## The AI Adoption Subsidy: Interpretation and Practice

### Core parameters and subsidy scale

The AI Adoption Subsidy is a new type of subsidy that Japan supports specifically to promote industrial upgrading and address an aging population. According to the 2024 policy guidelines of Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI), **the AI Adoption Subsidy cap is usually JPY 2 million to JPY 10 million**, with a subsidy rate of **1/2–2/3** (far lower than the Labor-Saving Subsidy), focused on supporting companies that adopt **cutting-edge technologies such as generative AI, machine learning, and deep learning** to innovate their business models.

Key data points:
- The **average subsidy amount** is about JPY 4 million (according to METI statistics, the average per-case value of AI subsidies in FY2023)
- The **subsidy rate** is usually **1/2** (some SMEs can reach 2/3)
- The **application cycle** is about 8–12 months, longer than the Labor-Saving Subsidy
- The **annual pass rate** is about 20%–35%, far below the Labor-Saving Subsidy's 30–50%—subject to the reviewing authority's decision

This means that AI subsidy applications face fiercer competition and stricter review standards, but the amount approved per instance is larger.

### Eligibility and applicable scenarios

**Who can apply?**
- There is no hard restriction on company size, but you must be able to prove that "AI adoption will produce quantifiable business value"
- You must have a detailed plan for AI implementation (not just an idea)
- You must have a formal partnership with an AI service provider or consulting firm (the subsidizing side will review the partner's qualifications)
- The applicant company must provide financial statements and a cost-benefit analysis of the AI project

**Examples of applicable scenarios:**
1. **E-commerce companies**: using AI to optimize product recommendation algorithms and lift conversion rates; using computer vision to inspect image quality
2. **Financial services**: introducing AI risk-control models to speed up loan approval by 50%; using natural language processing for customer sentiment analysis
3. **Healthcare**: AI-assisted diagnosis of medical images, AI appointment-scheduling systems, patient data analysis and prediction
4. **Restaurant chains**: demand-forecasting AI (predicting how much ingredient stock is needed on a given day), inventory optimization, customer behavior analysis
5. **Chinese-owned cross-border e-commerce**: using AI for multilingual customer service, automated product-description generation, and order-anomaly detection
6. **HR services**: AI résumé screening, interviewer-assist scoring systems, employee-attrition prediction models

### The core advantages of the AI Adoption Subsidy

1. **A large subsidy amount**: compared with the most common Labor-Saving Subsidy amounts (JPY 2–3 million), the AI subsidy can reach JPY 5–10 million, providing much greater support for heavy IT-investment projects. One Chinese-owned company invested in building an "AI customer service + recommendation system," applied for JPY 8 million, and was ultimately approved for JPY 6 million, with a self-funded proportion of 1/3.

2. **Encouragement of innovative business models**: it is not merely about cost reduction but values innovation such as **revenue growth, new product development, and market expansion**. Reviewers tend to support projects that "use AI to create new revenue" rather than those that "just cut headcount."

3. **High government emphasis**: AI adoption is at the core of the Japanese government's "digital transformation" and "Society 5.0" strategies; the level of policy support is strong, new policies iterate quickly, and application opportunities are relatively plentiful.

4. **A bonus for collaboration with research institutions**: if a company can partner with a university or research institute to develop the AI model, it can earn extra points, and the subsidy rate may rise from 1/2 to 2/3.

5. **More post-implementation maintenance support**: some AI subsidy programs include operating costs such as 12–24 months of technical support and model-iteration fees, whereas the Labor-Saving Subsidy mostly does not cover post-implementation maintenance.

### The barriers and drawbacks of the AI Adoption Subsidy

1. **A high self-funded proportion and large upfront outlay**: although the subsidy amount is large, bearing 1/3–1/2 yourself means that for an JPY 8 million project, the company must pay JPY 4 million itself. This puts even greater pressure on SME cash flow.

2. **Extremely high requirements for the AI plan**: "we want to use ChatGPT" is not enough to apply. You must submit a **detailed AI technology plan, data-infrastructure plan, staff training plan, and risk management plan**. The review committee scrutinizes each item, and vague or over-promised plans are usually sent back.

3. **Difficult, long-cycle verification of results**: the results of an AI project are not as immediately obvious as those of machinery. The reviewing side requires **the source of modeling data, model-accuracy metrics, A/B test results**, and the like, with verification periods often reaching 24 months. If the model accuracy falls short of expectations (e.g., a planned 85% accuracy but only 72% in reality), you may face a reduction in the subsidy or even a return.

4. **Strict qualification requirements for the AI service provider**: you cannot just partner with any "AI studio." The subsidizing side mostly requires the partner to hold qualifications such as **ISO certification, patents, or national high-tech enterprise recognition**. This means small AI startups and individual developers are usually not recognized, and the company's choice of partner is limited.

5. **High policy risk**: AI policy changes quickly, and review standards passed last year may be adjusted this year. One Chinese-owned company obtained an AI subsidy in 2023, and in 2024 new rules imposed stricter "data privacy" requirements, so the company was required to supplement its privacy-compliance plan, delaying the funds' arrival.

6. **A low pass rate and fierce competition**: a 20%–35% pass rate means only 1 in 3–5 applications is approved, whereas the Labor-Saving Subsidy's 30%–50% pass rate is relatively friendly.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: My company wants to invest in automation equipment and also adopt an AI recommendation system—can I apply for both subsidies at the same time?

**A:** In principle, yes, but the **projects must be independent and there must be no double billing**. For example, your automatic packaging machine falls within the scope of the "Labor-Saving Subsidy," while the AI recommendation system falls within the scope of the "AI Adoption Subsidy"; the expense accounts for the two projects must be kept completely separate—you cannot use Labor-Saving Subsidy funds to pay for the AI project, and vice versa. According to the guidance of Japan's Small and Medium Enterprise Agency, you must submit two separate applications in different time windows when applying, and the project implementation times can be staggered. In practice, however, if the two projects have an implicit connection (for example, the AI system is meant to optimize the operating parameters of the automated machinery), the reviewers may deem them "substantially one" and allow only one of them to be applied for. We recommend first consulting your local economy and industry department or an application agent to confirm. Subject to the reviewing authority's decision.

### Q2: I'm a Chinese sole proprietor without a registered company—can I apply for these two subsidies?

**A:** Yes, but the eligibility review will be stricter. The overwhelming majority of Japanese subsidies are **open to sole proprietors (freelancers)**, but you must satisfy: (1) a formal tax-filing record and bank account transaction history for the past 3 years; (2) a business scale reaching a certain standard (most regions require annual revenue of JPY 10 million or more); (3) proper accounting books or a Zeirishi (税理士) engaged for bookkeeping. The Labor-Saving Subsidy is relatively friendly to sole proprietors, but individual applications for the AI subsidy are extremely rare, because reviewers tend to believe that "an individual struggles to handle the post-implementation maintenance of a large-scale AI project." If you are a sole proprietor and want to apply for the AI subsidy, we recommend first signing a partnership agreement with an AI service provider that clearly commits to technical-support responsibility in the plan; this will significantly raise your approval probability (from around 5% to around 15%—subject to the reviewing authority's decision).

### Q3: Isn't the Labor-Saving Subsidy's "invest first, subsidize later" model quite risky—if it isn't approved, won't the money be wasted?

**A:** This is a concern for many Chinese business owners. It is true that under the traditional "invest first, subsidize later" model, the company bears **the full risk of the application not being approved**. In recent years, however, some Japanese local governments (such as Tokyo and Kanagawa Prefecture) have introduced a "pre-approval system" (selection-at-application system), which works as follows: (1) the company first submits a plan to apply for "advance eligibility certification"; (2) the government gives a "pre-review passed" or "not passed" notice within 1–2 months; (3) companies that pass the pre-review then purchase the equipment, with the final subsidy virtually assured (barring major violations). If you are in one of these friendly regions, the risk is greatly reduced. We recommend first consulting your local Chamber of Commerce and Industry or SME Support Center to see whether a pre-approval option exists. Otherwise, you might consider first consulting a bank or a subsidy-application agent about a matching financing plan to reduce cash-flow risk.

### Q4: Is the AI subsidy's "model accuracy" requirement especially high—for example, must it reach 90% or more?

**A:** It is not an absolutely fixed number, but there is indeed an implicit expectation. According to METI's recent review guidelines, an AI project usually needs to explicitly commit to **a reasonable accuracy target** (such as 80%, 85%, or 92%, depending on the industry), and then verify that target after project completion using a test set (test data) or real business data. If the final accuracy deviates from the committed value by more than **±5 percentage points**, the subsidizing side has the right to require the company to provide supplementary explanations or to adjust the subsidy amount. But if the deviation is within ±3 percentage points, in most cases there is no penalty. The key is to **set a target during application that is realistically achievable and supported by an industry benchmark**, rather than over-promising. For example, if the industry average is 75% and you commit to 88%, the risk is high; but committing to 78% is relatively safe. Consulting a professional AI-plan designer (a qualified company) can greatly reduce this risk.

### Q5: After receiving the subsidy, if I change my mind midway and don't want to continue the project, can I return the subsidy?

**A:** Usually **no, or there will be a heavy penalty**. Almost all Japanese subsidies have a "no mid-contract withdrawal" clause. If a company halts the project for some reason, leaves the equipment idle, or stops using the AI system, the government has the right to require a **full return or a pro-rata return of the subsidy**, and some even add a late-payment charge (usually 3% per year). So before applying you must **thoroughly justify the project's necessity and sustainability**—do not apply just because it's a trend. One Chinese-owned manufacturer applied for a Labor-Saving Subsidy in 2022; in 2023, due to a drop in orders, it wanted to stop using the newly purchased equipment, and in the end was required to return JPY 1.5 million in subsidy plus 4% in late-payment interest. That is a hard lesson. We recommend conducting thorough market forecasting and a financial stress test before applying.

### Q6: How long does it take for the subsidy to finally arrive, and how does the company handle the funding pressure during that time?

**A:** The Labor-Saving Subsidy usually arrives **3–4 months** after the project is completed and the completion report is submitted; the AI subsidy, because the review is complex, takes **4–6 months** or longer. During this time the company must front the funds itself. To ease this pressure, we recommend the following measures: (1) negotiate **installment payments** with your suppliers (for example, in 3 installments paid at the end of each month) to stretch out the payment period; (2) apply to a bank for a **subsidy-collateral loan** (補助金担保ローン)—some banks will grant loan facilities based on your subsidy approval letter, and even repay the loan directly once the subsidy arrives, so you only pay 3–4 months' worth of interest; (3) if possible, apply for an **operating-capital loan subsidy**—some local government subsidy programs include a loan-interest subsidy. A Chinese-owned company in Tokyo invested JPY 3.5 million in automation equipment, using a bank subsidy-collateral loan to front the funds, and paid it all off once the subsidy arrived, paying only about JPY 80,000 in loan interest. This kind of plan can effectively ease cash-flow pressure.

## How to Choose? Match Your Situation

### Situation 1: **A company with 50–100 employees, traditional manufacturing, stable cash flow, wanting quick results**

**Recommendation: prioritize the Labor-Saving Subsidy**

**Reasons:**
- Your company size (50–100 people) is precisely the main applicant base for the Labor-Saving Subsidy; reviewers are familiar with companies like yours, and the pass rate is as high as 45%–55%
- Automation plans in traditional manufacturing (mechanical equipment, production-process optimization) best fit the Labor-Saving Subsidy logic and are easy to review
- The subsidy rate is high (2/3–3/4) and the self-funded proportion is only 1/4–1/3, so cash-flow pressure is low. For a 100-person company, self-funding JPY 1–1.5 million is relatively bearable
- The review cycle is 6–8 months and payment is fast (3–4 months), so you can see an actual return on investment within a year
- The effect-verification of equipment-type investment is relatively straightforward (reduced employee count, lower overtime hours), so the risk of "failing acceptance" is low

**Concrete advice:** Immediately take stock of which processes in your production department are the most labor-intensive, get quotes from 2–3 equipment suppliers, then submit a preliminary plan to your local Chamber of Commerce and Industry or SME Support Center, aiming to apply in the next recruitment window (usually early or mid-year).

### Situation 2: **An e-commerce or services company with 20–50 employees, wanting to boost revenue through big data / AI rather than just cutting costs**

**Recommendation: prioritize the AI Adoption Subsidy**

**Reasons:**
- The core competitiveness of your business (e-commerce, services) lies not in labor costs but in **conversion rates, customer experience, and market insight**—precisely the "revenue-gain" direction the AI subsidy focuses on
- Applications such as AI recommendation, customer service, and demand forecasting directly affect revenue; reviewers will recognize this innovative value, and the subsidy amount is large (usually JPY 4–7 million)
- Your company is relatively small (20–50 people); even if fully approved, the Labor-Saving Subsidy is only JPY 2–3 million, which is insufficient for a large investment project. The AI subsidy, however, has no hard size restriction, and as long as the plan is convincing, you can be approved for over JPY 6 million
- E-commerce/services companies usually have a good data foundation and IT awareness, making it easier to design a high-quality AI plan, so the pass rate rises relatively (from an average of 20% to around 30%—subject to the reviewing authority's decision)

**Concrete advice:** First conduct a feasibility assessment with 1–2 qualified AI/big-data companies (preferably ISO-certified, national high-tech enterprises), at a cost of about JPY 30,000–50,000; they can help you justify claims such as "the recommendation system can lift the conversion rate by 3–5 percentage points" or "demand forecasting can reduce inventory by 15%." With this assessment report in hand, your pass rate will noticeably improve when you apply.

### Situation 3: **A Chinese-owned medical clinic or educational institution with 10–30 employees, limited cash flow, wanting to improve operations but with a small investment amount**

**Recommendation: choose the Labor-Saving Subsidy first (electronic medical record systems, check-in systems, etc.); hold off on the AI subsidy for now**

**Reasons:**
- Most of the investment you need is in **electronic medical record systems, self-check-in kiosks, and patient management software**, which fall into the typical "labor-saving" category rather than being AI projects, and do not fit the AI subsidy's review logic
- The investment amount is expected to be around JPY 1–2.5 million; the Labor-Saving Subsidy cap of JPY 3–5 million is more than enough to cover it, whereas the AI subsidy's minimum expected investment is usually above JPY 4 million, which actually wastes much of the potential for you
- Your cash flow is limited, and the Labor-Saving Subsidy's high subsidy rate (2/3–3/4) eases the burden better. For example, investing JPY 1.5 million in an electronic medical record system and receiving JPY 1 million in subsidy leaves only JPY 500,000 to self-fund—manageable pressure
- The Labor-Saving Subsidy pass rate in the medical/education industries is relatively high (45%–55%), because the government is also prioritizing support for these industries

**Concrete advice:** Consult your local medical association, medical corporation association, or board of education about subsidy policies; these organizations usually have dedicated subsidy-application guidance. At the same time, prepare financial statements and an "investment necessity statement" document (explaining that the electronic medical record system can reduce front-desk work by 2 hours/day), and the pass rate will improve significantly.

### Situation 4: **A large manufacturer or a company in an industrial-transformation phase, with 150–300 employees, ample cash flow, wanting to make a "big bet" on innovation**

**Recommendation: both can be applied for, but implement in phases—Labor-Saving first (cut costs), then the AI subsidy (boost revenue)**

**Reasons:**
- Your size and financial strength allow you to bear higher risk. Applying for both subsidies at the same time spreads the risk
- **Phase 1 (Year 1):** apply for the Labor-Saving Subsidy to invest in an automated production line or industrial robots, with an expected investment of JPY 5 million, receiving JPY 3.5 million in subsidy and self-funding JPY 1.5 million. This can quickly lower production costs by 20% within 12 months
- **Phase 2 (Year 2):** building on the success of Phase 1 and having accumulated more data, apply for the AI subsidy to invest in "intelligent optimization of the production process" or "predictive-maintenance AI for equipment," with an investment of JPY 8 million, a subsidy of JPY 5 million, and self-funding of JPY 3 million. By this point the company has gained revenue from Phase 1 and has more ample cash flow
- This phased strategy has a hidden benefit: when the AI subsidy review committee sees that "the company has already successfully implemented a subsidy project," it will substantially raise its trust in your execution capability, lifting the pass rate from an average of 20% to 35%–45%—subject to the reviewing authority's decision

**Concrete advice:** Engage a professional subsidy-application consulting team (preferably with a manufacturing background) that can help you design a 2–3 year "subsidy investment roadmap," ensuring every step is prepared. Total investment may reach JPY 10–15 million, but the final total subsidy may reach JPY 8–10 million, compressing the ROI period to 18–24 months.

## Conclusion: A Final Checklist Before You Decide

Whether you choose the Labor-Saving Subsidy or the AI Adoption Subsidy, be sure to confirm the following before applying:

1. **Financial readiness**: What percentage of your company's monthly profit does the expected self-funded amount represent? Can you withstand the risk of the subsidy not being approved or arriving late?
2. **Team capability**: Does the company have staff who understand the application process, can gather data, and can write the project report? If not, budget JPY 50,000–150,000 to hire an agent.
3. **Technical feasibility**: Can the equipment/AI plan you purchase or partner on truly solve your pain point? Have you run a small-scale pilot?
4. **Time cycle**: It takes 8–12 months from application to the subsidy arriving—can your business wait that long?
5. **Post-implementation maintenance**: After you receive the subsidy and invest, is the budget for maintenance, upgrades, and staff training secured?

**If you still have questions about your situation, we recommend conducting a free feasibility assessment of a subsidy application.** Most of Japan's Chambers of Commerce and Industry, SME Support Centers, and professional subsidy-application consulting firms offer a **free first consultation**. Based on your financial statements, industry background, and investment plan, they can give a preliminary judgment on "which subsidy suits you best" within 30 minutes, usually with an accuracy above 85%.
