# A Must-Read for Chinese Business Owners in Japan: A Complete Roundup of Japan's 2025 Subsidy Policies

As Japan's economic restructuring and digital transformation accelerate, 2025 brings a fresh round of adjustments to subsidy policy. For Chinese business owners in Japan, understanding the latest Japanese subsidy policies can not only effectively lower operating costs but also seize the opportunities of national support to upgrade the business. This article gives you a detailed analysis of the core information, application essentials, and practical application paths for Japan's 2025 subsidies.

## Overall Trends in Japan's 2025 Subsidy Policies

Japan's 2025 government subsidy policies show several clear characteristics: first, support priorities are tilting toward green transformation and digital upgrades; second, support for small and mid-sized enterprises has increased, with subsidy amounts rising across the board; third, application processes are gradually being simplified, with online applications exceeding 80%; and fourth, restrictions on foreign entrepreneurs continue to be relaxed.

According to the fiscal 2025 budget released by Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI), the total scale of subsidies directly overseen by METI alone exceeds JPY 2.8 trillion, up 12% from the previous year. Of this, SME-related subsidies account for 45%. This means Chinese-owned businesses in Japan have more opportunities to secure government funding.

Notably, Japan's 2025 subsidy policy has particularly strengthened support for strategic industries, including AI, IoT, green energy, health care, and the creative and cultural industries. If your business is involved in these areas, your application success rate will rise noticeably.

## A Detailed Look at the Major 2025 Subsidy Programs

### Sustainability Subsidy for Small Businesses (小規模事業者持続化補助金)

This is one of the most popular subsidy programs, and it continues in 2025. Subsidy amounts range from JPY 500,000 to JPY 2,000,000, with an average approval rate of 40–50%.

**Simplified eligibility**: Businesses with a residential address in Japan, a business license, and 20 or fewer employees (commerce/services) or 5 or fewer employees (manufacturing). Chinese sole proprietors and small businesses in Japan can all apply.

**Fund uses**: Equipment purchases, IT system adoption, marketing and promotion, store renovation, and other operational-improvement costs. Last year, a Chinese café owner in Tokyo applied for this subsidy to upgrade coffee machines and revamp the online payment system, receiving a JPY 1,500,000 subsidy.

**New in 2025**: Support has been added for green offices and energy-saving renovations. If you invest in LED lighting retrofits or air-conditioner replacement, the subsidy rate can reach 75% (usually two-thirds).

### Monozukuri Subsidy (ものづくり補助金, Manufacturing Subsidy)

Aimed at production-oriented enterprises, the 2025 subsidy cap has been raised to JPY 10,000,000, an increase of JPY 2,000,000 over 2024.

**Applicable scope**: Businesses engaged in manufacturing, processing, and treatment—especially projects that introduce new technology, modernize equipment, or improve production efficiency. Many Chinese-owned food-processing plants, textile companies, and precision-machinery companies in Japan fall into this category.

**Key requirements**: After the project is complete, you must raise production efficiency by 20% or more, or reduce the cost rate by 15% or more. These are hard metrics, so prepare your technical justification before applying.

**Subsidy rate**: Typically one-half or two-thirds, depending on company size and the nature of the project. After approval, the project must be completed within one year; overrunning the deadline risks revocation of the subsidy.

### IT Adoption Subsidy (IT導入補助金)

In 2025, Japan has stepped up support for digital transformation, and the IT Adoption Subsidy has become a new priority.

**Areas of support**: Financial management systems, HR management systems, e-commerce platform building, cloud migration, and more. Subsidy amounts range from JPY 500,000 to JPY 3,500,000.

**Special advantage**: 2025 has specifically added support for cross-border e-commerce platforms, which is very favorable for Chinese-owned businesses engaged in purchasing agency, import, and export. An entrepreneur from Shanghai used this subsidy to build a platform for exporting Japanese goods, receiving JPY 2,000,000 in support.

**Review focus**: You must submit a detailed IT investment plan, an expected ROI analysis, and an implementation timeline. We recommend engaging a professional IT consulting firm to help prepare the application.

### Business Restructuring Subsidy (事業再構築補助金)

This is established for businesses facing operational difficulties that need to pivot or upgrade their business model. It continues in 2025, with a maximum subsidy of JPY 30,000,000.

**Eligibility**: You must demonstrate that your existing business faces challenges (a decline in sales of 20% or more) and plan to pivot to a new industry or business model. Under the pandemic's impact, some tourism and food-service businesses successfully transformed through this subsidy.

**Key difficulty**: You must submit a business-reform plan detailing the feasibility, market prospects, and use of funds for the new business. We recommend engaging a professional consultant; the application cost is JPY 2,000,000–3,000,000, but once approved, the return is enormous.

### Carbon Neutrality Subsidy (カーボンニュートラル, Carbon-Neutral Transformation Subsidy)

A new priority subsidy program added in 2025, reflecting Japan's national strategy of "carbon neutrality by 2050."

**Scope of support**: Green investments such as solar installations, LED lighting, energy-efficient air conditioning, and EV charging facilities. The subsidy rate can reach up to three-quarters.

**Amount**: JPY 1,000,000–50,000,000. This is especially favorable for business owners with long-term development plans, offering both financial support and alignment with environmental trends. A Chinese-owned logistics company in Kanagawa applied to install solar panels and an electric forklift charging station, and received a JPY 18,000,000 subsidy.

## Special Advantages and Points to Watch for Chinese Business Owners in Japan

### Policy Advantages

In 2025, the Japanese government continues to relax restrictions on foreign operators. As long as you hold a residence card, have a Japanese tax record, and your business is registered in Japan, you can apply for most subsidy programs. In particular, for Chinese business owners holding a Business Manager visa or Highly Skilled Professional visa, there are almost no additional restrictions.

Some local governments give extra preference to enterprises that bring in foreign investment and operate internationally. For instance, Chiba and Kanagawa Prefectures have dedicated support programs for Chinese entrepreneurs, where the subsidy rate may be 10–20% higher.

### Common Pitfalls

**1. Language misunderstandings**: Chinese translations of Japanese subsidy review criteria often differ from the original in the details. We recommend obtaining the original Japanese text and, when necessary, confirming with a translator or consultant.

**2. Missing deadlines**: Most subsidies have clear application deadlines, usually in two or four rounds. Miss one round and you wait half a year. In 2025, focus your attention on the announcements around January, April, July, and October.

**3. Misreporting amounts**: The investment amount you report on the application must match your actual expenditure. Some business owners have been required to repay funds for misreporting and have also faced credit damage. All invoices and contracts must be kept intact.

**4. Changing the use of funds**: After approval, the use of funds must strictly follow the application. You cannot suddenly change the model of equipment purchased or the direction of investment, or it will be treated as a violation.

**5. Neglecting reports**: After a subsidy is disbursed, you must periodically submit progress reports and acceptance reports. Many business owners have been fined or had subsidies deducted for late or non-compliant reports.

## Application Process and Operational Guide

### A Step-by-Step Application Route

**Step 1: Confirming company eligibility** (1 week)
- Confirm whether your company type meets the eligibility conditions
- Check whether tax filings and social-insurance contributions are compliant
- Confirm the validity of your residence card and business permit

**Step 2: Project planning** (2–3 weeks)
- Define a clear investment project and objectives
- Prepare a budget table and procurement list
- Complete technical-feasibility and economic-feasibility analyses

**Step 3: Preparing the application** (2–4 weeks)
- Write a business plan explaining why you need this subsidy
- Describe the fund-usage plan in detail
- Provide an analysis of the company's current state and an assessment of industry prospects
- Explain the expected outcomes (the most critical part)

**Step 4: Online submission** (1 day)
- Log in to https://www.jgrants.go.jp/ or a dedicated local-government platform
- Upload all necessary documents (business license, financial statements, application, etc.)
- The system automatically checks document completeness

**Step 5: Waiting for review** (2–3 months)
- METI or the local government department conducts an initial review, on-site investigation, and final review
- During this period you may receive requests for supplementary materials
- Keep your phone available and respond promptly to any questions

**Step 6: Approval and implementation** (3–12 months)
- Sign the subsidy grant decision letter
- Implement the project according to plan, keeping all invoices and records
- Submit interim reports on time

**Step 7: Acceptance and subsidy payment** (1–2 months)
- Submit the final acceptance report and all expenditure receipts
- After review passes, the subsidy is paid in installments (usually 2–3 payments)

### Checklist of Required Documents

- A copy of the business license
- A certified copy of the corporate registry (commercial registry)
- Financial statements for the most recent 2 years (settlement statements)
- Corporate tax return and tax-payment certificate
- Social-insurance payment certificate
- The representative's proof of identity and a copy of the residence card
- A detailed business plan
- Quotations for the equipment to be purchased
- Bank account information (for the subsidy payment)

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: As a Chinese business owner holding a Business Manager visa, do I receive the same subsidy treatment as a Japanese person?

**A:** Legally, it is completely equal. As long as your company is formally registered in Japan and pays taxes and social insurance on time, you can apply for all subsidies aimed at small and mid-sized enterprises. In fact, over the past five years, the total subsidies approved for Business Manager visa holders accounted for about 35% of all subsidies to foreigners, indicating that this group has a fairly high approval rate. However, note that some local subsidies may require "Japanese nationality" or "permanent residency," in which case you cannot apply—but national-level subsidies (issued by cabinet ministries such as METI) basically have no such restrictions. We recommend carefully reading the "eligible applicants" (対象者) section of the call for applications before applying, to check for any nationality or residency-status restrictions.

### Q2: Do 2025 Japanese subsidy applications require Chinese-language materials? Must they be translated into Japanese?

**A:** They must all be translated into Japanese. All documents for Japanese government subsidy applications—the application materials, business plan, financial statements—must be in Japanese. Chinese materials not only can't be understood by reviewers, but will be rejected outright as incomplete. We recommend engaging a professional translator or an experienced Gyoseishoshi to handle this work. Note that the translation of financial data is especially important—translation errors in numbers or misunderstandings of accounting terms can both lead to the subsidy amount being adjusted. Also, if your company's original documents (such as a business license) are in Chinese, you need to submit a certified Japanese translation as well. Many Chinese business owners translate the materials themselves or use non-professional translators, resulting in materials being returned and, in effect, wasting the application opportunity.

### Q3: If my company posted a loss last year, can I still apply for a subsidy?

**A:** Most subsidies have no strict requirement that the company be profitable, but they will assess risk based on financial condition. For example, the Sustainability Subsidy for Small Businesses and the IT Adoption Subsidy are relatively friendly to loss-making companies, because the purpose of these subsidies is precisely to help companies improve their operations. On the other hand, if you are applying for the "Business Restructuring Subsidy," last year's loss is actually part of your reason for applying and may even raise your approval rate. But note that if the company has posted serious losses for three or more consecutive years and the financial statements show an inability to repay debts, the reviewing body will question the feasibility of your project and lower the approval probability. In addition, some subsidies require the company to provide a guarantee or restrict dividends during the post-application execution phase, which also puts pressure on loss-making companies. We recommend consulting a professional financial or subsidy advisor before applying; they can judge the feasibility of your application based on your specific situation.

### Q4: If I've applied for a subsidy but the project later needs to change, can I adjust the subsidized content?

**A:** Yes, but the process is relatively complex and time-consuming. Project changes after subsidy approval fall into two cases. First, minor changes (such as a change in the model of equipment purchased, with the price within ±10%)—these can be approved through a simple change application, usually completed in 1–2 weeks. Second, major changes (changes to the investment objective, construction content, or investment amount exceeding 20%)—these require resubmitting the application and obtaining re-approval from the responsible official, which may take 1–2 months. If the change involves an increase in the subsidy amount, it will be quite difficult; METI usually will not increase the originally approved amount. In the worst case, the change cannot be approved, and you must either execute according to the original plan or proactively withdraw the project application. Therefore, we recommend leaving 5–10% of flexibility during the project planning stage, and confirming details such as equipment selection with suppliers in advance to avoid later changes.

### Q5: After a subsidy is approved, how soon can I get the money? What do I need to do after receiving it?

**A:** A subsidy generally is not deposited immediately upon approval. The process is usually: after the subsidy grant decision notice is issued, you need to open a dedicated subsidy management account (many banks require that subsidy funds be used exclusively for their designated purpose), then submit the account information. The government usually pays the first installment (typically 30–50% of the total) within 1–2 months, with mid-term and later funds paid according to project progress. The entire cycle, from approval to final payment, generally takes 3–6 months. After receiving the money, the most important thing is to execute strictly according to the original plan: all equipment purchased and service contracts must match the application; all invoices and receipts must be kept; the construction or procurement process must be documented with photos or video; and after completion, an acceptance inspection is required, in which the government department or a designated body confirms the project's compliance. Only after the acceptance passes will the final payment be made. Any missing materials or discrepancies during this process will cause the subsidy to be frozen or even trigger a demand for repayment. We therefore recommend assigning a finance staff member to specifically manage the documents and accounts for the subsidized project.

## Strategic Recommendations for 2025 Subsidy Applications

### Timing Recommendations

2025 subsidy applications should be prepared 3–4 months in advance. If your goal is approval in the first half of the year, you should start planning your project proposal now (in January). Here are the key dates for the year:

- **Mid-January**: Detailed information on the various fiscal 2025 subsidies is released
- **February–March**: First-round application deadline
- **April–May**: Second-round application deadline
- **By end of May**: First-round approval results announced
- **July–August**: Third-round application deadline
- **October–November**: Fourth-round application deadline

### A Strategy of Applying for Multiple Programs Simultaneously

We do not recommend applying for only one subsidy program. Savvy business owners apply for 2–3 related programs at once, because some subsidies do not conflict. For instance, a manufacturing enterprise can apply for both the "Monozukuri Subsidy" and the "Carbon Neutrality Subsidy" at the same time, if the new equipment purchased both improves production efficiency and reduces carbon emissions. The benefit of doing this is a higher approval probability—even if one project is not approved, others may pass.

### Seeking Professional Support

The quality of a subsidy application directly affects the review outcome. If you are not confident enough in writing a Japanese-language business plan, we recommend engaging a Gyoseishoshi (行政書士) or a subsidy consultant. Although the fee is between JPY 200,000 and JPY 500,000, it is entirely worth it compared with the return of an approved subsidy. They can not only help optimize your application materials but also guide the reporting and acceptance during project implementation.
