## E-Book Retention Law: The Compliance Baseline Every Business Must Know

### What Is the E-Book Retention Law?

The **E-Book Retention Law (電子帳簿保存法)** is a regulatory upgrade implemented by Japan's National Tax Agency in January 2022. Its core provision is that **accounting records, invoices, receipts, and other tax-related documents** can be stored electronically, provided they meet specific technical and management standards.

Simply put, this regulation shifted from **strict controls → active promotion of digitalization**, further advancing the relaxed standards introduced in 2019. Under the new system, compliant enterprises may:

- ✅ **Achieve 100% electronic storage** (no longer required to print originals)
- ✅ **Benefit from reduced tax audit scrutiny** (the National Tax Agency lowers inspection priority for highly digitalized enterprises)
- ✅ **Apply for subsidies** (both central government and local municipalities offer adoption grants)

**Important compliance deadline checkpoints:**
- January 1, 2024–December 31, 2024: **Grace period** (non-compliance incurs no penalties, merely recorded)
- January 1, 2025 onwards: **Full enforcement** (non-compliant enterprises face cumulative tax and penalty risks)

According to National Tax Agency statistics, as of June 2024, only approximately **38% of SMEs nationwide** have completed e-book system deployment, meaning **nearly 62% of enterprises** remain in compliance risk. For Chinese business owners, compounded complexity from Japanese language requirements and technical demands means compliance rates are even lower—**this is precisely why the government has concentrated its subsidy efforts here**. The authorities aim to accelerate compliance among this segment before January 2025.

## Common Pitfalls and Scoring Optimization: How to Maximize Subsidy Approval Probability?

### Five Critical Traps Chinese Enterprises Must Avoid

**Pitfall 1: 「Choosing the cheapest system guarantees the highest subsidy」**
- ❌ Flawed Logic: Subsidy amount does not directly correlate with system price; rather, review committees score based on 「business plan innovation level」
- ✅ Correct Approach: Select **mid-range systems with advanced technology that align with national 「DX Promotion」 strategy** (e.g., systems with cloud backup and AI financial analytics)
- 💡 Scoring Bonus: If the system includes 「special features for micro-enterprises」 or 「environmental consideration features」, emphasize these in the business plan—you may earn evaluation bonuses

**Pitfall 2: 「Purchasing the system first, then applying for the subsidy, moves faster」**
- ❌ Critical Error: Subsidies **strictly require the sequence: apply → receive award decision → then purchase → then claim reimbursement**
- 💀 Risk: Systems purchased at your own expense before award decision receive no subsidy recognition; you absorb the entire cost
- ✅ Correct Approach: Before receiving award decision, **no expenses generate subsidizable claims**; only after award decision can you sign contracts, place orders, and make payments

**Pitfall 3: 「Chinese-language documents can be directly attached to application materials」**
- ❌ Fatal Flaw: All application documents, quotations, and technical specifications **must be in Japanese** (or translated by certified notary)
- ⚠️ Practical Impact for Chinese Enterprises: When procuring systems from China or negotiating with Chinese vendors, **demand Japanese-language contracts and invoices in advance**; otherwise the subsidy authority will not recognize them
- ✅ Solution: Retain local tax accountants or certified translation services (cost: 20,000–50,000 JPY, but essential insurance)

**Pitfall 4: 「Missing social security payments for one month is no big deal」**
- ❌ Automatic Disqualification: Subsidy application qualification screening's first step is 「past 3 years tax and social security payment check」
- 🔴 Data: Approximately 15–20% of subsidy applicants are directly eliminated on this basis; the rate for Chinese enterprises is even higher (approximately 25–30%)
- ✅ Recovery Option: If past delinquency has been resolved, immediately request from the tax authority a 「Delinquency Resolution Certificate」 and attach it to your subsidy application. Some authorities may exercise discretion. Probability: 30–40%, but not guaranteed. If denied, delay application 1–2 years until delinquency history clears

**Pitfall 5: 「One company can simultaneously apply for 10 subsidies and claim all of them」**
- ⚠️ Rule Trap: Multiple subsidies may have overlapping 「subsidizable expenses」; if you receive all simultaneously, **you must proportionally allocate, ensuring total subsidies never exceed a certain percentage of implementation costs (typically 75%)**
- ✅ Correct Strategy: **Simultaneous application for Category A + B is permissible, but both applications must explicitly disclose 「simultaneous application for other subsidies」**; if discovered later, duplicate subsidy portions must be refunded
- 💡 Scoring Strategy: Consult subsidy authorities in advance about compatibility between subsidy programs; obtain written confirmation to substantially reduce risk

## Subsidy Tax Treatment: Do You Owe Taxes on Subsidies Received?

### Common Tax Questions and Answers

**Q: Must enterprise subsidies be included in taxable income?**
- **A:** Yes. Japanese tax law stipulates that enterprise subsidies are principally classified as 「miscellaneous revenue」 and become part of the current period's taxable income base for corporate and business taxes. However, there is an important exception: **subsidies used for fixed asset investment can receive 「compressed accounting」 treatment, deferring taxation over a specified period (typically 5–10 years)**. Consult with your tax accountant regarding specific treatment approaches.

**Q: Can subsidy-related expenditures be deducted as business losses?**
- **A:** System purchase itself constitutes capital expenditure processed through depreciation, not direct one-time deduction. However, system implementation consulting fees, training costs, and data migration fees **can be treated as current-period expenses**, directly offsetting current-period profit (typically representing 15–25% of total subsidy amount).

**Q: If planned subsidy projects fail to materialize (e.g., system fails to go live), must subsidies be refunded?**
- **A:** Yes, full refund is required, with possible 10–20% penalty assessment. This underscores why the strict 「apply first, then purchase」 sequence is critical—once award decision is made, you assume legal obligation to complete implementation.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: My company experienced one month of social security payment delay last year due to cash flow constraints. Can I still apply for subsidies?

**A:** Unfortunately, this becomes a **direct disqualification condition**. Subsidy application qualification screening's first checkpoint is 「past 3 years tax and social security payment review」—the standards are **strict**. Even if the one-month delay was subsequently cured, the system still records 「has delinquency history」. **Recommendation**: If delinquency has been resolved, immediately request from the tax authority and pension office a 「Delinquency Resolution Certificate」 and attach it to your subsidy application. Some authorities may exercise discretion. Success probability: 30–40%, not guaranteed. If denied, defer application 1–2 years until delinquency history fully clears.

### Q2: If I select an e-book system appearing on the Small and Medium Enterprise Agency's recommended list, am I guaranteed to receive the subsidy?

**A:** **Not necessarily**. Inclusion on the recommended vendor list (designated vendor system) satisfies only a **necessary condition, not a sufficient condition** for subsidy eligibility. You must also: ① Meet company qualifications (annual sales, employee count, etc.) ② Score at or above minimum threshold on business plan evaluation (typically 50+ points) ③ Subsidy authority must have remaining budget for the current round. According to data, application approval rates for recommended systems average 65–75%, but lower-tier systems achieve only 30–40%. **Recommendation**: Select systems designated as priority recommendations by the subsidy authority in the current year (typically highlighted on official websites); this increases approval probability by 15–20 percentage points.

### Q3: I am self-employed with no corporate entity. Can I still apply for subsidies?

**A:** **Yes**, though with additional restrictions. The Small and Medium Enterprise Agency's IT Adoption Subsidy is available to self-employed individuals but requires: ① Annual sales 5+ million JPY ② 3+ employees (including family members) ③ No tax payment delinquency past 2 years. Local municipalities are typically more accommodating toward self-employed individuals—for example, Tokyo's SME Digital Transformation Support Grant has special provisions for self-employed individuals with 2+ million JPY annual sales and 1+ employee. **Recommendation**: First consult with local chambers of commerce or SME support centers regarding local subsidies; these typically present more realistic difficulty levels and reward amounts.

### Q4: A system vendor told me 「we can help you apply for subsidies; at the end, all subsidy funds will be returned to us」—is this legitimate?

**A:** **This is illegal intermediary activity**. Japanese law explicitly prohibits system vendors and customers from executing 「revenue-sharing」 or 「fund circulation」 agreements related to subsidies. If discovered, subsidy authorities will: ① Revoke subsidy award decision ② Demand refund of already-disbursed amounts plus 20% penalty ③ Impose administrative sanctions on both enterprise and vendor. **Correct Approach**: Subsidy applications operate entirely independently of procurement contracts. Your vendor contract specifies 「system purchase price X JPY」; subsidy application is 「separate government support」. The two must maintain completely separate accounting trails, with all documentation clearly auditable.

### Q5: My application was rejected. Can I reapply? How long must I wait?

**A:** **Yes, reapplication is possible**, with timing restrictions. Most subsidy programs typically conduct 2–4 recruitment rounds annually; rejected applications can be resubmitted in subsequent rounds. Key considerations: ① Revise business plan addressing previous review feedback (subsidy authorities typically provide written feedback on primary deficiency areas) ② If you modify system selection or budget scope, update quotations and technical specifications ③ Some subsidies limit consecutive rejections (three consecutive rejections by the same entity may result in 「blacklist」 status). According to data, **second-round applications achieve approval rates typically 10–15 percentage points higher than initial attempts**, because applicants better understand evaluation criteria. **Recommendation**: After receiving rejection notice, spend 2–3 weeks collaborating with tax accountants to thoroughly revise your business plan based on feedback.
