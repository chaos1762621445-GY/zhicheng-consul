# A Subsidy Application Guide for Chinese-Owned Trading and Logistics Companies in Japan

Chinese business owners running trading companies or logistics operations in Japan face increasingly fierce market competition and steadily rising operating costs. Yet many overlook an important opportunity — **the various subsidy and grant schemes offered by the Japanese national and local governments**. According to statistics from Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, in 2023 Japan disbursed over 1.2 trillion JPY in corporate subsidies, yet the application rate among most SMEs and foreign-owned businesses still stands below 30%.

This article provides Chinese-owned trading and logistics companies in Japan with a detailed application guide, covering the types of subsidies, application conditions, procedures, and points to watch for.

## Main Types of Subsidies Chinese-Owned Trading Companies Can Apply For

### Management Innovation Grant

The **Management Innovation Grant** is an important support scheme from Japan's Small and Medium Enterprise Agency, aimed at helping SMEs improve and innovate their operations. For trading companies, this subsidy is especially valuable.

Specifically, if your trading company plans to make innovative investments in any of the following areas, you can apply:

- **New product development or service innovation**: For example, shifting from traditional textile trading to high-value-added functional textile trading. On an investment of 3 million JPY, you can receive up to 1.5 million JPY (a subsidy rate of 50%).
- **Opening up new markets**: For example, planning to expand into Southeast Asian trade channels, which requires market research and building a new supply chain. On an investment of 6 million JPY, you can receive up to 3 million JPY.
- **Digitalizing business processes**: Upgrading your ERP system or introducing an EDI system for order automation. On an investment of 4 million JPY, you can receive a subsidy of 2 million JPY.

The key to applying for the Management Innovation Grant is preparing a "Management Innovation Plan" that demonstrates a clear three-year plan, financial projections, and innovation-outcome metrics. Chinese business owners often stumble at this stage because of differences in language or mode of expression, so we recommend engaging a professional consultant to assist.

### IT Adoption Subsidy

Logistics companies and trading firms rely heavily on information systems. The **IT Adoption Subsidy** subsidizes the cost of introducing information systems such as accounting software, inventory management systems, and WMS (warehouse management systems).

Subsidy amounts:
- Small businesses (5–20 employees): up to 1.5 million JPY
- SMEs (21–100 employees): up to 3 million JPY
- Subsidy rate: typically 3/4 of the actual investment

**Example**: Mr. Wang, originally from Shanghai, runs a trading company in Yokohama with 15 employees. He applied for the IT Adoption Subsidy to upgrade his inventory-tracking system. On a total investment of 2 million JPY, he ultimately received a subsidy of 1.5 million JPY, bearing only 500,000 JPY himself. From the second year onward, because inventory management efficiency improved by 30%, he saves about 1 million JPY per year in costs.

### Business Restructuring Grant (Jigyo Saikochiku Hojokin)

This is a flagship subsidy policy the Japanese government has introduced in recent years, especially suited to trading and logistics companies that need to transform their business model.

**Eligibility:**
- SMEs (whose capital or average monthly sales meet the conditions)
- When applying, the company must be undertaking restructuring activities such as business transformation, new product development, or opening a new business

**Subsidy amounts (2024):**
- Standard category (general): subsidy cap of 10 million JPY, subsidy rate 2/3
- Large-scale compensation category (for those severely affected by the pandemic): subsidy cap of 150 million JPY, subsidy rate 3/4

**Representative case**: A Fujian-owned company originally engaged mainly in textile import and export was hit by changes in international trade environmental regulations. It planned to transform into a supply-chain green-certification consulting provider, with a total investment of 50 million JPY. It applied for the Business Restructuring Grant and received a subsidy of 33 million JPY.

### Employment Expansion Grant (Employment Adjustment Grant)

Although this subsidy mainly targets employment-related investment, if you plan to expand your company, recruit logistics specialists, or hire management staff, you can apply.

**Subsidy conditions:**
- Number of new hires ≥ 3
- New employees are residents of Japan or eligible foreign residents
- The company must commit to maintaining stable employment throughout the subsidy period

Subsidy amount: about 600,000–900,000 JPY per new employee (varies by region and employee category).

## Special Channels for Logistics-Industry Grants

### Logistics DX Promotion Grant

The Japanese government pays particular attention to the digital transformation (DX) of the logistics industry. If you run a logistics company or a trading firm with an in-house logistics division, the following investment projects are eligible for the Logistics DX Promotion Grant:

**Eligible projects:**
- Purchase of automated sorting equipment (investment of 20–50 million JPY, subsidy rate 1/2–2/3)
- Development of transport-route optimization software (investment of 5–15 million JPY, subsidy rate 1/2)
- Electrification of delivery vehicles (investment of 8–20 million JPY, subsidy rate 1/3–1/2)
- Warehouse automation systems (high-rise racking, automated conveyor belts, etc.; investment of 30–80 million JPY, subsidy rate 1/2)

**Real case**: A Chinese-owned logistics company in Tokyo, with 3 warehouses and 80 employees, planned to invest 40 million JPY to build an automated sorting center. Through a Logistics DX Grant application, it received a subsidy of 24 million JPY, upgrading from manual sorting to semi-automation. Sorting efficiency rose by 45%, and staff could be reassigned to value-added service divisions.

### Local-Government Subsidies

Beyond national-level subsidies, local governments also offer subsidies aimed at businesses in their region:

- **Tokyo**: SME Management Capability Enhancement Grant, up to 6 million JPY
- **Kanagawa Prefecture**: SME Institutional Financing Interest Subsidy Program, subsidizing 50–100% of loan interest
- **Osaka Prefecture**: Commercial Innovation Support Subsidy, up to 5 million JPY
- **Fukuoka Prefecture**: New Product Development Support Subsidy, up to 3 million JPY

The application conditions for these local subsidies are often more flexible, and the restrictions on foreign-owned businesses and Chinese business owners are relatively fewer.

## The Complete Subsidy Application Process for Trading Companies

### Step 1: Self-Assessment and Project Selection (1–2 weeks)

Before formally applying, you need to clarify:
1. **Company status**: number of employees, annual revenue, current asset scale
2. **Problem diagnosis**: the main challenges you currently face (high costs, low efficiency, shrinking market, etc.)
3. **Direction of reform**: what you plan to invest in and the expected effect
4. **Financial capacity**: your own funds, and whether you can obtain financing support

**Recommended tool**: Prepare a "Company Status Analysis Sheet" listing key indicators such as revenue, costs, profit margin, main clients, and competitive weaknesses. This helps you quickly assess which subsidies best fit you.

### Step 2: Prepare the Required Documents (2–4 weeks)

Requirements differ by subsidy, but the basic documents include:

1. **Corporate registration certificate** (Certificate of All Recorded Matters)
2. **Financial statements for the past 3 years**: settlement reports, balance sheets, profit and loss statements
3. **Company overview**: company profile, organizational chart, employee roster
4. **Plan document**: business plan or management innovation plan (1,000–3,000 characters)
5. **Quotations/estimates**: quotation documents for the machinery, equipment, software, or consulting services to be invested in
6. **Financial projections**: sales forecast, cost-structure changes, and ROI calculation for the 3 years after implementation

Key point: financial projections must be realistic and credible. Many Chinese business owners tend to over-optimistic estimates (such as 50% sales growth), which often leads to rejection. We recommend basing your calculations on a growth rate of 15–25%.

### Step 3: Submit the Application (2–4 weeks)

Depending on the subsidy type, you can submit in the following ways:

- **Online system**: most Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry systems (such as the jGrants subsidy management system)
- **Paper application**: submit to a local industry promotion center or chamber of commerce
- **Local support organizations**: submit through a chamber of commerce or an SME Management Consultant

**Points to watch for:**
- Ensure all documents are complete and the Japanese translations are accurate
- If you have Chinese-language documents, provide official or notarized translations
- Submit at least 5 business days before the application deadline (in case of review issues)
- Keep proof of submission and timestamp records

### Step 4: The Review Period (4–12 weeks)

During the review, you may be asked to submit additional documents or attend an interview. For Chinese business owners, we recommend:

- Hiring a fluent Japanese-speaking agent to attend the interview
- Preparing a detailed business-plan presentation (5–10 minutes)
- Simulating in advance the questions reviewers might ask (cost control, market competition, risk management, etc.)
- Preparing supplementary materials such as on-site company photos, the office environment, and team photos

### Step 5: Grant Payment and Reporting (3–6 months)

Once approved, you usually need to:

1. **Sign the agreement**: confirm the subsidy conditions and obligations with the implementing agency
2. **Invest your own funds**: first make the investment using the company's own funds (generally you must front the cost)
3. **Collect invoices and payment proof**: keep all purchase receipts, invoices, and remittance records
4. **Report expenditures**: submit an expenditure report to the implementing agency, with originals or copies attached
5. **Grant payment**: after review and confirmation, the subsidy is usually transferred to the company account within 30–60 days
6. **Follow-up reporting**: you usually need to report on business results for 1–3 years after implementation

## Common Application Pitfalls for Chinese-Owned Trading Businesses in Japan, and How to Respond

### Pitfall 1: Overlooking the Quality of Japanese Documents

Many Chinese business owners think that as long as the content is good and the translation is accurate, that's enough — but reviewers focus heavily on the naturalness and professionalism of the Japanese. We recommend:
- Hiring a professional translator with an economics or trade background
- Using standard Japanese business terminology rather than literal translation
- Having a Japanese partner or advisor do a final review

### Pitfall 2: Plan Content That Is Too Grandiose or Unrealistic

Statements like "we will become the largest trading platform in Asia" arouse reviewers' suspicion. Instead, you should:
- Set specific, measurable goals (such as "achieve 20% sales growth and reduce costs by 15% within 3 years")
- List specific action steps and a timeline
- Provide market research data or competitor analysis as support

### Pitfall 3: Overlooking Local-Subsidy Opportunities

Many Chinese business owners focus only on national-level subsidies and overlook local-government ones. In fact, **local subsidies often have looser reviews, simplified application processes, and faster payment cycles**.

### Pitfall 4: Falsified or Non-Transparent Financial Statements

This is the most serious issue. Japan scrutinizes the authenticity of subsidy applications very strictly, and once false reporting is discovered:
- You must return the entire subsidy
- You may be added to a "subsidy fraud" blacklist
- It causes long-term damage to the company's credit rating

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: As a foreign-owned business, are there any restrictions on applying for Japanese subsidies?

**A:** Japan's subsidy policies place relatively few restrictions on foreign-owned businesses, but there are a few points to note:

First, the company must conduct substantive business activities in Japan. If you are merely registered in Japan but your actual business is overseas, you cannot apply. Second, the company must have a record of paying taxes in Japan, and you are usually required to submit tax returns from the past 2–3 years. Third, the legal representative of a foreign-owned business can be a foreigner, but you must provide proof of residence status and proof of tax payment.

For Chinese business owners, we recommend ensuring the following: the company has an independent office and staff in Japan, has a Japanese bank account and tax records, and its staff include at least some Japanese nationals or foreign personnel holding work visas. In most cases, none of these conditions pose a problem. If your company has operated in Japan for more than 3 years with a good tax record, the success rate of your subsidy application will improve significantly.

### Q2: I don't have enough of my own funds — can I borrow to invest and then apply for a subsidy?

**A:** Yes, but you need to follow a specific process. First, you need to be clear about which loans can be combined with subsidies. The Japanese government has introduced a "subsidy + financing" linked support policy, particularly through financing from the Japan Finance Corporation.

The concrete approach is: first apply to a financial institution for financing (usually at a low interest rate of 2–3%), and apply for the subsidy at the same time. Once both are approved, first make the investment using the financing, then use the subsidy to offset part of the financing cost. This can greatly reduce the company's actual burden.

For example, for a 5-million-JPY IT system: a 4-million-JPY loan (2.5% interest, 3-year term), 1 million JPY of your own funds, and a subsidy application of 1.5 million JPY. In the end, you used a 4-million-JPY loan and 1 million JPY of your own funds and received a subsidy of 1.5 million JPY — effectively lowering your out-of-pocket amount from 5 million JPY to 3.5 million JPY (including interest).

Note that the subsidy application must clearly state the financing amount and source; you cannot conceal it. If reviewers discover that you have misrepresented the source of your own funds, it may lead to rejection or clawback of the subsidy.

### Q3: How long does subsidy approval take? What is the typical timeline from application to receiving the money?

**A:** This depends on the type of subsidy. The overall timeline is as follows:

**Management Innovation Grant**: from application to final grant payment usually takes 6–9 months. Counting from the application deadline (usually a designated month), the review period is about 2–3 months; after approval you must submit an implementation plan; the investment execution period is 3–6 months; and the final expenditure report takes 1 month.

**IT Adoption Subsidy**: relatively short, about 4–6 months. Application period 2 weeks, review period 6–8 weeks, investment implementation period 6–8 weeks, expenditure report period 2–4 weeks.

**Business Restructuring Grant**: because it involves larger investment, it usually takes 8–12 months. Announcement and solicitation period about 1 month, review period 4–6 weeks, and after approval the subsidy implementation period is 6–12 months (depending on project scale).

**Important reminder**: Grant payment is usually not a lump sum. In most cases, you must front the project cost, then submit invoices and payment proof, and only receive the subsidy after review passes. In the entire process, the stages most prone to delay are "document preparation" and "expenditure report review." Therefore, we recommend leaving a buffer of 1–2 months beyond the officially published timeline.

### Q4: If my application is rejected, can I reapply? How long before I can apply again?

**A:** You can reapply, but you need to understand the reasons for rejection and make improvements. Common reasons for rejection include:

1. **The plan is not specific enough**: the solution is to rewrite the plan, adding more detailed market analysis, competitor analysis, and a 3-year financial projection (including specific sales figures and cost structure)
2. **Doubts about the financial statements**: ensure the settlement reports for the past 3 years are complete and accurate, and explain the reasons for any losses
3. **Insufficient project innovation**: confirm that the investment project has substantive innovation, avoiding a simple equipment upgrade
4. **Too low a proportion of your own funds**: some subsidies require a self-funded proportion of no less than 25–30%, so ensure you have sufficient own funds

Time limits on reapplication: **different subsidies have different rules, but you can usually reapply the following year or in the next solicitation period**. We recommend consulting an advisor or the implementing agency immediately after rejection to understand the specific direction for improvement. According to statistics, the success rate of reapplying after making improvements is about 60–70%.

I once met a business owner from Ningbo whose first application for the Management Innovation Grant was rejected because the plan content was too broad. She refocused on the specific project of "opening up the Southeast Asian market," added market research data, target customer analysis, and a detailed marketing plan, and succeeded the following year, receiving a subsidy of 3.5 million JPY.

### Q5: After receiving the subsidy, are there any ongoing obligations or risks?

**A:** Yes, and this is something many business owners easily overlook. The main obligations after receiving a subsidy include:

**1. Performance reporting obligation**: most subsidies require you to submit an annual results report for 1–3 years after implementation. The report covers sales data, cost-structure changes, employment status, and so on. Failing to submit or false reporting may lead to a demand to return the subsidy.

**2. Asset custody obligation**: equipment, systems, and other items purchased with the subsidy cannot be freely disposed of within a set period (usually 3–5 years). If you need to sell or transfer them, you must report to the implementing agency and may need to return part of the subsidy.

**3. Bookkeeping management**: you need to keep all original vouchers, invoices, contracts, and other subsidy-related records for 5–7 years for verification.

**4. Risk of unexpected audit**: the implementing agency may conduct spot checks after implementation (about 10–15% of companies are selected) to review whether you truly carried out the investment and business reform as declared.

**5. Tax impact**: in Japan, a subsidy is usually treated as company income and must be included in the corporate income tax calculation. However, if the subsidy is used for asset investment (such as equipment purchase), depreciation treatment may apply. We recommend consulting a tax advisor.

**Risk-prevention advice**: Set up a dedicated subsidy management folder recording all investments, expenditures, and results data. Hire an accountant or tax advisor to review it periodically. Ensure the company's actual operations remain consistent with the declared plan.
