# What to Do if You've Been Scammed by a Subsidy Agent? 5 Warning Signs to Identify Unscrupulous Service Providers

**If you've been scammed by a subsidy agent, the first step is not to accept defeat quietly—instead, immediately report the fraudulent application to the National Tax Agency, Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, or your prefectural SME Support Center. Preserve all contracts, payment records, and email correspondence as evidence. Consult the Legal Affairs Bureau's free legal advisory window to assess whether fraud has occurred, and if necessary, file a police report (110) or contact the Consumer Affairs Agency's Consumer Life Center (Hotline 188). Most importantly, the amount lost in a scam is often just the direct loss; the real trap is that the agent may have submitted false application materials on your behalf. If discovered later by the review authority, your company will be blacklisted for 3–5 years as a 「non-recipient entity,」 destroying your subsidy industry credibility. Therefore, within 72 hours of discovering the scam, you must proactively correct your application and clarify the truth to the subsidy administrator.**

## Why Are Chinese Business Owners in Japan Especially Vulnerable to Subsidy Agent Scams?

Japan's subsidy system does offer genuine support measures for small and medium enterprises and foreign-invested companies. However, because these opportunities are real, scams by dishonest agents are equally sophisticated. Chinese business owners face a unique dilemma:

**A double gap: language barriers and information asymmetry**. Japan's subsidy application information is primarily published on the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, and various municipal government websites. Documents are typically dense Japanese administrative terminology, and recruitment guidelines (募集要項) often span 30–50 pages. Even proficient Japanese speakers can easily misinterpret details. According to 2023 SME Agency statistics, Japan has approximately 1,500 subsidy projects annually, with total funding exceeding 200 billion JPY. However, the average SME subsidy approval rate is only 30–40%, and foreign enterprises face even lower rates of 15–25% due to stricter document review. This 「visible but unreachable」 situation creates fertile ground for dishonest agents to exploit.

**Three common scam tactics**:

1. **Service fee trap** (most common): Dishonest agents claim 「only pay the service fee of 50,000–150,000 JPY; once the subsidy is approved, you pay the final fee.」 In reality, they submit false documents or forge your seal. Once discovered by authorities, your company faces fines and blacklisting, while the agent has already disappeared.

2. **Advance payment scheme** (direct fraud): They demand upfront 「application review fees」 or 「document translation fees」 ranging from 30,000–300,000 JPY, promising refunds after approval. In reality, the materials are never submitted, and the phone line goes dead within a month.

3. **False guarantee scheme** (most deceptive): They exaggerate success rates (claiming 「95% success rate」 when the national average is 30–40%) or promise 「guaranteed approval within six months」 or 「full refund if rejected.」 Such commitments violate the Subsidy Proper Use Law. Once disputes arise, the law provides no recourse against dishonest agents, since subsidies are subject to government review authority and final government decision.

**Data warning**: According to a 2022 Consumer Affairs Agency report, consumer complaints involving subsidy service providers increased 87% year-over-year. The median defrauded amount is approximately 180,000 JPY, yet fewer than 30% of victims file police reports. Most business owners choose silence out of inconvenience or fear of exposure.

## Real Case: A Shanghai-Born Restaurant Owner's Subsidy Agent Scam Experience

Ms. Li is the founder of a restaurant group in Shinjuku, Tokyo. In late 2022, she wanted to apply for the "Monozukuri Subsidy" (Manufacturing Subsidy, maximum 11.5 million JPY) to upgrade her kitchen equipment. She contacted a 「subsidy application expert, Mr. Lee」 through a 「Japan business services provider」 on WeChat Moments.

**First warning sign**: The expert's self-introduction included no business license copy, only a blurry business card photo, claiming 「I don't want to expose my identity information to protect client privacy.」 Without overthinking, Ms. Li paid 150,000 JPY as the service fee.

**Second warning sign**: The expert requested very 「simplified」 materials—only company registration documents, three years of financial statements, and a 「business plan」 (essentially a two-page Word document). A normal Monozukuri Subsidy application requires 20–30 pages of detailed business plans, technical documentation, quotations, and organizational charts. The 「simplified version」 itself was a red flag.

**Third warning sign**: After application submission, when Ms. Li requested to review a copy of the submitted application, the expert refused, claiming 「government regulations prohibit disclosure during the application period」 (false: companies have the right to receive copies of their own application documents). Three months later, Ms. Li received an email from the SME Agency—**the application was rejected, citing 「application content inconsistent with facts, suspected false submission.」**

Only then did she realize the serious problem. Through a lawyer, she requested to review the original application documents from the government and discovered the hidden scheme: the 「equipment purchase cost budget」 in the application had been altered to double the amount, with three fraudulent equipment quotation forms attached—none of which Ms. Li had authorized.

**Resolution took four months and cost over 500,000 JPY**:

- Filed a police report at Narita Police Station (cost: free) → Police issued a case number, though actual arrest faced jurisdictional difficulties
- Consulted the Legal Affairs Bureau's legal advisor (free consultation; later litigation costs approximately 50,000–80,000 JPY)
- Submitted a correction notice to the SME Agency's false submission report department (free, but required English and Japanese documentation)
- Engaged a Gyoseishoshi to prepare authentic application materials and supplementary documents for reapplication (cost: approximately 120,000 JPY)
- Obtained an enterprise credit restoration confirmation certificate (free), ensuring non-blacklisting

Final outcome: Although Ms. Li eventually received the subsidy (approximately 8.8 million JPY, lower than the initial application because the budget had been inflated by the agent), the entire process took 15 months. During this period, her company was prohibited from applying for other subsidies. The estimated total loss exceeded 1.5 million JPY.

**The core lesson of this case**: The damage from dishonest agents is not limited to stolen service fees; more terrifying is that once false applications are discovered, the company's credibility collapses instantly. Future subsidy application eligibility is compromised for 3–5 years.

## Five Key Warning Signs to Identify Dishonest Subsidy Agents

### Warning Sign 1: Oversimplifying Application Processes, Promising 「the Fewer Documents, the Better」

**Standard subsidy application document volume**: Using Japan's two most common subsidies as examples—

| Subsidy Project | Required Document Pages | Submission Frequency | Difficulty Level |
|-----------------|----------------------|-------------------|------------------|
| Monozukuri Subsidy (Manufacturing) | 20–35 pages | 2–3 times yearly | High |
| IT Adoption Subsidy | 15–25 pages | 4 times yearly | Medium |
| Business Restructuring Subsidy | 25–40 pages | 2 times yearly | High |
| Small Business Continuity Subsidy | 8–12 pages | 3 times yearly | Low |

If any agent says 「I only need your 3 documents to complete everything,」 this is a **100% red flag**. Application form complexity is not bureaucratic inefficiency; rather, it ensures the subsidy genuinely supports the stated application purpose. Incomplete materials prevent government reviewers from verifying compliance, so they'll reject the application anyway. Dishonest agents simplify procedures because they're not conducting genuine applications.

**How to self-assess**:
- Log into the subsidy official website (e.g., Monozukuri Subsidy at www.monodzukuri-hojo.jp) and download that year's 「募集要項」 (recruitment guidelines)
- Navigate to the 「申請に必要な書類」 (Required Application Documents) section to review the official document checklist
- If the agent's requirements fall short of the official checklist by more than 20%, decline immediately

**Red-flag language from dishonest agents**:
- 「Don't worry, we have connections with the government; you don't need so many documents」
- 「Simplified versions actually have higher approval rates; too many documents invite nitpicking」
- 「We have specialized templates; you don't need to write much—just fill in the blanks」

### Warning Sign 2: Opaque Fee Structure, Demanding 「Payment First, Application Later」

**Standard fee structure for compliant service providers**:

The Japan Gyoseishoshi Association has established transparent fee standards. For subsidy application services, fees typically follow this structure:

| Fee Item | Standard Range | Description |
|----------|----------------|-------------|
| Basic application representation fee | 80,000–200,000 JPY | Depends on subsidy project complexity |
| Document translation fee (if needed) | 10,000–30,000 JPY | Calculated per page |
| Post-application consultation fee | 5,000–10,000 JPY/hour | Hourly billing |
| **Fee structures to avoid** | | |
| Advance application review fee | No legitimate justification | Non-standard industry practice |
| 「Refund after subsidy approval」 | Trap | Difficult to recover post-hoc |
| Hidden fees (e.g., 「translation rush fee」) | No transparent standard | Dishonest agents often add charges unexpectedly |

**Dangerous fee language**:
- 「Pay 100,000 JPY deposit now; pay remaining 150,000 JPY after approval」 (Problem: when does 「approval」 occur? The agent controls the definition)
- 「After the subsidy reaches your account, we'll deduct 20% as our service fee」 (Problem: subsidies are deposited into corporate accounts; the agent cannot directly deduct. This essentially demands you voluntarily transfer funds later, making recovery nearly impossible)
- 「Guaranteed approval; full refund if rejected」 (Problem: This promise violates the Subsidy Proper Use Law because government review is not a commercial contract subject to 「refund guarantees」)

**How to verify if a provider is legitimate**:
1. Request their Gyoseishoshi registration number (行政書士番号) and verify it through the Japan Federation of Bar Associations website: https://www.gyosei.or.jp/
2. Request a formal representation agreement (委任契約書), which must explicitly state: fee amount, payment method, payment timing, refund conditions, and confidentiality obligations
3. Request **copies of other successful cases' subsidy distribution notification letters** (with sensitive information redacted). Legitimate providers can ethically provide this

### Warning Sign 3: Exaggerating Success Rates, Claiming 「Guaranteed Approval」

This is the easiest lie to believe and the most lethal trap.

**Actual subsidy approval rate data (2023)**:

| Subsidy Project | Application Volume | Distribution Approvals | Approval Rate |
|-----------------|------------------|----------------------|---------------|
| Monozukuri Subsidy | ~8,000 applications | ~2,500 approved | **31%** |
| IT Adoption Subsidy | ~12,000 applications | ~4,200 approved | **35%** |
| Business Restructuring Subsidy | ~14,500 applications | ~3,700 approved | **25%** |
| Small Business Continuity Subsidy | ~25,000 applications | ~15,000 approved | **60%** |

Even the 「easiest」 Small Business Continuity Subsidy has only a 60% approval rate, meaning **even a perfect application has a 40% chance of rejection**. Any agent claiming 「90% success rate」 contradicts statistical reality. Moreover, Japanese government strictly protects applicant privacy; dishonest agents cannot provide verifiable proof of having 「successfully represented 500 companies.」

**Why dishonest agents make such claims**:
- They only work with 「easy-to-approve」 enterprises (those requiring minimal assistance), then take credit
- They never track application outcomes; after 「representation」 they disappear—if rejected, the business owner can't find them
- They target time-pressed entrepreneurs unwilling to investigate why their success rate exceeds official figures

**What legitimate consultants say**:
- 「According to official data, the Monozukuri Subsidy average approval rate is 31%. Our strategy is to write your application to industry-leading standards, improving competitiveness. However, I cannot guarantee approval」
- 「If your enterprise has strong conditions (competitive technology innovation, healthy finances), our clients achieve 45–50% approval rates based on historical data, but this is not a guarantee」

### Warning Sign 4: Refusing to Provide Contracts and Application Document Copies, Citing 「Confidentiality」

**Japanese law explicitly grants you the right to**:
1. Obtain a formal representation agreement (委任契約書) with your agent—legally mandatory; any refusal indicates a dishonest agent
2. Receive copies of submitted application documents—under the Information Disclosure Law (情報公開法), business owners can request copies of their own application documents from the SME Agency; this is both a right and **completely free**
3. Access all correspondence during subsidy review, including government supplementary inquiries (加算質問)

**Dishonest agent refusal language**:
- 「The review is ongoing; government regulations prohibit disclosure」 (False: You can directly request government records)
- 「Application documents contain trade secrets; we cannot show you」 (False: Agents have no authority to withhold your own documents)
- 「Contracts are complex; we'll use a WeChat agreement instead」 (Danger: WeChat agreements lack legal standing; no recourse if disputes arise)

**How to protect yourself**:
1. Before signing any representation agreement, demand a **formal representation agreement prepared by a licensed Gyoseishoshi**. Do not accept verbal agreements or WeChat screenshots.
2. The agreement must include:
   - Detailed fee breakdown and payment timing
   - Agent's scope of responsibility and liability limitations
   - Your right to obtain all application document copies
   - Dispute resolution venue (recommend your local district court)
3. After application submission, proactively submit an **information disclosure request** (情報公開請求) to your prefectural SME Support Center or SME Agency policy division to review your original application documents (cost: free; processing time: typically 2–4 weeks)

If an agent refuses to provide a contract or prevents you from accessing records, this constitutes fraud and should be reported to the Consumer Affairs Agency immediately.

### Warning Sign 5: Promising 「No Success, No Fee」 Refund Guarantees with Vague Details

A deceptively attractive promise that is actually the easiest trap.

**Why the promise itself is a trap**:

1. **Vague definition of 「success」**:
   - Dishonest agents might define 「success」 as 「government notified us the application was submitted」 (not success at all—just the process start)
   - True success means 「receipt of Subsidy Distribution Notification Letter」 (typically arrives after 6–8 months)
   - If your contract defines 「success」 by the agent's standards, you cannot win

2. **Refund legally unenforceable**:
   - If the agent says 「after subsidy approval, I'll help you transfer the refund from your bank」—this requires your voluntary action, and if you change your mind? No legal enforceability
   - If the agent says 「I'll directly deduct the service fee from the subsidy」—this violates the Subsidy Proper Use Law (subsidies are earmarked and cannot be diverted), so the agent cannot legally do this

3. **Common logic in fraud cases**:
   - Dishonest agent submits false application → Application rejected → They claim 「not our responsibility; your enterprise conditions were insufficient」 → Refuse refund

**Example problematic clause**:
```
「If the application fails to receive subsidy distribution approval,
we will refund the service fee.」
↓ Appears to protect you, but contains these loopholes:
- 「Fails」 might not be confirmed for 3+ years (some subsidies have appeals)
- Multiple supplementary inquiries may occur; agent claims 「client didn't cooperate」
- No refund timeline specified
```

**What legitimate providers promise**:

```
「Our fees comprise:
- Basic representation fee: 120,000 JPY (paid before application submission)
- Supplementary inquiry response fee: 5,000 JPY/hour (actual work-based)
- Final fee: 50,000 JPY (paid after receiving distribution notification)

If the application is rejected at final review (requiring official
government rejection notice), we will refund the 50,000 JPY final fee.
However, the 120,000 JPY basic fee is non-refundable
(representation work has been completed).」
```

Key: **Specific amounts, specific time nodes, specific conditions**.

## Truly Scammed? Five Essential Actions Within 72 Hours

If you discover your agent is dishonest or your subsidy application was rejected with suspected false content, this is an emergency requiring immediate action.

### Step 1: Cease All Communication with Dishonest Agent (Within 24 Hours)

**Immediate actions**:
- Refuse further payments or signing any new documents
- Preserve all correspondence (WeChat, email, transfer receipts—take screenshots)
- Contact your bank to report the account may be involved in fraud; ask if transaction records can be frozen (Japanese banks typically retain records 90 days)
- **Send a formal email in Japanese to the dishonest agent** clearly stating 「termination of representation agreement」 and demanding return of all documents and unused representation fees

**Why use Japanese**: If litigation becomes necessary later, a Japanese-language email carries greater evidentiary weight than WeChat.

**Email template** (have a lawyer or Gyoseishoshi help you refine):
```
件名：委任契約の終了について

[Dishonest Agent Name]様

本日、[Date]をもって、補助金申請代理業務に関する委任関係を
終了いたします。

以下について、[Number]日以内にご対応をお願いします：
1. 現在までの代理業務の進捗状況報告
2. 提出済みおよび未提出の申請書類の全部
3. 受領した代理費用[Amount]万円のうち、未使用分の返金

ご対応がない場合、消費者庁ならびに警察へ報告する予定です。

[Your Company Name]
[Representative Name]
```

### Step 2: Report False Application to Government Agencies (Within 24–48 Hours)

This step is critical because **self-correction carries far lighter consequences than later discovery**.

**Agencies to report and methods**:

| Agency | Contact Method | When to Report | Cost |
|--------|----------------|----------------|------|
| Subsidy Administrator (e.g., SME Agency) | 「お問い合わせ」 on each subsidy project's official website | Immediately after rejection | Free |
| Prefectural SME Support Center | Each prefecture's official website | Within 24 hours | Free |
| Police (Fraud Crime) | 110 phone or nearest police station | Within 48 hours | Free |
| Consumer Affairs Agency Consumer Life Center | Consumer Hotline 188 (24 hours) | Within 72 hours | Free |

**Specific procedures**:

**Report to subsidy administrator** (using Monozukuri Subsidy as example):
1. Log into Monozukuri Subsidy website (www.monodzukuri-hojo.jp)
2. Locate 「よくある質問」 (Frequently Asked Questions) or 「お問い合わせ」 (Inquiries)
3. Email or call the designated line stating:
   - Your application number (補助金申請ID)
   - 「I discovered false content in my application; I'm now submitting a correction notice (更正申請)」
   - Briefly describe the false content (「budget amount error」 or 「unauthorized document modification」)
   - Request 「correction notice acceptance certificate」 (この時点では、受理証明をいただきたい)

**Purpose of this step**: Create a record that you voluntarily corrected, rather than being caught. This is crucial for future enterprise credit restoration.

**File a police report** (obtain case number):
1. Call 110 or your nearest police station, stating 「詐欺と思われる事件を報告したい」 (I want to report a suspected fraud case)
2. Prepare:
   - Dishonest agent's name, phone, WeChat, email
   - Transfer amount and date
   - Any contracts or agreements (if available)
3. Police will issue a 「victim report acceptance number」 (被害届出受理番号). Keep this for later use with banks and courts

**File complaint with Consumer Affairs Agency** (formal report):
- Call Consumer Hotline 188 (free; Chinese-language service available; preferably in Japanese)
- After explaining, you can file a formal complaint with your local Consumer Center (消費者センター)
- Complaints are forwarded to police; if accumulated to a threshold, police may launch investigation against the dishonest agent

**Expected timeline and costs**:
- Reporting process: 1–2 weeks
- Cost: **Completely free**
- Resulting evidence: Police victim report acceptance certificate, agency report receipt confirmations from each department

### Step 3: Consult a Lawyer on Civil Compensation Options (Within Week 2)

Once confirmed as fraud, you have the right to pursue civil litigation for damages against the dishonest agent.

**Compensation scope includes**:
1. Direct losses: Service fees already paid (e.g., 150,000 JPY)
2. Indirect losses: Reapplication costs due to rejection from false submission (e.g., Gyoseishoshi re-representation fee 120,000 JPY)
3. Emotional damages: Business opportunity loss, enterprise reputation harm from fraud (typically 50,000–500,000 JPY in awards)

**Potential total claim: 300,000–800,000 JPY**.

**How to find reputable lawyers**:
1. **Japan Bar Association Federation website search**: https://www.nichibenren.or.jp/
   - Enter your prefecture and keywords 「詐欺」 「消費者問題」
   - Usually returns 5–10 law firms

2. **Legal Consultation Centers** (法律相談センター):
   - Nearly every prefecture has free or low-cost legal consultation windows
   - First 30-minute consultation typically free
   - Consultation fees: 0–5,000 JPY (varies by prefecture)

3. **Consult a Gyoseishoshi**:
   - Gyoseishoshi fees cheaper than lawyers (5,000–10,000 JPY/hour)
   - They specialize in 「subsidy-related disputes」
   - If litigation needed, they'll refer you to a lawyer

**Materials to prepare for first consultation**:
- All contracts/agreements with dishonest agent (WeChat screenshots acceptable)
- Bank transfer records (copy of account statements)
- Email correspondence screenshots
- Subsidy application rejection notification letter (with stated reasons)
- List of amounts paid

**Expected costs**:
- Initial consultation: 0–5,000 JPY (free or low-cost)
- Later litigation representation: **10–20% of recovery amount** (e.g., claiming 500,000 JPY recovery, lawyer fee approximately 50,000–100,000 JPY)
- Court filing fee: 400–1,000 JPY (very modest)

**Why consult by week 2**: Although civil litigation has no short statute of limitations, **evidence degrades over time**. The dishonest agent may destroy records or transfer assets. Earlier lawyer consultation means better evidence preservation.

### Step 4: Apply for Enterprise Credit Restoration (Weeks 3–4)

The most serious consequence: **After rejection, your company is blacklisted for 3–5 years as a 「non-recipient entity,」** meaning any future subsidy application is auto-rejected regardless of quality.

**Essential actions**:
1. Prepare a **correction/reapplication notice** (更正申請書 or 再申請書) with key points:
   - Explain the original application's false content
