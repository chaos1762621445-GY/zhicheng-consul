# How to Choose Between Subsidies and Loans? Policy Finance Combination Strategy for Chinese Business Owners in Japan

Chinese business owners in Japan facing expansion, equipment investment, or R&D often get stuck on a critical question: **Should I apply for a government subsidy, or take out a policy-backed loan? Can I use both?** This is not a small decision—choosing the wrong path could mean spending 6 additional months, paying an extra 500,000 yen in interest, or missing the application deadline entirely. This article benchmarks Japan's three major policy finance tools: **Japan Policy Finance Corporation loans (government-backed borrowing), MHLW and other government subsidies, and combination strategies**. Based on your company's size, cash flow situation, and financing urgency, we provide clear decision pathways. After reading this, you will be able to accurately determine which path fits you—and potentially save millions of yen in financing costs.

---

## Japan Policy Finance Corporation Loans: The Stable and Reliable 「Main Financing Channel」

### Core Parameters at a Glance

The Japan Policy Finance Corporation (日本政策金融公庫) is the primary government-backed financing channel for Japan's SMEs. For Chinese business owners in Japan, this is **the easiest option to get approved with relatively stable loan amounts**.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Single Loan Ceiling** | 20 million JPY (standard lending) / 48 million JPY (special lending) |
| **Interest Rate** | Approximately 0.46%–2.85% (2024 benchmark; subject to official announcement) |
| **Repayment Period** | Equipment loans typically 10–20 years; operating capital typically 5–10 years |
| **Collateral Requirements** | Generally requires a guarantor or collateral, but 「unsecured lending」 options available (ceiling 15 million JPY) |
| **Eligible Enterprises** | SMEs with 2,000 or fewer full-time employees; minimum 3 years of business track record in Japan |
| **Monthly Processing Volume** | Approximately 1,500 cases/month (national data) |

**Key Figures Explained**: The policy finance corporation's lending rate is typically 0.5–1.5 percentage points lower than commercial banks. If you need to borrow 10 million JPY at a 0.5% lower rate over 10 years, you save approximately 2.6 million JPY in total interest.

### Applicable Scenarios

**Policy Finance Corporation loans are ideal for the following situations:**

1. **Manufacturing/import-export businesses needing stable large-scale capital**—equipment purchases, factory expansion, inventory turnover. Loan amounts in the 5–20 million JPY range make the policy finance corporation the standard choice.

2. **Businesses established 3–5 years ago, still building credit history**—although commercial banks may overlook you, the policy finance corporation offers specialized programs like 「New Business Startup Loans」 and 「SME Management Strengthening Loans」 that are relatively favorable to young Chinese enterprises.

3. **Businesses needing cash urgently (timeline: within 2 months)**—Policy Finance Corporation review cycles run 2–4 weeks from application to actual disbursement, much faster than waiting for subsidies (which typically take 6–12 months).

4. **Import-export or retail businesses with large seasonal cash-flow fluctuations**—loans offer more flexibility than subsidies to bridge seasonal gaps.

### Advantages

- **Relatively high approval rates**: The corporation offers numerous financing programs for SMEs, with annual approval rates approximately 75–85% (subject to reviewing authority's decision). For Chinese businesses with 2–3 years of operating track record and clear accounting, approval rates typically exceed 70%.

- **Favorable interest rates**: Rates of 0.46–2.85% are far lower than commercial bank rates of 2.5–3.5%; long-term financing savings are significant.

- **Flexible repayment terms**: Equipment loans can extend 15–20 years, matching investment payoff cycles.

- **No specialized consulting required for basic applications**: Material checklists are clear; Chinese-language guidance is available online.

- **Compatible with subsidies**: Loans and subsidies can be applied for simultaneously on a first-come, first-served basis.

### Disadvantages and Hurdles

- **Requires 3+ years of Japanese business operating track record**—this is a hard requirement. Chinese startups in their first 2 years in Japan are essentially ineligible.

- **Collateral or guarantor usually required**: Although unsecured lending options exist, the ceiling is only 15 million JPY, and approval is stricter. Businesses with collateral see significantly faster approval and higher loan amounts.

- **Paperwork is complex in Japanese**: While Chinese-language guides are available, formal application documents are entirely in Japanese. Financial statements, trial balance sheets, business plans, etc. must all be written in standard Japanese—a common stumbling block for Chinese business owners that leads to requests for resubmission or delays.

- **Strict requirements for historical accounting records**: If prior-year accounting records contain significant gaps or tax filings are irregular, approval rates drop sharply. This is a common pitfall for Chinese enterprises—poor early accounting practices make later financing difficult or impossible.

## Government Subsidies: 「No-Repayment」 Funds, But Highly Competitive and Long Cycles

### Core Parameters at a Glance

Government subsidies are funds provided by central government ministries (METI, MHLW, etc.) and local governments that are **generally not required to be repaid**. For Chinese business owners in Japan, common subsidy programs include:

| Program Name | Maximum Subsidy | Subsidy Rate | Eligible Entities | Application Cycles |
|--------------|-----------------|--------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Small-Scale Business Sustainability Subsidy** | 2 million JPY | 2/3 | Businesses with 5 or fewer employees | Typically 2–3 times/year |
| **Monozukuri Subsidy** | 10 million JPY | 1/2–2/3 | Manufacturing / technology development | Typically 2 times/year |
| **IT Adoption Subsidy** | 4.5 million JPY | 1/2–3/4 | All industries (digitalization investment) | Typically 3–4 times/year (rolling application) |
| **Business Restructuring Subsidy** | Up to 150 million JPY | 1/2–3/4 | Business pivot / new product development | Irregular (typically 1–2 times/year) |
| **Local Government Subsidies** | 500,000–5 million JPY | Varies by municipality | Municipal eligibility varies | Irregular |

**Key Figures Explained**: The Small-Scale Business Sustainability Subsidy has a 2/3 subsidy rate, meaning if you invest 1 million JPY, the government covers approximately 667,000 JPY and you cover 333,000 JPY. Monozukuri Subsidies typically offer 1/2 or 2/3 rates, favoring technology investment enterprises.

According to the Small and Medium Enterprise Agency's public data, Monozukuri Subsidies approved approximately 8,000 cases in 2023 with a total subsidy distribution of approximately 110 billion JPY, averaging approximately 13.75 million JPY per case. However, **the ratio of applications to approvals is approximately 3:1**, meaning approval rates are approximately 33% (subject to reviewing authority's decision).

### Applicable Scenarios

**The following Chinese business owners are best suited for subsidy pathways:**

1. **Micro and startup enterprises (5 or fewer employees)**—the Small-Scale Business Sustainability Subsidy has the lowest threshold and is the first choice for Chinese entrepreneurs. While 2 million JPY may seem modest, it is sufficient for one small-scale expansion for service businesses like restaurants, retail, and consulting.

2. **Manufacturing, product development, and technology-innovation enterprises**—Monozukuri Subsidies are highly targeted, with ceilings up to 10 million JPY. If your enterprise involves manufacturing, product improvement, or process innovation, this subsidy offers exceptional ROI.

3. **Enterprises undergoing digital transformation**—IT Adoption Subsidies specifically support ERP, CRM, accounting software, and other system implementations, with ceilings up to 4.5 million JPY. For traditional Chinese trading companies transitioning to digital, this is an excellent opportunity.

4. **Businesses with clear project plans but limited cash**—if you know you need to invest 500,000–5 million JPY on a specific project (website redesign, equipment upgrade, new product prototyping) but cash flow is tight, subsidies excel at 「easing」 the burden.

5. **Projects with 6+ month anticipated cycles**—subsidy review and execution cycles are both lengthy, making them suitable for non-urgent projects.

### Advantages

- **「No repayment」**—this is the biggest advantage. If approved, the money is a government gift. Compared to financing interest, this is essentially free money.

- **Substantial amounts**: Single-project subsidies can reach hundreds of thousands to tens of millions of yen, often sufficient for one major investment for micro enterprises.

- **Generally accessible if eligible**: No 「3+ years operating track record」 hard requirements. Startups and newly-arrived Chinese business owners can apply for certain subsidies if they have business licenses and basic documentation.

- **Government endorsement boosts credibility**: Subsidy-approved enterprises gain higher credit ratings with banks and investment institutions, making subsequent financing easier.

- **Compatible with loans**: Subsidies and loans can be combined—subsidies reduce the self-funded portion while loans fill gaps. Ideally: Total investment 10 million JPY → Subsidy covers 6.5 million JPY → Financing 3.5 million JPY, significantly reducing personal cash pressure.

### Disadvantages and Hurdles

- **Extremely long cycles**—from application to approval typically requires 2–3 months; after approval, project execution takes 3–12 months; final reimbursement comes afterward. **The entire process typically requires 6–18 months**. If you need funds urgently, subsidies are not the solution.

- **Fierce competition, low approval rates**—average approval rates are only 30–40% (subject to reviewing authority's decision), with some hot programs dropping to 20%. Although Monozukuri Subsidies distribute 110 billion JPY, with 24,000 applications, competition is extremely fierce.

- **Detailed 「Business Plan」 submissions required**—subsidies are not simple form-filling; you must submit 5–20 pages of Japanese-language business plans, cost details, expected outcomes, etc. Many Chinese business owners give up at this stage due to language or planning constraints.

- **Strict post-approval audits**—after subsidy approval, government conducts project execution spot-checks (usually after completion). If cost inflation, unreported project changes, or timeline deviations are discovered, partial or full subsidy recovery is possible. Chinese enterprises often stumble here due to unfamiliarity with Japanese reporting standards.

- **Reimbursement-based, strains cash flow**—subsidies are not disbursed upfront; you advance funds, then claim reimbursement after completion. If cash flow is already tight, this process worsens pressure.

- **Funds are restricted to approved use**—approved subsidies can only be used for the specified project, not redirected elsewhere. For example, if you receive a 5 million JPY 「New Product Development Subsidy」, you cannot use it to supplement operating capital.

## Quick Comparison: Subsidies vs. Loans

| Dimension | Loans | Subsidies |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| **Approval Timeline** | 2–4 weeks | 6–18 months (including execution) |
| **Approval Difficulty** | Moderate (75–85% approval) | Difficult (30–40% approval) |
| **Financial Pressure** | Must repay on schedule + interest | No repayment, but advance funds then claim reimbursement |
| **Loan Flexibility** | Fixed (based on assessment) | Variable (depends on competition) |
| **Best Suited For** | Urgent capital needs, extended-timeline projects | Non-urgent, projects with clear innovation elements |
| **Preparation Difficulty** | Moderate (financial statements + simple plan) | High (detailed business plan + Japanese documentation) |
| **Risk Profile** | Default = debt collection | Non-compliant execution = subsidy recovery |
| **Ideal Users** | Businesses with stable cash flow, 3+ years operating track record | Startups, micro enterprises, innovation-focused businesses |

**One-sentence summary**: Loans mean 「I borrow now, repay later」; subsidies mean 「Government backs innovation, I advance funds, claim reimbursement after completion」. Loans are quick and stable; subsidies are larger but slower and harder.

## Combination Strategy: Maximizing Financial Efficiency Using Subsidies and Loans Together

Many Chinese business owners in Japan find the optimal solution is **subsidies + loans in combination**, not an either-or choice. The key is understanding the 「timing mismatch」 and 「financial complementarity」 between loans and subsidies.

### Golden Logic for Combination Use

Suppose you need 10 million JPY for a product upgrade project:

**Approach A: Loans Only**
- Borrow 10 million JPY @ 1.5% annual rate, 10-year term
- Total interest cost: Approximately 790,000 JPY
- Monthly payment: Approximately 104,000 JPY
- Personal cash pressure: Absorb 104,000 JPY fixed monthly expense

**Approach B: Subsidies + Loans (Recommended)**
- Apply for Monozukuri Subsidy, secure 6 million JPY subsidy (60% subsidy rate)
- Borrow 4 million JPY @ 1.5% annual rate, 10-year term
- Total interest cost: Approximately 320,000 JPY
- Monthly payment: Approximately 41,000 JPY
- **Interest savings: 470,000 JPY; monthly payment reduction: 63,000 JPY**
- Personal cash pressure: Absorb only 41,000 JPY fixed monthly expense

The tradeoff of this combination is: **spending an additional 6–12 months waiting for subsidy approval**. But if you can tolerate this timeline, interest savings often exceed opportunity costs from the wait.

### Practical Points for Combination Applications

1. **Timeline Planning**: Apply for subsidies first (6–12 month cycle), while simultaneously consulting with the Policy Finance Corporation about loan options (no need to rush the application). Once the subsidy is approved, use the approval letter when applying for financing—this significantly increases loan approval likelihood and interest rate discounts.

2. **Subsidies First, Loans Fill Gaps**: Subsidies typically do not cover 100% of costs. Use subsidies to cover primary investment costs (e.g., equipment), and financing to cover ancillary expenses (transportation, installation, testing). This maximizes subsidy usage efficiency while minimizing financing burden.

3. **Separate Account Management**: If both subsidies and loans are approved, **maintain separate accounts**. Subsidy funds in a dedicated account, loan funds in a separate account. This is critical for government audits later—commingling funds easily triggers non-compliance findings.

4. **Avoid Duplicate Applications**: You cannot apply for multiple subsidies for the same investment. If you are purchasing one piece of equipment, choose either Monozukuri Subsidy or IT Adoption Subsidy, not both. But you can apply for one subsidy + financing.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: I am a Chinese business owner who has been in Japan for 2 years with no prior subsidy or financing experience. Which should I choose first?

**A:** If your project is not extremely time-sensitive (not needed within 3 months), **prioritize subsidies**. Reasons: (1) Your business is young; the Policy Finance Corporation requires 「3+ years operating track record」, which you will meet in 1 year; (2) Younger enterprises receive more favorable subsidy policies—the Small-Scale Business Sustainability Subsidy has no age restrictions, and a 2 million JPY ceiling with 2/3 subsidy rate is sufficient for initial projects. Recommend applying for the Small-Scale Business Sustainability Subsidy first; even if unapproved, no loss incurs, and you can try financing after 1 year. But if the timeline is tight (need implementation within 2 months), go directly to financing since subsidy cycles are too long.

### Q2: My business has 6 years of operating track record with clean accounting. I want to expand the factory and need 5 million JPY. Which is more cost-effective: subsidy or financing?

**A:** **Recommend combination application**. First, apply for Monozukuri Subsidy or Business Restructuring Subsidy (if involving production line changes), aiming for approximately 3 million JPY subsidy (at 60% subsidy rate); simultaneously apply for 2–2.5 million JPY financing from the Policy Finance Corporation. This combination lets you secure 3 million JPY in 「free funds」 while keeping financing pressure to approximately 2.5 million JPY. At 1.5% financing rate over 10 years, the total cost is approximately 190,000 JPY. Pure 5 million JPY financing costs approximately 390,000 JPY; **the combination saves approximately 200,000 JPY in total interest**. The only cost is waiting 6–12 months for subsidy approval, which for factory expansion (inherently long-cycle) is acceptable.

### Q3: What exactly is the 「Business Plan」 for subsidy applications? I am not fluent in Japanese—can I hire a Chinese translator?

**A:** **Simple translation is not recommended**. The Business Plan is not ordinary document translation but a professional document demonstrating business capability and innovative thinking. Government review committees assess project feasibility, expected outcomes, and budget reasonableness based on this plan. Common Chinese business pitfalls: writing the plan in Chinese logic, then direct translation into Japanese, resulting in unclear logic and overly concise expression, leading to low review scores. **Better approach**: Hire a professional subsidy consulting agency (typically 500,000–1 million JPY in fees) to help you refine project logic and optimize presentation. Alternatively, consult your local chamber of commerce (商工会議所)—many offer free subsidy-writing guidance. The Small and Medium Enterprise Agency's official website also provides template samples for reference.

### Q4: I have already been approved for a subsidy and now need additional financing for other costs. Will the Policy Finance Corporation reject me because I have a subsidy?

**A:** **Not at all; it is actually a positive factor**. When the Policy Finance Corporation sees an applicant already received government subsidy approval, they view your project as officially vetted with relatively lower risk, making financing approval easier and sometimes offering better interest rates (possibly 0.2–0.5% lower). However, one critical requirement: subsidy and financing uses **must be separate**. You cannot use loan funds to supplement subsidy project shortfalls. If subsidies cover equipment, financing should cover complementary testing equipment or operating costs, not equipment cost gaps. The Policy Finance Corporation carefully examines fund usage during review; commingling causes financing rejection.

### Q5: My subsidy application was rejected. Can I immediately switch to financing? Will subsidy rejection affect financing approval?

**A:** **Subsidy rejection does not directly impact financing approval**. These are independent review systems. Subsidies are administered by METI or MHLW; financing is administered by the Policy Finance Corporation. However, there is indirect impact: if subsidy rejection cited 「unreasonable business plan」 or 「weak management foundation」, these assessments may be reconsidered during financing review. So do not rush into financing after subsidy rejection. **First, identify the rejection reason** (request feedback from the subsidy administrator), improve your business plan and financial data, then apply for financing. Applying 1–2 months after rejection typically yields better results.

### Q6: How long does subsidy reimbursement take? Could delays hurt my cash flow?

**A:** Subsidies follow **advance-first, reimburse-later cycles**. The entire process typically takes 3–6 months (subject to reviewing authority's decision). Steps are: (1) During project execution (3–12 months), you advance funds to suppliers/subcontractors; (2) After project completion (within 1 month), submit payment proof (invoices, receipts, remittance records) and final reports; (3) Government audit (1–2 months)—if documentation is complete and compliant, reimbursement is approved; (4) Reimbursement disbursement (2–4 weeks). **Maximum risk**: Incomplete materials or audited cost inflation trigger requests for resubmission or partial disapproval, delaying reimbursement 1–3 months. **Recommended measures**: Begin preparing complete payment proofs during project execution; save every invoice and remittance record; do not scramble after completion. For cash-flow-tight enterprises, **simultaneously secure financing as a buffer** to cover advance-funding gaps; once subsidies arrive, use them to repay the financing principal portion.

- **Cost Comparison**: 10 million JPY financing incurs approximately 790,000 JPY total interest (1.5%, 10-year term); 4 million JPY financing incurs only approximately 320,000 JPY. Saving 470,000 JPY.

**Key point**: The Policy Finance Corporation views 「already-subsidized-business」 favorably, offering easier approval and sometimes better rates. Financing first, then subsidy-waiting ensures survival even if subsidies fail. This is the lowest-risk dual-track approach. **Total timeline**: Financing ready in 2 months, subsidy arrives 6–12 months, overall project spans 8–18 months.

### Situation 3: Startup (Less Than 2 Years) + Urgent Financing (Need Money Within 2 Months) + Tight Cash Flow

**Recommended: Financing First, Subsidies as Backup**

Specific suggestions:
- **Step One**: Consult the Policy Finance Corporation about 「New Business Startup Loans」 or 「SME Management Strengthening Loans」 (more favorable to new enterprises). If you have Japanese domestic collateral or a guarantor, approval rates for 10 million JPY loans are approximately 70%; unsecured lending (ceiling 15 million JPY) requires strict review but remains easier than commercial banks.
- **Step Two**: Once financing is disbursed (arrives within 1 month), launch your project. Simultaneously, during the 3–6 month project execution, prepare subsidy applications. Many subsidies have no hard age requirements (e.g., IT Adoption Subsidy, Small-Scale Business Sustainability Subsidy)—just prove project reasonableness.
- **Step Three**: Once subsidies are approved (expected 9–12 months later), use them to supplement other project costs or directly repay financing principal.

**Risk Management**: New enterprises applying for financing face review of personal credit, household assets, and prior industry experience. If these are weak, consider consulting local 信用金庫 (credit unions) or chamber-recommended financing—sometimes terms are more lenient. **Total timeline**: Financing ready 1 month, subsidies arrive 9–12 months later. Minimal cash-flow pressure overall.
