# A Complete Subsidy Application Guide for Chinese-Run Clinics, Dental Offices, and TCM Practices in Japan

In recent years, more and more Chinese nationals have opened medical facilities in Japan, including clinics, dental (齿科) offices, and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) practices. Yet many operators are unaware of the various subsidy programs the Japanese government offers to medical facilities. In fact, from equipment purchases and renovations to operational support, there are many subsidy opportunities. This article walks you through the subsidy application paths for Chinese-run medical facilities in Japan in detail.

## The Basics of Subsidies for Medical Facilities in Japan

### The Three Main Sources of Subsidies for Medical Facilities in Japan

Clinic subsidies in Japan come mainly from three channels: **national-level subsidies**, **prefectural subsidies**, and **municipal subsidies**.

National-level subsidies are usually led by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW), with larger amounts and relatively fierce competition. For example, the Medical Facility Development Subsidy (iryo shisetsu seibi hojokin) can subsidize 30%–50% of the cost of building or renovating a clinic, with a single amount reaching tens of millions of yen.

Prefectural-level subsidies vary by region. Tokyo, for instance, periodically rolls out a "Medical Institution Management Support Project" that provides subsidies for the operational difficulties of small and medium-sized medical facilities. Such subsidies are usually in the range of JPY 1 million–5 million and are relatively easy to apply for.

Municipal-level subsidies are the most flexible. Many ward or town offices set up special subsidies to attract medical resources. Areas with large Chinese populations, such as Shinjuku Ward, Shibuya Ward, and Itabashi Ward, have all rolled out startup subsidies for newly opened clinics.

### The Difference Between Subsidies and Loans

Many medical operators confuse subsidies (grants) with loans. A subsidy is non-repayable support from the government and does not need to be paid back. A loan, on the other hand, must be repaid on schedule each month, with interest usually of 1%–3%. For a clinic with a large upfront investment, applying for both together often works best—use a subsidy to cover 40% of the initial cost and a low-interest loan to make up the remaining 60%.

## The Specific Categories of Clinic Subsidies

### Medical Facility Development Subsidy (iryo shisetsu seibi hojokin)

This is the largest national-level subsidy, used mainly for the construction, renovation, and expansion of medical facilities and for the purchase of medical equipment.

**Scope:** Opening a new clinic, expanding the scope of medical departments, purchasing high-end equipment such as CT/MRI.

**Subsidy rate:** Usually 1/3 to 1/2 of the eligible expenses (対象経費の1/3～1/2).

**Application requirements:**
- The opening physician must hold a Japanese medical license (ishi menkyo).
- The clinic must meet the building standards of the Medical Care Act (iryoho).
- Submit detailed architectural drawings, an equipment list, and a budget table.
- It must pass the review of an incorporated administrative agency under the MHLW.

**Application process:**
1. Preparation stage (1 month): gather relevant documents, consult an architectural design firm.
2. Written application stage (1 month): submit the application to the prefectural Regional Bureau of Health and Welfare.
3. Review stage (2–3 months): the ministry convenes an expert panel.
4. Decision notice stage: receive the subsidy decision notice.
5. Construction stage (3–6 months): begin construction and keep all vouchers.
6. Performance report stage (1 month): after completion, submit the settlement report and copies of invoices.

**Amount reference:** For clinic renovation costs of JPY 5 million, you can apply for a subsidy of JPY 1.5–2.5 million.

### Clinic Establishment Support Project (shinryojo kaisetsu shien jigyo)

A subsidy specifically for newly opened clinics. Although the amount is smaller than the facility development subsidy, the review requirements are relatively lenient.

**Eligibility:** New clinics open for no more than 3 years, with the physician being a full-time doctor.

**Subsidy amount:** Usually JPY 500,000–3 million, varying by region and department.

**Required documents:**
- A copy of the medical license (ishi menkyosho)
- A pledge (seiyakusho, promising not to close the clinic during the subsidy period)
- A business plan (eigyo keikakusho, including a 3-year financial projection)
- A building lease agreement or proof of ownership (tatemono chintaishaku keiyakusho or proof of title)
- A profit-and-loss statement (sonshu keisansho, financial statements for the past 3 months)

**Application deadline:** Most regions accept applications from March to May each year; confirm the deadline in advance.

## The Distinctive Features of Dental (Shika) Subsidies

### The Unique Advantages of Dental Grants

Compared with general clinics, dental offices have more specialized subsidy mechanisms. This is because of Japan's public-health policy promoting the "8020 Campaign" (keeping 20 natural teeth at age 80).

**Main subsidy programs:**

**1. Dental Treatment Unit Purchase Subsidy (chiryoyo unit konyu hojo)**
- A high-end dental treatment chair costs about JPY 2–4 million.
- Subsidy rate: 30%–50%.
- Applying body: a local dental medical association or the prefecture.

**2. Digitalization Equipment Subsidy (digital-ka setsubi hojo)**
- Includes intraoral scanners, CAD/CAM systems, digital X-ray devices, etc.
- Single subsidy: JPY 1 million–2 million.
- In recent years, Tokyo and Osaka Prefecture have both increased the budget for this type of subsidy.

**3. In-Home Dental Care Support Subsidy (zaitaku shika shinryo shien hojo)**
- Available to clinics that provide door-to-door dental services to patients.
- The subsidy covers travel costs and the cost of medical supplies for house calls.
- Monthly subsidy: JPY 100,000–300,000.

### Common Pitfalls in Applying for Dental Grants

Many Japanese dental clinics overlook one important condition when applying: you cannot make the purchase before applying for the subsidy. Even if you have already signed a purchase contract, paying in advance before the formal subsidy decision is issued will cause the application to be rejected.

Recommended process: first obtain the subsidy decision notice (hojo kettei tsuchisho), then sign the purchase contract with the equipment supplier. This way, you can offset the purchase cost directly with the subsidy.

## The Current State of and Opportunities for TCM Practice Subsidies

### The Legal Positioning of TCM Practices

TCM practices occupy a relatively special position among medical facilities in Japan. Strictly speaking, only personnel holding a Japanese medical license (ishi menkyo) or an acupuncture license (shinkyushi menkyo) may lawfully engage in medical practice. Therefore, many TCM practices register under the name of an "acupuncture clinic" (shinkyuin).

**Lawful TCM practice operating models:**
1. Hire employees who hold an acupuncture license (shinkyushi menkyo).
2. Apply for an acupuncture clinic business license (shinkyu shinryojo eigyo kyoka).
3. Provide traditional therapies such as acupuncture, cupping, and gua sha.

### Subsidy Paths for Acupuncture Clinics

Although acupuncture clinics are usually small in scale, they also have opportunities to apply for related subsidies.

**Applicable subsidies:**

**1. Traditional Medicine Equipment Purchase Subsidy (dento iryo setsubi konyu hojo)**
- Includes moxibustion devices, cupping tools, chiropractic beds, etc.
- Subsidy amount: JPY 300,000–1 million.
- Applying body: a local TCM association or a traditional-medicine promotion organization.

**2. Human Resource Development Subsidy (jinzai ikusei hojo)**
- If you hire trainees or interns, you can apply for employee training expenses.
- Subsidy amount: JPY 50,000–150,000 per month.
- Application deadline: in principle, apply before hiring.

**3. Applicability of the Clinic Establishment Subsidy (shinryojo kaisetsu hojo no tekiyo kanosei)**
- Some regions include acupuncture clinics within the scope of the clinic establishment subsidy.
- A newly opened acupuncture clinic can apply for JPY 500,000–1.5 million.

**A common difficulty:** Because of restrictions on medical qualifications, TCM practice operators often meet with rejection when applying for subsidies. The solution is to establish a partnership with a licensed acupuncturist (shinkyushi), with the acupuncturist as the subject of the subsidy application and the practice as the operator.

## Special Considerations for Chinese-Run Medical Facilities Applying for Subsidies

### Overcoming Language and Cultural Barriers

The biggest barrier for Chinese medical operators in Japan applying for subsidies is document preparation and administrative communication. Japanese subsidy application documents often use rigorous wording and complex phrasing, and many Chinese-language expressions are hard to convey accurately in Japanese.

**Translated documents that must be prepared:**
- Certified Japanese translations of degree certificates and professional qualification certificates (prepared by a notary public / koshonin).
- Japanese translation and certification of financial statements.
- A Japanese-language version of the business plan (direct machine translation is not recommended; it should be revised by a professional translator or consultant).

**Recommended approach:** Engage a Gyoseishoshi (administrative scrivener) or business consultant familiar with Japan's medical subsidy system to assist with the application. Although you will need to pay a consulting fee of JPY 15,000–30,000, it can significantly raise the subsidy approval rate.

### The Application Advantages of Chinese-Run Clinics in Japan

Conversely, Chinese-run medical facilities also have unique competitive advantages:

**1. Filling a medical gap:** Japan has a phenomenon among Chinese patients of "language barrier → delayed care → worsening condition." Clinics that provide bilingual Chinese-Japanese medical services are seen as filling a social need, and some regions award extra points for this.

**2. International medical cooperation:** If a clinic plans to establish academic exchange with Chinese medical institutions, or to train medical talent for both China and Japan, it can apply for the "International Medical Cooperation Promotion Project" (kokusai iryo kyoryoku suishin jigyo), with a subsidy usually of JPY 2 million–5 million.

**3. Regional revitalization subsidy:** Chinese-run medical facilities that open a clinic in a depopulating area can apply for the local government's "Regional Revitalization Evangelist Project" (chiiki kasseika dendoshi jigyo), with a subsidy rate that can even reach 75%–100%.

### Communication Strategy With Local Governments

Before applying for a subsidy, it is advisable to proactively visit the Medical Promotion Section or Industrial Promotion Section (iryo suishin ka or sangyo shinko ka) of the municipal office in the target area and consult directly about subsidy policy. The benefits of doing so are:

- Understanding the latest subsidy trends and timelines.
- Obtaining "informal guidance" from that department's staff, raising the application approval rate.
- Building a relationship with the local government, creating conveniences for future operations.

Example: A Chinese doctor who opened a TCM clinic in Kanagawa Prefecture, by consulting the town office in advance, discovered that the town happened to be promoting a "Senior Citizens' Health Maintenance Project" (koreisha kenko iji jigyo). Their acupuncture clinic met the conditions, and they ultimately obtained a subsidy of JPY 1.5 million.

## The Application Process and Document Preparation Checklist

### Standard Application Process (Using the Clinic Establishment Support Project as an Example)

**Phase 1: Information gathering (Weeks 1–2)**
- Visit the MHLW website to look up the latest subsidy policy.
- Log in to the prefectural Regional Bureau of Health and Welfare website to download the application guide and forms.
- Consult a Gyoseishoshi or a certified consultant.

**Phase 2: Document preparation (Weeks 3–4)**
- Compile a business plan (eigyo keikakusho), including:
  - Clinic overview (departments, number of beds, medical staffing)
  - Analysis of patient demand (why this area needs this clinic)
  - 3-year financial projection (monthly income-and-expense plan)
  - 5-year business goals
- Organize identity documents such as the medical license and graduation certificate.
- Prepare the lease agreement or real-estate title certificate.
- Obtain the architectural drawing confirmation (issued by an architect).

**Phase 3: Submitting the application (Week 5)**
- Convert all documents to electronic form (usually PDF).
- Log in to the application system (many regions have switched to online applications).
- Or deliver the paper documents to the designated department.
- Keep the application confirmation.

**Phase 4: Review period (Weeks 6–12)**
- Await the assessment by the subsidy review committee.
- You may receive a request for supplementary materials (tsuika shiryo teishutsu irai).
- Respond promptly, or the application will be deemed abandoned.

**Phase 5: Decision and implementation (After Week 13)**
- Receive the subsidy decision notice (hojo kettei tsuchisho).
- Sign the subsidy grant application form (hojokin kofu shinseisho).
- Begin construction or procurement (keep all invoices and construction certificates).
- Within 3 months, submit the report on the performance of eligible subsidy expenses (hojo taisho keihi jisseki hokokusho).
- Receive the subsidy amount (usually by bank transfer).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: I am a Chinese doctor with practice experience in China but no Japanese medical license. Can I apply for a clinic subsidy?

**A:** Not directly. All clinic subsidies in Japan require the applicant to hold a Japanese medical license (ishi menkyo). This is a hard legal requirement with no exemptions. However, you have the following options:

1. **Obtain a Japanese medical license:** You need to sit the Japanese National Medical Examination (ishi kokka shiken); before applying, you must complete 1–2 years of clinical training at a Japanese medical training institution. The pass rate is about 60%.

2. **Partnership model:** Partner with a doctor who holds a Japanese medical license, applying for the subsidy in the partner doctor's name, with you participating in management as a co-operator or deputy director. This model requires signing a detailed partnership agreement and clearly reflecting the division of responsibilities in the subsidy application.

3. **Switch to the acupuncturist route:** If you received acupuncture training in China, you can consider applying for an acupuncturist qualification (shinkyushi, which requires sitting the Japanese unified examination), then opening an acupuncture clinic. This route is relatively easy, and although the subsidy amounts are small, the review is simple.

### Q2: My clinic subsidy application was rejected, with the reason being "採算性が低い" (low profitability). What should I do?

**A:** This is a very common reason for rejection. The original intent of government subsidies is to support medical facilities with genuine operating prospects. If your business plan (eigyo keikakusho) shows that profitability is hard to achieve within 3 years, the review department will consider your clinic too risky. Response strategies:

1. **Revise the business plan:** Re-analyze your patient sources. If your clinic has special advantages (such as bilingual medical services or strong specialty capabilities), argue in detail why patients will choose your clinic. Provide a competitor analysis to demonstrate your differentiated advantages.

2. **Lower the upfront investment:** If the planned renovation cost is too high, consider ways to reduce costs, such as simplifying the design or purchasing used equipment. This way, even if the subsidy amount stays the same, your return on investment will be higher and the application easier to approve.

3. **Extend the payback period:** Some subsidies do not have a hard "profitable within 3 years" requirement, changing it to "achieve sustainable operations within 5 years." If your region has such a subsidy, you can switch to applying for it.

4. **Reapply:** Wait for the next application period (usually the same time the following year); by then you may have 3–6 months of trial-operation data. Replacing projections with actual patient and revenue data substantially raises credibility.

### Q3: In dental subsidy applications, why do some subsidies require "no procurement before the subsidy"?

**A:** This involves the "no prior commencement principle" (chakushu kinshi no gensoku) in subsidy administration. To prevent fraud and financial waste, the government requires that subsidy applications be made before the subsidy decision is formally issued. The specific logic is:

1. **Preventing false claims:** If procurement before application were allowed, applicants might overstate the amount (for example, actually buying JPY 100,000 of equipment but claiming JPY 500,000).

2. **Ensuring funds are earmarked for a specific purpose:** The subsidy must be used for the specific project stipulated in the subsidy decision and cannot be diverted to other uses.

**Correct process:**
1. Submit the subsidy application (there must be no procurement vouchers).
2. Await the subsidy decision notice (generally 2–3 months).
3. After receiving the notice, sign the procurement contract with the supplier.
4. Procure and keep the invoices and acceptance documents.
5. Submit the full set of vouchers when claiming reimbursement.

**Exceptions:** Some emergency subsidies or local government subsidies may allow "prior commencement approval" (jizen chakushu shonin), but this requires a separate application. It is advisable to always confirm the specific requirements of the subsidy before procurement.

### Q4: My incorporated medical clinic applied for a subsidy, but I now want to close the clinic due to the pandemic or business difficulties. Does the subsidy need to be repaid?

**A:** Yes. Most subsidies have a "repayment obligation" (henno gimu) clause requiring the applicant to continue operating the clinic for a specific period after the subsidy is approved. Violating this condition requires full or partial repayment of the subsidy. The specific requirements vary by subsidy type:

1. **Clinic Establishment Support Project:** Usually requires continued operation for 3–5 years after receiving the subsidy. Closing the clinic during this period requires repayment of the entire subsidy plus interest (an annual rate of about 2%).

2. **Medical Facility Development Subsidy:** The closure period is longer, usually 5–10 years, and the repayment amount is calculated based on "residual value" (zanzon kachi). For example, if you close in the 3rd year, you may only need to repay 60% of the subsidy.

3. **Regional revitalization subsidy:** The repayment-obligation period is the longest, reaching up to 10 years. Given the large policy support invested by the local government, the requirements for the company's long-term commitment are the strictest.

**Response strategies:**
- Before applying, be sure to conduct thorough market research to ensure the ability to operate continuously for 3–5 years.
- Draw up detailed articles of incorporation for the medical corporation (iryo hojin teikan), clearly defining ownership and the management structure.
- If you encounter business difficulties, first consult the subsidy administration department to see whether there is an option for a "temporary suspension" (ichiji kyushi) rather than closing outright.
- Keep all relevant financial documents to prepare for a possible review.

### Q5: When Chinese-run medical facilities in Japan apply for subsidies, are they treated differently because of "foreign national status"?

**A:** Not legally. Japan's subsidy system places no explicit restriction on the applicant's nationality; as long as you hold a valid medical license or relevant qualification and meet the subsidy conditions, you have the right to apply. However, in practice there are indeed some hidden barriers:

1. **Document quality issues:** Because of language differences, documents submitted by Chinese applicants are sometimes not rigorous enough, making them prone to requests for supplementary materials or outright rejection. The countermeasure is to hire a professional Japanese-language writing consultant.

2. **Lagging access to information:** Some subsidy information is only published on Japanese-language official websites, and Chinese operators are prone to missing the application window. It is advisable to join a medical association or dental association to obtain industry information in a timely manner.

3. **Low communication efficiency:** If you need to communicate with departments during the application process, language barriers may cause misunderstandings. Hiring a Chinese-speaking assistant or a Gyoseishoshi can greatly improve this.

**Key recommendations:**
- Actively apply to join the local medical association or dental association to obtain information through official channels.
- Establish direct contact with the relevant departments of the prefectural Regional Bureau of Health and Welfare.
- Regularly attend government-hosted subsidy information sessions (usually in Japanese and English, with simultaneous Chinese interpretation in some cities).
- Exchange experiences with Japanese clinics that have successfully obtained subsidies, and learn from them.
