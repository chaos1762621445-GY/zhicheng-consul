# A Complete Guide to Japanese Subsidy Application Agency Fees: Success-Fee Model vs. Fixed-Fee Model

To the Chinese business owners running companies in Japan: have you ever received a recommendation about government subsidies? Many people hear they can obtain several million yen in subsidies, yet are completely confused about the question "how are agency fees charged?" Today, I'll explain the fee structure of subsidy application agencies in the plainest terms, especially what the increasingly popular "success-fee model" actually means.

## Three Mainstream Fee Models in the Subsidy Agency Market

### Model 1: Fixed-Fee Model (定額制)

The fixed-fee model is the most traditional and most transparent way of charging. The agency charges the company an agreed fixed amount, and the fee must be paid regardless of whether the subsidy is ultimately obtained.

The specific characteristics of this model are:
- **Fee range**: usually JPY 500,000–1.5 million, depending on the complexity of the subsidy project
- **Payment timing**: generally split into two stages—a 30–50% deposit is paid at signing, and the balance is paid after the subsidy is approved
- **Suitable scenario**: for companies confident in their own qualifications and with a relatively clear-cut subsidy application

Here's a real example: a Chinese restaurant chain applied for the "Business Reconstruction Subsidy," and the agency charged JPY 1 million. The company had to pay JPY 500,000 at signing and another JPY 500,000 after receiving the subsidy approval letter. Even if no subsidy was ultimately obtained, this JPY 1 million fee would not be refunded.

### Model 2: Success-Fee Model (成功報酬型)

The success-fee model is an innovative model that has gradually become popular in recent years, and it's also the approach many Chinese business owners care about most.

**Core principle**: the company only needs to pay the agency fee after actually obtaining the subsidy. This model greatly reduces the company's risk.

**Specific fee structure**:
- **Fee ratio**: usually 8%–15% of the obtained subsidy amount
- **Payment timing**: after the subsidy is credited, the corresponding percentage is deducted from the company's account
- **Minimum fee**: some agencies set a minimum charge (such as from JPY 500,000), to prevent overly small subsidy projects from being unprofitable

**A real scenario**: a Chinese manufacturing company applied for the "Monozukuri Subsidy" (manufacturing subsidy), with an application amount of JPY 5 million. It ultimately received a subsidy approval of JPY 4 million.

- Under a 12% success-fee model: fee = JPY 4 million × 12% = JPY 480,000
- The company actually pockets: JPY 4 million − JPY 480,000 = JPY 3.52 million

### Model 3: Hybrid Model (ハイブリッド型)

This is a compromise between the two, and an increasing number of professional subsidy agencies adopt it.

**Structure**:
- The company pays a lower deposit (such as JPY 200,000–300,000)
- Plus a success fee after the subsidy is obtained (usually 5–8%)

**Advantage**: the agency's deposit covers basic operating costs, while the company's risk is relatively controllable. This is currently the relatively balanced approach in the industry.

## An In-Depth Analysis of the Success-Fee Model

### Why Choose the Success-Fee Model?

For Chinese businesses applying for a subsidy for the first time, the success-fee model has significant advantages:

1. **Zero-risk application**: if the subsidy is not approved, the company doesn't need to pay the agency fee. This is especially friendly for cash-strapped SMEs.

2. **More reasonable cost**: although a 12% fee rate looks relatively high, compared with obtaining a JPY 4 million subsidy, the JPY 480,000 fee is only 12% of the total, and this money is deducted directly from the subsidy, without extra strain on the company's cash flow.

3. **Aligned incentives**: the agency's income depends directly on whether it can help the company successfully obtain the subsidy, which motivates them to prepare the application materials and proposal more diligently.

4. **Suitable for larger subsidy projects**: for large subsidies like the Business Reconstruction Subsidy (subsidy amount JPY 5 million–15 million) and the Monozukuri Subsidy (JPY 1 million–10 million), the success-fee model is the most cost-effective.

### The Hidden Risks of the Success-Fee Model

However, Chinese business owners also need to be wary of the potential issues with this model:

**Risk 1: An opaque fee ratio**

Some agencies may promise 10% at signing, but after the subsidy comes through, add fees under names such as "tax handling" or "remittance handling charges." We recommend clearly stating in the contract: "The agency fee is X% of the subsidy amount and does not include any other charges."

**Risk 2: An "over-inflated" subsidy proposal**

To increase the chances of approval, some agencies may exaggerate the application amount. For example, a company that actually needs JPY 3 million to renovate a factory is advised by the agency to apply for JPY 5 million. In the end, even if it's approved, the company also has to pay a larger success fee.

**Risk 3: The after-sales issue when it's not approved**

Ask clearly: if it's not approved, will the agency attempt a free re-application? How many attempts are included within the scope of service?

## An Analysis of Factors Affecting Agency Fees

### 1. The Complexity of the Subsidy Project

The difficulty of handling different subsidy projects varies completely.

| Project type | Difficulty level | Fixed-fee range | Success-fee rate |
|---------|--------|-----------|---------|
| Small Business Sustainability Subsidy | ⭐ Low | 300,000–500,000 | 5–8% |
| IT Adoption Subsidy | ⭐⭐ Medium | 500,000–800,000 | 8–12% |
| Business Reconstruction Subsidy | ⭐⭐⭐ High | 800,000–1.5 million | 12–15% |
| R&D-type Subsidy | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Very high | 1.5 million–2.5 million | 15–20% |

**An illustration**: the Small Business Sustainability Subsidy has relatively simple requirements—mainly submitting financial statements and a business plan—so the agency fee is lower. The Business Reconstruction Subsidy, by contrast, requires in-depth analysis of the market, competitors, and financial projections, and may even require communication with financial institutions, so the agency fee is naturally higher.

### 2. The Company's Own Circumstances

A company's credit standing, financial condition, and past record all affect the agency fee:

- **High-quality companies** (high credit rating, sound finances, no violation record): agencies are more willing to take them on at a lower fee, because the approval rate is high
- **Risky companies** (with tax issues, large financial fluctuations, a complicated application history): the agency fee will rise by 10–20%, or they may simply decline to take the case

### 3. The Agency's Qualifications and Experience

- **Top-tier subsidy specialist firms**: usually higher fees (success fee 12–15%), but a success rate of 70–80%
- **Mid-sized firms**: moderate fees (8–12%), success rate 50–70%
- **Small or emerging agencies**: lower fees (5–8%), but a success rate that may be below 50%

## How to Identify a Reasonable Agency Fee?

### Three Key Questions

**Question 1: What is the ratio of the total fee to the subsidy amount?**

Generally speaking, the total cost of the entire subsidy application (including agency fees, expert consulting fees, material fees, etc.) should not exceed 15% of the subsidy amount.

For example, when applying for a JPY 3 million subsidy, keeping the total cost within JPY 450,000 is reasonable.

**Question 2: Are the fee terms in the contract fully transparent?**

A professional agency will clearly state in the contract:
- "The agency fee is X% of the approved subsidy amount"
- "This fee includes: proposal design, application drafting, subsidy management, report preparation"
- "Fees not included: the company's internal material organization, external expert consulting fees (payable separately if needed)"

**Question 3: Is there a plan for when approval fails?**

Ask the agency:
- If it's not approved the first time, will they file a second application for free?
- How many re-application opportunities are there?
- Is an extra fee required?

Reason: reduce initial risk while gaining professional guidance. If this attempt fails, you'll have accumulated experience and may apply yourself next time or find a more suitable agency.

Reason: your approval probability is already high, so paying a fixed fee may actually be cheaper than the fee model. For example, for a JPY 10 million subsidy, the success-fee model would require JPY 1.2 million, but the fixed fee might only be JPY 800,000–1 million.

**Budget suggestion**: prepare JPY 800,000–1.5 million depending on project complexity

**Recommendation**: negotiate a hybrid model or a lower fee rate

Reason: you're a major client, so the agency should give you a discount. You can negotiate for "JPY 100,000 deposit + 10% success fee on the subsidy" or "JPY 500,000 deposit + 8% success fee on the subsidy."

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: Can the subsidy agency fee be deducted from the subsidy, or does the company have to bear it itself?

**A:** This is the most frequently asked question. Under Japan's subsidy rules, subsidy usage is clearly restricted and must be used for the stipulated project content (such as buying equipment, conducting research, etc.). The agency fee usually cannot be deducted directly from the subsidy and must be borne by the company itself. **However**, if the success-fee model is used, many agencies will automatically deduct the service fee from the company's account after the subsidy is credited, so the company effectively uses part of the subsidy to pay the fee, and the actual cash-flow pressure is relatively small. We strongly recommend clearly agreeing on the payment method and timing in the contract to avoid later disputes. We recommend that companies, when applying for a subsidy, set aside a fee margin of 10–15% in their financial plan in advance, to cover the agency and other related costs.

### Q2: If the subsidy application is ultimately rejected, can the agency fee already paid be refunded?

**A:** This depends on the chosen fee model. **Fixed-fee model**: generally not refundable, because the agency has already put in the work cost (even without approval). The contract usually states that "the application result is independent of fee payment." **Success-fee model**: no fee needs to be paid at all—this is the core advantage of this model. **Hybrid model**: the deposit is generally not refundable (because the agency has already done the initial work), but no success fee needs to be paid. If you're worried about this before signing, we recommend choosing the success-fee model or specifically negotiating a clause in the contract for "one free re-application if not approved once." Some professional agencies proactively offer this service as a competitive advantage.

### Q3: Does the subsidy agency fee require an invoice, and can it be used for company tax purposes?

**A:** Absolutely. The subsidy agency fee falls under the company's "expert opinion fee" (専門家等の意見) or "consulting fee" (コンサルティング費) and is a tax-deductible expense. The agency should provide the company with a formal tax invoice (インボイス). This fee can be deducted under the "payment handling fee" (支払手数料) or "expert remuneration" (専門家報酬) account when filing corporate income tax, reducing the company's tax burden. **Important note**: obtaining the subsidy itself usually does not require income tax (as it's a government subsidy), but paying the agency fee is a normal business expense for the company and can be deducted for tax purposes as usual. We recommend confirming before engaging whether the agency is a "qualified invoice issuer" (適格請求書発行事業者), so you can fully utilize this expenditure in your tax filing.

### Q4: The agency says "guaranteed approval" or "99% approval rate"—is this true? Does such a promise affect the fee?

**A:** **Be wary of such promises.** Although Japanese subsidy applications have review standards, absolutely no agency can "guarantee approval." Even the best company with the most perfect proposal faces the risk of rejection. Any agency claiming "guaranteed approval" is either exaggerating or implying non-compliant practices. Figures like a "99% approval rate" should also be questioned—this may be filtered data (only taking on projects they believe have a high approval rate) and doesn't represent the overall level. As for the fee impact: some agencies charge higher fees on the back of such false promises. We recommend choosing professional firms that honestly tell you "our average approval rate is 60–70%, and our strength is strong proposal-design capability." Such firms are actually more trustworthy, and their fees are often more reasonable.

### Q5: I already made a preliminary application myself but was rejected, and now I want to find an agency to reapply. In this case, will the agency fee be more expensive?

**A:** This is a very practical question that many Chinese business owners have faced. **Frankly, it will be more complex.** First, you need to clarify the reason for the last rejection—was it because "the project content didn't meet the subsidy requirements" or because "the proposal design and application quality weren't good enough"? If it's the former, reapplying may require adjusting the project content itself, which means more work and a correspondingly higher fee. If it's the latter, the agency may be able to help you repackage the proposal at a lower fee. We recommend, at the first consultation, **proactively providing the agency with the reason for the last rejection** (which you can request from the application department). An honest agency will assess based on this: (1) whether reapplication is worthwhile; (2) how much additional work is needed; (3) how much the fee should be. Some agencies may say "this rejection reason is fairly serious; we recommend redesigning and reapplying in the next fiscal year," rather than reluctantly taking the case just to earn a fee. Such advice actually shows they are responsible. Generally speaking, the agency fee for "reapplying after rejection" will be 20–30% higher than the first time, because it requires cause analysis and a change of direction.
