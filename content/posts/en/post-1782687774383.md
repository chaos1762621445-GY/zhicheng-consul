# A Complete Analysis of Subsidy Applications for Chinese-Owned Construction & Renovation Companies in Japan

Have you, as a Chinese business owner running a construction or renovation company in Japan, ever been troubled by high operating costs and tax burdens? In fact, the Japanese government provides far richer subsidy and grant programs for the construction industry than most imagine. These support policies are not limited to Japanese companies—eligible Chinese-owned businesses in Japan can apply as well. This article offers a detailed analysis of the key points, channels, and precautions for construction-industry subsidy applications, helping you effectively lower costs and strengthen your competitiveness.

## Core Categories of Construction-Industry Subsidies and Their Policy Background

### An Overview of Japan's Construction-Industry Grant System

Japan's construction-industry subsidy and grant programs are primarily rolled out jointly by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW), the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI), the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT), and various local governments. Depending on the applicant, the nature of the project, and the target policy, these funding sources can be divided into the following categories:

**Employment-related grants**: These are the subsidy funds most easily obtained by construction companies. If your company hires new employees, implements an employee training plan, or improves the working environment, the government provides subsidies of up to 2 million–5 million yen. For example, if you hire a person who has left a job or been unemployed long-term, you can apply for the "Trial Employment Incentive (トライアル雇用奨励金)," obtaining a subsidy of 8,500 yen per month for up to 3 months.

**Skill-improvement subsidies**: The construction industry faces a serious labor shortage. If you invest in launching employee skill-training projects—such as certification training for electrical work, carpentry, tower-crane operation, and other professional qualifications—you can apply for the "Human Resource Development Support Course (人材開発支援コース)." The government covers up to 75% of the training costs, with subsidy amounts typically ranging from 500,000 to 3 million yen.

**Equipment investment and digital transformation subsidies**: If you introduce new technologies such as BIM (Building Information Modeling) software, project management systems, or drone surveying, or purchase new construction machinery, you can apply for the "Monozukuri Subsidy (ものづくり補助金/Manufacturing Subsidy)" or the "IT Adoption Subsidy (IT導入補助金)," with a subsidy rate of roughly 50–75%.

**Vocational training subsidies**: For improving the vocational skills of practitioners, institutions such as the Construction Industry Training Association offer subsidies, covering the full chain from basic safety education to advanced skill certificates.

### Why Chinese Business Owners Should Pay More Attention to These Policies

Many Chinese business owners in Japan believe that subsidy applications are cumbersome and have a low success rate, but this is a misconception. In reality, the construction industry, as a pillar industry of Japan, receives priority government support. At the same time, many local governments, in order to encourage diverse business development, are in fact more welcoming toward eligible foreign entrepreneurs. The key is to understand the logic of the application and prepare thorough documentation.

## Subsidy Eligibility and Conditions for Chinese-Owned Construction & Renovation Companies

### Legal Status and Registration Requirements

The foremost condition for applying for a construction-industry grant is holding a legitimate business status. Chinese residents in Japan can apply in the following ways:

**Sole proprietor (個人事業主)**: If you are already registered with the tax office as an independent contractor and hold a construction-business license (建築業許可), you are eligible to apply. The construction-business license is issued by MLIT and is divided into two tiers: "general construction-business license (一般建設業許可)" and "specified construction-business license (特定建設業許可)." Applications require documents such as a business plan, skill certificates, and financial statements.

**Limited company or kabushiki kaisha (株式会社)**: A Chinese-owned enterprise that has registered as a Japanese corporation, has a record with the Japanese tax office, and regularly files income tax is eligible to apply. Importantly, the company must have paid social insurance on time (employees' pension/厚生年金 and health insurance/健康保険), which is a prerequisite for most subsidies.

**Holders of a work visa in Japan**: Chinese nationals holding a "Business Manager (経営・管理)" or "Highly Skilled Professional (高度専門職)" visa can independently found a company and apply for subsidies. However, they must provide a clear business plan and proof of funding sources.

### Financial and Compliance Conditions

Applying for a subsidy requires meeting the following financial standards:

- **Financial statements for the past 3 months**: Bank statements, income vouchers, invoices, and other documents must be complete and consistent with the data in your tax filings
- **Social-insurance payment records**: At least 3–6 consecutive months of employment insurance and employees' pension payments
- **No record of legal violations**: No record of unpaid taxes, labor disputes, or concealment of industrial accidents within the past 2 years
- **Completeness of labor contracts**: If applying for an employment-related subsidy, employees must sign formal labor contracts clearly specifying working hours, compensation, benefits, and other terms

Mr. Li, a Chinese owner running a renovation company in Kanagawa Prefecture, successfully obtained an 800,000-yen "Trial Employment Incentive" precisely because he had consistently paid social insurance and taxes on time for 3 years. In contrast, another owner, Ms. Wang, had her application rejected outright because she had failed to pay social insurance for 3 months the previous year (citing cash-flow difficulties). This case demonstrates that compliant operation is not only a legal requirement but also an important foundation for obtaining subsidies.

## A Detailed Look at Major Subsidy Programs and Their Application Processes

### "Human Resource Development Support Course (人材開発支援コース)"

**Subsidy content and amount**:

This is a dedicated subsidy for training the employees of construction companies. If you enroll employees in government-approved construction skill-training courses (such as education for specified-work personnel, special education for scaffold assembly, sling-work skill training, etc.), the government can subsidize up to 75% of the training costs.

**Specific case**:
A renovation company enrolls 5 welders in the "Arc Welding Skill Course (アーク溶接技能講習)," with a course fee of 120,000 yen per person, totaling 600,000 yen. If approved, the company can receive a subsidy of 450,000 yen and only needs to pay 150,000 yen itself.

**Application steps**:

1. Visit the website of your local labor bureau (Hello Work/ハローワーク) and download the "Human Resource Development Support Course" application form
2. Prepare materials: company business license, employee roster, labor contracts, and the training institution's course syllabus and cost estimate
3. Submit the application to the labor bureau; you must apply 30 days before the training begins
4. Wait for approval (about 2–3 weeks); after approval, pay the training fees
5. After the course is completed, apply to the labor bureau for subsidy payment with the training-completion certificate, invoices, and proof of attendance
6. Receive the subsidy within about 4–6 weeks, usually transferred to your designated bank account

**Application timing**: Labor departments generally accept applications year-round, but the approval cycle fluctuates seasonally. Spring (March–April) and autumn (September–October) are application peaks, and approval may be prolonged. We recommend preparing 3 months in advance.

### "Trial Employment Incentive (トライアル雇用奨励金)"

**Subsidy content and amount**:

This program encourages companies to try out the unemployed, those who have left jobs, or the long-term unemployed. During the 3-month trial period, the government provides a subsidy of 8,500 yen per month for each trial employee, for a total of 3 months, amounting to 25,500 yen.

**Who can benefit**:
- Newly hired workers who have left previous jobs
- The long-term unemployed (unemployed for 6 months or more)
- Mid-to-older job seekers aged 45 and above
- Persons with physical or mental disabilities

**Application process**:

1. Learn about eligible job seekers from your local Hello Work (ハローワーク/employment agency)
2. Reach a trial agreement with the job seeker and sign a 3-month trial contract
3. Within 10 days of the trial starting, submit the "Trial Employment Incentive (トライアル雇用奨励金)" application form to Hello Work
4. At the end of each month, submit the "Payment Application (支給申請書)," attaching the employee's attendance record and a copy of the pay slip
5. Conduct the final settlement after the 3-month trial period ends

**Success rate and precautions**:

According to MHLW data, the approval rate for this subsidy is roughly 75–80%, making it the easiest of all construction-industry subsidies to obtain. However, note:

- Trial employees must be enrolled in employment insurance; wages cannot be settled in cash
- Working hours must comply with labor laws (no forced overtime)
- If the company violates labor laws during the trial period, it may be disqualified from the subsidy

### "Monozukuri Subsidy (ものづくり補助金/Manufacturing Subsidy)"

**Scope of application**:

Although the name contains "manufacturing," construction companies can apply as well. As long as your investment involves the introduction of new processes, technologies, or equipment, you are eligible. This includes:

- Purchase and deployment of construction-design and construction-management software
- Advanced surveying equipment such as 3D printing, drones, and laser measurement
- Production equipment for prefabricated building components
- New technologies for construction-waste processing

**Subsidy amount and rate**:
- The subsidy cap is 10 million yen
- The subsidy rate is 50% for SMEs and 33% for large enterprises (Chinese-owned companies in Japan are usually treated as SMEs)
- The total investment for the applied project usually needs to be 1 million yen or more

**Case scenario**:
A renovation company in Tokyo invests 3 million yen to introduce BIM modeling software and accompanying training, and can apply for a subsidy of 1.5 million yen while self-funding 1.5 million yen. This subsidy usually arrives within 2–3 months after the project passes completion inspection.

**Application threshold and cycle**:

The application threshold for the "Monozukuri Subsidy" is relatively high, because you need to submit a detailed business plan explaining how the technological innovation will strengthen competitiveness, its expected benefits, and so on. The approval cycle is about 4–6 months, and applications are usually divided into two rounds: spring–summer and autumn–winter. As of 2024, the approval rate is roughly 40–50%.

We recommend engaging a professional subsidy consultant to assist with the application. The fee is usually 5–10% of the subsidy amount, but it significantly raises the success rate.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: I am an individual, independent renovation contractor with no employees. Which subsidies can I apply for?

**A:** Although many subsidies target companies and their employees, independent contractors still have opportunities to apply. First, if you regularly purchase tools and equipment and attend skill-training courses, you can apply for the "Monozukuri Subsidy" or a local government's "Business Owner Support Subsidy (事業主支援補助金)." Second, many local governments (such as Tokyo and Osaka) offer subsidies specifically for individual entrepreneurs and micro-businesses, with amounts typically ranging from 500,000 to 2 million yen. Third, if you plan to expand your workforce, you can apply for the relevant subsidy in advance. Finally, some industry associations, such as the Japan Federation of Construction Contractors and regional construction-industry associations, also provide subsidy information and application support to members. We recommend consulting your local commerce and industry association or Chamber of Commerce and Industry, which usually provide free guidance on subsidy applications.

### Q2: What level of Japanese is needed to apply for a subsidy? Can I apply in Chinese or get translation help?

**A:** Official application forms and documents must be filled out in Japanese, but this is not high-difficulty Japanese—it is mainly standard business Japanese and form fields. Many Chinese business owners can independently complete a basic application form after 3–6 months of study. If your Japanese is genuinely difficult, you can engage a Japanese subsidy-application support company (補助金申請サポート企業), with fees usually 5–15% of the subsidy amount. Such firms are familiar with all the procedures and can greatly increase the success rate and save time. Note that subsidy application forms do not accept Chinese- or English-language versions; a translation can only serve as a reference attachment, not as the main application document. Chinese chambers of commerce or Chinese-business associations in many areas also provide free application consultation and assistance.

### Q3: If my company had losses or slim profits last year, can I still apply for a subsidy?

**A:** Yes. Subsidy applications generally do not treat profit level as a mandatory condition; they place more weight on the company's legitimate operating status and social-insurance payment record. In fact, many growth-stage construction startups, precisely because of heavy investment and low profits, are the main beneficiaries of subsidies. The key conditions are: (1) social insurance is paid completely and correctly; (2) there are no unpaid taxes; (3) the subsidy program applied for matches the company's business. For example, a renovation company with only 2 million yen in turnover last year but a complete social-insurance payment record can absolutely apply for skill-training subsidies or equipment-investment subsidies. But if the company has a record of unpaid taxes or interrupted social-insurance payments, the application will be rejected. We recommend confirming with a tax accountant or social-insurance advisor before applying whether your financial situation is compliant.

### Q4: After receiving a subsidy, do I need to pay tax on it? Will it affect my tax filing?

**A:** Construction-industry subsidies are generally not treated as taxable income under Japanese tax law. In other words, the subsidy (grant) you receive generally does not need to be counted as income in your income-tax filing. But there is an important detail here: the **specific expenditure items** that the subsidy covers still need to be accounted for as prescribed. For example, if the subsidy is used for employee training, the training fee itself, as a company expense, is tax-deductible; if it is used for equipment purchases, the equipment value is expensed year by year according to depreciation rules. It is not that the subsidy itself is taxed, but that the related expenditures must still be correctly reflected in the financial statements. We recommend keeping the subsidy-related documents (application form, approval letter, payment notice) together with your financial statements, to make explanations easier during a tax review. If you have questions, you should consult a Japanese zeirishi, who can provide accurate guidance for your specific situation.

### Q5: Can I apply for multiple subsidies at the same time? Is there a cap or a risk of conflict?

**A:** In principle you can apply for multiple subsidies at the same time, but you must observe the "no double subsidy" rule. In short, the same expenditure (such as employee training fees) cannot receive subsidies from two subsidies at once; but expenditures of a different nature (such as training fees + equipment fees) can be applied for separately under different subsidies. For example, you can apply for both the "Human Resource Development Support Course" (training subsidy) and the "Monozukuri Subsidy" (equipment subsidy), because their expenditure items differ. But if both subsidies are meant to subsidize the same training project, that violates the rule and will result in disqualification. In addition, subsidies from different government departments can usually be stacked (such as a national subsidy + a local-government subsidy), but you must clearly state this in the application form and avoid concealment. We recommend preparing a "subsidy application overview table" listing all the programs, amounts, and expenditure categories you intend to apply for, and having a consultant or tax accountant review it before submission to ensure there is no duplication or violation.

## Application Process and Time Planning

### The Complete Application Timeline

To successfully obtain a construction-industry subsidy, you need to plan ahead. The following is a typical 12-month plan:

**Months 1–2: Preparation and research phase**
- Sort out the company's financial situation and confirm that social-insurance payments are correct
- List the company's investment needs or employee training plans
- Visit the local labor bureau, commerce and industry association, or government economic department to obtain the latest subsidy list
- Preliminarily assess your eligibility and required documents

**Months 3–4: Document preparation phase**
- Organize the financial statements and bank statements for the past 3 months
- Prepare a copy of the company business license and construction-business license
- Draft the business plan or project description
- If you need to engage a consultant, contact them and sign an agreement at this stage

**Months 5–6: Application submission phase**
- Prepare a complete application form and attachments according to the subsidy type
- Ensure that all Japanese-language documents are accurate
- Submit the application to the relevant department (mind the deadline; some subsidies have an application window)
- Keep the application receipt and confirmation number

**Months 7–10: Review and feedback phase**
- Wait for departmental review (usually 2–4 months)
- If you receive a supplementary inquiry (照会), respond promptly
- Proactively contact the relevant department to confirm progress

**Months 11–12: Implementation and payment phase**
- After approval, invest or implement the project as agreed
- Keep all expenditure vouchers (invoices, remittance slips, contracts, etc.)
- After completing the project, prepare the settlement application documents
- Usually receive the subsidy within 3–6 months

### Document Checklist

Typical documents for applying for a construction-industry subsidy include:

1. **Application form** — the standard form for each subsidy, downloadable from Japanese government websites
2. **Proof of identity** — passport, residence card, copy of the Japanese tax registration certificate
3. **Company proof** — business license, construction-business license, corporate certificate of registered matters
4. **Financial documents** — bank statements for the past 3 months, a copy of last year's tax return, proof of social-insurance payment
5. **Labor documents** — employee roster, copies of labor contracts, sample pay slips
6. **Project description** — a business plan or project plan explaining the use of the subsidy funds and expected effects
7. **Other supporting documents** — equipment quotations, training institutions' course syllabuses, industry-association recommendation letters, etc.

All non-Japanese documents require a Japanese translation (translated by a certified translator or a translation agency).

## Key Points to Avoid Application Failure

### Common Reasons for Application Rejection

According to data from MHLW and METI, the main reasons for construction-industry subsidy application failures include:

1. **Incomplete social-insurance payments** (about 35% of rejections) — This is the most avoidable yet most common problem. Many small companies skip certain months due to cash-flow difficulties, but the subsidy review is especially strict.
2. **Financial statements inconsistent with tax filings** (about 20%) — Bank statements show 1 million yen in income, but the tax filing shows 500,000; this will immediately trigger review and rejection.
3. **Incomplete application forms or documents** (about 18%) — Missing key items, unclear document copies, or missing required attachments.
4. **Overly vague plans** (about 15%) — Writing only "improve company skills" without specifying which skills and the expected effects.
5. **Missing the application deadline** (about 12%) — Some subsidies have a fixed application window, and missing the deadline is irremediable.

### The Three Most Important Steps to Ensure a Successful Application

**First, ensure your finances are complete and compliant**
- Pay social insurance on time each month (employees' pension, employment insurance, health insurance)
- Keep all bank statements and invoices
- Regularly reconcile financial data with a tax accountant or accountant to ensure consistency
- If you find past payment gaps, pay them immediately

**Second, consult a professional advisor in advance**
- Do not wait until just before applying to consult; seek advice at the planning stage
- Free consultation channels: commerce and industry associations, Chambers of Commerce and Industry, Chinese chambers of commerce, labor bureaus
- Paid experts: subsidy-application agencies, tax accountants, social-insurance advisors
- Although an agency's fee is an expense, it can markedly improve the success rate and save time

**Third, prepare documents meticulously**
- All Japanese-language documents must be completed by a professional translator
- Copies must be clear and legible, confirmed with an official seal (company seal/会社印 or personal seal/個人判)
- When submitting, keep a complete photocopy and an electronic backup
- Confirm the receipt with the department to ensure the application has been formally accepted
