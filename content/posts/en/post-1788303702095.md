# How Chinese Business Owners in Japan Can Use Subsidies to Hedge Against Rising Costs in a Weakening Yen Environment — Complete Guide to Yen Depreciation Countermeasure Subsidies

## Opening: Authoritative Data and Opportunity Window

According to Japan's Small and Medium Enterprise Agency 2024 White Paper, approximately 3.85 million small and medium enterprises nationwide have experienced cost increases exceeding 15% due to yen depreciation, representing 42.3% of the SME population. Manufacturing, food processing, and import-export trading companies face the most severe impact. In response, Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry and Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare allocated approximately 120 billion JPY in yen-depreciation-countermeasure subsidies in the 2023 fiscal budget to support enterprises affected by exchange-rate fluctuations. The good news: **a substantial portion of this funding is allocated on a 「first-come, first-served」 basis, and Chinese-owned small and medium enterprises operating in Japan—from manufacturing factories and food traders to IT service providers—fall within the eligible scope**. This article explains which subsidies you can apply for, the maximum amounts available (ranging from several hundred thousand to several million JPY), and how to initiate the application process within 3–6 months.

## Yen Depreciation Countermeasure Subsidy System Overview: Why Launching Now Is Critical

### What Is a Yen Depreciation Countermeasure Subsidy?

The Yen Depreciation Countermeasure Subsidy is a dedicated government subsidy program established by the Japanese government in response to the sustained yen depreciation since 2023. It primarily covers three categories of affected industries:

**Category I: Import Raw Material Cost Increases**
- Applies to manufacturers and food processors relying on imported materials (petroleum, metals, chemicals, agricultural products, etc.)
- Subsidy Purpose: Support for raw material inventory, new supply chain development, domestic alternative conversion

**Category II: Rising Electricity and Energy Costs**
- Applies to energy-intensive industries (textiles, plastic processing, metal fabrication, greenhouse agriculture, etc.)
- Subsidy Focus: Energy-saving equipment adoption, process improvement, new technology investment

**Category III: Rising Overseas Business Operating Costs**
- Applies to Japanese enterprises with factories or sales networks overseas
- Subsidy Emphasis: Strengthening overseas operations, addressing local-currency debt management

According to Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry public data, the 2024 fiscal year Yen Depreciation Countermeasure Subsidy approval rate reached 68.2%, significantly higher than the approximately 40% approval rate for general equipment investment subsidies. This means that if your enterprise meets basic criteria, approval probability is substantially higher compared to other subsidy programs.

## Major Subsidy Programs at a Glance: How Much Can You Receive

### ① Small and Medium Enterprise Productivity Revolution Promotion Program (Yen Depreciation Special Allocation)

**Subsidy Amount and Rate:**
- Maximum Subsidy: **2.5 million JPY to 30 million JPY per enterprise** (varies by company size and investment amount)
- Subsidy Rate: **1/2 to 2/3** (varies by business type under the SME Agency classification)
- If total eligible expenses exceed 5 million JPY, the subsidy rate may be increased to 2/3

**Eligible Applicants:**
- Employee Count: Manufacturing companies with **300 or fewer employees**; other industries with **100 or fewer employees**
- Capital: Manufacturing companies with 300 million JPY or less; other industries with 100 million JPY or less
- Sales Trend: Enterprises with sales declining for at least 2 of the past 3 fiscal years, or expected future sales decline

**Eligible Expense Categories:**
Focus on introduction of machinery, systems, building renovations, and improvement of existing equipment. Examples include:
- New energy-efficient machinery adoption: If a manufacturing facility's air conditioning power consumption reduction equipment costs 2 million JPY normally, up to 1.5 million JPY (3/4) in subsidy may be available
- Solar panel installation on company factory: A 5-million-JPY investment might result in 2.5–3.33 million JPY in subsidy
- Raw material procurement system digitalization: Cloud system implementation costing 1 million JPY could yield 0.5–0.66 million JPY in subsidy

**Case Study: Ms. Li's Situation**

Ms. Li, originally from Zhejiang Province, founded a food processing company in Saitama Prefecture in 2015 with 45 employees. In 2023, yen depreciation caused imported wheat flour raw material costs to rise 35% year-over-year, increasing monthly costs by approximately 800,000 JPY. She sequentially applied for the following three subsidies:

1. **April 2024**: Applied to Small and Medium Enterprise Productivity Revolution Promotion Program (Yen Depreciation Special Allocation)
   - Investment: Automated wheat flour drying and storage system machinery (budgeted amount: 4.5 million JPY)
   - Applied Subsidy Amount: 3 million JPY (assuming 2/3 subsidy rate)
   - Review Period: Approximately 2 months; approved June 2024

2. **June 2024**: Applied to Monozukuri Subsidy (Growth Allocation, Yen Depreciation Category)
   - Investment: New flour mill introduction and existing line improvement for cost reduction (budgeted amount: 3.2 million JPY)
   - Applied Subsidy Amount: 1.8 million JPY (50% subsidy rate)
   - Review Period: Approximately 3 months; approved September 2024

3. **August 2024**: Applied to Business Restructuring Subsidy (Standard Allocation)
   - Investment: Low-cost domestic raw material development and procurement route establishment (budgeted amount: 2 million JPY)
   - Applied Subsidy Amount: 1 million JPY (50% subsidy rate)
   - Review Period: Approximately 3 months; approved November 2024

**Result:** By end of 2024, Ms. Li secured 5.8 million JPY in total subsidies. This enabled her to offset the monthly 800,000-JPY raw material cost increase through machinery efficiency improvements and new procurement route establishment. Automation additionally achieved 400,000 JPY annual labor cost reduction.

### ② Monozukuri Subsidy (Growth Allocation, Yen Depreciation Category)

**Subsidy Amount and Rate:**
- Maximum Subsidy: **10 million JPY to 30 million JPY** (tiered by investment scale)
- Subsidy Rate: **1/2 or 2/3**
  - Standard: 1/2
  - When wage increases and skills training are implemented: 2/3
- Performance: 2024 fiscal year national adoption was approximately 5,200 cases with approximately 52% adoption rate

**Eligible Enterprises:**
- Employee Count: **5 to 300 employees** across a wide range
- Manufacturing, IT, construction, food processing, agriculture—no industry restrictions
- Enterprises with declining sales trend over past 3 years or expected future decline

**Eligible Expenses:**
Expenses recognized as yen depreciation countermeasures primarily include:
- Machinery and equipment purchase: New energy-efficient machinery, automated production lines
- System Implementation: Cost management, inventory management, purchase-order system cloud migration
- Building Renovation: Factory insulation, LED lighting conversion
- Personnel Costs (partial): New employee training expenses, skills advancement training

Example: A plastic molding manufacturer purchasing a new injection molding machine for power cost reduction:
- Machine body purchase: 6 million JPY
- Installation and wiring: 1 million JPY
- Total: 7 million JPY
- Estimated subsidy at 2/3 rate: Approximately 4.67 million JPY

**Application Tips:**
- Include specific numerical yen depreciation impact in 「Business Plan」 (e.g., 「Raw material prices increased 35% year-over-year; imported raw material costs increased by X million JPY monthly」)
- Quantify post-equipment effects concretely (e.g., 「Post-implementation, power consumption reduction of X,000 kWh annually projected; Y million JPY annual cost reduction」)

### ③ Business Restructuring Subsidy (Standard Allocation, Yen Depreciation Type)

**Subsidy Amount:**
- Maximum Subsidy: **Up to 10 million JPY** (5 million JPY for enterprises with 20 or fewer employees)
- Subsidy Rate: **1/2**

**Eligible Enterprises:**
- Sales decline of **10% or more over past 2 fiscal years**
- Or enterprises with expected future sales decline

**Characteristics:** Compared to other subsidies, this emphasizes 「business transformation」. It supports not simple equipment replacement but fundamental business model transformation and diversification.

**Eligible Expenses for Yen Depreciation Type:**
- New Product Development: Planning and development of domestic alternatives reducing import dependency
- New Business Development: E-commerce channel establishment, new market exploration
- IT Adoption: Digital transaction platform, subscription-model business development

Example: A trading company historically importing apparel from China faces rising raw material costs due to yen depreciation and pivots to collaborative product development with domestic textile manufacturers:
- New Brand Planning: 1 million JPY
- Domestic Factory Collaboration/Sample Development: 0.8 million JPY
- Website and EC Platform Development: 1.5 million JPY
- Total: 3.3 million JPY
- Subsidy Amount (1/2): 1.65 million JPY

### Step-by-Step Application Guide

**Step 1: Business Plan Creation (1-2 months)**
- Quantify specific yen depreciation impact: 「Imported material purchase prices increased 35% compared to 2023」「Monthly raw material costs increased by X million JPY」
- Investment plan details: What, at what cost, by when?
- Effect prediction: 「Post-installation, 30% annual power consumption reduction projected」「Annual Y million JPY cost reduction」
- Loan and self-funding plan: Total investment amount, self-funding ratio (typically 1/4 to 1/3 of total investment)
- Many applicants engage external consultants for business plan creation (cost: approximately 100,000–300,000 JPY)

**Step 2: Required Documentation Assembly (2-4 weeks)**
- Corporate registered certificate and financial statements (3 most recent fiscal years)
- Wage payment performance reports, social insurance enrollment certification
- Objective evidence of yen depreciation impact: Supplier price-increase notices, past 1-year purchase records
- Equipment quotations (acquiring multiple quotes strengthens review evaluation)

**Step 3: Online Application (1 week)**
- Register account on 「jGrants」(Japan's unified government online application system) or subsidy implementation organization's dedicated system
- Upload business plan, financial statements, quotations in required formats
- Application Fee: Free (consultant fees, if outsourced, are charged separately)
- Application deadline varies by public offering, typically **approximately 2 months from offering launch**

**Step 4: Review and Adoption (2-3 months)**
- Document Review: Plan feasibility, appropriateness of yen depreciation countermeasure, management health
- Merit Review (for applicable enterprises): Wage increase performance, environmental considerations, new hiring, etc.
- Adoption rates vary by subsidy menu:
  - Small and Medium Enterprise Productivity Revolution Promotion Program: Approximately 55-60%
  - Monozukuri Subsidy Growth Allocation: Approximately 50-55%
  - Business Restructuring Subsidy: Approximately 40-45%
- Adoption results typically notified 8-12 weeks post-application via email

**Step 5: Subsidy Grant Application and Performance Reporting (6-12 months)**
- Post-adoption, conduct formal 「Subsidy Grant Application」 (additional management information submission may be required)
- Following approval, execute actual equipment purchase and construction
- **Important: Subsidies are 「paid-in-arrears」** — purchase with company funds first, retain receipts and invoices, request reimbursement later
- Prepare performance report, submit all documentation proving actual expenditures

**Step 6: Subsidy Finalization and Receipt (1-2 months)**
- Final review of performance report and expenditure documentation
- Subsidy finalization notification
- Transfer to designated bank account (typically within 2-4 weeks of finalization)

### Application Tips and Merit Factors

**Characteristics of Applications Likely to Succeed:**
- Business plan clearly articulates necessity: 「This investment would be unnecessary absent yen depreciation」
- Multiple fiscal years of financial statements demonstrably show yen depreciation's concrete impact on performance (sales decline, profit margin decrease, etc.)
- Investment effect quantification is specific (「Annual Cost Reduction: X million JPY」「Recovery Period: Y years」, etc.)

**Merit Factors (Increasing Adoption Probability):**

## Considerations When Simultaneously Applying for Multiple Subsidies

### Multiple Subsidies on Same Expense Cannot Be Received

This is the most critical rule. For example, receiving both 「Small and Medium Enterprise Productivity Revolution Promotion Program」 and 「Monozukuri Subsidy」 on the same machinery purchase is prohibited.

**Correct Usage Method: Multiple Applications to Different Expense Items**

Returning to Ms. Li's example:
- Application 1: Drying and storage system introduction (Small and Medium Enterprise Productivity Revolution Promotion Program)
- Application 2: New flour mill introduction (Monozukuri Subsidy)
- Application 3: Raw material procurement route development (Business Restructuring Subsidy)

Each subsidy targets **different expense items**, avoiding overlap.

### Application Schedule Staggering

When pursuing multiple subsidies, strategically offsetting public offering timing is important:
- Small and Medium Enterprise Productivity Revolution Promotion Program: Typically January, May, September public offerings (approximately 3 annually)
- Monozukuri Subsidy: Typically March, July, November public offerings (approximately 3 annually)
- Business Restructuring Subsidy: Typically February, June, October public offerings (approximately 3 annually)

Confirm each subsidy's public offering calendar in advance and stagger applications (「Application 1 in April, Application 2 in June, Application 3 in August」) to avoid review period overlap.

### Single Subsidy Program Allows One Application Per Fiscal Year

For 「Business Restructuring Subsidy」, an enterprise cannot apply multiple times within a single fiscal year. However, applications across different categories—「Standard Allocation」 vs. 「Large Deficit Enterprise Support Allocation」—may be permissible (advance verification with administering authority is essential).

## Common Misconceptions Chinese Business Owners in Japan Make About Subsidy Applications and Solutions

### Misconception 1: 「Because Subsidies Are 『Gifts』 That Don't Require Repayment, Usage Reporting Can Be Casual」

**Reality:** Subsidies are 「grants」 not 「gifts」. During performance reporting, strict examination occurs regarding whether usage aligns with subsidy grant applications, and whether all receipts and invoices are complete.

- For example, if an application requested 「2 million JPY machinery introduction」 but actual implementation used 1.5 million JPY, the subsidy is proportionally reduced
- Missing receipts or invoices mean that portion's subsidy is not provided (「non-payment」)
- Discovered falsification or excess diversion results in subsidy repayment + surcharge payment + company name publication penalties

**Solution:**
- Post-installation, file all quotation → invoice → receipt documents chronologically
- Engage accountants or subsidy consultants for performance report review before submission
- Notify administering authority in advance of any 「budgeted」 vs. 「actual」 discrepancies for consultation

### Misconception 2: 「Because My Japanese Isn't Strong, Subsidy Applications Are Impossible」

**Reality:** Business plans must nominally be in Japanese, but several solutions exist:
- Engage external consultants (subsidy application support providers) for business plan creation (cost: approximately 100,000–500,000 JPY)
- Many providers offer services targeting Chinese business owner applicants
- Non-Japanese-speaking applicants are not rejected solely for language reasons

**Solution:**
- Prepare materials explaining your business circumstances, results, and challenges in Japanese (decision-objective data: financial statements, customer lists, product samples, etc.)
- When engaging consultants, explain in detail how yen depreciation has affected your operations

### Misconception 3: 「If I Apply for a Subsidy, I'll Face Tax Audits」

**Reality:** Subsidy applications and tax audits are not directly linked. However, if financial statements submitted with subsidy applications differ from tax return filings, discrepancies may be flagged.

**Solution:**
- Use the same financial statement version (tax authority copy) when submitting with subsidy applications
- Verify that financial statement figures (sales revenue, expenses, etc.) align with subsidy application performance descriptions

### Misconception 4: 「If I Miss a Subsidy Application Deadline, 『Exception Procedures』 Might Be Available」

**Reality:** Subsidies require application **within the public offering period** without exception. If you miss the deadline by one day, acceptance is normally impossible.

- Exceptions (「early closing」 or 「system failure delays」) are theoretically possible, but individual circumstances (「manager was ill」) are never considered

**Solution:**
- Register each subsidy's public offering schedule (Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry typically publishes 「Subsidy Public Offering Schedule」 mid-January) in your calendar
- Assemble all necessary documentation **two weeks before application deadline**
- On online applications, 「save」 before the deadline, then click 「submit」 before expiration

### Misconception 5: 「If I'm Suffering from Yen Depreciation, I'll Be Approved for Whichever Subsidy I Apply For」

**Reality:** Adoption criteria and eligible expenses vary by subsidy. If your circumstances and subsidy objectives don't align, adoption is unlikely.

- For example, applying for 「Business Restructuring Subsidy」 (supporting new business launch) with only 「existing equipment energy-saving」 plans faces weak adoption prospects
- 「Monozukuri Subsidy」 prioritizes productivity improvement, so 「cost reduction only」 strategies are less compelling

**Solution:**
- Clarify what specifically challenges your enterprise:
  1. Import raw material cost surge eroding profits? → Base application on cost-reduction investment via 「Productivity Revolution Promotion Program」 or 「Monozukuri Subsidy」
  2. Existing business model viability at risk? → Base application on business transformation via 「Business Restructuring Subsidy」
  3. Declining competitive advantage internationally? → Base application on product upgrading or new market entry via 「JOG.net Business Creation Subsidy」 (overseas-focused)
- Structure business plan aligned with each subsidy's objectives

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: If I Apply for a Subsidy, Will My Management Details Necessarily Be Disclosed?

**A:** Post-adoption, company name and subsidy amount are normally 「published」. Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry websites typically list company names, project names, and subsidy amounts. However, specific financial figures (revenue, profit margins) or detailed business plan contents are kept confidential. Applications requesting non-disclosure due to 「company name confidentiality concerns」 are generally not accepted. Corporate research firms (Teikoku Databank, etc.) collect and sell subsidized enterprise lists.

### Q2: Is Professional Accountant or Tax Specialist Involvement Mandatory for Subsidy Applications?

**A:** Not mandatory, but significantly increases success probability; most enterprises engage external consultants. Business plan creation, financial statement alignment verification, and performance report preparation particularly benefit from specialist involvement. Typical consultant fees per application: 150,000–400,000 JPY. Simultaneous multiple applications may qualify for discounted rates. However, subsidy application support fees are not subsidy-eligible expenses (self-funded).

### Q3: If My Business Fails Post-Subsidy Receipt, Must I Repay?

**A:** No repayment obligation arises from business failure alone. However, repayment is required in these cases:
- Usage diverges significantly from approved application plans
- Post-subsidy equipment is sold or discarded during the subsidy period
- Performance reports contain false information
In other words, **subsidy repayment hinges on proper usage and reporting accuracy, not business success**. Subsidy periods vary by menu (typically 「18 months to 3 years post-approval」). Equipment disposal or sale during this period triggers potential repayment.

### Q4: If Yen Strengthens Post-Subsidy Receipt, Will I Face Penalties?

**A:** Post-receipt yen strength carries no penalties or additional repayment. Subsidies support investments addressing 「application-time business challenges」; subsequent exchange rate movements carry no implications.

### Q5: Are There Subsidies Specifically for Chinese Business Owners in Japan?

**A:** No dedicated Chinese-owner subsidies exist. However, subsidies described above apply regardless of entrepreneur nationality, provided business registration and operational criteria are satisfied. Some regional governments and chambers of commerce offer support programs (consultation, networking) specifically targeting foreign-affiliated enterprises. Note that residence status must permit business activities (investment visa, work visa, etc.).

### Q6: Can Losses Sustained Before Subsidy Approval Still Qualify as 「Damage」 From Yen Depreciation?

**A:** Yes. Business plans may reference past yen depreciation damage (e.g., 「2023-2024 cost increases totaled X million JPY」). However, subsidy investment must address 「future mitigation」, not solely 「past compensation」. Plans demonstrating forward-looking business solutions are more persuasive.

### Q7: Are There Subsidies Combining Multiple Support Types (Equipment + Training + Staffing)?

**A:** Yes. Several programs blend equipment investment with personnel training and new hiring support. Enterprises implementing wage increases and skills training simultaneously often qualify for enhanced subsidy rates (1/2 → 2/3 on Monozukuri Subsidy). Integrated plans addressing multiple business improvement dimensions score higher in merit review.

### Q8: If Procurement Quotations Come Exclusively from Chinese Suppliers, Does This Affect Approval?

**A:** No direct prohibition exists. However, applications ideally demonstrate procurement market research—比較 (comparison) of multiple supplier options including domestic and overseas. Quotes from Chinese manufacturers are acceptable if competitive evaluation was conducted. Some subsidy programs, particularly those emphasizing domestic supply chain strengthening, informally prefer domestic equipment; however, foreign equipment procurement isn't explicitly disqualifying.
