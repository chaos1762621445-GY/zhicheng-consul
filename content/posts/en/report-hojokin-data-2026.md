# Japan Subsidy & Grant Data Report 2026｜Adoption Rates, Caps & the Application Landscape for Chinese Businesses in Japan (Official Data Edition)

When it comes to Japan's subsidies (補助金) and grants (助成金), the internet is full of contradictory claims: some say they are "easy money," others insist that "nine out of ten applicants get rejected." So what is the reality?

This report does exactly one thing: **it lays out the first-hand data published by Japan's various competent government agencies.** Every figure comes from official sources such as the SME Agency (中小企業庁), SMRJ (the Organization for Small & Medium Enterprises and Regional Innovation, Japan / 中小機構), and the Immigration Services Agency (出入国在留管理庁), each tagged with the name of the agency and its publication year. As a subsidy application agency serving Chinese business owners in Japan, we want you to see the true adoption rates, caps, and application environment clearly before you apply — so you can decide with data rather than hearsay.

> Compliance note: All adoption rates, amounts, and subsidy rates in this article are official figures published as of the application period. The outcome of any individual application is subject to the reviewing authority's decision, and no organization can "guarantee approval."

## 1. The Overall Subsidy Landscape: Many Applicants, Limited Slots — But Meaningful Sums

In 2026, Japan's SME subsidy system is in the middle of a major reorganization. Take the flagship Monozukuri Subsidy: its 23rd round (deadline May 8, 2026) is the **final round** under the current framework. From FY2026, it will be consolidated into the "New Business Expansion / Monozukuri, Commerce & Services Subsidy" (新事業進出・ものづくり商業サービス補助金).

For Chinese business owners in Japan, two basic facts need to be established first:

- **Subsidy rates generally range from 1/2 to 2/3** — meaning the government covers half to two-thirds of your investment cost, and you cover the rest.
- **Competition for adoption (approval) is fierce** — the adoption rate for the major subsidies has long sat in the 30%–45% range, meaning **more than half of applicants are rejected**, and the quality of the application document directly determines success or failure.

Below, we break down the real data for each of the main subsidies one by one.

## 2. Monozukuri Subsidy: Adoption Rate Steady in the 30% Range

The Monozukuri Subsidy (official name: ものづくり・商業・サービス生産性向上促進補助金 — Manufacturing, Commerce & Services Productivity Improvement Promotion Subsidy) is the benchmark for equipment-investment subsidies. According to the adoption results **officially published by SMRJ (中小機構):**

| Round | Applications | Adopted | Adoption Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 23rd (final round · published 2026.8.7) | 1,275 | 448 | **35.1%** |
| 22nd (published 2025.10) | 2,453 | 825 | 33.6% |
| 21st | — | — | 34.1% |
| 20th | — | — | 33.6% |
| 19th | — | — | 31.8% |

**What the data means:**
- The adoption rate has held steady at **31%–35%** across multiple rounds, with roughly **70% of applicants rejected** — this is not a subsidy you win by "casting a wide net."
- Applications fell from 2,453 in the 22nd round to 1,275 in the 23rd, reflecting a wait-and-see mood during the transition, yet the adoption rate did not loosen accordingly.
- The scheme will be consolidated into a new subsidy, but the historical adoption data remains an important reference for gauging how strict the review is.

(Data source: SMRJ 補助金活用ナビ official disclosure, August 7, 2026; Tokyo management-support organization compiled figures)

## 3. IT Adoption Subsidy: A Notably Higher Adoption Rate — The Entry Point of Choice for SMEs

If the Monozukuri Subsidy is a "high-bar equipment investment," the IT Adoption Subsidy is the more accessible "entry-level digitalization subsidy." According to the latest adoption results for the IT Adoption Subsidy 2025, **officially published by the SME Agency (中小企業庁)** via Mirasapo plus:

| Category (枠) | Applications | Adopted | Adoption Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total across 7 deadlines (published 2026.1.20) | 8,676 | 3,780 | **43.6%** |
| ├ Standard category | 2,456 | 930 | 37.9% |
| ├ Invoice category (invoice-system support) | 6,143 | 2,808 | 45.7% |
| └ Security measures promotion category | 77 | 42 | 54.5% |

**What the data means:**
- The overall adoption rate of **43.6%** is nearly 10 percentage points higher than the Monozukuri Subsidy, making it **a more realistic starting point** for SMEs with limited funds who want to test the waters first.
- The **Invoice category has the highest adoption rate at 45.7%**, and also the largest application volume (6,143) — reflecting a concentrated surge in SME digitalization demand under Japan's invoice-system reform.
- For Chinese micro and small businesses just getting started, without large equipment-investment plans yet, the IT Adoption Subsidy is often "the most cost-effective first subsidy."

(Data source: SME Agency Mirasapo plus official disclosure, January 20, 2026; SMRJ disclosure)

## 4. SME Labor-Saving Investment Subsidy 〈Standard Type〉: Up to JPY 100 Million

Faced with Japan's severe labor shortage, the Labor-Saving Investment Subsidy is the most talked-about new scheme in recent years, replacing manpower with equipment such as IoT, robots, and AI. According to the **official public-offering guidelines from the SME Agency / SMRJ:**

| Number of Employees | Subsidy Cap (with the substantial wage-increase exception applied) |
|---|---|
| 5 or fewer | JPY 7.5 million (JPY 10 million) |
| 6–20 | JPY 15 million (JPY 20 million) |
| 21–50 | JPY 30 million (JPY 40 million) |
| 51–100 | JPY 50 million (JPY 65 million) |
| 101 or more | JPY 80 million (**JPY 100 million**) |

**Subsidy rates:**
- SMEs: **1/2** (raised to 2/3 when the minimum-wage increase exception is met)
- Small-scale enterprises / small-scale business operators / rehabilitation-stage businesses: **2/3**

**What the data means:**
- The cap rises in tiers with company size, reaching a maximum of **JPY 100 million** — one of the highest ceilings among current equipment-investment subsidies.
- Subsidy rates run 1/2–2/3 — **raising wages** is the key lever for boosting the rate: companies willing to raise employee pay can capture a higher share of subsidy.
- It suits Chinese-owned businesses in labor-intensive industries such as food service, retail, beauty, and manufacturing — precisely the fields hit hardest by labor shortages and best positioned to benefit from "labor-saving" equipment.

(Data source: SME Labor-Saving Investment Subsidy Project 〈Standard Type〉 official public-offering guidelines, SMRJ, FY2025–2026)

## 5. The Business Restructuring Subsidy Has Ended: Shift to Successor Schemes

The Business Restructuring Subsidy (事業再構築補助金), once the one with the highest subsidy cap, **stopped accepting new applications in 2026** (post-adoption procedures continue). The former "transformation and restructuring" demand is now carried by more finely targeted schemes such as the Labor-Saving Investment Subsidy, the New Business Expansion Subsidy (新事業進出補助金), and the Growth Acceleration Subsidy (成長加速化補助金).

A note for Chinese business owners: **be wary of the timeliness of old ads for "Business Restructuring Subsidy application services"** — the scheme no longer accepts new applications, so don't be misled by outdated information. When choosing a subsidy, always defer to each competent authority's **official public-offering guidelines for the current fiscal year.**

## 6. The Application Environment for Chinese Businesses in Japan: A Steadily Expanding Base

Behind the subsidy application environment lies the continued expansion of the Chinese economic presence in Japan. According to figures published by the **Immigration Services Agency (出入国在留管理庁, Ministry of Justice):**

- **The total number of foreign residents reached 3,956,619** (end of June 2025), a record high, up by roughly 187,000 (+5.0%) in half a year.
- **The number of Chinese nationals residing in Japan topped 900,000 for the first time**, accounting for about **1/4** of all foreign residents — the most prominent group.
- Of these: mid- to long-term residents 3.686 million / special permanent residents 270,000 / specified skilled workers 336,000.

**What the data means:**
- The absolute base of Chinese business owners in Japan is expanding, meaning **more and more Chinese-owned corporations qualify to apply for subsidies.**
- But the entire application process is in Japanese, confronting complex government documents — this is the core obstacle behind many Chinese owners "qualifying yet never applying."
- Japan's subsidy system **places no restriction on the nationality of the company or its operators**; it only requires being a legally operating Japanese corporation. Chinese-owned and Japanese-owned companies are **completely equal** in eligibility — the gap lies only in information and document quality.

(Data source: Immigration Services Agency, "Number of Foreign Residents as of the End of June 2025 (令和7年6月末現在)," published October 2025)
