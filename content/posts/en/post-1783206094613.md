# An In-Depth Look at the Monozukuri Subsidy: The Golden Channel for Equipment Investment by Chinese-Owned Factories in Japan

## What Is the Monozukuri Subsidy? Why Chinese Factory Owners Must Understand It

The Monozukuri Subsidy (manufacturing subsidy) is a policy-based subsidy program that Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) has run since 2012, with the main goal of supporting SMEs in updating production equipment, innovating processes, and undergoing digital transformation. For Chinese-owned factories in Japan, this is not merely financial support—it is an important opportunity to achieve an industrial upgrade.

According to 2023 statistics, the average subsidy amount for the manufacturing subsidy is between 1 million yen and 10 million yen, and some programs can even reach over 30 million yen. This means that if your factory is planning to purchase a new CNC machining center, automated welding equipment, or an intelligent inspection system, by applying for the subsidy you can reduce your actual out-of-pocket ratio from 100% to 25%-50%, significantly lowering the company's investment burden.

The key point is—**this subsidy is available to all SMEs registered in Japan, including Chinese wholly-owned or joint-venture factories.** As long as your company meets the employee-count and capital requirements, you are eligible to apply.

## Basic Eligibility Conditions for Chinese-Owned Factories in Japan Applying for the Manufacturing Subsidy

### Company Size Requirements

The primary condition for applying for the Monozukuri Subsidy is company size. Japan defines the standard for manufacturing SMEs as:

- **Number of employees**: 300 or fewer
- **Capital**: 300 million yen or less

For most Chinese-owned factories in Japan, these two conditions are relatively easy to meet. However, note that if your factory is a wholly-owned or controlled subsidiary of a large enterprise, it may be regarded as a large enterprise and lose eligibility.

### Business Condition Requirements

The subsidy application has certain requirements regarding the company's business condition, including:

- The company has been registered and established for at least 1 year
- No recent record of filing false information or evading taxes
- No overdue, unpaid bank loans
- An actual production and operation site in Japan

For a newly established Chinese-owned factory, if it is within its first year, you can wait until the conditions are met before applying, or apply for a special subsidy aimed at startups.

### Business Plan Requirements

The most important point is that you must submit a business plan that meets the requirements. This plan needs to explain:

- Your current business challenges and competitive weaknesses
- The improvement targets after introducing new equipment or processes (typically requiring a production-efficiency improvement of 15% or more)
- The specific investment items and a detailed budget
- The expected economic benefits and market outlook

This is also why many Chinese factory owners are rejected on their first application. They often just list the equipment they want to buy, without clearly explaining how these investments will solve existing problems and enhance competitiveness.

## A Detailed Explanation of the Types and Amounts of the Manufacturing Subsidy

### Standard Frame (Standard Plan)

The **Standard Frame** is the most mainstream application type for the Monozukuri Subsidy.

- **Subsidy rate**: typically 1/2 (50%) of the total required investment
- **Subsidy cap**: 10 million yen
- **Target projects**: production equipment purchases, process improvements, product development, etc.
- **Minimum investment**: 1 million yen (amounts below this are usually not accepted)

**A typical case**: a Chinese-owned precision machinery factory in Nagano Prefecture planned to invest 6 million yen to purchase an imported five-axis machining center. By applying through the Standard Frame, it successfully obtained a 3 million yen subsidy, reducing the actual out-of-pocket cost from 6 million yen to 3 million yen.

### Recovery Frame (Recovery Support Plan)

For companies severely affected by COVID-19 or other sudden events, the Japanese government has introduced the **Recovery Frame**.

- **Subsidy rate**: up to 2/3 (about 67%)
- **Subsidy cap**: 15 million yen
- **Special condition**: you need to prove a significant decline in the company's sales or profits

This is attractive to Chinese-owned factories that once experienced a sharp drop in orders due to the pandemic.

### Support for Introducing Large-Scale Equipment (Large-Scale Equipment Special Support)

For companies planning to purchase large-scale equipment exceeding 5 million yen:

- **Subsidy rate**: 1/2 to 2/3
- **Subsidy cap**: 30 million yen
- **Review period**: relatively longer (4-6 months)

## The Actual Application Process and Timeline for Chinese-Owned Factories in Japan

### Phase 1: Preparation and Assessment (2-3 months before application)

**Step 1: Confirm eligibility**

First, contact your local Chamber of Commerce and Industry or SME Support Center to confirm whether your company meets the basic conditions. Many local support organizations have Chinese-speaking staff who can provide an initial consultation.

**Step 2: Clarify investment needs**

Communicate thoroughly with your production department and equipment suppliers to determine:
- Exactly which equipment to purchase
- The detailed specifications and prices of the equipment
- The procurement and installation timeline
- The expected production-efficiency improvement data

This step is very important. We recommend collecting formal quotations from 3 or more equipment suppliers, which is required in the application.

**Step 3: Prepare the business plan**

This is the most time-consuming and most important step. You need to prepare:
- An analysis of the company's current situation (including market, products, costs, quality, etc.)
- A diagnosis of business challenges
- The solution (how new equipment or processes will help solve the problems)
- A 3-year business forecast (sales, profit, return on investment, etc.)
- An employee training plan

Many rejected applications fail right here. We recommend hiring a professional application support organization to assist, typically costing between 300,000 and 800,000 yen, but able to significantly improve the approval rate.

### Phase 2: Formal Application (1-2 weeks)

**Step 4: Online application**

The Monozukuri Subsidy uses an online application system entirely. You need to:
- Log in to METI's subsidy management system
- Fill out the application form (in Japanese, but you can work with a translation)
- Upload all required documents (business license, financial statements, business plan, etc.)
- Pay the application fee (usually not required)

**Required document checklist:**
- A corporate registration extract or sole-proprietor certificate
- Financial statements (settlement reports) for the most recent 3 years
- Balance sheet and income statement
- Formal quotations from equipment suppliers (3 or more)
- Business plan
- Organization chart and employee roster

### Phase 3: Review and Results (3-4 months)

After you submit the application, the subsidy secretariat conducts two rounds of review:

**First review** (document review):
- Check whether the application materials are complete
- Evaluate the feasibility and innovativeness of the business plan
- Review financial soundness

**Second review** (if necessary):
- Some applicants are invited to an on-site review or video interview
- The reviewer will ask the company's representative about specific details of the business plan
- This step may require Japanese-language or professional interpretation assistance for Chinese business owners

The entire process takes about 3-4 months. Review results are usually announced in June and December.

### Phase 4: Obtaining the Subsidy and Completing the Project (6-18 months)

After obtaining the subsidy, you do not receive the cash immediately. The process is:

1. **Grant decision**: formal notification that the subsidy has been obtained
2. **Project implementation**: purchasing equipment and carrying out work as planned
3. **Completion report**: after the equipment installation is complete, submitting a completion certificate, invoices, and proof of payment
4. **Subsidy payment**: after the secretariat reviews and confirms, the subsidy is generally paid in 2-3 installments

**Timeline**: from obtaining the grant decision to finally receiving the full subsidy usually takes 6-18 months. Therefore, the company needs to prepare sufficient own funds to invest first, and cannot rely entirely on the subsidy.

## Key Points and Common Mistakes for Chinese-Owned Factories in Successful Applications

### Keys to Success

**1. Thorough situation analysis with data support**

A successful application does not say "we want to enhance competitiveness," but lets the data speak. For example:

- Our current capacity utilization is 70%; after purchasing new equipment, it can rise to 85%
- Our current defect rate is 2%, while the industry average is 0.8%; by introducing an automated inspection system, we expect to reduce it to 1.2%
- Our current output per person is 4 million yen per year, while the industry average is 4.5 million yen; we need to close this gap

**2. A clear correspondence between investment and effect**

The application must clearly explain: invest in this equipment → solve this problem → achieve this goal.

For example: "Purchase a CNC machining center priced at 6.5 million yen → reduce manual machining time and lower labor costs → raise output per person from 4 million yen to 4.5 million yen, saving about 5 million yen in annual labor costs."

**3. Realistic business forecasts**

Many Chinese business owners are overly optimistic when writing business forecasts, claiming "sales will increase 30% after the investment." Such forecasts are easily questioned by reviewers. A more realistic approach is:

- Make conservative forecasts based on data trends from the past 3 years
- State the assumptions of the forecast (such as "assuming existing customer orders remain stable")
- Provide multiple scenarios (best case, expected case, conservative case)

**4. Strengthen human-resource and organizational factors**

Equipment investment alone is not enough. An excellent application emphasizes:
- The employee skills training plan
- Improvement of management processes
- The refinement of the quality assurance system
- Talent recruitment or retention plans

**5. Emphasize environmental and social value**

In recent years, subsidy reviews have increasingly valued factors such as environmental protection, energy conservation, and sustainable development. If your investment project reflects these values, your score will improve. For example: the new equipment consumes 20% less electricity than the old equipment, or reduces the defect rate to lessen environmental pollution.

### Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Providing only an equipment list, with no strategic thinking**

Some Chinese business owners paste the supplier's equipment catalog directly into the application, with no explanation of why the equipment is needed. The result is predictable—rejection.

**Mistake 2: Chaotic or unreliable financial data**

Japan's subsidy review demands very high financial authenticity. If there are problems with your tax filings or accounting statements, it is very hard to get approved. We recommend consulting a professional Japanese tax advisor first.

**Mistake 3: A business plan full of traces of stiff Japanese translation**

Chinese thinking and Japanese thinking differ. Some Chinese business owners directly use translation software to translate a Chinese-language plan, resulting in stiff Japanese expression and unclear logic. The best approach is to hire a professional to write it in Japanese from scratch.

**Mistake 4: Ignoring the reviewer's background and considerations**

Subsidy reviewers are usually experts in the industry. They will ask you some in-depth technical questions. If you lack a deep understanding of the equipment, processes, and effects you are investing in, you will be exposed in the review interview.

**Mistake 5: An investment project scope that is too broad or vague**

"We want to carry out digital transformation, so we need to purchase an ERP system, IoT devices, computers, etc."—this kind of description is too broad. A better approach is to focus on 1-2 core problems, invest in 3-5 key pieces of equipment, and explain in depth how they solve the problems.

## How to Maximize the Actual Return on the Subsidy

### Complementary Financing Strategy

Even after obtaining a 50%-70% subsidy, the company still has to bear 30%-50% of the investment. For SMEs, this out-of-pocket portion can be a strain. The recommended financing mix is:

- **Government subsidy**: 50%-70%
- **Policy-based financing**: for example, low-interest loans from the Japan Finance Corporation, which can cover 20%-30%
- **Own funds**: 10%-20%

This can minimize the pressure on the company's own funds.

### Timing of Equipment Purchases

A common question is: when is the most cost-effective time to buy equipment?

The recommended sequence is:
1. **Preparation period** (2-3 months before application): complete the needs assessment and obtain supplier quotations, but do not sign a purchase contract
2. **Application period** (submitting the application): continue communicating with suppliers, but maintain flexibility
3. **Review period** (awaiting results): if approved, immediately sign a contract with the supplier and initiate procurement
4. **Purchase period** (6-12 months): complete equipment procurement and installation
5. **Operation period**: run for more than half a year to accumulate performance data

Never purchase equipment in advance before approval, because the subsidy has time regulations, and purchases made after the deadline may not be claimable.

### Maximizing Comprehensive Returns

The true return on investment is not only the direct improvement in production efficiency, but should also include:

- **Cost savings**: reductions in labor, energy, and defect costs
- **Quality improvement**: higher product yield, fewer customer complaints, and enhanced brand value
- **Market expansion**: increased production capacity, allowing you to take on more orders
- **Employee value**: improving employee skills and satisfaction through new-technology training

A Chinese-owned electronic parts factory invested 2.5 million yen to purchase an automated inspection machine and obtained a 1.25 million yen subsidy. On the surface, it saved 50% of the cost. But the actual return was: the defect rate dropped from 3% to 0.8%, a major customer consequently increased orders by 30%, and the factory earned about 6 million yen in additional profit within 3 years. This kind of comprehensive return is where the real value lies.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: My factory is a Chinese wholly-owned company registered in Japan. Can I legally apply for the Monozukuri Subsidy?

**A:** Absolutely. The Monozukuri Subsidy places no restriction on a company's nationality or ownership background; you simply need to meet Japan's SME size standards (300 or fewer employees, capital of 300 million yen or less). The Japanese government has never used the nationality of a company's owner as a criterion for subsidy eligibility. In fact, there are many successful cases of Chinese-owned factories in Japan. Note that the company's tax filings, labor management, social insurance contributions, and the like must be fully compliant. If there are problems in these areas, it will directly affect the approval rate of the subsidy application. We recommend a full compliance check-up before applying.

### Q2: After the subsidy is approved, must I execute exactly according to the plan in the application? Can I purchase different equipment or adjust the budget?

**A:** This is a very practical question. In principle, the content of the subsidized project (what equipment to purchase, the investment amount, etc.) cannot deviate too much from the content of the application. But in practice, certain changes are allowed. The key rules are: (1) the total investment amount cannot be significantly reduced, because this directly affects the subsidy amount; (2) the price variation of individual equipment usually cannot exceed 10%-20%; (3) if the change involves a fundamental change to the essence of the subsidized project, you must notify the secretariat in advance and obtain consent. We recommend keeping some flexibility when communicating with suppliers, and consulting the subsidy secretariat's rules before formally signing a purchase contract. If a major change occurs without notification, once discovered you may be required to return part of the subsidy.

### Q3: Will applying for the subsidy affect my company's tax filings? For example, increase income tax?

**A:** This is a concern for many Chinese business owners. The key point is: **the subsidy itself is not company income and is not taxable.** However, the business benefits brought about after the subsidy (such as increased profits) are taxable. Specifically, the subsidy is used to purchase fixed assets (equipment), and the company subsequently reflects the cost of these assets through depreciation. New equipment may improve production efficiency and profit, and this increase in profit is subject to corporate income tax. From an accounting perspective, the subsidy should be recognized as "subsidy income," but tax planning can be done through fixed-asset investment, depreciation, and the like. We recommend communicating with your Japanese tax advisor in advance to formulate the optimal tax strategy and maximize the actual return on the subsidy. Dishonest practices (such as concealing subsidy income) will bring serious consequences, including tax penalties and clawback of the subsidy.

### Q4: Our first application was rejected. When can we reapply? What content do we need to revise?

**A:** The subsidy program usually has 2-3 application opportunities per year (spring, summer, autumn, etc.), so even if you are rejected this time, you can reapply in the next application period. But a rejected application must be seriously analyzed and improved. The recommended approach is: (1) request review feedback from the secretariat or support organization to understand the specific reasons for rejection; (2) invite a professional application agent to analyze the weak points of the application; (3) further refine the situation analysis, supplement more detailed financial data, and update the business forecast; (4) if the plan has changed (such as an increase or decrease in the investment amount, or an adjustment of goals), fully explain the reasons in the new application. Usually, the success rate of a second application, carefully revised, improves markedly. Many successful cases were approved only on the second or third attempt.

### Q5: The equipment we want to invest in is an imported product and relatively expensive (about 20 million yen). Can such a large investment apply for the subsidy? Is the application difficult?

**A:** A large investment (exceeding 10 million yen) can absolutely apply. Japan has a dedicated "Large-Scale Equipment Special Support" plan, with a subsidy cap of up to 30 million yen and a subsidy rate of up to 2/3. But it is indeed relatively more difficult, mainly because: (1) the review is stricter and requires more thorough justification of why such expensive equipment must be purchased; (2) the calculation of return on investment must withstand scrutiny; (3) the company's financial condition and debt-servicing ability will be a focus of review; (4) an on-site review or video interview may be required. For imported equipment, we recommend: providing the equipment manufacturer's background information, a performance comparison analysis, and a detailed explanation of why domestic equipment cannot meet the need; working with the supplier to produce an investment-benefit analysis report; and preparing thorough technical materials and application cases. This kind of large application usually requires the assistance of a professional application agency, and the cost invested (an agency fee of 300,000-800,000 yen) often yields a return of several times that amount.
