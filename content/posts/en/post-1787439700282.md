# What Subsidies Can Chinese Real Estate & Brokerage Companies in Japan Apply For? The Ultimate Guide

## Opening: Why Government Subsidies Must Be on Your Radar

According to 2023 public data from Japan's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, the total transaction volume of Japan's real estate distribution market reached approximately 18 trillion JPY, with small- and mid-sized real estate brokerage firms accounting for over 65% of transaction volume. Meanwhile, statistics from the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry show that the real estate services industry faces an annual talent shortage of approximately 8,000 people. Because of this industry's supporting role in Japan's economy, the Japan SME Agency and Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare allocate budgets in the hundreds of billions of yen annually to real estate and property grant programs. In other words, **the government urgently needs to encourage businesses in this field to expand operations, create employment, and advance digital transformation**. Chinese real estate brokerage companies in Japan fully meet the definition of SME subsidy recipients. Depending on business scale, employee recruitment plans, and equipment investment content, **first-year subsidy amounts can range from 3 million to 50 million JPY, with subsidy rates typically at 30–50%**. This article will systematically break down the main types of subsidies, amounts, eligibility, and application procedures that Chinese real estate brokerage enterprises in Japan can access.

## Overview of Major Subsidy Systems for Real Estate Brokerage Firms

### Four Major Subsidy Systems

Chinese real estate brokerage enterprises in Japan can primarily focus on the following four major subsidy systems:

**1. Employment Adjustment Subsidy (雇用調整助成金)**
- Administering Agency: Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare
- Applicable Scenarios: Maintaining employment due to management difficulties, or planning to expand staff recruitment
- Subsidy Amount: 1–3 million JPY per month (calculated based on number of employees and working hours)

**2. Human Resource Development Grant (人材育成支援助成金)**
- Administering Agency: Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare
- Applicable Scenarios: Skills training for employees, obtaining real estate-related certifications
- Subsidy Amount: 0.3–0.8 million JPY per person per month; annual maximum 10 million JPY

**3. Business Succession Subsidy (事業承継補助金)**
- Administering Agency: Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry
- Applicable Scenarios: Equipment and human resource investments during business succession or transfer
- Subsidy Amount: Up to 100 million JPY

**4. Monozukuri / Commerce / Service Productivity Enhancement Promotion Subsidy (生産性向上補助金)**
- Administering Agency: Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry
- Applicable Scenarios: IT adoption, business process improvement, new service development
- Subsidy Amount: Up to 150 million JPY (subsidy rate 1/2–2/3)

The first two are most widely applicable to real estate brokerage enterprises.

## Employment Adjustment Subsidy: The Easiest-to-Approve Employment Maintenance Grant

### System Design and Subsidy Amount Details

**The Employment Adjustment Subsidy (雇用調整助成金)** is a core system used by Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare to stabilize SME employment. The real estate industry, experiencing significant seasonal transaction fluctuations (spring moving season, year-end uptick), is viewed favorably by the government when applying for this subsidy.

Subsidy calculation method:

| Employee Type | Subsidy Rate | Example Case |
|---------------|--------------|--------------|
| Full-time employees on leave/reduced hours | 2/3 of base salary | Monthly salary 3 million JPY → subsidy 2 million JPY |
| Dispatch/contract employees | 2/3 of base salary | Monthly salary 2 million JPY → subsidy 1.33 million JPY |
| Overtime reduction | 2/3 of reduced wages | Overtime reduction 0.6 million JPY → subsidy 0.4 million JPY |

**Key Numbers**:
- Subsidy coverage period: Maximum 2 years (cumulative)
- Application intervals: Usually monthly or quarterly filings
- Processing cycle: Approximately 2–3 months after application (subject to the reviewing authority's decision)
- 2023 actual approval rate: Approximately 88% (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare statistics)

### Application Eligibility and Requirements

1. **Company Size**: Regular employees 20 or more and fewer than 300 (certain regions have relaxed limits)
2. **Business Conditions**: Sales for the most recent 3 months down 20% or more year-on-year, or impacted by external shocks like COVID-19
3. **Employment Insurance**: Company and all eligible employees must be enrolled in employment insurance with no payment arrears
4. **Labor Management**: Maintain work hour records demonstrating actual working hours matching wage payments
5. **Employment Duration**: Target employees must have worked continuously at the company for 6 months or more

**Common Mistakes by Chinese Entrepreneurs**: Many startup real estate brokerage companies with fewer than 20 employees give up on applying. However, certain regions (such as Tokyo and Kanagawa) have a "small business exception" allowing companies with 10 or more employees to apply. We recommend first consulting your local public employment office (ハローワーク).

## Human Resource Development Grant: Hire and Subsidize Simultaneously

### Training Subsidy Details and Coverage

This program is particularly beneficial for real estate brokerage enterprises, as the industry heavily depends on employee certification rates and professional knowledge. Training for Japan's Real Estate Transaction Agent (宅地建物取引士) certification typically takes 3–6 months and costs 1.5–3 million JPY. Government subsidies can cover 30–50% of these costs.

**Quick Reference Subsidy Table**:

- **New Employee Training**: 0.6 million JPY per person per month (up to 6 months; total maximum 3.6 million JPY per person)
- **In-Service Skill Enhancement**: 0.3–0.5 million JPY per person per month (up to 12 months)
- **Certification Training**: Direct subsidy of 50% of training costs; maximum 3 million JPY per person
- **Annual Company Cumulative Subsidy Limit**: 30–60 million JPY (depending on company size)

**Specific Eligible Expense Categories** (must fall within these ranges for subsidy):
- External training program fees (Real Estate Transaction Agent, Real Estate Appraiser, and other professional certifications)
- Online learning platform subscriptions (real estate law, market analysis courses, etc.)
- Internal instructor training, mentorship fees (requires formal training agreement)
- Training materials and reference books
- Employee wages during training period (can account for 60–80%)

**Non-Eligible Items**: Daily office supplies, work uniforms, transportation costs, and other typical business expenses.

### Commonly Confused Details

1. **Employee Status Restrictions**: Only those enrolled in employment insurance qualify as subsidy recipients; contractors or daily-wage temporary workers are ineligible
2. **Pre-Planning Requirement**: Must apply at least one month before starting training; retroactive applications are not supported
3. **Attendance Rate Requirement**: Trainees must maintain 80% or higher attendance; excessive absences affect subsidy amounts
4. **Outcome Verification**: Certification-focused subsidies require submission of certification copies as completion proof

## Business Succession Subsidy: The Strongest Support When Taking Over or Transferring

### Maximum 100 Million JPY Subsidy for Successor Enterprises

If your Chinese real estate brokerage enterprise is facing business succession (generational changeover, equity transfer) or planning to acquire a competitor, the Business Succession Subsidy is specifically designed for this scenario. The government is particularly generous with this subsidy because it wants to support business continuity to maintain market stability.

**Subsidy Amount Range**:
- Base subsidy: 60–100 million JPY (depending on successor enterprise's employee count and investment scale)
- Subsidy coverage period: Within 24 months after succession
- Subsidy rate: 2/3 (e.g., 150 million JPY investment qualifies for up to 100 million JPY subsidy)

**Specific Eligible Expense Ranges**:
- Equipment and system investments accompanying business succession (equipment, software, system migration costs)
- New Business Launch (post-acquisition startup initiatives)
- Human Resource Development necessary for management resource succession (post-takeover employee training)
- Professional Consultation Fees (lawyer, accountant, management consulting fees)
- Incumbent Employee Treatment Improvement (wage adjustments, benefit improvements to maintain team stability)

**Core Eligibility Requirements**:
1. Business succession to be implemented within 2 years, or implemented within the past 2 years
2. Predecessor enterprise is an SME (for real estate industry, typically capital under 50 million JPY)
3. No cross-investment or human relationships between successor and predecessor enterprises (in M&A cases)
4. Successor enterprise receives support from a Certified Support Agency (認定支援機関)

## Application Process and Timeline: From Application to Fund Transfer

### Five Key Stages of the Standard Process

**Stage 1: Preparation Period (1–2 months before application)**
- Tasks: Confirm eligibility, prepare required documents, consult support agencies
- Required Documents: Corporate registration extract, latest 3 years' financial statements, employment insurance enrollment proof, employee roster
- Time Required: 10–15 days
- Recommendation: Consultation at this stage is free; don't skip it

**Stage 2: Application Period (within public-offering deadline)**
- Application Windows: Vary by subsidy type; typically 2–4 recruitment windows per year
- Employment Adjustment Subsidy: Year-round (continuous acceptance)
- Human Resource Development Grant: Primarily spring (March) and autumn (September)
- Productivity Enhancement Subsidy: 2–3 times yearly; each window 2–3 weeks
- Business Succession Subsidy: 1–2 times yearly, typically around March and September
- Submission Method: Through each ministry's official system (jGrants) or via support agency proxy
- Alert: No late submissions accepted; submit at least 3 days early

**Stage 3: Review Period (2–3 months after application)**
- Document Review: Official agency reviews application documents and eligibility compliance
- On-Site Investigation (Possible): Some subsidies conduct enterprise visits
- Public Review Committee (Possible): Major subsidies may require oral presentation before professional reviewers
- Review Notice: Approved applicants receive "Subsidy Disbursement Approval Notice"; actual expenses only begin afterward

**Stage 4: Implementation Period (6–24 months after approval)**
- Business Implementation: Execute equipment purchase, staff hiring, system development per approved plan
- Results Report: Submit detailed results report with supporting materials approximately 6–12 months after completion
- Report Contents: Invoice copies, payment proofs, employee labor contracts, training outcomes, system demonstrations, etc.

**Stage 5: Subsidy Disbursement Period (1–2 months after report review)**
- Amount Confirmation: Official review of report determines final subsidy amount (may be reduced if expenses fall short of projections)
- Bank Transfer: Payment by bank transfer; fund arrival typically takes 2–4 weeks

**Full Process Duration: Approximately 6–12 months from application to fund receipt**

### Common Timeline Bottlenecks and Solutions

| Bottleneck | Common Cause | Delay | Chinese Enterprise Preventive Measures |
|------------|-------------|-------|----------------------------------------|
| Application Deadline Missed | Unaware of application windows; Japanese email notifications overlooked | Loss of 6-month opportunity | Register with support agency for reminders; set phone calendar alerts 30 days in advance |
| Eligibility Review Rejection | Employee social insurance payment incomplete; company registration address differs from bank-registered address | 1–2 months re-application | Conduct eligibility pre-check 30 days before application; confirm with tax and social insurance departments no arrears exist |
| Report Review Delay | Incomplete supporting materials (missing invoices, completion inspection reports) | 2–3 months | When purchasing equipment, explicitly request 「subsidy-application invoices」 (with company name and project details); retain periodic system development demonstration videos |

## Common Mistakes and Value-Add Points in Subsidy Applications

### Five Major Errors and Countermeasures

**Mistake 1: "My company has fewer than 20 employees, so I definitely can't apply"**
- Fact: Some subsidies (Productivity Enhancement Subsidy, Business Succession Subsidy) have no strict company size limits; Employment Adjustment Subsidy offers regional and small-business exceptions. Consult your local public employment office or prefectural economic development bureau first—many Chinese entrepreneurs miss several-million-yen subsidies due to this misconception.

**Mistake 2: "I already spent the money; can't I just report it retroactively?"**
- Fact: Almost all subsidies follow the principle of 「approval first, expense after」. Retroactive expenses generally receive no subsidy. The only exception is certain emergency support subsidies with strict look-back periods (typically not exceeding 3 months). Recommendation: List procurement plans in your application; begin purchases only after obtaining approval.

**Mistake 3: "The subsidy goes into company accounts; I just report it at tax time"**
- Fact: Subsidies must indeed be reported as 「subsidy income」in corporate income tax filings, but this typically doesn't increase tax burden (subsidies usually don't count as taxable income, only accounting profit). The key: You must fully document every expense and matching invoice in your results report; tax authorities conduct cross-checks. Chinese entrepreneurs must discuss tax treatment with their accountant (税理士/Zeirishi) in advance.

**Mistake 4: "I don't speak Japanese well; I'll let the intermediary handle the entire application"**
- Fact: While you can delegate to support agencies or administrative scriveners (行政書士/Gyoseishoshi), the company principal remains responsible for application content. If later review reveals data inconsistencies with reality, not only will the subsidy be reclaimed, the company faces administrative penalties 「improper receipt」(不正受給)—fines of 1.5× the subsidy amount or higher. Recommendation: Even when delegating, personally review and approve every line of the application.

**Mistake 5: "I'll apply for multiple subsidies simultaneously; different administering agencies won't coordinate"**
- Fact: Japan's subsidy systems across ministries are now highly integrated; the J-Grants system automatically detects multiple applications from the same company. If one business project receives double-subsidy funding (called 「overlapping subsidy」/ 重複補助), all funds are reclaimed. However, in Ms. Lee's case, her three subsidies targeted different business projects (succession, human development, system investment), so legally sound. The key: Clearly specify at application that each subsidy's eligible expense ranges don't overlap.

### Three Value-Add Points (Improve Approval Likelihood)

**Value-Add 1: Engage a Certified Support Agency**
- Certified agencies include: Accounting firms (税理士 / Zeirishi offices), SME Management Consultants (中小企業診断士 / SME Management Consultant), chambers of commerce, bank management consulting departments, etc.
- Value Reason: These organizations maintain strict quality control on application materials; official review teams approve applications from their recommendations at 10–15% higher rates
- Cost: Consultation typically 80,000–300,000 JPY (depending on subsidy amount); negligible against multi-million-yen subsidies
- Chinese Enterprise Advantage: Many certified agencies in Tokyo, Yokohama, Osaka and other Chinese communities employ Chinese-speaking consultants

**Value-Add 2: Clearly articulate 「job creation plan」or 「regional contribution」in your application**
- Government prioritizes subsidies driving local employment. If your real estate brokerage plans to hire 5+ employees within 12 months, open new branches, or expand service areas, detail this in your application narrative to boost review scores
- Specific Phrasing: Include hiring numbers, expected salary levels, training plans, projected local tax contributions, etc.

**Value-Add 3: Prepare thorough 「Business Plan」and 「Management Innovation Plan」**
- Don't submit only minimum-required documents. Additionally provide: 3-year financial projections, market analysis, competitive advantages
- For real estate brokerage enterprises, emphasize: Increasing Chinese population creating market demand, foreign capital investment interest in Japanese real estate and other macro context

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: My real estate brokerage just launched less than a year ago; can I still apply for subsidies?

**A:** Most subsidies require 1+ years of operation (some require 3 years). However, two exceptions exist: **Employment Adjustment Subsidy** is relatively lenient toward startups, requiring only 3+ months of operation and employment insurance enrollment; **Business Succession Subsidy** if you're taking over an existing enterprise, the predecessor company's longevity counts, unaffected by your startup status. Recommendation: If you're founding a real estate brokerage, consult your local public employment office first on whether special exceptions apply. Additionally, some local governments (Tokyo, Kanagawa) offer 「startup-phase enterprise subsidies」with smaller amounts (2–10 million JPY) but easier application.

### Q2: What level of Japanese does subsidy application require? Can I apply entirely in Chinese?

**A:** Official application documents must be in Japanese; Chinese-only applications are not accepted. However, you have two options: **Option 1** Hire an administrative scrivener (行政書士/Gyoseishoshi) or Certified Support Agency to handle the application (cost approximately 50,000–150,000 JPY depending on subsidy complexity); they manage translation and document compilation. **Option 2** Utilize some local governments' and chambers of commerce 「multilingual consultation windows」(多言語相談窓口) with Chinese-speaking staff to understand application requirements. Tokyo Chamber of Commerce and Yokohama International Affairs Bureau offer such services. Consultation is free, but actual application requires professional handling. Additionally, AI translation tools (Google Translate, DeepL) now have high accuracy; you can draft initially with AI assistance, but final versions must be reviewed by Japanese-fluent professionals.

### Q3: Once subsidy funds arrive, must I execute 100% according to the application plan? Is there room for changes?

**A:** **Some flexibility exists, but must be requested in advance**. If business implementation requires adjusting procurement items, changing employee recruitment numbers, or delaying project completion, you must request 「change approval」(変更承認) from the administering agency. Changes typically fall into two categories: **Minor changes** (supplier changes, unit price adjustments ±10% or less) can be directly modified in reports; **major changes** (project scope, budget overruns exceeding 20%, delays exceeding 3 months) require advance change application with approximately 1–2 weeks approval. A common Chinese entrepreneur mistake is privately modifying plans, resulting in reports vastly differing from applications, triggering 「improper receipt」determinations. Recommendation: Reserve 10–15% budget flexibility to avoid forced plan changes later.

### Q4: I qualify for both 「Employment Adjustment Subsidy」and 「Human Resource Development Grant」; can I apply simultaneously?

**A:** **Absolutely yes, and this is common practice**. These two subsidies cover different expense categories: the former reimburses wage losses from employee leave or reduced hours, the latter covers training costs and new wages during training. If you clearly specify at application that the same employee both received reduced-hour subsidies and participated in skills training (without timing overlap or minimal overlap), it's completely legal. Example: Employee Zhang received reduced-hour subsidies from January–March due to COVID-19, then received Human Resource Development Grant subsidy for real estate certification training from April–June; both are independent. Key point: **The same expense during the same period cannot be subsidized by two different programs simultaneously**. Recommendation: Confirm eligible expense boundaries with your support agency.

### Q5: My enterprise applied for a subsidy in 2023; can I apply for the same subsidy again in 2024?

**A:** **Depends on the subsidy rules and your company's new circumstances**. Most subsidies operate on an 「annual application system」meaning each fiscal year has independent recruitment and review. Therefore, the same company can apply separately in 2023 and 2024, but each requires a new business plan and new results report. However, one restriction exists: **The same company, same project, same eligible expense** typically cannot be reapplied in consecutive years. Example: If 2023's Productivity Enhancement Subsidy funded a CRM system purchase, 2024 cannot reuse the same subsidy for a CRM upgrade package (same system project); however, you could apply for 「online property viewing app development」as a new project. Additionally, Business Succession Subsidy is a one-time subsidy (apply once within 24 months of succession); cannot be reapplied. Recommendation: Consult your support agency whether 2024 has new subsidy categories better matching your needs.

### Q6: If my subsidy application is rejected, can I reapply? What are common rejection reasons?

**A:** **Yes, reapplication is possible after understanding and addressing rejection reasons**. Common rejection reasons for real estate brokerage enterprises include:

1. **Ineligible (most common, ~40%)**: Insufficient employees, social insurance payment arrears, business license information inconsistent with application content
2. **Unclear Business Plan (~25%)**: Project objectives vaguely described; insufficient market-demand justification
3. **Excessive Cost Estimates (~20%)**: Procurement prices significantly differ from market rates; reviewers deem it wasteful of subsidy funds
4. **Incomplete Documentation (~10%)**: Missing key supporting materials such as contracts, quotations, corporate tax records
