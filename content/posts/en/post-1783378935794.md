# The Complete Guide to the Business Restructuring Subsidy: A 60 Million JPY Opportunity for Chinese-Owned Businesses in Japan to Transform and Upgrade

The challenges facing Chinese business owners in Japan are multidimensional—shifting supply chains, changing consumer demand, and a market landscape reshaped in the post-COVID era. If you're considering transforming and upgrading your business, the **Business Restructuring Subsidy (事業再構築補助金)** could be a game-changing opportunity. This key Japanese government support policy offers subsidies of up to 60 million JPY, providing a safety net for the innovative transformation of Chinese-owned businesses in Japan.

This article takes an in-depth look at the core content, eligibility requirements, how to obtain the subsidy, and common pitfalls, helping you craft a precise strategy for upgrading your business.

## An Overview of the Business Restructuring Subsidy

### What Is the Business Restructuring Subsidy?

The Business Restructuring Subsidy is a policy-based subsidy program launched by Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) in 2021, designed to help businesses respond to the impact of COVID-19 and structural economic change and achieve sustainable growth through business restructuring. Put simply, if your company plans to undertake a **major business-model reform, new product development, market expansion, or service innovation**, the government will shoulder part of the investment cost.

This subsidy is open to businesses and sole proprietors of all industries and sizes, including Chinese-owned businesses in Japan. Since the policy took effect, subsidies have been distributed over multiple rounds, cumulatively helping tens of thousands of companies complete their transformation.

### Subsidy Amount and Subsidy Rate

The Business Restructuring Subsidy's amount is divided into three tiers based on company size:

| Company Type | Subsidy Amount | Subsidy Rate |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Large enterprises | Up to 60 million JPY | 2/3 |
| Mid-tier enterprises | Up to 40 million JPY | 3/4 |
| SMEs / small businesses | Up to 20 million JPY | 3/4 |

Here, the "subsidy rate" means that if you are an SME investing in a 10 million JPY transformation project, the government subsidizes 7.5 million JPY, and you only have to bear 2.5 million JPY yourself. For cash-strapped startup-type companies, this is undoubtedly enormous support.

## Core Points for Applying for the Business Restructuring Subsidy

### Basic Requirements You Must Meet

**1. Company Nature Requirements**
- SMEs as defined by Japan's Small and Medium Enterprise Agency (based on capital and employee count)
- Sole proprietors
- Incorporated companies
- Foreign-invested companies (as long as they are registered in Japan)

Chinese-owned businesses in Japan, whatever their corporate form, generally qualify as long as they are legally registered in Japan. But note: companies placed on a "blacklist" (tax arrears, labor violations, etc.) are excluded.

**2. Business Condition Requirements**
This is the key threshold for applying. The subsidy system requires companies to prove they face "business difficulties":

- The **ordinary profit** or **operating profit** for the most recent 6 months prior to application has fallen more than 10% compared with the same period in the past
- Or the overall operating revenue of your industry (by Japanese industrial classification) has fallen more than 10%

The purpose of this condition is to prevent misuse of the subsidy. Even a well-performing company qualifies if the overall business climate of its industry has declined. For example, many Japanese retailers whose individual stores are profitable still qualify because the entire retail industry has been hit by e-commerce.

**3. Business Restructuring Requirements**
This is the most core condition—your transformation plan must fit the definition of "business restructuring." According to official guidance, the following situations can be recognized as business restructuring:

| Type of Transformation | Specifics | Example |
|--------|--------|------|
| New field expansion | Entering an entirely new business domain | Food-service company → elderly care services |
| Business conversion | Changing the main product or service | Traditional retail → online store |
| Industry conversion | Switching the entire industry classification | Textile manufacturing → medical devices |
| Business reorganization | Optimizing the organization through mergers or spin-offs | Several small companies merging to gain scale |
| Global expansion | Expanding into international markets | Exporting products to Southeast Asia |

The types most commonly used by Chinese-owned businesses in Japan are **new field expansion** and **business conversion** (change of business model).

### Key Elements of the Application Documents and Business Plan

The success or failure of a subsidy application is 70% determined by the quality of the **business plan (事業計画書)**. This is not a simple financial statement but a systematic argument that must cover:

**1. Current-State Analysis**
- Why has the company fallen into difficulty? Is it an industry problem or a management problem of its own?
- How are competitors responding to the same challenges?

**2. Content of the Restructuring**
- Exactly which project will be invested in? (Down to the precise amount in JPY)
- Why can this project solve the problem?
- Supporting market research data (consumer surveys, market-size data, etc.)

**3. Implementation Schedule**
- A specific 12–24 month execution plan after obtaining the subsidy
- Milestone metrics for each key node

**4. Financial Plan**
- A 3–5 year forecast of revenue and profit (which must be conservative yet persuasive)
- The payback period on the investment

**5. Method of Fundraising**
- A clear source of self-financing (bank loans, own funds, etc.)
- Why you are capable of securing the matching financing

For Chinese-owned businesses in Japan, a common weakness is unclear financial data (messy books, lots of cash transactions). We recommend starting to organize your books half a year before applying, and even hiring a professional accountant to assist.

## The Application Process and Timeline

### Advancing the Application Step by Step

The Business Restructuring Subsidy uses a "public offering" (公募) format, typically with **2–4 rounds** of application windows each year, and each round's application cycle runs 2–3 months.

**Phase 1: Preparation (3–6 months before application)**

- In-depth diagnosis: analyze the company's current state and confirm whether it meets the eligibility requirements
- Project conceptualization: clarify the direction of transformation and conduct a preliminary feasibility study
- Fund planning: estimate the total investment amount and subsidy needs
- Financial organization: organize the past 2–3 years of financial statements to ensure accurate data

At this stage, we recommend **consulting a professional advisor**. Many companies' self-prepared application documents are rejected, and the biggest reason is an inadequate understanding of "business restructuring."

**Phase 2: Application Preparation (1–2 months before application)**

- Write the business plan (usually 2,000–3,000 characters, in Japanese)
- Compile a detailed investment budget table and financial forecast table
- Prepare basic documents such as company information and the representative's background
- Revise and refine repeatedly to ensure rigorous logic

**Phase 3: Formal Application (while the application window is open)**

- Submit through the online system designated by METI (a system using GビズID)
- The operator's electronic signature is required
- It must be completed before the deadline (usually 23:59 at the end of the application period)

**Phase 4: Review (2–3 months after application)**

- METI and the certified support organization conduct a formal review and a content review
- They may request supplementary materials or revisions to the plan
- The list of selected companies is published (selection is usually possible once the score reaches a certain threshold)

**Phase 5: Subsidy Disbursement (6–12 months after selection)**

- Sign a subsidy agreement with the government agency
- Begin executing the business plan and incurring investment expenditures
- Submit expenditure reports and progress reports every six months or by phase
- Upon final completion, submit a completion report and an expenditure settlement report
- The government pays the subsidy based on actual expenditures

**The entire process, from application to receiving the subsidy, typically takes 8–14 months.** During this period, the company must **self-finance and invest upfront**, so arranging financing in advance is very important.

### Important Timeline Reminders

- **Don't miss the application window**: The public-offering period is usually only 2–3 weeks; miss it and you'll wait 3–6 months
- **Allow time for document preparation**: From deciding to apply to submitting takes at least 4–8 weeks
- **Retain execution flexibility**: After selection you can adjust the details of your plan based on market conditions, but you cannot change its fundamental direction

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: I'm a sole proprietor (自営業者). Can I apply for the Business Restructuring Subsidy?

**A:** Yes. The Business Restructuring Subsidy is fully open to sole proprietors, and the eligibility requirements are essentially no different from those for incorporated companies. However, sole proprietors need to pay special attention to the following:

First, you need to submit your finalized tax returns (確定申告書) and books (帳簿) for the past 3 years to prove your current business situation. Many sole proprietors are rejected because of messy books, so we strongly recommend hiring a Zeirishi (税理士) to organize them.

Second, the subsidy ceiling for sole proprietors is 20 million JPY (the SME standard). If the transformation project's investment scale is very large (such as setting up a factory), you may need to convert the sole proprietorship into a corporation, which gives you more room to maneuver.

Finally, the self-financing requirement applies equally. With a subsidy rate of 3/4, a 20 million JPY project requires 5 million JPY of self-financing. Sole proprietors can raise funds through: small loans (offered by the Japan Finance Corporation), family borrowing, or injecting personal assets into the business.

Before formally applying, we recommend consulting your local Society of Commerce and Industry (商工会議所) or a certified support organization for the Business Restructuring Subsidy; they will assess feasibility based on your specific situation.

### Q2: My company had good profits last year, but my industry has hit hard times this year. Can I still apply?

**A:** Absolutely. This is precisely one of the situations the Business Restructuring Subsidy is meant for. The logic of the subsidy is not "help only loss-making companies" but "help companies that are proactively transforming under difficult operating conditions."

One of the eligibility requirements is that the operating revenue of your industry (as classified by the Japan Standard Industrial Classification) has fallen 10% or more year over year. This means that even if your own company is doing fine, you qualify as long as the overall business climate of your industry has declined.

For example: a high-end restaurant with a 15% profit margin in 2023, but whose food-service industry saw overall revenue fall 12% year over year, is eligible to apply. It can use the subsidy to invest in a delivery system, a central kitchen, or a pivot to new formats such as lunch bento boxes, in order to adapt to industry change.

If you're unsure whether your industry meets the standard, you can check the "specific industries" list published by METI. In each round of the subsidy's public offering, it is disclosed which industries have been recognized as struggling.

### Q3: When does the Business Restructuring Subsidy money arrive? Do I need to front the funds?

**A:** Yes, you need to front the funds. This is a key issue that must be clear before applying for the Business Restructuring Subsidy.

The specific process is:

**Month 1**: Submit the application
**Months 3–4**: Selection is announced
**Months 4–5**: Sign the subsidy disbursement decision with the government
**Months 5–12**: Execution period; the company invests and spends on its own
**Months 13–14**: Submit the completion report and expenditure claim
**Months 15–16**: The government reviews and verifies the expenditures
**Month 17**: The subsidy is paid (reimbursed)

Overall, **from making the expenditure to receiving the subsidy typically takes 12–18 months.** This means the company must raise all the funds itself, including the subsidized portion. So before applying, you must ensure you can obtain sufficient transformation-investment funds from banks, investors, or other channels.

For many SMEs, this is a challenge. The solutions are:

1. **Apply for financing from the Japan Finance Corporation at the same time**: Many government financing programs offer preferential rates and relaxed conditions to companies that have applied for subsidies
2. **Show financial institutions your subsidy selection notice**: Once selected, banks will be more receptive to your financing application
3. **Spend in phases**: Rather than investing all at once, spend gradually across 3–4 phases, which reduces the pressure of fronting funds early on

Some professional subsidy agencies can help you coordinate financing arrangements, which in a sense is worth the investment.

### Q4: My transformation plan involves international business expansion (global expansion), such as exporting products to Southeast Asia. Does this count as business restructuring?

**A:** Yes. Global expansion (expanding into international markets) is a form of transformation explicitly recognized by the Business Restructuring Subsidy.

In this case, your business plan needs to focus on arguing:

1. **The necessity of international markets**: With the domestic market shrinking, why is going international an inevitable choice? For example, Japan's aging population is causing domestic consumption to shrink while Southeast Asia's young consumer population is growing—that's a strong argument
2. **Market research on the target market**: Provide data on the target country's market size, consumer demand, competitive landscape, and so on
3. **Specific investment content**: Are you building an overseas sales network? Setting up a factory? Partnering with agents? The investment amount and expected return must be clear

What's especially favorable for Chinese-owned businesses in Japan is that many Chinese owners have a natural understanding of and network advantages in the Chinese, Taiwanese, and Southeast Asian markets, which can become a competitive advantage in the application.

But note: the subsidy's "global expansion" does not include simply selling imported goods within Japan. It must be a business that starts from Japan and develops your own products or services overseas.

### Q5: What are the consequences if a subsidy application is rejected, or if the project isn't completed after selection?

**A:** This is a very important risk reminder. The subsidy system has strict performance and oversight requirements:

**Rejection at the application stage:**
- There are no economic or legal consequences; you simply cannot obtain the subsidy
- But if false information is found in the application materials, you may be blacklisted and unable to apply for any government subsidy for the next 3–5 years

**Inability to complete the project after selection:**
- This is a serious breach
- You must return the subsidy already received, plus interest (3–4% per year)
- You are placed on a credit blacklist, affecting the company's financing and future applications for government support
- You may face public criticism from METI

**Partial completion of the project or insufficient expenditure:**
- The subsidy is refunded in proportion to actual expenditure
- If you planned to invest 20 million JPY but actually spent only 15 million JPY, the government subsidizes only 11.25 million JPY (3/4 × 15 million JPY)
- There is no additional penalty in this case, but you lose part of the expected subsidy

**Takeaways:**
- Before applying, you must fully argue the project's feasibility and not blindly overestimate your capabilities
- After selection, keep communicating with the administering agency, and report difficulties and apply for changes promptly if they arise
- Keep all expenditure receipts to prepare for the final inspection

Many companies are forced to return the subsidy because they cannot raise funds midway or the market environment changes and they cannot complete the plan. That's why financing and risk assessment before applying are crucial.

## Practical Tips When Applying

### Choosing the Right Certified Support Organization

When applying for the Business Restructuring Subsidy, you must engage a "certified support organization" (認定支援機関) to assist with review and guidance. This is not optional but a hard requirement.

Certified support organizations include:
- Societies of Commerce and Industry (商工会議所)
- Chambers of Commerce (商工会)
- Financial institutions (banks, credit unions)
- SME Management Consultant (中小企業診断士) firms
- Accounting firms and Zeirishi (税理士) offices

**Selection Tips:**

1. **Prioritize organizations with subsidy application experience.** Not all certified support organizations have hands-on experience; choose those that have already helped companies successfully apply for subsidies 5 or more times
2. **For Chinese-owned businesses in Japan, prioritize organizations that can provide Chinese-language service or have Chinese-speaking staff.** This reduces communication barriers
3. **Understand the fee structure.** Some organizations are free (such as Societies of Commerce and Industry), some charge 10,000–50,000 JPY, and some charge a percentage of the subsidy amount. Ask clearly before choosing
4. **Judge an organization's quality by whether they ask you for detailed financial and industry data.** A professional organization will dig deep into your company and industry rather than just going through the motions

### Avoiding Common Application Pitfalls

1. **Overstating the subsidy amount**: Many companies reverse-engineer their project with the goal of obtaining a subsidy, causing the project to drift away from real needs. The correct approach is to first determine your genuine transformation needs, then see whether they meet the subsidy requirements

2. **Underestimating execution difficulty**: The financial forecasts in the plan are too optimistic, so after selection the company can't deliver. We recommend using conservative forecasts to leave yourself room to adapt

3. **Ignoring supporting capabilities**: Applying for a manufacturing-upgrade subsidy without the corresponding technical staff; applying for a sales-channel subsidy without a marketing team. The subsidy can only subsidize hardware investment; software and manpower must be provided by the company itself

4. **Insufficient document preparation**: Hastily submitting incomplete or erroneous application documents. We recommend revising at least 3–5 times before formal submission and having people from different angles review it

5. **Ignoring subsequent oversight**: Many companies think that once they get the subsidy, all is well. In fact, the subsequent progress reports, expenditure verification, and completion inspections are equally strict. Be prepared to cooperate over the long term

In Japan, other government support programs also target business transformation and upgrading, such as the **Monozukuri Subsidy (ものづくり補助金 / Manufacturing Subsidy)** and the **Sustainability Subsidy (持続化補助金)**.

|--------|--------|--------|------|
| Business Restructuring Subsidy | Up to 60 million JPY | All industries, major transformation | Innovation and transformation |
| Monozukuri Subsidy | Up to 10 million JPY | Mainly manufacturing | Improving production efficiency |
| Sustainability Subsidy | Up to 2 million JPY | All industries, small businesses | Improving productivity |

For Chinese-owned businesses in Japan, **the Business Restructuring Subsidy is usually the best choice** because it offers the largest amount and the broadest support scope, not limited to manufacturing or IT.

Some companies can even apply for multiple subsidies at once (the same project cannot be subsidized twice, but different projects can). For example, a company undergoing digital transformation can apply for the Business Restructuring Subsidy to support the change in its business model, and also apply for the IT Adoption Subsidy to support system implementation.
