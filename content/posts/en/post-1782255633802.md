Chinese business owners running enterprises in Japan—are you still managing your operations with paper ledgers and Excel spreadsheets? Is your company website one you built years ago, long since out of step with the times? If the answer is yes, then the **Digital Transformation Subsidy (DX subsidy)** rolled out by the Japanese government is an opportunity made just for you.

According to statistics from Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI), as of 2024 the digital transformation rate among Japanese SMEs was only around 35%, and among Chinese enterprises this figure is even lower. But government subsidies can cover up to **75% of DX investment costs**, which means that if you invest 2 million yen in building an ERP system and an official website, you can receive up to 1.5 million yen in subsidy. This article will tell you in detail how to seize this opportunity.

## The Basics of the DX Subsidy: Four Key Points You Need to Know

### What Is the Digital Transformation Subsidy

The Digital Transformation Subsidy—known for short as the "DX subsidy" or "Digitalization Promotion Subsidy"—is a policy support program established by the Japanese government to help SMEs achieve business digitalization. The core aim of this subsidy is to push Japanese enterprises to shake off their reliance on paper documents and build modern information systems.

For Chinese enterprises in Japan, this subsidy is especially valuable. Many Chinese-run wholesale trading companies, restaurant chains, logistics enterprises, and manufacturing plants have long hesitated to undertake digital reform because of the large upfront investment. The DX subsidy fills precisely this funding gap.

### Eligibility and Basic Conditions

**Who can apply?**

- Japan-registered SME corporations (including foreign-capital enterprises)
- Sole proprietors and partnerships
- Enterprises with between 5 and 300 employees (adjusted by industry)
- A certain level of revenue over the past 3 years (typically an annual revenue of no less than 5 million yen)

**Key point: Chinese wholly owned enterprises whose owners hold Japanese residency status, joint ventures with Japanese partners, and companies where a Chinese national serves as the representative director** all qualify to apply, as long as they are formally registered Japanese corporations. Japanese nationality is not required.

### Subsidy Amount and Rate

- **Base subsidy amount**: 500,000 yen to 5 million yen
- **Subsidy rate**: up to 75% for SMEs (up to 80% in specific regions and industries)
- **Self-funded portion**: enterprises must bear 25%–20% of the cost

Here is a concrete example: if you plan to invest 3 million yen to build an ERP system, construct a corporate website, and purchase servers and related IT infrastructure, the subsidy can cover 2.25 million yen, and you only need to put up 750,000 yen yourself.

### Application Cycle and Time Planning

- **Application deadline**: usually one round each in March and September every year (specific dates adjusted annually)
- **Review period**: about 2–3 months
- **Subsidy period**: generally complete the project within 1–2 years
- **Fund disbursement**: after project completion, an inspection is conducted; it usually takes 3–6 months to receive the full subsidy

## The Application Process: A Step-by-Step Guide to Getting the Subsidy

### Step 1: Confirm Eligibility and Project Feasibility (1–2 weeks)

Before formally submitting your application, you need to confirm the following:

1. **Confirm enterprise eligibility**
   - Obtain a certificate of registered matters (registry transcript) from the Japan Legal Affairs Bureau
   - Confirm employee headcount and prior-year revenue
   - Confirm whether there are any tax or social insurance arrears (arrears make approval impossible)

2. **Sort out the DX project content**
   - Identify your current business pain points (e.g., slow order processing, chaotic inventory management, no online sales channel)
   - Decide on the digitalization approach (off-the-shelf software or custom development?)
   - Estimate the total investment
   - Assess the payback period

3. **Consult an IT service provider or system integrator**
   - Obtain a detailed project quotation (invoice/estimate)
   - Understand the implementation timeline (usually 6–12 months)
   - Clarify the scope of after-sales support

### Step 2: Prepare Application Materials (2–3 weeks)

This is the step most prone to problems. Application materials must be written in Japanese and must meet the review standards of the subsidy committee. The main materials include:

**Required documents:**
- Application form – in the designated format, explaining your DX project in 200–400 characters
- Business plan – describing in detail the current problems, improvement measures, and expected effects, usually 500–800 characters
- The IT service provider's estimate and proposal
- Company profile and organizational structure
- A copy of the prior-year financial statements
- The representative's identity documents and a copy of their residence card

**Key material – how to write the business plan:**

This document is the most critical. Reviewers use it to judge whether your project is genuine, feasible, and effective. We recommend including the following:

- **Current-state analysis** (150 characters): Specifically list the digitalization problems in your current operations. Don't say vaguely "poor management"—give concrete numbers, such as "financial reports currently require manual preparation, taking 3 days each month; stocktaking requires 5 people working for 2 days; order entry has a 30% manual error rate."

- **Improvement measures** (200 characters): Explain how introducing the new system or new processes will specifically solve the above problems.

- **Expected effects** (150 characters): Describe the improvement outcomes quantitatively, such as "financial reporting time reduced from 3 days to 4 hours; stocktaking efficiency raised by 80%; order error rate reduced to below 3%."

- **Investment content** (100 characters): Following the IT service provider's proposal, explain what systems you will purchase, what custom development will be done, and what training is needed.

### Step 3: Submit the Application to the Subsidy Committee (1 day)

- Submit online (most regions now offer electronic applications)
- Or mail it to the designated Chamber of Commerce and Industry or subsidy administration office
- Keep the submission receipt (acceptance number)

### Step 4: Wait for Review and Initial Feedback (2–3 months)

- The subsidy committee conducts eligibility review and project scoring
- Some applications may be asked to supplement materials or undergo an interview
- Final result notification (approved, conditionally approved, or not approved)

### Step 5: Sign the Subsidy Grant Decision and Implement the Project (6–12 months)

- Sign the "Subsidy Grant Decision Notice" with the subsidy committee
- Implement the IT project according to plan, keeping all payment receipts and invoices
- Stay in communication with the IT service provider to ensure project progress

### Step 6: Submit the Completion Report and Subsidy Claim (after project completion)

- Assemble the project completion certification materials
- Write a project summary report describing the actual improvement outcomes
- Compile all cost payment receipts (VAT invoices, payment transfer receipts)
- Submit the subsidy claim form in order to have the subsidy deposited

**Key reminder**: From project launch to receiving the subsidy, the entire cycle usually takes 12–18 months. Enterprises must plan their cash flow well, because the subsidy is only disbursed after the project is complete, and the upfront costs must be advanced by the enterprise itself.

## Common Application Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

### Pitfall 1: Assuming you can claim IT investments already paid for

**Wrong approach**: The enterprise already spent 1 million yen last year building a website and now wants to retroactively claim a subsidy.

**Correct approach**: The DX subsidy must be applied for first, with investment made afterward. Only after the subsidy decision notice is issued can the enterprise launch the related IT investment. Projects already paid for do not qualify for the subsidy.

### Pitfall 2: Exaggerating improvement effects or falsifying data

Reviewers spot-check accepted enterprises to verify the actual implementation of the project. If false reporting is discovered, the subsidy will be revoked, the full amount already received must be repaid, and the enterprise will face administrative penalties. It is better to estimate effects conservatively than to exaggerate.

### Pitfall 3: Choosing a non-reputable IT service provider

Some unscrupulous IT companies will collude with enterprises to issue false invoices for profit. Such practices are extremely dangerous. You should choose a system integrator (SI company) or IT consulting firm that holds a proper business license and has a good reputation; their quotations will withstand review, and false invoices will be detected.

### Pitfall 4: Neglecting project inspection and effectiveness evaluation

After a DX subsidy project is complete, the subsidy committee will commission a third party to conduct an on-site or document-based inspection. The enterprise should prepare:
- Screenshots of the system in actual use
- Records of employee training
- Data comparisons (such as before-and-after comparison of financial reporting time)

If you cannot provide genuine proof of results, the subsidy may be partially cancelled.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: My enterprise was only established in Japan a year ago—can I still apply for the DX subsidy?

**A:** This depends on the specific subsidy policy requirements. Most DX subsidy programs require an enterprise to have been operating for at least one year and to submit proof of the prior year's revenue (such as the previous year's tax return or an accounting audit report). If your enterprise was just established, you usually need to wait until you meet the conditions before applying. However, we recommend consulting your local Chamber of Commerce and Industry or Bureau of Economy, Trade and Industry directly, because some regions have dedicated subsidy programs for startups with potentially more flexible conditions. In addition, if your enterprise is a branch or affiliate of a domestic Japanese company with the financial backing of a parent company, some subsidy schemes may take the parent company's operating history into account. In short, don't give up on applying just because your enterprise is young—consult first, then decide.

### Q2: Will applying for the DX subsidy have any adverse effect on the enterprise's taxes or social insurance?

**A:** Absolutely not. The DX subsidy is support funding provided by the government for a policy objective; by nature it is a "subsidy" (policy subsidy), not a loan, and does not need to be repaid. Receiving the subsidy will not increase the enterprise's tax burden or social insurance costs. It should be noted that the subsidy itself must be treated as income when filing corporate income tax (classified under the "non-inclusion of subsidies, etc. in taxable income" rules), but national policy has already taken this into account and provides corresponding tax preferences for subsidies. The only thing the enterprise needs to do is keep all project implementation records and cost payment receipts for inspection by the tax office and the subsidy committee. As for social insurance, receiving a subsidy does not increase social insurance contribution obligations. However, if the enterprise achieves profit growth or headcount increases through digital reform, this may naturally lead to higher social insurance costs—but that is the result of improved business performance, not a side effect of the subsidy. We recommend consulting your accountant or tax accountant (Zeirishi) before applying; they will give you the most accurate advice.

### Q3: What if costs rise during the IT project implementation and exceed the original planned budget?

**A:** This is very common in IT projects. First, it must be clear that the subsidy amount is fixed at the time the "Subsidy Grant Decision Notice" is issued and generally will not be increased simply because project costs rise. There is, however, some room for adjustment within a certain range. Suppose you originally planned to invest 3 million yen and had 2.25 million yen in subsidy approved. If the actual cost rises to 3.3 million yen (a 300,000 yen overrun), you will need to raise more of your own funds to cover that additional 300,000 yen. The subsidy, however, is still disbursed at 2.25 million yen, unchanged. To avoid this risk, we recommend: (1) choosing an experienced IT service provider at the application stage and asking them for a clear fixed-price quotation (fixed-price contract); (2) clearly defining the project scope and the charges for additional work in the contract with the IT service provider; (3) reserving a 5–10% contingency budget. If project costs do rise substantially (by more than 20%), you can apply to change the subsidy plan, but this requires submitting a change application to the subsidy committee, the approval of which is fairly strict, so it is not advisable to rely on it.

### Q4: I want to apply for the DX subsidy, but my company is very small (only 3 employees)—do I qualify?

**A:** Most DX subsidy programs set the eligibility threshold at "SME," and under Japan's definition, a manufacturing enterprise with no more than 300 employees and capital of no more than 300 million yen qualifies as an "SME." Your 3-person enterprise fully meets this standard. In practice, however, some regions or specific subsidy programs may have an implicit preference for scale—reviewers may lean toward supporting enterprises that already have a certain scale and can more quickly show significant improvement outcomes. But this is not a hard restriction. A 3-person enterprise with a clear, highly feasible digital transformation project and clearly defined improvement effects has every chance of being approved. For example, a Chinese sole proprietor in Tokyo running an online shop and a physical store applied for a 200,000 yen system upgrade subsidy and was successfully approved. The key is that your business plan must clearly explain why you need to digitalize and what specific improvements digitalization will bring to this small team. Don't sell yourself short just because your enterprise is small.

### Q5: How long does it take for the subsidy to actually be deposited? Can I advance my own funds to start the project during the application period?

**A:** This is a cash-flow concern many business owners have. From approval to actual deposit of the subsidy, the entire cycle usually takes 12–24 months, specifically: application–review (2–3 months) – subsidy decision – project implementation (6–12 months) – completion report submission – inspection review (1–2 months) – subsidy claim – final disbursement (2–4 weeks). In total, from application to receiving the money takes at least 12 months and, at the slower end, possibly more than 20 months. During this period, the enterprise must advance its own funds. To cope with this, we recommend: (1) ensuring the enterprise has sufficient liquid funds; (2) considering negotiating staged payments with the IT service provider—for example, 30% at signing, 40% mid-project, and 30% on completion—to ease the pressure of a one-time outlay; (3) some banks or credit institutions offer "subsidy-anticipation guaranteed loans," allowing the enterprise to apply for a loan to advance funds once it has the subsidy decision, then repay the loan with the subsidy. We recommend doing your financial planning well before applying for the subsidy, ensuring a 3–6 month capacity to advance funds.
